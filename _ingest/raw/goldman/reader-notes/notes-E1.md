**David Solomon (Chairman and CEO): views shift from 2025 to 2026.**
- **Jan 2025:**
  - Compute is "very expensive", so GS picks use cases where it can "move the needle".
  - Engineering productivity is the obvious first use case: work that took "a handful of engineers weeks" now takes very little time.
  - Knowledge workers come second.
  - Enterprise process change is harder than individual productivity. CEOs are "slow and prudent".
- **Jan 2026:**
  - The technology is "not losing steam", but enterprise deployment may be slower than expected.
  - He rejects the job-apocalypse view. The pace of change, not its nature, is what's new.
  - He plays down the scope of OneGS 3.0: it covers six processes first, then "probably six more". KYC and onboarding is the named one.
  - Efficiency is framed as capacity to reinvest in growth, not as a cut to headcount.
  - The Sept 2026 Bellevue release repeats the "people are our greatest asset" line.

**Osman Ali (global co-head, QIS, GSAM).** Main points:

- Over 50% of 12-month stock returns come from what the market thinks, not fundamentals.

- LLMs improve price discovery in small caps and EM, but herding creates new, predictable inefficiencies.
- Team size held at about 100: "Net Net is larger certainly, but it's not materially larger".

> "I think investing is a zero sum game." — Osman Ali, co-head QIS (2026-05-01, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/will-ai-make-markets-less-efficient)

> "something that will on net make the markets probably less efficient and more predictable" — Osman Ali (2026-05-01, same URL)

> "more than 50% of what we think drives the stock's return over the next 12 months is not the fundamentals of the business." — Osman Ali (2026-05-01, same URL)

> "So we are about a 100 person team around the world." — Osman Ali (2026-05-01, same URL)

**Kash Rangan and Eric Sheridan (GS Research).** The 2023 episode was euphoric: "copilots", knowledge work in the "middle of the bell curve" being automated, software rising from 50bp to 350bp of US GDP. By June 2024 they are still bullish but more measured:
- Rangan says no "killer application" has appeared yet, and argues infrastructure comes first, then platforms, then applications (his "IPA" sequence). He says incumbent-led capex is less risky than the 1990s cycle.
- Sheridan says capex as a share of revenue (cloud companies spend more than 30% of cloud revenue on capex) is in line with past cycles. He would get worried if scaled consumer apps don't appear within 6-18 months.

> "We'll all have copilots." — Kash Rangan, Software Research (2023-02-13, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/02-21-2023-sheridan-rangan)

> "Spending is certainly high today in absolute dollar terms. But this capex cycle seems more promising than even previous capex cycles." — Kash Rangan (2024-06-25, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/top-of-mind/gen-ai-too-much-spend-too-little-benefit)

> "I’m loathe to use the word “bubble” because I don’t believe that AI is a bubble" — Eric Sheridan, Internet Research (2024-06-25, same URL)

**Joseph Briggs (GS economics).** Estimates gen AI will automate 25% of tasks and add 9% (9.2%) to TFP and 6.1% to GDP over the decade, built on 15% gross labor-productivity upside. Assumptions about task exposure and reallocation explain more than 80% of the gap with Acemoglu. He has raised the US GDP forecast by only 0.4pp by 2034 and admits the macro gains could come later than people expect.

**Allison Nathan** hosts every podcast and edits Top of Mind. She keeps pushing guests on hype, on jobs and on whether teams are smaller.

## 5. Tailwinds / headwinds / challenges

**Tailwinds:**
- **Engineering productivity on a 12,000-engineer base (ADMITTED).** Solomon calls it the "most obvious" use case and says GS would hire more engineers if returns allowed.
- **Falling token costs and Jevons paradox (ADMITTED: Lee, Posnett).** Cheaper AI should open new use cases at GS.
- **Deregulation, an M&A and IPO rebound, and the AI capex boom feeding banking fees (ADMITTED).** Solomon says 2026 "could be one of the best M&A years ever". Posnett expects more AI-related strategic M&A.
- **Proprietary data as a moat in quant investing (ADMITTED: Ali).** "we have an enormous amount of data", built up over 37 years.
- **AI capacity redeployed to growth rather than cuts (ADMITTED).** This also neutralizes headline risk about job losses. (INFERRED)

**Headwinds:**
- **Process re-engineering is hard and slow (ADMITTED).** Solomon says OneGS 3.0 is "hard work" and needs changes to "the human capital we use around that process".
- **Compute cost (ADMITTED, 2025).** Compute is "very expensive". Covello's in-house finding: AI updated model data at "six times the cost" of doing it manually.
- **Enterprise adoption lag (ADMITTED: Lee, Solomon 2026).** "harder to get the technology deployed in the enterprise."
- **Regulatory and compliance constraints on model use (ADMITTED).** These shape how the AI Assistant was built.
- **Power and chips constrain the client ecosystem (ADMITTED by GS Research).** HBM undersupply runs 3%/2%/1% in 2024/25/26. CoWoS packaging is a bottleneck. Interconnection queues run 40-70 months.
- **Cyber and geopolitics (ADMITTED).** Solomon's "what keeps you up" list is the same in 2025 and 2026.
- **Efficiency gains may be "competed away" (Covello's argument; INFERRED risk for GS).** Every rival bank has the same tools, so AI may lower costs without raising margins.
- **Talent competition with Big Tech (INFERRED).** Senior hires keep coming from AWS, Google and Amazon AGI, and GS opened an office next to their campuses.
- **Crowded valuations and an AI-bubble risk to markets franchises (ADMITTED by Research).** Shiller P/E is in the 97th percentile. Only the most bullish AI scenario delivers above-average S&P returns (Mueller-Glissmann).

## 6. Outside voices / skeptics

**Daron Acemoglu (MIT, outside).** His arithmetic: about 20% of tasks are exposed to AI, and 23% of those are cost-effective to automate within 10 years, so AI touches 4.6% of all tasks. With 27% cost savings, that gives roughly 0.53-0.66% TFP and 0.9% GDP over the decade.
- He left out the GitHub Copilot study showing 56% savings because its HTML task was too simple.
- He doubts scaling laws.
- He gives low odds of superintelligence even over 30 years.
- He says new task creation is "not a law of nature".
- He sees the risks of moving too fast in 2024 as asymmetric and favors more regulation.

> "these truly transformative changes won’t happen quickly and few—if any—will likely occur within the next 10 years." — Daron Acemoglu, MIT (2024-06-25, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/top-of-mind/gen-ai-too-much-spend-too-little-benefit)

> "But that impact is not a law of nature." — Daron Acemoglu (2024-06-25, same URL)

> "That’s why it’s important to resist the hype and take a somewhat cautious approach" — Daron Acemoglu (2024-06-25, same URL)

**Jim Covello (GS Head of Global Equity Research; an internal skeptic, included here).** His arguments:
- At about $1tn of build-out cost, AI has to solve complex problems, and it "isn’t designed to".
- The internet was cheap from day one; that technology starts expensive and gets cheap is "revisionist history".
- Nvidia's monopoly (compare ASML) may block cost declines.
- Summarization output is often "illegible and nonsensical".
- Cost savings get arbitraged away, so there is no multiple expansion.
- He nonetheless says stay long chips, then utilities, then hyperscalers, because "bubbles can take a long time to burst".
- He gave enthusiasm 12-18 months (from June 2024). Corporate profitability is the variable to watch.

**Lee's later framing:** Lee's Jan 2025 rebuttal says DeepSeek "answers some of Jim's principal concerns". It is a running internal debate that GS broadcasts deliberately.

> "So, the crucial question is: What $1tn problem will AI solve?" — Jim Covello, Head of Global Equity Research (2024-06-25, same URL)

> "The idea that technology typically starts out expensive before becoming cheaper is revisionist history." — Jim Covello (2024-06-25, same URL)

> "we’ve found that AI can update historical data in our company models more quickly than doing so manually, but at six times the cost." — Jim Covello (2024-06-25, same URL)

> "I’ve never seen a stock decline only because it’s expensive" — Jim Covello (2024-06-25, same URL)

**Brian Janous (Cloverleaf Infrastructure; former Microsoft VP of Energy).**
- Utilities have had no load growth in about 20 years.
- AEP has 80-90 GW of load requests, of which about 15 GW is real.
- The grid needs more than 100 GW added to roughly 800 GW at peak.
- Dublin has a de facto moratorium on new data centers until 2028.
- Data-center PUE is already about 1.1, so little efficiency is left to extract.
- The US has "lost the ability to build". Talen/Susquehanna unlocked no new power.

> "this is a task better suited for 1930s America, not 2030s America." — Brian Janous, Cloverleaf (2024-06-25, same URL)

**AI Forward 2023 guests (sizzle-reel lines only):**
- Bill Gates: "one company that creates the personal agent". This anticipates the 2025 agent talk.
- Greg Brockman: "keep letting reality hit yourself in the face".
- Others: Blaise Agüera y Arcas ("Promethean moment"), Daphne Koller (digital biology) and Ron Conway (policy-makers).

## 7. Market-structure links

- **Platforms in client execution (2019).** The hiring email explicitly ties the tech strategy to "digital platforms that seamlessly integrate execution, analytics and content". This is the Marquee-style, platform-first framing of the Argenti era, though Marquee is not named.
- **AI and market efficiency (2026; the strongest market-structure item).** Osman Ali's thesis covers four points:
  1. **Small caps and EM:** LLMs scale price discovery into these corners and make them more efficient.
  2. **Retail, passive and hedgers:** the clearing price now comes from price-insensitive passive money, sentiment-driven retail, hedgers and alpha-seekers. That mix leaves room for alpha.
  3. **Herding:** identical LLM answers cause crowding, which produces predictable reversion. That favors quants who model "the investor's psyche", including what tools retail investors use.
  4. **Autonomous funds:** Lee asks about "fully autonomous investing machines" as a possible new asset class. Ali answers with the zero-sum argument.
- **Dealer and platform effects (INFERRED from Solomon and Ali).** AI's biggest stated internal targets are back and middle office (KYC and onboarding) and engineering, not trading desks. The bucket has no claims about AI in market-making or FICC electronification.
- **Named counterparties.** The bucket names no trading venue, dealer platform or tokenization rail (Tradeweb, MarketAxess, ICE, CME, Canton and the like). The counterparties it does name are tech and AI vendors and adjacent firms:
  - Nvidia, TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, ASML and AMD (chip supply chain)
  - Hyperscalers: AWS, Azure, Google and Meta
  - OpenAI, Anthropic (Claude Computer Use) and DeepSeek
  - ServiceNow (80% faster resolution time), Salesforce, SAP and Oracle
  - Utilities: AEP, EirGrid and Talen
- **Data economy as market structure (Posnett).** She expects data-licensing deals (publishers, social media, stock photography), synthetic data marketplaces and personal-data marketplaces. This is a possible new asset or venue category.

## 8. Surprises / note-worthy theses

1. **GS publishes its own internal AI argument.** The Head of Global Equity Research (Covello) is a public AI skeptic while the former CIO (Lee) is the public bull, and the firm stages their debate across Top of Mind and the podcasts. This is good study material on how a bank manages credibility: hedge the house view, and sell research on both sides of a capex supercycle that GS also earns banking fees from.
2. **Solomon plays down OneGS 3.0.** In Jan 2026 he calls the reimagine-the-firm label bigger than the reality ("narrower than that grand vision") and names KYC and onboarding as the first of six processes. This is a deliberate expectations reset, useful for judging later productivity claims. Also notable: he frames AI as capacity for growth with roughly flat headcount, and the engineering count is "12,000" in both Jan 2025 and Sept 2026. That flat number fits his "redeploy, don't cut" message.
3. **AI could make markets *less* efficient.** Ali's herding thesis runs against the consensus view, which Lee acknowledges. The implication for market structure: if retail investors and LLM agents converge on the same answers, reversion becomes a harvestable factor. Firms that model AI-driven flows gain an edge. The team size held at about 100 is a concrete data point on AI and headcount.
4. **The power-versus-data bottleneck flips by early 2025.** Posnett says power now matters more than data. Janous (2024) says the debate "has now been settled" in favor of power. GS Research is consistent: data centers rise from 3% to 8% of US power by 2030, needing 47 GW of new capacity and roughly $50bn for generation. This connects to GS's commodities, utilities-banking and infrastructure franchises. (INFERRED)
5. **How close the predictions came.** Rangan's "we'll all have copilots" (Feb 2023) and Gates's "personal agent" (May 2023) foreshadowed Lee's hands-on agent testing in Jan 2025. Meanwhile Covello's 12-18-month deadline for compelling use cases, set in June 2024, passed without the enthusiasm fading. Investor enthusiasm through Solomon's Jan 2026 "not losing steam" reading suggests the skeptics were wrong on timing so far. That doesn't settle the ROI question; Solomon himself flags possible "recalibrations".
6. **Senior engineering hires come from Big Tech, and the bucket shows no reverse flow.** AWS, Verizon/Yahoo, Google (twice) and Amazon AGI supply the leaders, and the Bellevue office is effectively an AWS/Microsoft talent play. Kotsovinos's AI-safety background, hired into AWM, suggests governance of client-facing AI in wealth management is a priority. (INFERRED)
