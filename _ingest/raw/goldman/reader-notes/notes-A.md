## 4. Mission / identity statements (evolution)

- **2019-2020 (L19, L20, ID20):** PURPOSE "We advance sustainable economic growth and financial opportunity"; POSITIONING "...mobilize our people, culture, technologies and ideas to advance the success of our clients, broaden individual prosperity and accelerate economic progress for all." Core values distilled to four: Partnership, Client Service, Integrity, Excellence (2019 introduced; Malaysia cited under Integrity).
- **2021 (L21):** no purpose box; replaced by "Our Strategy to Produce Higher, More Consistent Returns."
- **2022-2023 (L22, L23):** Purpose rewritten: "We aspire to be the world’s most exceptional financial institution, united by our shared values of partnership, client service, integrity and excellence." Societal language ("sustainable economic growth," "prosperity... for all") disappears.
- **2023 (L23):** identity = "a preeminent global investment bank"; three strategic objectives: Harness One GS; Run world-class, differentiated and durable businesses; Invest to operate at scale. Desired outcomes: trusted advisor, employer of choice, mid-teens returns through the cycle.
- **2024 (L24):** core values only; no purpose statement.
- **2025 (L25):** values anchored to history — the 14 Business Principles "written by John Whitehead in 1979."
- Sustainability ($750bn: ~$150bn 2020, ~$300bn 2021, ~55% by 2022) and D&I content are prominent 2019-22 and essentially absent from the 2023-25 letters (INFERRED de-emphasis; no explicit retraction in text).

## 8. Surprises / note-worthy theses

1. **Purpose statement was rewritten as the consumer bet died.** "Advance sustainable economic growth and financial opportunity" (2019-20) became "the world’s most exceptional financial institution" (2022), then gave way to the 1979 Whitehead principles (2025). Identity moved from societal purpose to excellence/heritage. Good case for mission drift following strategy.
2. **The consumer admission is one sentence.** Across seven letters, the retreat from the "digital consumer bank of the future" gets one line in 2022, a euphemism in 2023, silence in 2024, and a footnote for the Apple Card exit in 2025. Useful lesson on how CEOs manage narrative around failed diversification. The success metric was later redefined as "durable revenues" = financing + AWM fees, which excludes consumer.
3. **"Durable revenue" moved from new businesses to the old dealer.** In 2020 durability was supposed to come from TxB/consumer/alts. By 2025 the biggest durability story is FICC/Equities financing ($11.4bn, 37% of markets revenue): prime, repo, secured lending. That is balance-sheet intermediation, not platform software. The "raise the floor" thesis (Dees, 2023) underpins this.
4. **Electronic market-making figures appear only in 2020.** The 99.9%/80%/75% electronic stats and the market-structure breakout appear once and never again in the letters, which talk about "intermediation" and "financing." Electronic execution is treated as table stakes, while financing and the client wallet (top-150 franchise) are what GS sells to shareholders.
5. **Targets as a governance device.** Public targets, KPI tracking (">30 KPIs... meet or exceed 95 percent"), resegmentation and PSU-only pay for the Management Committee form a deliberate accountability system. Watch the quiet moving of goalposts (net → gross alts; top 100 → top 150; "through the cycle" language once 2022-23 ROE fell short).
6. **The private-credit pivot sets up its own risk.** In 2024 Solomon calls private credit the "fulcrum" trend and forms Capital Solutions. In 2025 he warns about private-credit underwriting and AI-exposed software borrowers. The firm is long the theme on both the origination and the asset-management sides.
