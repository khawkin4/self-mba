## 2. Key events

- **2017-18**: GS first explores its own digital custody offering and a crypto trading desk, then shelves the plan because the market was "exclusively retail led" (McDermott, item 2). https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/06-01-2021-crypto-a-new-asset-class
- **Early 2021**: GS co-leads the EIB's first digital bond: €100mn, 2-year, on public Ethereum, issued under French law, with SocGen and Santander. It settled T+1 versus the usual T+5, without a CSD or custodian. Cash leg: per McDermott in 2022, the bond settled on-chain DvP against a "synthetic CBDC", i.e. tokenized cash held at the central bank. https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/from_briefings_10-june-2021
- **Mar 2021**: GS crypto trading desk goes live, "narrow initially": CME futures and cash-settled NDFs. Bitcoin content is distributed on Marquee. https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/03-05-2021-mathew-mcdermott
- **Mar 2021**: GS client survey: ~280-300 respondents, 40% have crypto exposure, 61% expect holdings to rise, 76% see BTC at $40-100K by year-end, 22% see >$100K (same URL).
- **May 2021**: TOM #98 (https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/top-of-mind/crypto-a-new-asset-class). McDermott lays out GS's planned product build:
  - NDFs and CME BTC/ETH futures (ETH on an agency basis)
  - principal trading in CME BTC futures and BTC-linked securities
  - synthetic long/short BTC ETNs in Europe via prime brokerage
  - lending structures and structured notes
  - wealth-management access via fund or note products
- **May 2022**: TerraUSD collapse. McDermott says GS welcomes regulation "as a regulated bank". He says GS is investing in "blockchain infra" alongside Growth Equity and the Firmwide Strategy Group. https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/05-18-22-mathew-mcdermott
- **2022-11-03**: Datonomy launched with MSCI (owner and sole administrator) and Coin Metrics. It is sold as a data feed and announced by the head of Marquee Client Strategy. https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2022/introducing-datonomy-11-03-2022
- **Dec 2022**: TOM #114 on FTX, Luna and Celsius. Crypto market cap is down ~70% from the May-2021 peak to below $900bn. BTC/ETH volumes are down ~65%. DeFi TVL is down ~80% to ~$42bn. The hit to US investors is ~$500bn of a ~$1.5tn decline. https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/top-of-mind/the-winter-of-cryptos-discontents
- **2023-04-17**: Marquee Visual Structuring expands to crypto options. https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2023/gs-expands-visual-structuring-into-equities-crypto-and-precious-metals
- **2024-11-18**: GS announces it will explore a spin-out of GS DAP into "an industry-owned distributed technology solution", subject to regulatory approval. Tradeweb is the first strategic partner, bringing fixed income trading and liquidity. DAP "leverages solutions developed by Digital Asset". https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2024/announcement-18-nov-2024
- **Jun 2025**: IB piece. Digital-asset M&A was $15.8B in 2024 versus $1B in 2019, and $6.4B YTD 2025. Stablecoin supply is ~$220bn, ~1% of US M2. https://www.goldmansachs.com/what-we-do/investment-banking/insights/articles/why-digital-asset-adoption-is-accelerating
- **2025-07-23**: BNY and GS tokenized MMFs. BNY keeps the official books and records, and "mirror tokens" sit on GS DAP. It is described as the first time in the US this has been done. https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/bny-goldman-sachs-launch-tokenized-money-market-funds-solution
- **Summer 2025**: GENIUS Act is signed into law, the first federal stablecoin framework. Circle IPOs. https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/after-a-summer-of-stablecoins-whats-next

## 3. People & views

**Mathew McDermott (Global Head of Digital Assets, GS).** He is the constant voice from 2021 to 2025, and his framing shifts in three steps:
- **2021, crypto access desk.** His justification is "client demand, pure and simple". Bitcoin is an emerging asset class. The constraints are the ban on US banks trading physical crypto, mandates, and access. Trading-desk talk dominates: basis trades, CME futures, NDFs.
- **2022, digital assets as infrastructure.** After Terra he moves away from price and redefines "digital assets" to include tokenized traditional assets and digital money. He stresses four benefits: precision/atomic settlement, risk reduction, transparency/fractionalization, and economic efficiency. He also concedes that crypto behaved as a risk-on asset, not an inflation hedge. He is positive on both private and public chains, "with various safeguards".
- **2024-25, permissioned rails.** The language becomes "permissioned distributed technologies", industry-owned infrastructure, collateral mobility, and MMF tokens as collateral. Crypto-linked trading continues, but tokenization and collateral now lead the story.

## 4. Mission / identity statements

- **GS Digital Assets boilerplate (2024 and 2025):** "focused on accelerating the potential adoption of digital assets and a blockchain-powered financial system through accessibility, education, and the development of an innovative product offering". It offers "capital markets solutions using blockchain technology (including GS DAP®) and a suite of crypto-linked trading products."
- **DAP spin-out rationale:** a standalone company "independent of Goldman Sachs" will "provide the future runway for digital financial services by ensuring a fit-for-purpose, long-term solution". GS signals that it will not own the rails outright.
- **McDermott 2025:** the strategy has three legs: "client-led tokenization activities, crypto-linked product offerings, and blockchain investments", plus traditional IB services to the sector. He calls it "Our multifaceted strategy is driven by client demand".
- **McDermott 2022:** GS welcomes regulation "as a regulated bank", which positions regulated status as a competitive moat.

## 5. Tailwinds / headwinds / challenges

**Tailwinds**
- Institutional demand in 2021: hedge funds, macro, corporate treasurers (negative rates), private wealth (ADMITTED). Liquidity rose: BTC spot ~$300mn to ~$1.5bn per day, CME BTC futures ~$200mn to ~$2bn per day, April 2020 to April 2021 (ADMITTED).
- The 2025 US regulatory pivot (ADMITTED, McDermott 2025):
  - Trump executive orders and a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve
  - SEC rescinds SAB 121, which "lowering the onerous regulatory capital requirements" on custody
  - SAB 122, the GENIUS and STABLE Acts, EU MiCA, the UK Digital Securities Sandbox, Hong Kong's Stablecoin Bill, the UAE
- Spot ETF approval: $IBIT was the most successful ETF launch ever (ADMITTED, Coupal).
- Collateral mobility, 24/7 markets and stablecoins as a collateral cog (ADMITTED). The BCG/Ripple $18.9tn tokenized-RWA-by-2033 forecast is cited approvingly. *Flag: this is a vendor forecast, not a GS one.*

**Headwinds**
- US banks barred from trading physical crypto in 2021. GS works around it with cash-settled derivatives (ADMITTED).
- "inconsistent regulatory actions around the globe" and fraud, especially in hot storage (ADMITTED, 2021).
- Crypto proved a risk-on asset, not an inflation hedge. BTC tracked the Nasdaq, down ~30% YTD in 2022 (ADMITTED, McDermott 2022).
  > "And perhaps a little less than the inflationary hedge" — Mathew McDermott (2022-05-18, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/05-18-22-mathew-mcdermott)
- Valuations "a little out of kilter" in crypto VC in 2022 (ADMITTED).
- The 2022 winter: Luna, Celsius, FTX. Market cap down ~70% (ADMITTED in GIR data).
- (INFERRED) The firm's tone shifted from price and trading (2021) to infrastructure and permissioned DLT (2022 onward). The pivot followed the crypto crash and the regulatory hostility of 2022-23. The bucket has **no 2023 or early-2024 strategy statements**, a visible silence during the Gensler-era SEC.
- (INFERRED) The DAP spin-out suggests a single-bank-owned ledger can't win network adoption. Competitors and clients won't onboard to a Goldman-owned rail, so GS mutualizes it. This mirrors the history of Tradeweb itself and of other industry consortia.
- (INFERRED) "Mirrored record tokenization" means BNY keeps the official books and the token is a mirror. Tokenization is therefore additive, not yet a replacement for transfer-agent infrastructure.

## 6. Outside voices / skeptics

- **Michael Novogratz, Galaxy (2021, bull).** Crypto is an "official asset class". Payments, DeFi and NFTs will be built on Ethereum. He predicted Gensler would deliver a clear framework "within his first nine months". *Flag: this proved wrong; the enforcement era followed.* He also said "Uniswap could become a bigger exchange than the CME or the NYSE".
- **Nouriel Roubini, NYU (2021, the main skeptic).** Crypto is neither a currency nor an asset. He calls enterprise DLT "BINO". His argument reads as a direct critique of GS's later permissioned-DLT strategy:
  > "The idea that technology can resolve the question of trust is delusional." — Nouriel Roubini, NYU Stern (2021-05-24, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/top-of-mind/crypto-a-new-asset-class)
  > "Any treasurer who invests in something that falls 15% in value overnight will be fired." — Nouriel Roubini (2021-05-24, same URL)
- **Michael Sonnenshein, Grayscale (2021, bull).** GBTC is a closed-end-like OTC product. The remaining gaps are "plumbing": prime-broker access and an ETF.
- **Alan Cohen, ex-SEC senior policy advisor to Clayton and former GS Global Head of Compliance (2021).** Regulation is multi-agency. He covers the Howey test, Telegram/Kik/Ripple, and the ETF prerequisite of fair underlying prices. He warns that bearer-like anonymity will be resisted:
  > "the anonymity promised by some crypto assets will inevitably meet intense regulatory opposition" — Alan Cohen, former SEC Senior Policy Advisor (2021-05-24, same URL)
- **Dan Guido, Trail of Bits (2021).** About half of 2,000 new Ethereum contracts had known vulnerabilities, and DeFi usually has centralized backdoors.
  > "It’s a wild west out there for developers building applications on the blockchain." — Dan Guido, CEO Trail of Bits (2021-05-24, same URL)
- **Michael Gronager, Chainalysis (2021).** Illicit activity was 0.34% of transactions in 2020, down from ~2% in 2019. Blockchain transparency helps law enforcement.
- **Jay Clayton, ex-SEC chair (2022, cover quote only):**
  > "The collapse of FTX is ﬁrstly an age-old lesson that unregulated markets are dangerous." — Jay Clayton, former SEC Chairman (2022-12-13, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/top-of-mind/the-winter-of-cryptos-discontents)
- **Timothy Massad, ex-CFTC chair (2022).** Compares state crypto regulation to pre-1929 blue-sky laws:
  > "Saying that US crypto entities are well-regulated by state" — Timothy Massad, former CFTC Chairman (2022-12-13, same URL); the sentence continues past a line break.
- **Marcel Kasumovich, One River (2022).** The problem is "the absence of regulatory clarity", not of regulation. *Flag: the scrape splits this quote across columns.*
- **Gary Gorton, Yale (2022).** Contagion was avoided only because "blockchain technology isn’t currently interoperable with the real world", and that will change. This implies tokenization rails could reintroduce contagion.
- **Brian Brooks, ex-acting OCC head and Strategy board member (2025, pro).** Use cases ranked: offshore dollar savings first, remittances second, payments last. The GENIUS Act mirrors bank supervision. USDC/Tether will dominate, while bank tokens aim at cheaper funding and loyalty programs.
  > "There's just a sense that supervision equals safety." — Brian Brooks, former Acting Comptroller of the Currency (2025-09-02, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/after-a-summer-of-stablecoins-whats-next)
  > "Well, it depends on if you trust your betters or if you'd rather be free." — Brian Brooks, on CBDCs vs stablecoins (2025-09-02, same URL)
- **Barry Eichengreen, Berkeley (2025, skeptic).** Points to free-banking-era notes, the 2008 MMF "breaking the buck" and SVB. Stablecoins threaten singleness of money, and Treasury demand is only "marginal".
  > "So, what's at risk is what economists refer to as a singleness of money." — Barry Eichengreen, UC Berkeley (2025-09-02, same URL)
  > "There is nothing like a deposit insurance fund for stablecoins." — Barry Eichengreen (2025-09-02, same URL)

## 7. Market-structure links

- **Tradeweb.** First strategic partner for GS DAP (Nov 2024). It brings "trading and liquidity capabilities across the fixed income spectrum". Chris Bruner (Tradeweb CPO) talks of "access, liquidity, and interoperability". This is the clearest dealer–venue tie-up in the bucket: a dealer-built ledger plus a dealer-owned electronic venue.
- **Digital Asset (Canton's developer).** DAP "leverages solutions developed by Digital Asset" (2024 and 2025 releases). *Ambiguity: Canton Network is never named in these texts; the link is only implied.*
- **BNY.** LiquidityDirect (MMF portal) plus the BNY Digital Assets platform are integrated with GS DAP. BNY remains the official record-keeper. Five fund managers take part: BlackRock, BNY Investments Dreyfus, Federated Hermes, Fidelity, GSAM. This is a cross-bank (GS/BNY) build, not a single-firm one.
- **MSCI and Coin Metrics.** Datonomy is a GICS-style taxonomy for coins. MSCI owns and administers it, and it is distributed via GS developer/Marquee. It is GS's data/platform play in crypto.
- **Marquee.** Bitcoin research content (2021), Datonomy (2022), Visual Structuring for crypto options (2023).
- **CME.** GS's first crypto products were CME BTC/ETH futures. CME BTC futures activity was up ~900% year on year in April 2021.
- **EIB with SocGen and Santander.** Multi-dealer digital bond on public Ethereum. French law removed the need for a CSD or custodian, which is a direct threat to the CSD/custodian layer (McDermott hedges: "they could continue to interplay").
- **Exchange M&A (2025).** Coinbase–Deribit, Robinhood–Bitstamp, Stripe–Bridge: crypto venues consolidating.
- **Paxos.** McDermott notes it sits behind PayPal's bitcoin payments, converting to fiat.
- **Prime brokerage.** In 2021 clients' top request was spot crypto access via prime brokers. GS's answer was synthetic ETN long/short exposure.

## 8. Surprises / note-worthy theses

1. **GS is giving away the rail.** A wholly owned platform built in-house is being spun out to be "industry-owned", with Tradeweb as first partner. *Study angle:* network-effect infrastructure in finance tends to end up mutualized (DTCC, Tradeweb's dealer origins, SWIFT). A bank that wants the rail adopted must give up control and take value from usage.
2. **Roubini's "BINO" critique against GS's 2024 "permissioned" thesis.** In 2021 Roubini dismissed private, permissioned ledgers as Blockchain In Name Only. By 2024 GS calls permissioned DLT "the next structural change". The *study* question is what permissioned tokenization delivers that a shared database cannot. GS's 2025 answer is collateral mobility, atomic settlement and 24/7 markets.
3. **The quiet tonal pivot from crypto as an asset class to tokenization as plumbing.** In 2021 McDermott talked basis trades and BTC price surveys. In 2025 he talks MMF tokens as collateral. Crypto-linked trading stays in the product list, but the narrative lead changes after the 2022 winter. Notably, the bucket has no GS strategy statements from 2023 to mid-2024.
4. **Tokenized MMFs are a collateral play, not a retail play.** Mirror tokens let MMF shares move as collateral. They compete with or complement stablecoins as the "cash leg" of tokenized markets. Brooks's point that banks issue tokens to lower funding costs connects to this.
5. **Gorton's warning on interoperability.** Crypto's 2022 collapse didn't spread *because* the rails were disconnected from TradFi. Projects like DAP, the BNY MMF tokens and Tradeweb integration are exactly the bridges that could carry contagion next time.
6. **GS Research was internally split in 2021.** Pandl compared bitcoin to gold, Currie saw it as a pro-cyclical network asset, and Currie/Sprogis expected BTC to lose its crown to ether. This was a real house debate, not a party line, and is useful for showing how sell-side research hedges a contested asset.
