## 7. Market-structure links

- **Clearing houses:** GS co-authored "A Path Forward for CCP Resilience, Recovery and Resolution". It asks for:
  - CCP own-capital contributions in two tranches of the default waterfall;
  - assessment rights capped at 1x default-fund contribution;
  - compensation for losses from recovery tools;
  - bail-in-able debt for resolution.

  Nicolas Friedman (GS Global Co-Head of Counterparty Risk): "Together, these recommendations form a path forward to aligning incentives and enhancing financial stability through even stronger CCPs". This is the clearest dealers-versus-infrastructure stance in the bucket.
- **Financing over flow:** Prime, portfolio financing, repo, mortgage lending and structured lending become the growth line. GS calls financing "durable", which suits the balance sheet more than electronic venues do. There are **no mentions of Tradeweb, MarketAxess, ICE, CME, Trumid, Marquee, tokenization or digital assets** anywhere in this bucket.
- **TxB as a platform:** "simple, seamless, API-based", "cloud-based global platform", payments into 160+ countries in 120+ currencies, and "unencumbered by legacy banking infrastructure". The 2026 releases describe transaction banking within GBM "Other". This suggests TxB was moved from Platform Solutions into GBM (INFERRED, unannounced in the bucket).
- **Consumer as platform-partnering:** Apple Card (launched 2019), the GM card and GreenSky point-of-sale lending. All were exited, and the Apple Card went to Chase, a direct competitor.
- **Discount window (March 2020):** GS used it jointly with other large banks as a signal of system liquidity.

## 8. Surprises / note-worthy theses

1. **Equities financing overtook intermediation in Q3 2026** ($3.37bn vs $3.08bn), and combined FICC and Equities financing was about 37% of 2025 markets revenue ($11.45bn of $31.06bn). GS's markets engine is increasingly a balance-sheet lending business wrapped around trading. That changes how to think about electronification risk to dealers: spread compression on venues matters less when prime and repo carry the growth.
2. **The consumer arc can be read straight from the boilerplate.** "Consumer banking" entered the corporate description in 2021 and left by late 2023. Losses came in sequence: Marcus in Q1 2023, GreenSky in Q2-Q4 2023, PFM in Q3 2023, GM card in 2024 and Apple Card in January 2026. Scherr, the consumer architect, retired in 2021 and was replaced by a capital-markets financier. The bucket gives a dated case study of strategy reversal.
3. **The SCB as a strategic variable.** It ran 6.7% to 6.6%, then 6.4%, 6.3%, 5.5%, 6.4% (disputed) and 6.2%, and finally 3.4%. The fall to 3.4% coincided with the dividend rising from $3.00 to $5.00 in two years and ROE reaching about 20% or more in 2026. Exiting consumer and principal investments was partly a play to lower stress losses. Solomon makes that link himself ("reduce our capital intensity").
4. **The 2020 targets were met late, via a different mix.** The >13% ROE target was missed in 2022-2023 (10.2% and 7.5%) and hit in 2025 (15.0%) and 2026 (about 20%). It was reached through GBM financing and AWM fees, not through the "newer businesses such as Transaction Banking and Consumer Banking" named in 2020.
5. **The governance lesson from 1MDB is spelled out:** clawbacks of about $174m, a reputational-risk committee with control-side members "empowered to stop any transaction", and certification of sovereign use of proceeds. This is rare candor in a press release and is useful for a study note on culture and controls.
6. **The 2025-2026 leadership pattern:** triumvirate co-heads everywhere, a "One Goldman Sachs" office run by two senior markets and banking relationship figures, and AWM private-credit leaders elevated to the Management Committee. This signals that succession depth and alternatives are the priorities, and a CAO now owns the AI-led "OneGS 3.0" operating overhaul.
