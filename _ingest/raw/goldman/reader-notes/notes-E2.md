## 3. People & views

**Marco Argenti (CIO; formerly AWS VP of Technology; formerly at Nokia).** He is the house evangelist, and his views move in clear steps:
- **2023:** knowledge revolution, hybrid AI, ROI focus.
- **2025:** hybrid human/AI workforce, the GS AI assistant, culture-aware agents, and "everybody becomes a manager".
- **Late 2025:** verifiability is the bottleneck, so redesign every process to be machine-checkable.
- **2026:** models become the operating system, agents bill by tokens, and power is the constraint.

His consistent counterweights are grounding, humans in the loop, and judgment.

> "The revolution here is the revolution of knowledge," — Marco Argenti, CIO (2023-04-18, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/ai-could-revolutionize-human-knowledge-goldman-sachs-marco-argenti-says)

> "Everyone I speak with has ROI in mind as almost the first-order priority." — Marco Argenti, CIO (2023-12-21, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/a-new-generation-of-ai-tools-and-models-is-emerging)

> "The first AI “layoffs” could eventually emerge, in which AI models will be replaced by better AI tools or humans if they perform poorly compared to their peers." — Marco Argenti, CIO (2025-01-09, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/what-to-expect-from-ai-in-2025-hybrid-workers-robotics-expert-models)

> "really like your desktop for AI." — Marco Argenti, CIO, on the GS AI assistant (2025-03-25, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/ai-exchanges-cio-marco-argenti-on-the-future-of-ai-in-the-workplace)

> "The failure mode here is not technological, it’s organizational." — Marco Argenti, CIO, Fortune op-ed (2025-07-09, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/fortune-we-must-prepare-ai-natives-to-shape-the-future-of-work)

> "We are looking at every single process in the firm right now and thinking about how AI could interact with this process," — Marco Argenti, CIO (2025-10-07, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/what-will-it-take-for-companies-to-capitalize-on-the-rise-of-ai)

> "As a result, those who own the models will own the new operating systems that power AI agents." — Marco Argenti, CIO (2026-01-22, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/what-to-expect-from-ai-in-2026-personal-agents-mega-alliances)

Other Argenti points (paraphrased, not quoted):
- **Elastic agent workforce.** GS could "surge agents" for quarter-end or earnings season, the way cloud servers scale.
- **Culture.** Agents hired from outside are like first-day employees: smarter but "not culturally smarter". GS will need Asimov-style "tenets" for corporate agents.
- **Live internal examples.** Draft research; Private Wealth Management portfolio analysis that summarizes dozens of positions with "a Goldman lens"; a translation tool tuned to GS financial language and acronyms.
- **Controls.** Grounding against sources, preventing prompt injection and data exfiltration, and seeking "mindful disruptors" rather than reckless ones.

**George Lee (Co-Head, GS Global Institute).** He frames the gap between how fast the technology improves and how fast enterprises adopt it. In 2024 he said "It’s hard to find an antagonist to that core theme." He expects shorter "troughs of disillusionment". In 2025 he pointed to an advantage for de novo firms over incumbents and to the "magic button hypothesis". In 2026 his line was "breeding faster horses versus building the automobile."

> "people are feeling compelled to invest, even without perfect clarity on ROI, because this is so competitively consequential." — George Lee, Co-Head GS Global Institute (2024-04-16, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/george-lee-in-conversation-with-tony-pasquariello-the-ai-revolution)

**Chris Churchman (Head of Marquee; co-chair of the GBM AI Working Group).** He is the practitioner-builder and the most concrete voice in the bucket on AI in markets.

> "we've gone from a world where users learn software to where software learns users." — Chris Churchman, Head of Marquee (2026-08-24, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/building-ai-systems-for-capital-markets)

> "But like a demo is judged on its best day, whereas a product is judged on its worst day, right?" — Chris Churchman, Head of Marquee (2026-08-24, same URL)

> "Don't bet against the model, basically. That's the key." — Chris Churchman, Head of Marquee (2026-08-24, same URL)

> "there's a huge danger here that in the era of AI, we outsource our reasoning to these models, and we have cognitive atrophy" — Chris Churchman, Head of Marquee (2026-08-24, same URL)

His arc of engineering eras runs: prompt engineering, then context engineering, then agentic engineering (now), then environment engineering, then "mandate engineering" ("what are AI allowed to do under whose authority, in whose name?"). He expects agent autonomy to grow once there is "an insurance market or a liability market" for agents.

> "The really important point to keep in mind is that disruption tends not to be a linear process." — Jan Hatzius, GS Research Chief Economist (2025-10-07, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/what-will-it-take-for-companies-to-capitalize-on-the-rise-of-ai)

**Jung Min (global co-head of TMT, Investment Banking).** "There are hundreds of use cases that are just as verifiable as software development". He sees the maker / checker / signer chain as ripe for agents and expects workforce automation "much faster than people think".

## 6. Outside voices / skeptics

- **Daron Acemoglu (MIT), July 2024.**
  - Only about 4.6% of tasks will be cost-effectively automated within 10 years, giving about 0.5% productivity and about 1% GDP gains.
  - He doubts scaling laws: what does "doubling data" or "doubling capabilities" mean?
  - He doubts superintelligence within 10 years, and possibly at all on the LLM architecture.
  - On the trillion-dollar spend: "I expect both will happen" (waste and seeds).
  - On the Autor finding that new technologies create new occupations: > "But that is not a law of nature." — Daron Acemoglu, MIT (2024-07-16, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/a-skeptical-look-at-ai-investment)
  - Paraphrase: he says it "takes a big leap of faith" to expect HAL-level intelligence from next-word prediction.
- **Jim Covello (GS Head of Global Equity Research; an inside skeptic, but the bucket's sharpest one).**
  - Quotes:
    > "What trillion-dollar problem is AI going to solve?" — Jim Covello, Head of Global Equity Research (2024-07-16, same URL)

    > "There's nothing about AI that's cheap today, right?" — Jim Covello (2024-07-16, same URL)

    > "So there's a huge FOMO element to this" — Jim Covello (2024-07-16, same URL)
  - **Thesis:** AI is an expensive technology meant to replace low-cost labor, which inverts the internet case of cheap replacing expensive.
  - **Nvidia bottleneck:** a single-supplier choke point, like ASML in lithography, means costs may not fall.
  - **Failed hype precedents:** VR, blockchain, metaverse.
  - **Investor advice:** keep buying infrastructure, because "bubbles take a long time to burst". Watch corporate profits, since negative-ROI experiments are cut first.
  - **In-house rebuttal:** Allison Nathan cites Kash Rangan and Eric Sheridan as seeing it very differently, though Sheridan would worry if no killer app arrives within 6-18 months.
- **Ken Griffin (Citadel founder/CEO), June 2026.** Bullish on AI's power, skeptical of corporate claims about it.
  - **Anecdote:** CEOs at a dinner shared four or five productivity stories, and "Not one involved AI". They were about ML, optimization, and digitization.
  - **Agentic research case:** paper replication fell from 6-8 weeks to 2-3 hours; "this is a Master's or PhD level job". He was "shocked and depressed" at first.
  - **Jobs:** no headcount cut at Citadel. "I will take every single productivity gain I can get".
  - **Moats:** "competitive moats are being filled in at lightning speed", which points to a "golden age of entrepreneurial activity". At the same time, compute costs form a moat for high-margin firms.
  - **Return to office:** remote work is a bigger cause of weak youth employment than AI, citing a Fed paper.
  - **China and Taiwan:** China leads in "about 67 to 68 out of 74 technologies". Losing Taiwanese chips would cut US GDP 8% in six months.
  - **Data centers:** "build that damn data center in America" with dedicated generation; nuclear and natural gas.
  - **Hedge funds:** the industry's cost of capital is about the risk-free rate + 4%. Citadel returns $25-30bn to LPs to protect alpha per dollar.
  - **Ribbing GS:** "We do summarize Goldman Sachs' research, I'm sorry to say."

  > "Not one involved AI." — Ken Griffin, Citadel founder/CEO (2026-07-09, https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/citadels-ken-griffin-on-ai-us-china-tensions-and-us-data-centers)

  > "Now, of note, there's no reduction to headcount at Citadel on the back of this breakthrough, right?" — Ken Griffin (2026-07-09, same URL)

  > "the competitive moats of our society, within our corporate society, are all being filled and at breathtaking rates." — Ken Griffin (2026-07-09, same URL)

## 7. Market-structure links

- **Marquee as the AI front end to clients.**
  - Marquee AI (internal only, Aug 2026) breaks a question into research topics, then pulls research, trading-floor "flows, trade ideas" commentary, and Market View widgets ("Pinterest for capital markets", where vendor data is turned into expert widgets, "millions" of them). It writes and runs Python and returns sentence-level grounded output.
  - Visual structuring (a derivatives scenario and backtest tool) is to be wrapped in an agent so that "the user just has to express intent".
  - Implication: GS is turning dealer content and analytics into an intent-driven agent layer on its own platform. That competes for the same client attention as vendor terminals and multi-dealer venues. (INFERRED; no venues are named.)
  - Marquee also distributes gated content: Lee's *Breaks of the Game* episode (item 2), and research disclosures via "Marquee ContentStream" (item 10 boilerplate).
- **Market-making and AI (Griffin on Citadel Securities).**
  - Mahajan cites "double digit market share" in equities, futures, and treasuries.
  - Transformer models have improved the ability "to both price and manage risk", for "a number of other leading market making firms" as well.
  - Compute is now a core input. Firms "compete for compute access with everybody", and large market makers spend hundreds of millions on it. "people who have lower margin businesses won't be able to bear that cost impact". This implies scale concentration among electronic liquidity providers.
- **AI and market efficiency (closest material to the missing "less efficient?" episode).**
  - Griffin: thanks to alternative data and AI, "the here and now is just becoming far more transparent". Beating the quarter has "gotten far more difficult". The edge moves to multi-year vision about transformative products.
  - Kunal Shah: in FX, automation and systematic trading scaled the business while humans focus on "providing liquidity and serving clients".
  - Churchman: automating the junior trader's chat price request. This connects to RFQ automation on electronic venues (INFERRED).
- **Credit market structure from the AI capex boom.**
  - **IG supply share:** AI-related issuance was $10bn (1% of IG supply) in 2024, $108bn (about 7%) in 2025, and about 18% in 2026.
  - **Duration:** 40% of 15-year-plus IG issuance in 2026 is AI-linked. Amazon is now the highest duration weight in the IG index (it was #20 last year). Google is #18 (it was #86).
  - **Concessions:** new-issue concessions rose from 2-3bp to as much as 20bp on a large hyperscaler deal.
  - **Constraints:** concentration limits bind before leverage does. If hyperscalers grew to the size of the largest bank issuers, that would add about $510bn of IG capacity. The largest US bank issuers each have under $175bn of index-eligible debt and under 2.3% of the index.
  - **Overflow:** borrowing spills into private credit and infrastructure ($4.5tn dry powder), CAD, GBP, AUD, and JPY markets, project finance, ABS once data centers are finished, high yield, and leveraged loans.
  - **Hedging:** clients use credit as an AI hedge, but Ablon warns of a scenario where "credit will go tighter and stocks will go down".
  - **Tooling:** the GS credit desk builds thematic "baskets" such as the AI leader basket.
- **Tokenization, venues, and named counterparties.** Tradeweb, MarketAxess, ICE, CME, Canton, and stablecoins are not mentioned anywhere in this bucket.

## 8. Surprises / note-worthy theses

1. **GS's own Head of Equity Research was the most prominent AI skeptic (July 2024), while the CIO was its evangelist.** GS published both views under one brand. That is useful for studying how a firm "owns" both sides of a theme. Covello's Nvidia/ASML single-supplier argument is a clean market-structure idea: costs fall only when the bottleneck has competitors.
2. **"Demo is judged on its best day, product on its worst day."** Churchman's build lessons are a playbook for any incumbent: don't bet against the model; build what the model can't learn (entitlements, mandates, how data connects). The firm's moat is institutional context, not model capability. It also matches Argenti's 2023 call that value moves to the application, tooling, and compliance layer.
3. **Mandate engineering and the liability market.** Agent autonomy in finance will be capped by authority and liability frameworks ("under whose authority, in whose name"), not by capability.
