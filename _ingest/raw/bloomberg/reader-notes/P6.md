- Two different forecasts from the same house: GenAI $1.3T by 2032 (Mar-2024, Singh/Rana) vs $1.8T by 2032 (Nov-2025, quoted by BISL's Pruzinsky) — and AI agents >$270B by 2032. Forecast revised up ~38% in 20 months; no explicit reconciliation.
- Bloomberg's own execution stack is pitched as multi-asset ("Unlike vendors confined to a single asset class") with ML in TCA for 10+ years and rule engine since 2019; IBVAL is described as using "venue data and ML to predict the next trade print" and doubling Asia coverage 50k→100k bonds by year-end 2025.
- Buyside is anti-24/7 in Europe (>75% oppose) while tokenization/crypto real-time settlement "may resurface" T+0 debate; only 20% back T+0, 11% real-time settlement; 79% back T+1 (2027).
- Consolidated tape: 72% want pre+post-trade UK equity tape; only 24% favour EU's L1 design; 28% would swap feeds; UK FCA prioritising fixed-income CT first. Only one bidder at EU tender stage.
- Crypto narrative arc through BI: McGlone's bullish 2020-22 calls → ETF-led institutionalisation (Balchunas/Seyffart 2024) → $153B crypto-ETF assets and "bottleneck moved from regulation to execution" (2026). Commodity ETFs drew ~2x crypto's net inflows in 2025.
- Private markets everywhere: asset-manager outlook (private credit), podcasts (Dimensional skeptical, HPS/Ares bullish), APAC wealth (illiquid tracking pain), Bloomberg product (IBOR + private asset booking). Bloomberg is selling the plumbing for the private-markets boom.
- Content-type shift: 2019-22 items are Terminal-first research; 2025 items are marketing/event recaps and "Functions for the Market" pieces that sell <GO> functions.
- Contradiction/nuance: the buyside survey says AI is least important tech priority, yet Bloomberg's product messaging stresses agentic AI as "next potential step" — Bloomberg is selling ahead of demand.
