## 2. Key events (dated)

1. **2019-03-22 / 05-10**: eFront acquired for $1.3B. It is Aladdin's first big move into private markets ("BlackRock to Acquire eFront").
2. **2020-04-07**: Aladdin moves onto Microsoft Azure ("BlackRock and Microsoft Form Strategic Partnership…").
3. **2020-05-11**: PNC sells its entire 22% stake; BlackRock buys back $1.1B directly. This ends BlackRock's bank-affiliate ownership era ("Secondary Offering… Held by PNC").
4. **2020-11-23 / 2021-02-01**: Aperio acquired for $1.05B. Start of the direct-indexing and personalisation push.
5. **2021-02-22**: Aladdin Data Cloud on Snowflake. Aladdin becomes a data platform as well as a workflow tool.
6. **2021-12-07**: iShares US post-trade servicing goes from State Street alone to four providers ("Diversifying Post-Trade Service Providers").
7. **2022-05-18**: BlackRock projects $5T of bond ETFs by 2030 and ties bond ETFs to the electronification of bond trading.
8. **2022-12-06**: Tradeweb credit trading and data are integrated into the Aladdin OEMS (cross-ref).
9. **2023-12-11**: Phase 2 of the Tradeweb integration adds AllTrade all-to-all for Aladdin credit traders (cross-ref).
10. **2024-01-12**: GIP deal and a "strategic re-architecture" that embeds the ETF and Index business across the firm (FY2023 release).
11. **2024-03-20**: BUIDL launches on Ethereum with Securitize; BlackRock takes a strategic stake in Securitize.
12. **2024-05-01**: BlackRock leads Securitize's $47M round; Tradeweb co-invests (cross-ref).
13. **2024-06-05**: Founding investor in the Texas Stock Exchange.
14. **2024-06-30 / 2025-03-03**: Preqin bought for ~$3.2B and closed. Aladdin moves into private-markets data and benchmarks.
15. **2025-06-12**: Investor Day sets 2030 targets: $35B+ revenue, and private markets plus technology at 30%+ of revenue.
16. **2025-11-12**: First electronic Mexican repo trade on Tradeweb. BlackRock is the client and connects through Aladdin (cross-ref).
17. **2026-01-15**: FY2025. AUM $14T, record $698B flows; the release positions BlackRock as a "foundational player in both traditional and decentralized financial markets".
18. **2026-07-01**: US Treasury selects iShares IVV and ITOT for Trump Accounts.
19. **2026-10-14**: Q3 2026. AUM $15.5T, adjusted margin 47.8%, AI Infrastructure Partnership's first deployment.

## 5. Mission / identity statements (boilerplate drift)

- 2019-01 (FY2018): "BlackRock helps investors build better financial futures. As a fiduciary to our clients, we provide the investment and technology solutions…"
- 2020-01 to 2020-11: "BlackRock’s purpose is to help more and more people experience financial well-being. As a fiduciary to investors and a leading provider of financial technology, our clients turn to us for the solutions…" The "purpose" language arrives here.
- 2021-01 onward: "…we help millions of people build savings that serve them throughout their lives by making investing easier and more affordable." This version is stable through 2026.
- 2025-03 (Preqin close) and 2025-11 (BlackRock México): "a fiduciary to investors and a provider of financial technology". "Leading" is dropped in these two releases, while other 2025–26 releases keep it.
- About Aladdin (2019): "Aladdin was first built to manage" BlackRock's business, and BlackRock "continues to be the largest user". In 2020–22 it becomes the "end-to-end investment management and operations platform" that "provides a common language across the investment lifecycle".
- Investor Day 2025 (Small): "We're a fiduciary business. We serve only clients." The 2030 ambition is "leveraging its scale and technology to connect investors to the long-term growth of capital markets".
- FY2025: "a scale operator in public and private markets investing and technology". Q3 2026: "a partner across investing, financing, and technology"; "both a trusted advisor and destination for global client capital".

## 6. Tailwinds / headwinds / challenges

**Tailwinds**
- ETF growth runway: ETF assets are under 3% of the markets they access, and bond ETFs are 2% of the bond market (ADMITTED, 2021-12 and 2022-05).
- Regulators are putting bond ETFs on a "level playing field", and US insurers use ETFs more freely: 8 of the top 10 insurers use them (ADMITTED, 2022-05).
- Clients are consolidating to fewer managers (ADMITTED, CNBC 2025-01 and Investor Day).
- The Department of Labor is opening 401(k)s to private markets, which Fink says will create demand for Aladdin, eFront and Preqin analytics (ADMITTED, 2025-10 and 2026-04).
- The DB-to-DC shift drives retirement-income demand (ADMITTED, LifePath 2020 and 2024).
- AI and infrastructure capex: an estimated $10T infrastructure need by 2033, a $1.5T data-centre estimate, and the AI Infrastructure Partnership (ADMITTED, 2025–26).
- Tokenization and digital wallets: $4.1T sits in digital wallets (ADMITTED, 2025-10).
- Political alignment: Trump Accounts and an administration "very open" to business (ADMITTED, 2025–26).

**Headwinds and challenges**
- "Meaningful headwinds for the asset management industry" (ADMITTED, FY2018).
- Workforce restructuring three times: $60M (2018), $91M (2022), and $61M (2023, focused on Aladdin and illiquid alternatives) (ADMITTED).
- In 2022, revenue fell 8% and AUM 14%, with cash outflows of $77B and FX drag on technology revenue (ADMITTED).
- Institutional index outflows run through the whole period, from -$53.7B in 2018 to -$119B in FY2025 (parsed, ADMITTED in the tables). INFERRED: growth is coming from ETFs, active and privates while classic institutional indexing shrinks.
- Concentration risk in ETF servicing (ADMITTED, 2021-12). The risk factors list disruption at third parties "integral to" the ETF platform (ADMITTED, 2020-05 and 2024-06).
- Private-credit stress: First Brands, Tricolor, "idiosyncratic risk", "less liquidity", and the 5% redemption gate (ADMITTED, CNBC 2025-10 and 2026-04).
- Integration risk from GIP, HPS and Preqin, and "failure to effectively manage the development and use of AI" was added as a risk factor by 2024 (ADMITTED, Preqin release).
- "Increasing focus from stakeholders regarding ESG matters" appears as a risk factor (ADMITTED, 2024-06).
- GAAP vs adjusted gap widens. FY2025 GAAP EPS $35.31 vs $48.09 adjusted; Q3 2026 GAAP operating margin 30.0% vs 47.8% adjusted, driven by acquisition-related noncash items (ADMITTED). INFERRED: this is the cost of the M&A strategy.
- Lower hiring intensity: "we probably need fewer future hires" because of technology (ADMITTED, 2025-10).

## 8. Market-structure links

**Aladdin as a connectivity layer to trading venues.** This runs through the Tradeweb cross-refs.
- *Dec 2022 (Tradeweb release):* a "multi-year partnership" to "seamlessly integrate Tradeweb’s credit trading solutions and data" into the **Aladdin OEMS**. It covers corporate, muni and EM bonds.
  - Phase 1: common clients can see **Ai-Price**, real-time prices on ~25,000 corporate bonds, inside Aladdin. The Dec 2023 release adds that phase 1 also delivered **Liquidity Score** and select dealer **axe streams**.
  - What each side gets: Tradeweb "will strengthen its credit platform and broaden its network through greater connectivity with the Aladdin platform".
  - Chris Bruner (Tradeweb CPO) says Aladdin users get Tradeweb's institutional, wholesale and retail liquidity plus related Treasuries and ETFs.
  - Kamya Somasundaram (Aladdin GM) says significant opportunities remain to accelerate electronification.
- *Dec 2023:* phase 2 gives Aladdin users trading US and European credit access to **AllTrade** (anonymous all-to-all), **Rematch** (unmatched Dealer Sweep risk) and **Tradeweb Direct** (retail streams in 26,000 securities).
  - Planned next: direct **RFQ** and **AiEX** access from Aladdin.
  - Elisabeth Kirby (Tradeweb Head of Market Structure) explicitly cites Aladdin's "broad institutional client base".
  - Somasundaram, by then Global Head of Aladdin Partnerships, cites "more seamless access to liquidity".
  - Context from the release: AllTrade was 32% of in-competition HG RFQ and 44% of HY. Tradeweb's fully electronic HG TRACE share was 16.6%.
- *Nov 2025:* Mexican repo. Aladdin OEMS users "can connect directly to the Tradeweb platform". BlackRock was the first client and Santander CIB the first dealer.
  - Coverage is specials, GC and triparty, with Mexican government collateral, sold to Afores and investment funds.
  - INFERRED: BlackRock acts as anchor client, Aladdin is the pipe, and the venue gets order flow, a pattern repeated across asset classes.
- INFERRED: the pattern that matters strategically is the order management system as a distribution chokepoint. Venues pay in integration effort and data (Ai-Price on Aladdin) to sit inside Aladdin's workflow, which gives Aladdin leverage over venues similar to its leverage over custodians.

**ETF post-trade (power over service providers), 2021-12.**
- Before: State Street alone serviced US iShares. After: State Street, BNY Mellon, Citi and JPMorgan each handle a subset of the $2.3T, covering custody, fund admin, fund accounting and transfer agency.
- Process: a ~2-year due diligence; the transition was to start in H2 2022 and take 18 months. The Irish-domiciled ETF RFP was still ongoing.
- Stated reason: "robust operating model… mitigate concentration risk."
- INFERRED: a single client splitting a $2.3T book four ways is pure buyer power. It disciplines fees and service levels and reduces single-vendor operational risk, which BlackRock lists as a risk factor ("disruption to the operations of third parties whose functions are integral to BlackRock’s ETF platform").

**Bond ETFs as market plumbing (2022-05).**
- ETF growth "helped drive advances in electronic trading and algorithmic pricing of individual bonds".
- Electronic IG share was 36% in March 2022 vs 21% in early 2019 (Coalition Greenwich). European corporate e-trading grew 61% between 2017 and 2020.
- Institutions use bond ETFs to "price individual bonds and portfolios, reduce transaction costs, manage liquidity, and hedge risk".
- Record bond-ETF flows in 2022. Fixed-income ETF flows were $159B in FY2025 (parsed).

**Data and index providers.**
- Preqin, Clarity AI, Snowflake and Azure. BlackRock was "a client of Preqin for many" years before buying it.
- Small (2025) says the aim is to build "the data, the benchmarks, the risk models and analytics that transformed public markets" for private markets, which would make BlackRock an index and benchmark provider for privates. INFERRED: that competes with MSCI/Burgiss, S&P and LSEG.
- IVV and ITOT are S&P-index ETFs (Trump Accounts).

**Exchanges.** BlackRock is a founding investor in the Texas Stock Exchange (2024-06), citing liquidity and market efficiency.

**Tokenization rails (BUIDL and Securitize).**
- BUIDL = BlackRock USD Institutional Digital Liquidity Fund, launched 2024-03-20 on Ethereum.
- Holdings: 100% cash, T-bills and repo. Aims for a $1 stable value. Dividends accrue daily and are paid monthly as new tokens. Transfers run 24/7/365 between pre-approved investors.
- Legal structure: Reg D 506(c) and 3(c)(7); $5M minimum; not exchange-listed.
- Service providers:
  - BlackRock Financial Management: manager.
  - BNY Mellon: custodian, administrator and digital/traditional interoperability.
  - Securitize: transfer agent, tokenization platform and (Securitize Markets, a broker-dealer) placement agent, paid an upfront plus quarterly NAV-based fee.
  - PwC: auditor.
  - Ecosystem: Anchorage, BitGo, Coinbase and Fireblocks.
- BlackRock made a strategic investment in Securitize, and Joseph Chalom joined its board. Securitize's $47M round on 2024-05-01 was led by BlackRock with Hamilton Lane, ParaFi and **Tradeweb**.
- So BlackRock is simultaneously client, shareholder and board member of its tokenization agent, and Tradeweb is a co-investor.
- By 2025-10 Fink calls BUIDL "the largest cash money market fund that’s tokenized" and talks of tokenizing ETFs.
- FY2025 earnings break out "Digital assets" as their own product line and ETF flow line.

**Dealers and banks as counterparties.**
- Morgan Stanley, Citi and Evercore ran the PNC secondary.
- Barclays advised on Preqin; Goldman advised Preqin.
- Santander was the first dealer on the Mexican repo platform.

## 9. Shifts over time

**Fink's annual earnings quote, year by year**

| Release | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| FY2018 (Jan 2019) | Scale and diversification; "most diversified global asset management and technology services firm"; industry "headwinds"; capital return. Defensive and operational. |
| FY2019 (Jan 2020) | Clients want a "strategic partner" for "thought leadership on macro and geopolitical issues" and "whole-portfolio solutions powered by technology"; Aladdin/eFront drove tech revenue to "almost $1 billion"; sustainability "acceleration". |
| FY2020 (Jan 2021) | Peak stakeholder register: "all our stakeholders", "purpose", "voice and values", "advance sustainable investing", "resilient economy"; tech revenue passes $1B. |
| FY2021 (Jan 2022) | Record growth; diversification (active over 60% of organic base-fee growth); Aladdin $1.3B. ESG words disappear, replaced by "Every dollar… treated with the same care". |
| FY2022 (Jan 2023) | Trust and "fiduciary standards"; consistency in a bad market; record Aladdin sales in volatility; long-term investing. No sustainability language. |

| FY2025 (Jan 2026) | "Unified platform"; $400B private fundraising goal; growth channels now include "digital assets and tokenization"; "traditional and decentralized financial markets". |
| Q3 2026 (Oct 2026) | "Investing, financing, and technology"; origination, "hyperscaler financings", the AI Infrastructure Partnership; BlackRock "at the center of how that capital is deployed". The firm now presents itself as a financier and capital allocator as well as an asset manager. |

**Other arcs**
- **ESG.** It peaks in 2020–21: the Microsoft sustainability collaboration, Clarity AI, Aladdin Climate, and "sustainability its standard for investing". After that it largely vanishes and reappears only as a risk factor (2024). Energy talk in 2026 is framed as "self-reliance" (solar plus gas).
- **Crypto and tokenization.** None until BUIDL in 2024. Fink then admits he was not always a proponent ("I grow and learn", 2025). By 2026 digital assets are a reporting category.
- **Private markets.** They move from an "illiquid alternatives pillar" (2019, eFront) to buying the data layer (Preqin 2024–25) and to a top-5 private-markets platform at $322–336B (parsed) with a 401(k) push.
- **Technology.** Aladdin goes from internal risk system (2019) to cloud (2020), data cloud (2021), venue connectivity (2022–25) and private-markets data (2025). Technology revenue goes from $785M (2018) to roughly $1.5B (2023) and keeps growing at a ~12% ACV pace.

## 10. Surprises / note-worthy

1. **Tradeweb co-invested in Securitize alongside BlackRock (2024-05).** The bond-venue partner, Aladdin's integrated credit venue, also sits on BlackRock's tokenization rail. That is a three-way BlackRock, Tradeweb and Securitize tie that the Tradeweb study should note.
2. **BlackRock invested in an exchange (TXSE, 2024)** and was first client on a new electronic repo market (Mexico, 2025). The buy-side is underwriting market infrastructure, not just using it.
3. **The BUIDL release discloses its own conflict.** BlackRock's equity stake in its placement agent "creates a conflict of interest for the Investment Manager". This is unusual candor in a launch release.
4. **Fink's 401(k) argument turns regulation into an Aladdin sales pitch.** Strict fiduciary rules mean "you have to justify every investment", so Aladdin, eFront and Preqin become "the engine" of that justification (2025-10).
5. **Institutional index runs persistent outflows** (to -$119B in FY2025, parsed) even as total flows hit records. The old core business is quietly shrinking while ETFs, privates and tech carry growth.
6. **Transcript and figure inconsistencies.**
   - Fink on CNBC says "$241 billion in the last quarter" while the release says $281B.
   - "ETS" appears for ETFs, and "Troy Kohler" for Tricolor.
   - IBIT "over 100 billion" (Oct 2025) vs total digital-assets AUM of $78.4B (Dec 2025, parsed).
