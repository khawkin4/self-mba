# Bucket B — Larry Fink chairman's letters 2023-2026 + Fink/Goldstein Economist op-ed (Dec 2025)

Reader notes: all 5 records read in full (~260k chars). Record titles abbreviated below as
L2023, L2024, L2025, L2026 (annual chairman's letters) and ECON (Economist op-ed reprint, 2025-12-01).
Note: the 2026 letter's date is given only as "2026-03" in the corpus; quotes use 2026-03-01 as a placeholder day.

## 1. Record list

- 2023-03-15 | Larry Fink's 2023 Annual Chairman's Letter to Investors (L2023) | Written days after SVB failed: "dominoes" of easy money, an "economy of fragmentation", the retirement "silent crisis", climate as investment risk recast as "choice", Voting Choice, a short digital-assets section; first single letter to all stakeholders (replacing the separate CEO letter and shareholder letter).
- 2024-03-26 | Larry Fink's 2024 Annual Chairman's Letter to Investors (L2024) | "Time to rethink retirement": parents' story, US capital markets vs bank finance, the retirement age/Social Security, LifePath Paycheck, infrastructure and debt, "energy pragmatism" introduced, GIP acquisition, firm re-architecture.
- 2025-03-31 | Larry Fink's 2025 Annual Chairman's Letter to Investors (L2025) | "The democratization of investing": BlackRock no longer "a traditional asset manager"; GIP/HPS/Preqin rationale, $68T infrastructure demand, 50/30/20, "index private markets", private assets in 401(k)s, AI power demand, Bitcoin vs dollar, "every asset can be tokenized".
- 2025-12-01 | Larry Fink & Rob Goldstein, The Economist op-ed on tokenization (ECON) | Tokenisation is roughly where the internet was in 1996; a "bridge built from both sides"; regulators should update the existing rulebook; guardrails (buyer protection, counterparty risk, digital ID).
- 2026-03 | Larry Fink's 2026 Annual Chairman's Letter to Investors (L2026) | "Growing with your country": self-reliance, wealth inequality, AI concentrating gains; country cases (US Trump Accounts and a Social Security investment fund; India JioBlackRock; Japan NISA; Germany pensions); "energy abundance" including solar; $14T AUM, 2030 targets, ~$150B in digital assets.

## 4. People

- **Larry (Laurence D.) Fink** — Chairman and CEO throughout. Born 1952 ("I’m 71" in L2024). Started on Wall Street in 1976, at First Boston, where he says he "lost $100 million on a series of bad trades", which he credits for BlackRock's focus on risk management. Signature views: capital markets over bank finance; retirement as the firm's "core business"; investing as an "act of hope"; "energy pragmatism"; democratizing private markets; tokenization. Increasingly personal and populist tone: parents (L2024, L2026), grandchildren, "capitalism did work—just for too few people" (L2025).
- **Martin Small** — CFO from 2023 (16 years at BlackRock). Coined "units of trust" for client dollars. (L2023)
- **Gary Shedlin** — Former CFO of about 10 years, moved to Vice Chairman covering strategic clients. (L2023)
- **Rob Goldstein** — COO. Co-author of the Economist op-ed, cited again in L2026 ("As Rob Goldstein and I wrote late last year"). The only executive Fink co-bylines; arguably a succession signal (INFERRED).
- **Mark Wiedman** — Left after 20 years (led iShares, Corporate Strategy, Global Client Business). Long seen externally as a potential successor; the letter notes his exit warmly with no context. (L2025)
- **Bayo (Adebayo) Ogunlesi** — GIP CEO, a First Boston colleague of Fink. Joined the Board after GIP closed in Oct 2024. Quoted in FT on infrastructure customer service. (L2024, L2025)
- **Charles "Charlie" Hallac (1964-2015)** — First employee and Aladdin's inventor, credited in L2025's origin story.
- **Board:** Beth Ford left in 2022 (spouse became Minnesota SBI CIO). Greg Fleming (Rockefeller Capital) and Kathleen Murphy (ex-Fidelity) nominated in 2025. Marco Antonio Slim Domit left in 2025. Gregg Lemkau (BDT & MSD) appointed in early 2026. The direction is toward wealth-management and private/deal expertise (INFERRED).
- **Outside figures quoted or used:** Jensen Huang (Nvidia; AI infrastructure partnership in L2025, skilled trades in L2026); Mukesh Ambani (JioBlackRock partner: "This is our baby boomer generation…", L2026); Volodymyr Zelenskyy (Fink's calls, Ukraine reconstruction framework, L2023); Mario Draghi (EU integration and capital markets, L2025, L2026); Ezra Klein and Derek Thompson (*Abundance*, permitting, L2025); Sens. ## 6. Tailwinds / headwinds / challenges

**Tailwinds**
- Banks retreating from lending, so credit moves to markets and private credit (ADMITTED: L2023 SVB, L2024 "banks and public lenders move away from the middle market", L2025, L2026).
- Government deficits push infrastructure onto private capital ($68T demand by 2040; "Governments can’t fund infrastructure through deficits", L2025) (ADMITTED).
- AI capex and power demand: data centers at $40-50B each and 1GW each (L2025); the AI Infrastructure Partnership and Aligned Data Centers (L2026) (ADMITTED).
- Higher yields make bonds viable again: "they can meet that target by investing almost entirely in bonds" (L2023); $10T of cash on the sidelines (L2025) (ADMITTED).
- Retirement policy: auto-enrollment, SECURE 2.0, Trump Accounts, 401(k) private-markets framework "significant momentum" (L2026), plus NISA, Ireland auto-enroll and German reform (ADMITTED).
- Global ETF adoption and European first-time investors; digital brokers (Trade Republic, Scalable, Monzo, Revolut, N26, Upvest) (ADMITTED).
- Crypto legitimization: Bitcoin ETP record, stablecoin reserves management (ADMITTED).

**Headwinds / challenges**
- Political polarization over ESG. "Opinions diverging across regions… especially in the U.S.", which "creates challenges for a truly global asset manager" (L2023). The transition "has become more contentious in the U.S." (L2024) (ADMITTED, though critics are never named).
- 2022 market drawdown cut AUM by $1.7T (ADMITTED, L2023).
- Backlash to the 2024 retirement-age and Social Security ideas (ADMITTED, L2026).
- Private markets are illiquid and opaque; 401(k) providers are unfamiliar with them; "the industry isn't structured for a 50/30/20 world" (ADMITTED, L2025).
- Tokenization needs digital ID, regulatory updates and counterparty standards. The US/UK/EU are "falling behind" on where tokenized trading happens (ADMITTED, ECON and L2025).
- US debt and the dollar's reserve status (ADMITTED, L2024 and L2025).
- Permitting delays ("13 years to build a power line"); grid capacity; China's lead in nuclear, EVs, solar and battery supply chains (ADMITTED).
- Return to office and culture erosion after Covid (ADMITTED, L2023).
- Integration risk from three large acquisitions at once, plus senior talent turnover (Wiedman) (INFERRED). L2026 stresses its "long history of successful integrations".
- Concentration and size criticism (INFERRED from the defensive "only 3% share" line, L2023).
- Liquidity mismatch among illiquid-heavy asset owners. Fink flagged this "third domino" in 2023, and it is ironic given the later private-markets push (ADMITTED as a market risk, L2023; the irony is INFERRED).

- Critics of ESG are never named. L2023 refers to "many people with opinions about how we should manage our clients’ money". L2024 cites "activists on the far left and right who say that countries have to choose between renewables and oil and gas". These match the Republican-state AG and treasurer boycotts and climate activists of 2022-23, though no state, politician or group is named.
- Critics of the retirement ideas: "It produced some criticism for even suggesting Social Security needed a fix" (L2026). The critics are not named.
- Policymakers cited approvingly: Booker, Young, Cassidy, Kaine (bipartisan, chosen deliberately), Draghi, India's policymakers on gold, Japan's government on NISA.
- Klein and Thompson's *Abundance* quoted on permitting and the federal government "failing to weigh risk" on nuclear (L2025). The letter takes on centre-left supply-side language.
- WSJ article on disillusioned Gen Z voters, cited as the data point that "concerned me more than" any other (L2024).
- Sorkin's 1929 book, used as a cautionary tale for tokenization safeguards (ECON).
- No regulators are quoted by name. Regulation is described generically ("Regulators should aim for consistency", ECON).

## 8. Market-structure links

- **Bond ETFs as plumbing:** "Bond ETFs connected the fragmented fixed income market with transparent and liquid on-exchange trading" and will "become further integrated into an increasingly modern, electronic fixed income marketplace" (L2023). ECON: bond ETFs connected "dealer markets with public exchanges". 2023 figures: $760B iShares fixed income platform, more than 450 bond ETFs. This is the Tradeweb and portfolio-trading link: ETF creation/redemption baskets and portfolio trades depend on electronic bond venues. Tradeweb itself is never named (INFERRED).
- **Scale and trading costs:** scale gives clients "tighter bid-ask spreads when trading securities, and more diversified service-provider relationships" (L2023). This states BlackRock's leverage over dealers and venues openly.
- **Venues named:** NYSE and MarketAxess appear only as examples of identity verification for trading (L2025). No mention of Tradeweb, Bloomberg as a venue, ICE or LSEG. Bloomberg appears only as an analogy: Preqin is to private markets "what Bloomberg terminals did for stocks and bonds" (L2025). S&P Global is credited as the data source for the TSR charts. The S&P 500 is the benchmark for "index private markets".
- **Index/data ambition:** Preqin (190k funds, 60k managers), then indexing private markets, then a standardized benchmark for 401(k) plan sponsors (L2025, L2026). Preqin has been unified onto Aladdin and eFront, "tripling our desktop reach" (L2026). This puts BlackRock directly into data/index-provider economics, a competitor to MSCI (Burgiss), S&P, LSEG and Bloomberg private-markets data (INFERRED).
- **Aladdin as a connectivity layer:** "operating system that unites all of BlackRock" (L2023); 130,000+ users, "Aladdin AI copilots", "enhancements in openness supporting ecosystem partnerships" (L2024); more than half of sales multi-product; $1.5B technology revenue in 2023 and more than $1.6B by 2024; 16% organic ACV growth in 2025; "extensive embedded network across the global investment ecosystem" (L2026).
- **Capital markets vs bank lending:** over 70% of US non-financial corporate financing comes from markets vs about 65% bank-funded in China (L2024); "Bank lending is constrained" (L2025); "Banks alone can’t finance what a growing economy needs" (L2026).
- **Tokenization rails:** SWIFT described as "routing emails through the postal office" (L2025). Instant settlement, 24/7 markets, fractionalization, on-chain voting. Real-world-asset tokens up about 300% in 20 months (ECON). The single digital wallet holding ETFs, digital euros, tokenized bonds and private-credit fractions (L2026). The BUIDL tokenized treasury fund ("largest tokenized fund in the world"), $65B of stablecoin reserves and nearly $80B of digital-asset ETPs (L2026). India's UPI shows rails that need not be blockchain (L2026).
- **Retail rails:** ETF savings plans via Trade Republic, Scalable Capital, ING, Lloyds, Nordnet, Boursorama, Monzo (£1 minimum), N26, Revolut; Upvest minority stake; Human Interest minority stake (L2023-L2025). Partners Group private-markets SMA in a single subscription document (L2026).
- **Proxy plumbing:** Voting Choice now covers about half of index equity AUM, with more than $500B of clients participating (L2023). Retail pilot in 2024. Calls for "more proxy advisors" (a dig at the ISS/Glass Lewis duopoly). Voting Choice is gone from the 2025 and 2026 letters, replaced by "tokenization… democratize shareholder voting" (L2025).

## 9. Shifts over time

- **ESG and climate language:** "ESG" appears zero times across all five records. Rough counts by year: "climate" 5/4/0/0 and "energy transition" 8/5/0/0 for 2023/2024/2025/2026. "Leading in sustainable investing" is a strategy pillar in 2023 and 2024 and disappears in 2025. The arc: 2023 "climate risk is investment risk… not… environmental police" plus transition finance as a business; 2024 "energy pragmatism" (decarbonization plus security; $300B in traditional energy vs $138B transition; "never supported divesting"); 2025 pragmatism recast as power for AI data centers, SMR nuclear and permitting, with no climate framing; 2026 "Energy abundance and affordability", where solar is justified by speed, cost and China supply-chain security, and the stated aim is "not to favor one technology over another". Decarbonization appears once in 2025, and only to describe China's strategy.
- **Stakeholder framing:** 2023 opens by addressing "all of our stakeholders" in one letter. By 2026 the frame is nation-centric ("Growing with your country", self-reliance, the 250th anniversary), aligned with a protectionist and nationalist political climate.
- **Retirement:** 2023 "silent crisis"; 2024 retirement age, Social Security, Australian Super, LifePath Paycheck; 2025 private assets in 401(k)s ("nine extra years… with your grandkids"), baby bonds; 2026 Trump Accounts, a Cassidy-Kaine Social Security investment fund, and Preqin as the 401(k) benchmark provider. Retirement moves from a social cause to a distribution channel for private markets (INFERRED).
- **Private markets:** 2023 $35B raised, private credit and infrastructure "particularly if we see less lending from banks"; 2024 GIP and "portfolio of the future"; 2025 "not who we are anymore", 50/30/20, indexing private markets; 2026 $400B fundraising target and at least 30% of revenue from private markets and technology by 2030.
- **Digital assets:** 2023 "the media’s obsession with Bitcoin", FTX, permissioned chains, "elevated risks"; 2024 a single line on Bitcoin ETFs; 2025 "Tokenization is democratization", "every asset", plus Bitcoin as a threat to the dollar; Dec 2025 measured "internet in 1996" bridge and an admission that "At first it was hard for the financial world—including us—to see the big idea"; 2026 a $150B digital-asset business and "lead the charge" on wallets. The language peaks in 2025 and becomes more institutional and regulatory in late 2025-26.
- **AI:** 2023 one passing mention; 2024 systematic investing and Aladdin copilots; 2025 AI power demand, the Huang partnership, and AI as Europe's "lifeline"; 2026 AI as an inequality risk, the labor-market question, skilled trades, and Aladdin as an "AI beneficiary".
- **Tone:** from an institutional fiduciary voice (2023) to memoir-style personal storytelling and populist economics (2024-26: "capitalism is working—just not for enough people").

## 10. Surprises / note-worthy

1. Fink, issuer of the record Bitcoin ETP, writes that America "risks losing" reserve-currency status to Bitcoin if deficits balloon (L2025). He hedges his product and his macro view in the same letter.
2. The "third domino" warning in 2023 about liquidity mismatches in asset owners overweight illiquids. Two years later the firm pitches 20% private allocations and private assets in 401(k)s.
3. The Preqin rationale is explicitly to become an index-maker ("index private markets just like… the S&P 500"). That would move BlackRock from index licensee to index/benchmark owner and make it a data competitor to S&P, MSCI, LSEG and Bloomberg.
4. MarketAxess is the only trading venue named in four years of letters, and it appears as an identity-verification example rather than as a liquidity venue. Tradeweb is never mentioned despite BlackRock's central role in electronic bond and ETF trading.
5. Succession is almost absent. 2023 mentions a "significant birthday" and says "my most important responsibility now is growing and mentoring leaders". 2025 notes Wiedman's exit. 2026 expands the GEC with "emerging enterprise leaders". Goldstein is the only co-bylined executive. Fink names no successor and gives no timeline.
6. In 2026 Fink endorses a partly market-invested Social Security fund and praises "Trump Accounts" (with nuance about how they are funded), while openly predicting renewed criticism. Retirement policy is the area where he is most willing to take political risk.
