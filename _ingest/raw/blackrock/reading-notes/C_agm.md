# Bucket C — BlackRock Annual Shareholder Meetings (2021, 2022, 2023)

Source: 3 AGM transcripts (2021 Refinitiv StreetEvents edited transcript; 2022 and 2023 FactSet CallStreet corrected transcripts). All read in full. Bracketed names like "[ph] X" are the transcriber's phonetic guesses; spellings of questioner names are unreliable and are given as they appear in the transcript. All meetings were virtual; Andrew Dickson (Corporate Secretary) chairs procedure and reads grouped/paraphrased questions; Fink answers or routes to directors/stewardship heads.

## 1. Record list

- 2021-05-26 | 2021 Annual Shareholder Meeting Transcript | Post-PNC-exit charter clean-up, a PBC-conversion proposal from James McRitchie (presented by Rick Alexander), and an SEIU racial-equity-audit proposal settled out; Q&A covers board size, DEI, voter-rights statement, private prisons, fossil-fuel engagement, China, buybacks and bitcoin.
- 2022-05-25 | 2022 Annual Shareholder Meeting Transcript | Bear-market backdrop (Ukraine, inflation); The Shareholder Commons (Sara Murphy for McRitchie) pushes "beta stewardship"; Q&A brings the first anti-ESG "political preferences" grouped question, state anti-boycott laws, BlackRock's admission it will back fewer climate proposals, Voting Choice, China, CEO pay ratio.
- 2023-05-24 | 2023 Annual Shareholder Meeting Transcript | Three shareholder proposals from both flanks — NCPPR (anti-DEI audit), Paul Rissman (climate stewardship to "engineer decarbonization"), Code Pink (ITA defense ETF report) — all defeated; Fink says "Our role is not to engineer real world decarbonization"; regional-bank crisis framed as a capital-markets opportunity; Aladdin's SVB-week usage; new stewardship head Joud Abdel Majeid.

## 2. Key events (dated)

1. 2021-05-26 — Charter amendments after PNC's May-2020 exit from its BlackRock stake: holders of 15%+ may call special meetings, supermajority requirements removed, "significant stockholder" provisions eliminated. All passed. (2021 AGM)
2. 2021-05-26 — SEIU Pension Plans Master Trust racial-equity-audit proposal withdrawn after BlackRock agreed to an external review of its DEI strategy "next year". (2021 AGM) The 2023 meeting reports an "independent assessment" of racial equity has since been done. (2023 AGM)
3. 2021-05-26 — Mercy Investment Services / Boston Trust Walden climate-proxy-voting proposal: proponents say BlackRock's mid-2020 voting shift satisfied them but they "were unable to withdraw"; their statement praises BlackRock. (2021 AGM)
4. 2021-05-26 — McRitchie PBC-conversion proposal gets ~2.3% for, ~97% against. Say-on-pay ~93% for / 6% against. (2021 AGM)
5. 2021 — Board's Nominating & Governance Committee renamed Nominating, Governance and Sustainability Committee (March 2021); Hans Vestberg (Verizon CEO) joins board; ESG goals tied to executive pay. (2021 AGM)
6. 2021-05-26 — Fink on bitcoin: "intrigued," studying it as a possible gold-like asset, but says it's mainly day-trading and dealers profit from wide spreads. (2021 AGM)
7. 2021 metrics cited: tech platform revenue passed $1bn (2020); sustainable AUM grew 64% organically vs industry 27%; stewardship "focus universe" grew from 440 to 1,000+ carbon-intensive companies (>90% of portfolio Scope 1+2 emissions); $295bn traded with diverse brokers in 2020. (2021 AGM)
8. 2022-05-25 — McRitchie/Shareholder Commons stewardship-externalities proposal gets ~3.6% for / 95% against; say-on-pay ~95% / 4.6%. (2022 AGM)
9. 2022-05-25 — First grouped anti-ESG question (Scott Shepard and others) calling climate/racial-equity advocacy "personal and political preferences"; question from Adam Howser on state laws that require divestment from firms that boycott energy. Fink: "we are against divestiture"; no policy restricting energy investment; BlackRock hosted dinners between energy companies and environmental groups. (2022 AGM)
10. 2022-05 — Stewardship's May 10 paper signals fewer votes for climate shareholder proposals. Sandra Boss calls the change "tactical" and gives three reasons: SEC loosened rules on which proposals are allowed, so more prescriptive proposals reach votes; the energy shortage after Russia's invasion; and the rise of management "say on climate" votes. (2022 AGM)
11. 2022-05-25 — Voting Choice: available to institutional clients with $2tn+ of index equity assets (pension funds serving 60m+ Americans); "the only firm in the world to announce" it, and the aim is to extend it to individual investors once rules allow. (2022 AGM)
12. 2022-05-25 — China: about 2% of AUM is invested in China, over 75% of that through indexes. Fink also cites US–China trade of $700bn+, Chinese holdings of US securities of ~$2.1tn and US holdings of Chinese securities of ~$1.2tn. (2022 AGM)
13. 2022-05-25 — CEO pay ratio 198:1 defended by Bill Ford. Board at 17 directors, 14 of them current or former CEOs; a question on splitting the Chair/CEO roles is rebuffed by Gordon Nixon. (2022 AGM)
14. 2023-05-24 — Three shareholder proposals. NCPPR anti-DEI civil-rights audit: <1% for. Paul Rissman climate-stewardship report: "just shy of 10%" for, the highest of any shareholder proposal in the three years. Code Pink ITA report: ~8% for. Say-on-pay ~92% / 8%, the weakest of the three years; say-on-pay frequency 99% for annual. (2023 AGM)
15. 2023-05-24 — Fink rejects "engineer real world decarbonization" as BlackRock's role; NCPPR notes Fink sent no annual letter to CEOs in 2023 and "didn't mention equity at all." (2023 AGM)
16. 2023 — SVB/First Republic: Fink says the regional-bank crisis will "fuel another round of growth in capital markets"; Aladdin usage spiked in the week after SVB, and wealth-platform usage "more than doubled" after the bank failures. (2023 AGM)
17. 2023 — Joud Abdel Majeid has replaced Sandra Boss as Global Head of Investment Stewardship. Senior leadership roles were reshuffled to strengthen "One BlackRock" connectivity. 2022 AUM fell $1.7tn on markets and the dollar, yet BlackRock took ~one-third of long-term industry flows. (2023 AGM)

## 4. People

**BlackRock insiders**
- **Larry Fink** (Chairman & CEO, all three years). His views: fiduciary to clients and "not our money"; client choice; scale is a virtue; crises are opportunities; globalist on China. On climate he moves from "tremendous investment risk… opportunity" (2021) to "fair and just transition… against divestiture" (2022) to "not to engineer real world decarbonization" (2023). Curious but sceptical on crypto (2021).
- **R. Andrew Dickson III** (Managing Director & Corporate Secretary): runs procedure, groups and paraphrases questions, announces votes.
- **Sandra (Sandy) Boss** (Senior MD, Global Head of Investment Stewardship, GEC; 2021–2022). 2021: expanded the climate focus universe to 1,000+ companies and will vote against directors without credible transition plans. 2022: explains the "tactical" drop in support for climate proposals; says <1% of BlackRock's votes are on shareholder proposals and it backed 90% of management director nominees in 2021.
- **Joud Abdel Majeid** (Global Head of Investment Stewardship, GEC; 2023): indigenous-rights answer stressing disclosure, best-efforts monitoring, and government responsibility.
- **Gordon Nixon** (Independent Director; Chair, Nominating, Governance & Sustainability Committee; 2021–2022): defends the large board (16 seats in 2021, 17 in 2022) and the combined Chair/CEO role ("there is no right or wrong").
- **Murry Gerber** (Lead Independent Director; 2021, also cited in 2022): director pay is "competitive, but is not an outlier"; the board and its committees met 46 times during the pandemic.
- **William (Bill) Ford** (Independent Director; Chair, Management Development & Compensation Committee; all years). Defends equity pay and an equity burn rate of 0.8% (2020). Ownership requirements: $10m CEO, $5m President, $2m other GEC members, $500k directors. Defends the 198:1 pay ratio, noting >95% of CEO pay is at risk.

## 5. Mission / identity statements

- 2021: "helping more and more people experience financial well-being" (stated purpose, repeated every year). Fink: "BlackRock is fulfilling a need in the financial services industry by focusing clients on the long-term and long-term investing" (paraphrase; the sentence breaks across lines).
- 2021: stewardship described as "part of our fiduciary duty"; largest stewardship team. "100% of the money we manage is as a fiduciary to our clients."
- 2022: "we are the asset advisor. We're not the owner of these assets."
- 2022: the strategy pillars are to keep alpha at the heart of BlackRock, accelerate iShares, build private markets, build out Aladdin, deliver whole-portfolio solutions, and be "the global leader in sustainable investing."
- 2023: "We are fiduciary to our clients. The money we manage belongs to them." "One BlackRock" business model; "BlackRock has grown as a role of the capital markets has grown"; founded "based on a belief in the long-term growth of the capital markets." The 2023 strategy list drops "global leader in sustainable investing" in favour of "transition investing" and bond ETFs (INFERRED from wording).

## 6. Tailwinds / headwinds / challenges

**Tailwinds**
- ADMITTED: Record flows. Net inflows were $391bn in 2020 and $540bn in 2021. 2022 brought $300bn+ (~one-third of industry flows) while peers had outflows; Q1 2023 added $110bn+.
- ADMITTED: Technology revenue grew from $1bn (2020) to $1.3bn (2021) to $1.4bn (2022), with record Aladdin net sales.
- ADMITTED: 2021 organic base fee growth of 11%, the highest on record.
- ADMITTED: Growth areas are bond ETFs, outsourced CIO mandates, cash, private markets and transition investing.
- ADMITTED: Bank disintermediation after SVB is seen as a capital-markets tailwind.
- ADMITTED (2021): Sustainable AUM grew 64% organically. ADMITTED (2022): sustainable AUM passed $500bn.

**Headwinds and challenges**
- ADMITTED: 2022 market drop cut AUM by $1.7tn. Fink calls the share price "disappointing" in the near term (2023).
- ADMITTED: Political attack from both sides. The grouped "personal and political preferences" questions (2022, 2023), the state anti-boycott/divestment laws (2022), and "recent political attempts to limit BlackRock's ability to manage climate risk" (2023 question).
- ADMITTED: Proxy voting is hampered by "significant regulatory and logistic hurdles" to extending Voting Choice to all investors (2022).
- ADMITTED (Boss 2022): SEC rule changes brought more prescriptive E&S proposals; the energy shortage creates conflicting demands.
- INFERRED: Say-on-pay support fell from ~95% (2022) to ~92% (2023), and support for climate-side proposals rose to ~10% (Rissman). Both flanks are getting louder.
- Claimed by Rissman, not admitted by BlackRock: client attrition (the UK's largest pension fund); slower growth in Europe (~one-third of business); an activist calling for Fink's resignation.
- INFERRED: Governance criticism keeps returning: board size (16–17), the combined Chair/CEO role, director pay, dilution, the 198:1 pay ratio. Questions on dilution and board size come back every year.
- ADMITTED (2021): DEI "a great deal of work to do"; China human-rights questions every year.

## 7. Outside voices / skeptics (every named questioner and proponent)

**2021-05-26**
- *Rick Alexander for James McRitchie* (Item 5, convert to a public benefit corporation). Argument: the governance guidelines commit the board to shareholders "and no one else"; BlackRock (with $9tn under management) won't "tell management what to do" about destroying natural capital. Its $5.5tn index business delivers market returns cheaply instead of improving market-wide returns. Systematic factors explain up to 94% of returns, so system stewardship would help diversified holders. "Not meant to appeal to altruism." Board opposed. **Vote: ~2.3% for, ~97% against.**
- *SEIU Pension Plans Master Trust* — racial equity audit of impact on non-white stakeholders and communities of color. Withdrawn after BlackRock agreed to an external DEI review "next year". Fink, asked by a shareholder: "nothing is better than transparency"; the review starts after the DEI strategy's foundations are in place.
- *Mercy Investment Services + Boston Trust Walden + co-filers*: review climate proxy voting for gaps between stated climate-risk views and votes. They saw a mid-2020 shift toward supporting climate proposals and voting against directors at laggards. They praised BlackRock but "were unable to withdraw." No vote result was read for this item. INFERRED: it was not on the ballot (only 5 items are listed and Item 5 was McRitchie's).
- *[Philip George, Walter Cunan, H. Amos Goodall]* — why 16 directors? Fink: diversity of geography and expertise; meetings are "lively, sometimes even confrontational"; directors may visit any office unannounced. Nixon: annual evaluation; tech expertise added as Aladdin grew.
- *[David Dados]* — why didn't director pay drop during COVID? Gerber: Semler Brossy reviews it; pay is competitive, not an outlier; 46 board and committee meetings.
- Unnamed — why give shares (dilution)? Ford: burn rate 0.8%; >60% of NEO pay in deferred equity; ownership guidelines.
- Unnamed — sustainability goals. Fink: 100% of active and advisory strategies are ESG-integrated; 100+ sustainable products; Aladdin Climate; net zero by 2050; temperature-alignment metrics; "can't do it alone."
- Many shareholders — DEI. Fink: target of +30% Black and Latinx US representation, and double the number of Black and Latinx senior leaders by 2024. Black MDs and directors are up 14% and Latinx up 9%. The corporate credit facility's pricing is tied to DEI progress; there is a supplier code; $295bn traded through diverse brokers.
- "More than a dozen" shareholders — why sign the CEO voter-rights statement (state voting laws)? Fink: "this is not politics… this is about our principles"; 300+ companies signed.
- Unnamed — investing in companies with negative externalities, including private prisons. Fink: index clients choose the index and BlackRock has no right to deviate. It offers ESG Advanced ETFs that screen out 14 sectors and require an MSCI ESG rating of BBB or better, and is pushing index providers for more sustainable indices.
- *[Ortho Oman, Roberta Giordano, Ben Cushing]* — action against fossil-fuel companies without Paris-aligned plans. Boss: focus universe up to 1,000+ companies; may vote against directors responsible for climate oversight.
- *[Moira Birth]* — indigenous communities, deforestation. Boss: natural-capital commentary; asks companies for location-level sourcing disclosure.
- Unnamed, plus *[Nancy Mancias]* — China and human rights. Fink: UN Global Compact; follows US rules; China's retirement crisis; "big believer of global capital markets". (INFERRED, outside knowledge: Nancy Mancias is a known Code Pink campaigner.)
- *[William Athold]* — communities. Fink: BlackRock Foundation, $589m philanthropic commitment, Emergency Savings Initiative ($50m), COVID relief ($50m).
- *[Joseph Volcan, Itehab Chowdry]* — buybacks and a stock split (shares above $800). Fink: $1.2bn buyback target for 2021; "I just don't see any value in" a split.
- *[Scott Shepherd and Tim Smith]* — how proxy votes are decided. Fink and Boss: sole aim is long-term value; conflicts of interest are managed transparently. (INFERRED, outside knowledge: Scott Shepard is an anti-ESG activist, linked to NCPPR's Free Enterprise Project, and Tim Smith is with Boston Trust Walden, a pro-ESG investor. Opposing camps grouped into one question.)
- Unnamed — bitcoin. Fink: "I'm not the best spokesperson"; studying infrastructure and regulation; possibly gold-like; dealers capture the spread.

## 8. Market-structure links

- **Index providers as gatekeepers:** "BlackRock does not choose the components of the index". Named indices are the FTSE 100, S&P 500 and Russell 2000. BlackRock is "actively engaged with the index providers" for ESG variants. ESG Advanced ETFs rely on **MSCI** ESG ratings (BBB+ cutoff). (2021) Over 90% of equity assets are indexed (2022); 75% of China exposure arrives via standard indexes (2022). So index and data vendors (S&P, FTSE Russell/**LSEG**, MSCI) effectively set much of BlackRock's exposure and ESG screens.
- **Dealers and spreads:** Fink says crypto profits go to "broker dealers" via wide bid-ask spreads (2021). That is the incumbent buy-side view of dealer intermediation, relevant to venue electronification.
- **Diverse broker program:** $295bn of securities traded with diverse brokers in the US in 2020 (2021). Shows BlackRock directs order flow to chosen counterparties.
- **Aladdin as connectivity and risk layer:** usage spiked after SVB. Clients used "enterprise level broker and trade restrictions," modelled exposure "across the yield curve," and Aladdin Wealth usage "more than doubled" (2023). Directors added for tech expertise "with the growth of Aladdin" (2021). Aladdin Climate (2021).
- **Bond ETFs:** named as a strategic priority and Q1-2023 growth leader ("expanding the markets for bond ETFs"; "precision ETFs" for tactical liquidity) (2023). Bond aggregate index down nearly 10% (2022).
- **Voting Choice:** technology-enabled pass-through proxy voting for $2tn+ of index equity assets; regulatory and logistical hurdles (2022). That is a plumbing change in corporate governance.
- **Capital markets vs banks:** the regional-bank crisis "will ultimately fuel another round of growth in capital markets" (2023). A disintermediation thesis supportive of venues, private credit and ETFs.
- No mention of Tradeweb, MarketAxess, Bloomberg, ICE or tokenization in these three transcripts. Refinitiv (now LSEG) appears only as the 2021 transcript vendor and FactSet as the 2022–23 vendor.

## 9. Shifts over time

- **Climate:** 2021 is opportunity and leadership ("climate risk is investment risk"; temperature metrics; net zero "by 2050 or sooner"; even climate proponents praise BlackRock). 2022 brings "fair and just" transition, "against divestiture", energy security, and a "tactical" pullback on climate proposals. By 2023 the line is "not to engineer real world decarbonization," pure client choice, and "Let me leave it there." The 2023 opening remarks never mention climate or sustainability except "transition investing."
- **Who attacks:** 2021 critics come mostly from the progressive side (PBC, racial equity, private prisons, China, deforestation), plus questions about the voter-rights statement. 2022 adds the first grouped anti-ESG "political preferences" question and state anti-boycott laws. In 2023 formal proposals come from both flanks (NCPPR right; Rissman and Code Pink left), and BlackRock opposes all three. A questioner calls this "hypocritical."
- **Stakeholder language:** 2021 is full of "stakeholder capitalism" and "all our stakeholders". 2022 keeps "full range of stakeholders" in the voice answer. 2023 shifts to "We are fiduciary to our clients. The money we manage belongs to them" and a "powerful simplicity" of client value equals shareholder value.
- **DEI:** 2021 sets numeric targets for Black and Latinx representation and ties them to credit-facility pricing. 2022 reports analyst-class demographics. 2023 reports an independent assessment and the Count Me In campaign, then defends DEI in legal terms ("equal opportunity").
- **Crypto:** only 2021 (curious but sceptical); no mention in 2022–23.
- **Tech/Aladdin:** grows steadily in prominence. Revenue from $1bn to $1.3bn to $1.4bn; by 2023 Aladdin gets its own Q&A answer as crisis infrastructure.
- **Shareholder-proposal support** climbs: 2.3% (2021) to 3.6% (2022) to ~10% / ~8% for the climate-side proposals (2023), while the anti-DEI proposal gets <1%.

## 10. Surprises / note-worthy

1. **The 2021 climate proponents (Mercy, Boston Trust Walden) praised BlackRock and only stayed on the ballot because they "were unable to withdraw."** Two years later another climate proponent (Rissman) calls BlackRock "timid." It is a sharp reversal in how climate investors view the firm.
2. **BlackRock's own stewardship head admitted in May 2022 that it would back fewer climate proposals.** She tied this partly to the SEC's changed definition of permissible proposals, so a regulator loosening the rules produced *less* support from the largest asset manager.
3. **Fink's crypto critique in 2021 is about market structure:** dealers' bid-ask spreads capture the economics. This is the thesis that a regulated, ETF-wrapped product (later IBIT) would compress those spreads.
4. **A single 2021 question grouped Scott Shepard (anti-ESG) with Tim Smith (pro-ESG).** INFERRED identification. Both flanks were asking how proxy votes are decided, the same pressure that led to Voting Choice.
5. **Rissman cited BlackRock's own 10-K and its own ~25% GDP-loss estimate against it. Code Pink used BlackRock's own ">3°C" fact-sheet metric.** Activists turn BlackRock's disclosures and data tools into ammunition, a cost of transparency.
6. **Fink's answer to "too big" is that BlackRock holds only ~2% of global financial assets. In the same meeting he says BlackRock has "the largest stewardship team in the industry" and that over 90% of equity assets are indexed.** Both are true, but they sit in tension with the concentration critique.
