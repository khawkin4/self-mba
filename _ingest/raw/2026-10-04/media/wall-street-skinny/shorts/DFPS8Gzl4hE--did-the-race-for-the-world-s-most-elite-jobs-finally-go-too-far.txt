# Did the race for the world's most elite jobs finally go TOO FAR?
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DFPS8Gzl4hE | NA | NAs | 3100 views

Two of Wall Street's biggest players, JP Morgan and Apollo, just took important steps that could finally put an end to one of the most chaotic and controversial practices in high finance— early hiring into investment funds. Previously, private equity firms traditionally recruited from investment banks after analysts had completed training, had a few months of deal experience, proven their qualifications or lack thereof, and had time to consider the next steps in their careers. But as the race for talent has become increasingly competitive over the past few years, the most elite private equity firms have started hiring earlier and earlier to ensure they get the best candidates before someone else does. Last year, recruitment began before analysts even started training. So, within days of graduating from college, candidates were rushing to get job offers for two years ahead. We're talking late-night phone calls, multiple intensive interview stages, and limited-time offers . Pure madness. Who initiated this process? As one recruiter we spoke to said: the hiring cycle begins when Apollo gives the go-ahead. Even if other firms didn't want to get involved in this competition, they couldn't sit idly by and watch their competitors snatch up the most desirable specialists. The result is that the level of professional burnout has increased rapidly. Investment banks found themselves with an “ army of zombies” instead of employees. Private equity firms weren't impressed when these hired workers finally came to them, as young talent burned out before they even sat down at their desks. They were so busy securing their future that they forgot to figure out if they really wanted these positions. A frustrated JP Morgan said last week: "If you accept an offer within 18 months of starting work, you are fired." But wait, that doesn't make sense. You are punishing candidates for participating in a game whose rules they did not create. In this game of survival, private equity firms, led by the most elite mega-funds, were the first to make concessions. This week we got the answer. Apollo announced on Wednesday that it will not start hiring for 2027 until next year. Yesterday, General Atlantic did the same. Finally, a little common sense. We hope this is the start of a smarter hiring schedule that will benefit everyone: banks, private equity firms, and most importantly, the talent themselves.
