I've used the analogy before of like a cereal bin that's sitting on a shelf and you go in and get cereal from the top and whatever's at the top gets taken out first and whatever's at the bottom is kind of just the crumbs. Or you can think about it like a dispenser hanging on the wall and everything falls through as the bottom falls out, quote unquote. And so within your capital structure, you have your debt investors and your equity investors. Debt investors typically get paid less in terms of their return and they wear less risk because as you said, if a company goes through a bankruptcy, restructuring, etc., etc., they're much higher in that capital structure when it comes to priority of getting paid back. Equity investors historically have much higher returns, they get paid much more for wearing the risk that if there is in fact an event of default, a bankruptcy, something that goes wrong, they are the ones who typically get wiped out. And secure Secured means there is something some physical thing typically anchoring you to reality. But it may not be like a building, it may not be like a computer, it could be cash flows from the business that are securing your stake on that company. Then as you get lower and lower down that capital structure, you lose that seniority, you become junior, and you may lose that security as well. And then equity investor, it's just a hope and a prayer of like, "Please God, let there be something left over when this all goes wrong for me."
