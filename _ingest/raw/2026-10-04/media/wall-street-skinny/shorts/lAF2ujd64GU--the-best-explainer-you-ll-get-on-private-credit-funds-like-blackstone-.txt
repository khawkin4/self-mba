This seems like low risk, high reward, which is obviously amazing. But, that was the bull case. It was what happens when everything goes right. And any debt investor knows you have to always think about what can go wrong. There's a saying, "Equity investors have to pick winners, debt investors or credit investors just have to avoid the losers." Because if you buy into equity, you are buying into essentially upside that is unlimited. Yes, you can lose it all, but if things go well, they can go to the moon. That's why venture capital firms, for example, are so aggressive. Most of their investments are going to fail, but they cannot afford to miss the next 10 profit, the next SpaceX, because one winner can make the entire fund. Now, credit is the opposite. If things go well, your upside is basically capped at par. You collect your interest payments, and then you get paid back at maturity. If things go poorly, the company can default, and then the question becomes, "Okay, well, how much money am I actually getting back?" And so, when analyzing any kind of investment that is backed by the cash flows off of a pool of loans, two key assumptions are what percent of the loan pool is going to default, and then if there are defaults, how much are you going to be able to recover. And this is incredibly important for investors who have bought into funds like BCRED or OBDC, because what they bought into is levered. Meaning, right, investors, they bought in, they put in, say, $100, and then the fund went out and borrowed an additional 100, and so they made $200 worth of loans. When everything's performing, you get returns like what we've seen for the past 5 years. But when the loans stop performing, the leverage that juiced the returns on the way up is the same leverage that amplifies the losses on the way down. Back to this point, what could go right?
