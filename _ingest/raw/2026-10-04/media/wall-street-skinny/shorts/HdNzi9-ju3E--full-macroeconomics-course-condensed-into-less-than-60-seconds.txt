Here's the thing. There are lots of different interest rates, and the one that the Fed just held flat is the overnight Fed funds rate. They actually don't control the longer end of the yield curve. That is set by the market. And right now, with oil prices spiking, it's stoking inflation fears, which is pushing up rates in the long end of the curve. Now, the Fed can, in fact, influence those longer-term rates through something called quantitative easing or QE. The Fed steps in as a buyer, buying long-dated Treasury bonds and mortgage-backed securities. They create new money deposit into the accounts of banks like JP Morgan or Morgan Stanley. That additional demand pushes yields down, so causes the longer end of the curve to drop. Is the Fed doing that? No. The Fed is an independent body with two goals. Number one, maximize employment, and number two, keep inflation around 2%. By quantitative easing when there are real inflation issues, that would be like pouring gasoline on the fire.
