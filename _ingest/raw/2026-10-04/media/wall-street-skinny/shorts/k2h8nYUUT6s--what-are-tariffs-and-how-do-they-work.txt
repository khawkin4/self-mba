# What are tariffs and how do they work?
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2h8nYUUT6s | NA | NAs | 38000 views

What the hell is a tariff and why do you even care? I'm going to break it down as simply as I can, making some broad generalizations here. A tariff is just a tax paid on something that is imported. Who pays those taxes? The businesses importing the goods. Whom do they pay their taxes to? Their own government. So, pretend that I need to buy a camera to record this video. Let's say that camera is made in the country of Govia. their cost and profit involved in making and selling that camera to a US company called let's say cameras are us. We'll call it $50. Now, let's say cameras are us marks it up by another $50 to sell it to me the consumer. If there's no tariffs and forgetting about sales taxes, the cost to me is $100. If the US now put a $10 tariff on Govia, the total cost of the camera is now $110. That $10 has to be paid to US customs. So, what happens? Well, there's a few options. One, the Govian manufacturers and exporters can lower the price of the camera they've made and sold to the US from $50 to $40. That takes the price of the camera when I buy it from $110 back down to $100. But the Govian company loses some of its profit margin. If they don't want to do that and the cost of the camera coming into the US is still $50, the US company cameras are us can then lower their markup from $50 down to $40. That would also take the price that I pay as the US consumer from 110 back down to 100. But then it's the US company that's eating into their profit margin. And if neither the Govian manufacturer nor the US company selling the product want to change their profit margins, then the cost of that tariff gets passed along to the US consumer. Me, I'm now either paying 10% more for the exact same product or I'm not going to buy it at all and I will look for a cheaper option. There is however a difference between a tariff and a trade deficit or surplus.
