What does Bravo TV have to do with the Federal Reserve? It turns out economists have been using pop culture to predict recessions for almost a century. We're counting down the top 10 and you'll never believe what comes in number one. Number 10, the Hemline Index. In 1926, economist George Taylor said skirts get shorter in boom times. Well, at the time, women wore silk stockings, which were really expensive. So, showing those off was a sign of exuberance. Pretty impressive that this theory came out 3 years before the crash of 1929. Number nine, there's a theory that women go blonder when the economy is good because highlights are expensive upkeep. But, as someone who personally switched from highlights to my natural color, I can tell you I'm spending just as much or even more. Still, every economic cycle we get a New York Times piece about Recession Brunette. Number eight is the Lipstick Index. This comes from Leonard Lauder of Estee Lauder, who in 2001 said that women switch from higher price point luxury spends to lipstick when times are tough. Worked well in 2001, but never really again. Number seven, the High Heel Index. An IBM researcher argued that heel heights go up when the stock market goes down. It tracked well in 2008. I mean, I still have the Louboutins that I bought back then. But, thanks to 2020, I think we can all safely hunker down in our sneakers now. Number six, the Hot Waitress Index. Not my words, okay? Hugo Lindgren published this theory in New York Magazine that the servers at restaurants get hotter when the economy tanks and models or actors get pushed out of higher paying gigs. Basically, the plot of Vanderpump Rules. Follow so you don't miss part two. We're recovering one that Alan Greenspan reportedly swore by.
