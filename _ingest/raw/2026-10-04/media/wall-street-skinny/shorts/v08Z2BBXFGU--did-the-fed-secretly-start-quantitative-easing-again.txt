# Did the Fed secretly start Quantitative Easing again?
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v08Z2BBXFGU | NA | NAs | 4100 views

The Fed just secretly start quantitative easing. The internet might have you think so. People are pointing to the fact that the Fed's Soma portfolio, which stands for system open market account, bought 20 billion three-year notes and nearly 15 billion 10-year notes at the auctions this week. And they're saying quantitative easing is back. Are they right? Is this secretly happening? Let's explain. This is the Fed's Soma portfolio since quantitative easing started after the great financial crisis in 2008. Over the past few years, the Fed has been in a period of quantitative tightening where they've been shrinking their balance sheet by allowing aging securities to roll off. They were allowing up to 25 billion in treasuries to roll off each month. But at their last meeting, they announced that they reduce that number to 5 billion starting in April. So, what does that mean in practice? Well, the US Treasury auctions off debt every single month all year long. Every month, we get new 2-year notes, threeear notes, 5ear notes, and sevenyear notes. But we only get new tens, 20s, and 30s once a quarter in May, August, November, and February. Those issues are then reopened in the following two months auctions and added on to. It's May right now, so it's a quarterly refunding month where we are getting new 10, 20s, and long bonds. The Fed has something like 76 billion in Soma Holdings of Treasuries maturing this month, meaning they're getting their principle back from all those old bonds. If they do nothing, their balance sheet will shrink by 76 billion. So, it needs to replace something like 71 billion to stay on track. That's what the Soma participation in the Treasury Auctions means. They're just replacing most of what's rolling off while allowing their balance sheet to shrink only slightly. So, no, the Fed is not secretly quantitative easing. All eyes will be on the Fed today at 2 p.m.
