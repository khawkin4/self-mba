# The TRUTH about AI “Taking Jobs”: Morgan Stanley’s Head of US Thematic Research Weighs In
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v5bT9Rr1mBA | NA | NAs | 5300 views

I think we're seeing some of these changes happening beneath the surface now. So our economists produced an interesting report last month looking at the professions that are most dependent on AI, and how does unemployment compare to typical rates in these fields? They found an increase in unemployment rates about 50 basis points above normal for occupations more exposed to AI among the youngest group of workers. So, I think these are just the first signs that we've seen in some of the official macroeconomic data. We also did a lot of survey work on our part. We surveyed companies that have been implementing AI for at least 12 months and asked about their hiring decisions over the past year, finding a net job loss of 5% in these industries. But it is very important to understand what is behind this number. This is the number of new hires minus layoffs minus positions for which no replacements are being sought. And a significant part of this workload falls on the last item— positions for which new people are not being hired. So now, in my opinion, companies are considering personnel decisions: someone is leaving, someone is finding a new opportunity. The threshold for filling this vacancy is significantly higher than it was in the past. We are not seeing mass layoffs yet. We don't see entire groups of workers being replaced en masse by automation, but the process is beginning, and gradual, small changes are taking place.
