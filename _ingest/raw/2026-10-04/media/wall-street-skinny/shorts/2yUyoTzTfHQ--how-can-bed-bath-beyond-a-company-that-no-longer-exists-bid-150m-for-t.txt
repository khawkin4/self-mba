# How can Bed Bath & Beyond -- a company that no longer exists -- bid $150M for The Container Store?
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2yUyoTzTfHQ | NA | NAs | 23000 views

Bed Bath &amp; Beyond is back from the dead and buying The Container Store for $150 million. Now, if you're like me and wildly confused because last you saw Bed Bath &amp; Beyond had gone bankrupt and not the type where you go in for a facelift and you come out the other side looking fresh and new. No, no, they had gone the liquidation route. Put them in the ground, put a fork in them, bury them, they're dead. So, how exactly is it that now they are out and about and they are trying to spend $150 million to buy another company? The answer is wild. But before we get into it, a couple of basics. When a company gets in trouble they can enter into a chapter 7 or chapter 11 bankruptcy proceeding. Chapter 11 is kind of like you're having health problems, but you're going in for surgery. The company emerges from bankruptcy new, improved, able to operate, able to service their debt, and able to start fresh and clean. This actually happens a ton. I mean, companies go through chapter 11 and they come out the other side all the time. Look at any airline, they've probably gone through a chapter 11. Now, there's also something called chapter 7, which is liquidation. They just start selling everything and they use the proceeds from the sale to pay the lenders back. Now, weirdly, Bed Bath &amp; Beyond actually did do a chapter 11, although they ultimately liquidated everything. So, they closed down their stores, they sold all their inventory, and the main thing they also sold was their brand. They sold their brand to overstock.com. And so, what we are seeing now is actually overstock.com operating as Bed Bath &amp; Beyond buying The Container Store. This is less a case of Kris Jenner going in for a facelift and coming out looking beautiful and amazing and by the way as she does, it's more a case of The Talented Mr. Ripley and assuming someone else's identity. Although, in this case less murder and more just bought the identity for the highest price during a liquidation.
