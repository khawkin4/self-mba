This is about two to three times more expensive. So why is Hugging Face so valuable to Nvidia? I'll explain it to you in less than three minutes. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has called the AI ​​ecosystem a “ five-layer cake,” with Nvidia being the main player in the second layer, chips, and Anthropic and OpenAI being the main players in the fourth layer, models. But did you know that Nvidia also has its own models? Although they are open, which brings us to an important branch: closed versus open. We'll figure out what that means in a moment. To get you started, let's discuss which companies have which models and whether they are open or closed. Companies developing advanced models mostly rely on closed- scale models. In fact, Anthropic has never produced models with open scales. Hyperscalers, on the other hand, have both . Chinese companies mostly have open-scale models, and Nvidia has not only never released a closed-scale model, it is paying $13 billion for a company that is the center of open AI. To understand why, let's take a step back. There are two separate steps to building and deploying a large language model (LLM) . The first is learning. Labs take text, such as the sentence "The dog is sleeping," and break each word into tokens , each of which is assigned a string of numbers called a vector. They then perform mathematical operations to associate each word with the most likely word that follows it. To do this, the model has trillions of parameters, also called weights, which on a simplified scale can be thought of as a coefficient when performing a regression. For financial experts, it's like calculating the beta coefficient of a stock. Plot the return of, say, Meta relative to the return of the S&amp;P, and the slope of the line will be your beta, or in this case, our parameter or weight. At the beginning of the training, these weights were random noise, but with each round they got closer to something meaningful. In fact, a study was conducted in 2013 where they took the vector for "king", subtracted the vector for " man" and added " woman", and the result turned out to be close to the vector for "queen". So, after training, the end result is a file with billions or trillions of parameters that are captured. And this brings us to the important second stage— inference, where the question of open or closed scales becomes relevant. A closed model means that developers do not share their model. Instead, they keep it private, and it can only be used for a fee to them or their licensing partners. But the most interesting thing, going back to Nvidia, is that the chips for inference—that is, for the user to be able to ask a question and get an answer—don't have to be as complex as the chips for learning. In fact, many leading companies and cloud giants are now developing their own inference chips, instead of relying solely on Nvidia. And this brings us to the open- source ecosystem, where developers allow anyone to upload a file and run their model. Why would they do that? Well, let's compare the number of model parameters, from less than a billion to 10 trillion, with the amount of memory required to run it. The more parameters, the more powerful the model, but it usually runs slower and requires more computing resources. With less than 100 GB of memory, which roughly supports about 100 million parameters, you can run the model locally on your own computer. Beyond that, you usually need to rent computing power from a cloud provider, where inference chips, such as Nvidia GPUs, are located in data centers, which explains why companies like Google and Alibaba give away models for free. They make money from computing. And that's why Nvidia also needs an open source ecosystem. The biggest competitive threat to Nvidia is that its largest customers are building their own inference chips. By owning Hugging Face, the resource everyone turns to for open-source models, Nvidia provides customer diversification and can make its deployment software that runs on their chips the path of least resistance.
