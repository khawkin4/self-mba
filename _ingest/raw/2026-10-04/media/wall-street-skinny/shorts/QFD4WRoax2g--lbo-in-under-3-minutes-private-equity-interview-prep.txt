What is a leveraged buyout? Simply put, it is the purchase of a company financed primarily through debt. So if you or your parents have ever bought a house, you probably did a tiny LBO, because buying a house is financed very similarly to a leveraged buyout. So, how does it work? Well, let's imagine we buy a house for $10 million. Unless you are extremely wealthy, which you probably have to be to buy a house like this, you won't be paying 10 million in cash. Instead, you go to the bank and say, "Hey, can you lend me some money?" Maybe you only need to deposit, say, $3 million in cash, and you get the remaining $7 million from the bank. And maybe your goal is not to live in this house. This is investment property. You want to buy a house, renovate it, and then sell it for a higher price and make a profit. This is exactly what a private equity firm does. They buy companies with the aim of improving their business and selling them for more than they bought them, and use leverage, i.e. debt, to increase their profits. So, the way a private equity firm increases the value of a business is by trying to improve its operations. They are trying to increase sales. They are trying to cut costs, and this will improve a metric called EBITDA. EBITDA is a measure of operating cash flow. You will often value a company based on a specific EBITDA multiple. To put the numbers into perspective, let's imagine you buy a company that generates $1 million in EBITDA. You cut costs, you increase revenue, and now when you sell it, say, in 5 years , EBITDA has grown to $1.5 million. Now you apply the same multiplier of 10, and your valuation has gone from $10 million to $15 million . The second thing that happens during this period is that the company generates cash flow. And with these cash flows, they repay the debt. Now let's do some very simple calculations. You borrowed $7 million from the bank. Over these 5 years, you generated $1 million in cash flow. So, you take that 7 million, subtract 1 million , and you're left with 6 million in debt. Now we want to calculate the profitability for the sponsor. So, the sponsor is not necessarily interested in how much the value of the house has increased. The fact that the amount increased from 10 to 15 million. This is great. But they are interested in the invested funds and the profit they receive. Remember, they only invested $3 million of their own money. How much money do they get back? They will receive $15 million after the sale of the business. Then they take $6 million from this amount , give it to the bank, and as a result, $9 million is left. This brings us to the final part . We want to calculate the internal rate of return (IRR). So, they invested $3 million. The cash they received was $9 million. If we look at this over a 5-year period, we get an IRR of 25%.
