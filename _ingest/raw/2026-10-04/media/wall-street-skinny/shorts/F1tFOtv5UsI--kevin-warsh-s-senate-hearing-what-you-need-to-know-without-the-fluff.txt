# Kevin Warsh's Senate Hearing: What You Need to Know WITHOUT the Fluff
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F1tFOtv5UsI | NA | NAs | 45000 views

What two things does the new Fed chair want? I watched Kevin Warsh's Senate hearing today so you don't have to. Forget the political theater or the fact that he's super rich. Here's what you actually need to know. I was on the trading floor when Warsh was a Fed governor during the financial crisis. This guy was a straight-up hawk, okay? Meaning he favored higher interest rates and a smaller Fed balance sheet. But guess what? He agreed to break out the bazooka in the form of quantitative easing, which had never been done before in the United States, to save the economy from Armageddon in 2008. So, what does he want to do now as Fed chair 20 years later? He wants two things. The first is old-school Fed policy using short-term interest rates rather than the Fed's balance sheet to impact the economy. In Warsh's own words, "Using the Fed's balance sheet to move the economy is indirect. It hits financial instruments first, and most American families don't even own those." He'd rather use short-term borrowing rates as the transmission mechanism. Translation, less quantitative easing, more simple rate policy, and likelier a steeper yield curve. That's a total regime change from what we've seen for pretty much the past two decades. The second is to have the Fed stay in its lane. No messing with fiscal policy, meaning Treasury borrowing and lending, to protect its independence from politics. Remember, the Fed officially has two mandates: controlled inflation and maximum employment. But there's an unofficial third mandate that nobody really talks about: keeping interest costs on US debt manageable. With $37 trillion in debt, every basis point that rates move higher cost taxpayers billions. The Fed can't ignore this, but they can't constantly be solving fiscal problems with monetary policy solutions, either, if they want to maintain their independence. So, here's the bottom line. Warsh is a hawk who's proven he'll be dovish when absolutely necessary. He wants to run a tighter, more traditional Fed, and he says he'll keep politics out of it. If he pulls it off, it's the biggest regime change at the Fed in 20 years. If he doesn't, the third mandate basically becomes the only mandate, and the Fed then becomes an extension of the executive branch. Watch the yield curve and you'll know whether or not he's succeeding.
