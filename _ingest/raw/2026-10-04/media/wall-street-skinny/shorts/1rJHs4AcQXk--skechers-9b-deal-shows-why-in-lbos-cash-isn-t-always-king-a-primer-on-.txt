Often times what will happen is a private equity firm buys the company. Management may get something called rollover equity where they basically don't sell out entirely. They sort of roll some of their shares into the new business. So they actually do participate in the upside. But again, that's usually reserved for like management. In this case, 3G is offering shareholders to elect to get either the $63 in cash or take $57 in cash plus what's called one equity unit. Now, an equity unit, think of it as like stock in the new company, but it's private. It doesn't trade. It's also important to note that there's a limit on how many existing shareholders can choose that second option. So 3G wants to make sure that they're going to own 80% of the equity and the rollover equity is capped at 20%. But it potentially incentivizes some of these shareholders to sell because I would be a little bit like I know the share the company's not doing great but like I want to see what happens with these tariffs. I don't necessarily want to just cash out now at like a low when I'm down 20%. Participate somehow in future upside, right? Aside from just I'll keep my job and hopefully the company stays afloat. Exactly. It's like you were selling your house to someone who is going to flip it. And you're like, "Hey, listen. I'm selling you my house for $500,000. I know once you repair it, you're going to sell it for a million. I'm willing to sell it to you for maybe $450, but I want to participate in that upside when you get it done. If you don't get it done, I'm in there with you. Everyone's interests are aligned on the back end." That's exactly right. For them, it might seem like an attractive option.
