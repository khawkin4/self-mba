Back in the day, businesses could deduct all the interest that they paid on their debt. So, simple idea. If a company is earning $100 operating their business, but they're paying $100 in interest, they would get to deduct the $100 of interest from their income, leaving them with literally no taxable income and paying no taxes at all. That changed in 2017 during the first Trump administration. They put limits on how much companies could deduct in interest payments. EBIT, basically all that it is is it's the income that you earn from operating your business. EBIDA on the other hand is a proxy for operating cash flow, but more importantly, IBIDA is always higher than EBIT because literally to calculate it, it's EBIT plus DNA. So if your EBIT is 100 and your DNA is 50, EBITDA is 150. Now, why do we actually care? Backing up to the Trump tax cuts, what it said is that from 2018 to 2022, companies could deduct interest on their debt up to 30% of IBIDA. I'm using our example where we said IBIDA is 150. So, 30% of 150 is 45. Now, going back to that company has $100 of income and they have interest of 100. That would mean they could deduct $45. So, their taxable income would be 100 -4 or $55. But starting in 2023, the limit on those deductions got dropped from 30% of IBIT DA to 30% of EBIT. So instead of having 30% of 150, we're now looking at 30% of 100. So that's only $30 of deductions we can take. So instead of 100 minus 45, you're now saying 100 minus 30. So now you're paying taxes on 70 instead of 55. That might not seem like a big number, but for private equity firms, literally known for doing leveraged buyouts. Leverage is a fancy word for debt.
