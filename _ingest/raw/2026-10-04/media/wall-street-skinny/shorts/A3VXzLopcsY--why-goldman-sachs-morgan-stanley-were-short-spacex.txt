# Why Goldman Sachs & Morgan Stanley were Short SpaceX
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A3VXzLopcsY | NA | NAs | 56000 views

Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and all the bookrunners on the SpaceX IPO, they stand to make a ton of money if the SpaceX share price drops below the IPO price of 135. And I know that sounds crazy, but this is actually a pretty typical feature of an IPO. So, here's the thing. We were all told that the SpaceX IPO was a $75 billion offering. Guess what? Goldman, Morgan, all those banks, they actually went out to the market and they sold $85 billion worth of stock. What happens next is over the next 30 days, if the share price of SpaceX drops below the 135 offer price, all of those bookrunners, because they're basically short, need to go out into the market and buy SpaceX shares to put upward pressure on the stock. That is how they're acting as a stabilizing agent. They become buyers in the event of the share price going down. However, if the share price does well, then they have the option from SpaceX to exercise the green shoe, which is this overallotment option, and so they actually don't have to go to the market to buy that back. And so, the end result is that if the share price does well, green shoe's exercised, they don't have to buy anything. If the share price does poorly, they've sold at 135 and they're buying at a lower price. So, they would stand to make money if the share price does poorly.
