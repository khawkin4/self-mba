When we're talking about any kind of M&amp;A deal, when there's a stock component, there is uncertainty for the buyer and the seller. If you're a seller, you care about getting a certain amount of value. You've been promised that you're going to be getting 2775. You want to make sure that you get that even if the share price of Netflix drops. But on the flip side, the buyer, if their share price goes down, what they're focused on is how many shares am I giving to the seller in order to make this deal work? Because they're basically going to have to issue equity. and the more equity that they have to issue, the more diluted it's going to be, the less this deal looks good from sort of an affordability standpoint. The way that you can protect both of these parties is by doing something called a collar. And the way that they've structured this in this Netflix transaction is if you think about a graph, if Netflix share price is between 97 and 119, then Netflix promises to give the shareholders of Warner Brothers $4.50. and the exchange ratio, meaning how many shares you are getting of Netflix, is going to be adjusted in that band to come up with a fixed value of $4.50. However, if the share price is under $97, then you're going to get a fixed exchange ratio of 0.046, but above a certain share price, it would get fixed at 0.376. So, that's something that's pretty typical in M&amp;A transactions whenever you have stock-based consideration because of that uncertainty in between when the deal is agreed upon and when the deal actually closes.
