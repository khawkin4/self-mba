# What the xAI / X merger teaches us about who the real losers were in the all stock deal
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKo9YyqMdEk | NA | NAs | 4600 views

If you saw headlines, XAI bought Twitter aka X in an all stock deal and thought, "Who cares? Elon owns both." Here's why it actually matters. So, Elon owns a majority stake in both, but he doesn't own 100%. There are external investors. And now, their ownership stake is getting diluted. Now, if you're not familiar with what that means, let's go ahead and break that down. So, say you owned 25% of XAI and we're told that it's worth $80 billion. Okay? So 20% time 80 billion is 20 billion. That's the value of your stake today. Now when XAI buys Twitter, right, X for $33 billion, they're not using cash, right? In this case, it's an all stock deal. So they're basically issuing new shares of XAI. That means that the combined value of the equity at a $33 billion valuation is 33 plus 80 or 113. So your 25% stake now drops right to 20 over 113 to only 18%. So your piece of the AI pie, what you likely invested in XAI for has now shrank, right? That's the real cost of these stock deals. They reduce how much of the company you own and therefore your claim on any future cash flow. So you instead of previously having 25% claim, now you only have 18%. Now you might say to yourself, "Yeah, but we got Twitter, right? We got X in this deal." You did. But the question is, was X actually worth $33 billion or $45 billion when you factor in the debt? Especially when it was a funding round that Elon Musk was a part of that helped set the price for X in the first place. Now, the reality is that private investors who were buying into XAI were investing into Elon Musk and his vision. So, they knew what they were signing up for when they invested. And they will likely look at this as like even if they're overpaying for Twitter, it's a small price to pay to be an early in an AI startup that's competitive in the AI
