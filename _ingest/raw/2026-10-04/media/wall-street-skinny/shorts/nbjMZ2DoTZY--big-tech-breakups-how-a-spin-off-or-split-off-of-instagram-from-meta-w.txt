Zuckerberg apparently considered spitting off Instagram in 2018 amid antitrust scrutiny. But what does that actually mean? With the government going after not only Meta, but Google and other tech giants, I thought this would be a good chance to break down the different ways that these companies can break up because it might impact you. Odds are you either own stock in a number of these companies directly or indirectly through the NASDAQ, S&amp;P, etc. Okay, so what are the options on the table for companies to break up? So there's basically two main methods. The first is you can do a sale, right? You can sell off a division in something called a devestature. Now for Meta with Instagram, that probably wouldn't work that well. Estimates of the valuation of Instagram are in like the hundred billion dollar plus range. So there's very few companies out there that could actually buy them. Now theoretically, it could be another tech giant like an Apple, Amazon, etc. But that brings us back to our initial problem, which is that monopoly concern, which brings us to the second option, public market separation techniques. And that includes things like spin-offs, equity carveouts, split-offs, a whole bunch of different things. But the one that was actually named by Zuckerberg is what's called a spin-off. So in a spin-off, Meta would take Instagram and they would basically turn it into a separately publicly traded company. So, if you owned shares of Meta after the spin-off, you'd have shares in both Meta and Instagram. It's a clean break and the value of Meta would be lower, right? Because it no longer owns Instagram. But again, because you own Instagram, you now own both. So, you basically have as much value as you had before the spin-off. So, that was the option that Zuckerberg mentioned in 2018. Now, another thing which he didn't specifically mention, but could be an option on the table is something called a splitoff. Now, in this case, as a shareholder, you get the option of whether you want to stay an investor in Meta or exchange your shares for shares of this newly created Instagram stock. Now, this results in two separate investor bases. And it actually functions like a share buyback for Meta because the investors who decide they want to own Instagram have to give up their Meta shares in order to exchange them for shares in Instagram. Now, while this is less common, it is something that potentially is on the table for Meta or other tech giants who might be forced by the government to do a breakup. Now, the irony of all this is that while most companies resist breakups, history shows that when companies are operating as separate entities, like post a spin-off or a split-off, they usually are going to perform better than if they had stayed together. And the reason is because of something called the conglomerate discount which basically just means that if you are operating a conglomerate you are penalized in the market because of almost like that lack of transparency. So by separating these companies, it potentially unlocks value for investors.
