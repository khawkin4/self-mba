# If the Fed won't cut rates...could stablecoins be the key to reducing borrowing costs in the US?
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RwpbsdXHc3Q | NA | NAs | 1200 views

How could cryptocurrencies of all things actually help lower interest rates in the United States? That might sound wild at first, but smart money is betting on it. And the key player, stable coins. Stable coins are a type of cryptocurrency designed to maintain a stable value, usually pegged to a currency like the US dollar. Unlike Bitcoin or Ethereum, they're not meant to fluctuate in price. They're meant to be useful to store value, transfer funds, and act like digital cash. And there's currently something like 260 billion of them in circulation as of July 2025. Now, why are they so appealing? One, they offer a way to operate outside the formal banking system. For hedge funds moving assets cross border, savers in high inflation countries or just those who want a more flexible way to hold dollars digitally, stable coins are becoming an increasingly attractive option. There are two big macro trends driving this demand. First, investors are moving deeper into the crypto ecosystem. As more capital flows into the space, there's growing demand for a stable medium of exchange. Something to park your value in when you're not trading tokens. Secondly, much of the trepidation about investing in cryptocurrencies is the lack of a tie to something in the real world. Stable coins bridge the gap between fiat currencies and digital assets. Those pegged to the dollar are now emerging as a powerful new vehicle for holding and moving money, especially in emerging markets and decentralized finance. Now, here's where it gets really interesting. How stable coins actually hold their value. Stable coin issuers typically back each coin with short-term highquality assets like US Treasury. So, when demand for stable coins rises, those issuers are going to need to go out and buy more tea bills to back the growing supply. This creates a new source of demand in the treasury market. And when there's a big new buyer of tea bills, prices go up and yields or interest rates come down. In other words, if we see a surge in stable coin usage, we could see a persistent bid for short-term treasuries. That demand could help push short-term interest rates lower even as the Fed doesn't cut rates and even as other market forces like increased issuance push in the opposite direction. It's a wild feedback loop and one that might pick up steam given the fact that the secretary of the US Treasury Scott Besset himself estimated that stable coins could easily reach a $2 trillion market cap in the coming few years. So in essence, the rise of crypto which is often seen as a threat to traditional finance might actually help the US government finance its debt more cheaply.
