A financial model is a visual and mathematical representation of a series of thoughts. You can look at a company's filings. You can talk to the C-suite. You can go down to the factory floor and examine their operations. And at the end of the day, you then need to be able to come up with a coherent thesis about what it all means and what it's worth. And the financial model is the tool that enables you to do that in a standardized way with basic inputs that we can all agree on and basic outputs that are then backed up by this process that you've gone through. That then can be quickly looked at, parsed through, and interpreted by more senior people who are ultimately making the decisions. And that is also then used to go and sell this company to the people who are going to invest in it or to identify opportunities for that company to improve itself, to increase its valuation, etc. etc. So, it is simply a tool. A tool is only as good as the people who are using it. It's only as good as the inputs you give it. And it only has meaning if you're able to then take everything it spits out and know whether or not it's true and know why it's true. Just by being able to replace the process of financial modeling, I don't know that you are going to achieve some massive cost savings for investment banks that doesn't have additional knock-on effects.
