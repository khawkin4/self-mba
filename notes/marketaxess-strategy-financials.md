# Note · MarketAxess strategy & financials — a high-margin compounder whose engine stopped compounding while every headline still said "record"

> Corporate-finance / strategy note · Maps to **Course 01** (Accounting — reading *record* headlines against segment data and changing definitions), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — margins, fee capture, capital return), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — share loss in the core vs diversification), **Course 07** (Data, Analytics & Decision-Making — denominators and self-defined KPIs), **E2** (Capital Allocation — buybacks, a debt-funded ASR, small deals), **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations — how the scoreboard was redrawn) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[marketaxess-hub]]; companions [[marketaxess-ice-deal]], [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]]

- **Source:** MarketAxess corpus `_ingest/raw/marketaxess/articles.jsonl`: **32 earnings releases** (4Q18 → 3Q26), **7 annual Letters to Stockholders and Clients** (FY2019–FY2025), the **2021 Virtual Investor Day** deck and the **Dec 9 2025 Goldman Sachs** deck plus the same-day targets release, and the deal, board and capital releases cited below. Caveats: earnings texts are the narrative only and are **cut at ~20k characters**, so full-year ADV and share tables for FY23–FY25 are missing; 2019–21 releases are short summaries with no fee-per-million (FPM) line. Where a figure is not in the text it is marked *n/r*. Decks are PDF extractions with garbled layout, so their numbers are paraphrased, not quoted. No call transcripts.
- **Core claim:** Revenue went from **$511.4M (FY19) to $846.3M (FY25)**, about **8.8% a year** (derived), but almost all of that came in one year: FY20 was **+34.8%** at a **54.4%** operating margin, and FY20→FY25 compounded at only **~4.2%** (derived). Operating margin then slid to **45.5% (FY22), ~42% (FY23–24) and ~41% (FY25 ex-notable items)**; diluted EPS ex-notables of **$7.39 (FY25)** is still below **$7.85 (FY20)**. MKTX's own Dec 2025 deck admits **US credit revenue shrank at a (2%) CAGR over 2020–24** while non-US credit grew ~15%. Total credit fee per million fell **$184.78 (FY21) → $127 (3Q26), −31%** (derived), and US high-grade share went **22.8% (4Q20) → 18.4% (4Q24)**. Through all of it the releases and letters kept counting *consecutive record years* (11th in FY19, 17th in FY25). As the core stalled, the scoreboard moved: HG share → HG+HY → *composite* → *three channels*; Open Trading savings, EM/Eurobond share and PT share each dropped out. Capital went to small, mostly undisclosed-price deals (LiquidityEdge ~$150M the only priced one), a dividend that rose from $0.51 to $0.78 a quarter, and finally a **$300M ASR with JPMorgan funded with $220M of revolver** — seven and a half months before the board agreed to sell to ICE ([[marketaxess-ice-deal]]).

**Reads that matter:**
- **One great year, then a plateau.** FY19→FY20 added $177.7M of revenue; FY20→FY25 added $157.2M in five years (derived). The FY21 release replaced the old *record earnings* banner with "2-year compound annual growth rate of 17%", the first sign the yearly comparison had stopped flattering.
- **Margin went down while revenue went up.** Expenses rose from $260.5M (FY19) to $504.4M (FY25), +94%, against revenue +65% (derived). Headcount went 527 → 891 before the 2025–26 cuts. The deck's own summary is a *42% operating margin in 2024*, against 54.4% four years earlier.
- **EPS has not recovered its 2020 peak.** $7.85 (FY20) vs $7.39 ex-notables (FY25), despite buybacks. 2026 EPS growth was helped by the ASR: 1Q26 says the ASR drove an ~6% share-count reduction, "enhancing EPS growth".
- **Volatility is the swing factor, and MKTX says so.** 2Q21: "Second quarter results were heavily impacted by low levels of credit market volatility". 3Q21: "Credit spreads and credit spread volatility have remained at historically low levels throughout the year". FY25 letter: "continued tight credit spreads and low credit spread volatility". 2Q26 revenue was flat against a quarter "which benefited from elevated levels of event-driven market volatility".
- **Fee capture is the slow leak.** FY21 $184.78 → FY22 $166.96 → FY24 $150 → FY25 $139 → 3Q26 $127. In 2022 MKTX blamed bond duration only — "There have been no changes to the U.S. high-grade fee plan." — while also citing "dealer migration to fixed-distribution fee plans that command lower transaction fees". From 2024 the explanation became mix: "due principally to protocol mix" (4Q25). The growth protocols (PT, blocks, Mid-X, RFQ-hub) are the low-fee ones (see [[marketaxess-protocols-automation]]).
- **US credit is shrinking inside a growing company.** Dec 2025 deck: US credit revenue (2%) CAGR 2020–24, non-US credit +15%, total +4%; and the headline "A return to U.S. credit revenue growth is fundamental to our future success". FY25 revenue outside US credit was $406M (+10%), so US credit was ~$440M (derived). 2Q26: "a 9% decrease in U.S. credit commission revenue"; 3Q26: −4%.

## 2. KPI drift and the disclosure retreat

The pattern: each time a headline metric stopped rising, the release led with a broader or newer one, and the old one was later dropped or paused, usually with a methodology reason.

| When | What changed | What it hid or revealed |
|---|---|---|
| FY19–FY20 | Headline: *U.S. high-grade … percentage of FINRA's high-grade TRACE* (19.0% → 21.6%; 4Q20 record 22.8%) | The core metric while it rose |
| **4Q21 (Jan 2022)** | Headline becomes "Combined U.S. high-grade and high-yield trading volume as a percentage of FINRA’s combined high-grade and high-yield TRACE trading volume" (19.5% vs 21.1%) | HG fell from its 22.8% record; HY volatility could carry the blend. The FY21 letter gives no HG or HY share at all |
| **2Q22–3Q22** | *Composite corporate bond* share added and headlined; 3Q22 opens "Record levels of estimated market share" while HG was down (30) bps YoY (21.4% → 21.1%); a "3Q19 Pre-Pandemic Levels" comparison column appears | Composite blends HG, HY, EM (TRACE-reportable) and **Eurobonds measured against MKTX's own TraX data**, described as covering ~75% of Europe (2Q22), ~70% (3Q22) and "which is currently estimated to represent approximately 80% of the total European market" (4Q23). A moving own-data denominator |
| FY22 letter | Standalone HG share disappears from the letter; composite 19.9% leads | Last year of share-gain language |

| **4Q24 release (Feb 2025)** | Open Trading **price improvement** dollars no longer given (last: $119M in 3Q24); Concannon introduces the **three channels** (client-initiated, PT, dealer-initiated) | Savings had fallen from >$1B (FY20) to $702M (FY23) |
| **1Q25 onward** | Release *Table 1B* switches from estimated market share to FPM; HG/HY share and Open Trading share leave the narrative; headlines lead with channel ADV *records* | 1Q25 admits "lower estimated market share"; 3Q25 says low volatility "dampened our market share and revenue growth in U.S. credit" |

**Two smaller tells.** (1) FY23 Open Trading savings are **$702M** in the 4Q23 release but **~$470M** in the FY23 letter, with no reconciliation; both are self-measured against best dealer cover and CP+ (definition in the releases). (2) Excluding the boilerplate *stockholders of record*, the word *record* appears 45 times in the scraped 4Q22 release, 29 times in 1Q26, 10 times in 2Q26 and **zero** times in 3Q26 (derived count, scraped text) — the first release written after the sale agreement has nothing left to call a record.

**Why this matters (inference):** none of these changes is false, and most come with a methodology note. But every redefinition moved in the direction that kept the headline rising, and the quantity investors most needed — US HG/HY share and US credit revenue — had to be reconstructed from tables and, finally, from the Dec 2025 deck. The Tradeweb study found the same habit from the other side (context, from [[tradeweb-credit]]: Tradeweb's own TRACE estimates for PT share until the May 2023 flag).

**The arithmetic (derived).** FY21 revenue of $699M was ~14% of the *current* $5B opportunity; FY25's $846M is ~17%. Reaching even the base-case $8.5B by 2031 at 17% capture would need ~$1.45B of revenue, ~9% a year from FY25. The Dec 2025 targets imply FY28 revenue of **$1.07–1.10B** and an operating margin of roughly **43–45%** from FY25's 41.0% ex-notables. Actual 2026 YTD (to 3Q): revenue **+4%** ($662M vs $637M) and ex-notables margin **+20bp** (42.6%) — about half the revenue target in a year with a record volatile Q1. The targets also depended on market volume: they are explicitly conditional on ~6% composite ADV growth, so a calm market voids them by construction. Note that the 2021 *50% international* goal and the 2025 *~50% non-US credit* share are different metrics; the second includes US rates and services.

## 6. What the claims don't prove

1. ***Record revenue* is not record performance.** Seventeen consecutive record-revenue years coexist with a falling margin, EPS below 2020, and a shrinking US credit franchise. A record is a level; the question is the rate and the mix.
2. **Composite share is partly self-measured.** The Eurobond and EM legs used MKTX's own TraX coverage (70–80% of Europe, ~60% of EM per 4Q23), and the coverage estimate itself moved. When those legs were dropped (Jan 2024), the composite history could no longer be checked forward.
3. **Open Trading savings are MKTX's own construct.** Taker savings are measured against best disclosed dealer cover, provider savings against CP+ — MKTX's own model price. The FY23 figure differs by ~$230M between release and letter. None of it is audited or benchmarked against a third-party price (same critique as [[tradeweb-ai-price]]).
4. **FPM decline is explained, not quantified.** Duration, product mix and protocol mix are each named, but no bridge splits the −31% into price, mix and duration. Fixed-distribution fee migrations (both directions) muddy it further.
5. **Channel *records* are volume, not revenue.** Block, PT and Mid-X ADV records sit beside US credit commission declines (−9% 2Q26). Without revenue by channel, the three-channel scorecard cannot show whether the strategy is working.
6. **Deal returns are invisible.** Only LiquidityEdge has a price. Pragma's 2024 revenue roughly equals its expenses. No ROIC objective is stated for any deal.
7. **The targets were conditional on the market.** ~6% composite ADV growth is a market assumption, not a management lever.
8. **Truncation caveat.** FY23–FY25 full-year tables are cut off in the scrape; some shares may have been in tables we cannot see. The claim here is about the *narrative and headline*, which is what most readers see.

## 7. Strategic read (inference — not stated by MarketAxess)

- **The franchise was a volatility-linked toll road on US credit, and the toll fell.** FY20 was the combination of peak volatility, peak HG share and peak FPM. Since then all three mean-reverted together. The company diversified the *mix* (non-US credit to ~half of revenue) faster than it repaired the *core*.
- **Diversification bought growth at lower margins.** Treasuries (~$4–5 FPM), Pragma (breakeven), RFQ-hub and services all grew, but each carries lower margin or fee than US HG RFQ did. That is why revenue rose 65% while operating income rose only ~36% FY19→FY25 (derived).
- **Disclosure retreat is a lagging indicator of competitive loss.** Each dropped KPI marked a front where share had turned: HG (2021), EM/Eurobonds (2024), PT (2024), Open Trading savings (2024–25). Context, from [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]]: Tradeweb's fully electronic HG TRACE share went 7.3% (Q4 2019) → 18.8% (Q2 2024); from [[tradeweb-ai-price]], The DESK put Tradeweb at 18.0% vs MKTX 16.0% of IG+HY TRACE in June 2026.
- **The ASR reads as a bridge, not a conviction buy.** Targets, a debt-funded ASR and a *comprehensive review* announced the same day, followed by merger-related costs from 1Q26, fit a board testing whether the standalone story could re-rate the stock before choosing a sale ([[marketaxess-ice-deal]]).
- **Compared with Tradeweb (context, from [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]]):** Tradeweb grew revenue $775.6M → ~$2.05B (2019–25, ~17.6% a year) with adjusted EBITDA margin rising 45.5% → ~54%. MarketAxess grew ~8.8% a year with EBITDA margin falling to 49.7%. One compounded on breadth (rates, repo, retail, workflow); the other was concentrated in the market where it was losing share.

## 8. Canon lenses

- **Measurement as strategy** ([[canon-doerr-measure-what-matters]]): OKR logic says pick the key result that tells you if you are winning. MKTX kept swapping the key result. A metric redefined whenever it stalls has stopped being a measurement.
- **Luck vs skill** ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]): the record years line up with volatility shocks (2020, 2022, 1Q26). Strip volatility out and the skill signal — share and FPM — was negative from 2021.
- **Durable advantage** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): network economies in all-to-all were real, but switching costs were low once Tradeweb wired the workflow around the trade ([[tradeweb-credit]]). A network power without a switching-cost moat erodes at the margin, which is what share data show.
- **Disruption from adjacent workflow** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): the incumbent optimised its best customers' RFQ while the rival won on PT, spotting and voice processing — the jobs the incumbent treated as non-core.
- **Capital allocation as the CEO's job** ([[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]]): seven letters talk about volumes, protocols and records; none states a return-on-capital framework. The capital story has to be pieced together from releases.
- **Margin of safety** ([[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]]): buying back stock with borrowed money at a time of falling core revenue spends the margin of safety that a debt-free balance sheet provided.

## 9. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For management:** the business stayed highly profitable (EBITDA margin ~50%, ~$385M FCF), debt-free until a deliberate ASR, and diversified into growth markets (EM passed $1T in 2025; services $111.6M). Blocks, PT and Mid-X are where the market is going, and low FPM on them is the price of entry. Disclosure changes came with methodology notes and real data problems (the TRACE PT flag; TraX coverage).
- **Against:** the share decline was visible in the company's own numbers from 2021, the technology rebuild promised for 1Q22 took until 2025–26, and investors were given a moving scoreboard. EPS below 2020 after five years of *records* is the bottom line.
- **Both:** a 25-year category creator can be well run and still lose share when the market's centre of gravity shifts to workflow. The test of the reporting is whether a reader could see that shift from the headlines; here they could not.

(b) If US credit revenue was ~$440M in FY25 and FPM had stayed at FY21's $184.78 instead of $139, roughly how much higher would US credit variable revenue have been (assume all US credit revenue is variable and scales with FPM)?

(b) $440M × (184.78/139 − 1) ≈ **$145M** more (derived; crude, ignores fixed fees and mix). That is roughly the whole revenue gain FY20 → FY25.
