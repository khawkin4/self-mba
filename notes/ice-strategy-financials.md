# Note · ICE strategy & financials — a 20-year record-revenue compounder that lurches between debt-funded mega-deals and deleveraging, with adjusted numbers carrying the "all-weather" story

> Corporate-finance / strategy note · Maps to **Course 01** (Accounting — GAAP vs non-GAAP, amortization of acquired intangibles), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — CAGRs, deal multiples, leverage), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — diversification as strategy), **E2** (Capital Allocation — buyback cycles, hurdle rates), **E3** (M&A — antitrust, repricing, divestitures), **E8** (Stakeholder & IR — how "record" is defined) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[ice-hub]]; companions [[ice-marketaxess-deal]], [[ice-mortgage-technology]], [[ice-energy-benchmarks]], [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/ice/articles.jsonl`, **69 items, Apr 3 2018 – Jul 30 2026**. The core is the **31 earnings releases, 4Q18 (2019-02-07) to 2Q26 (2026-07-30)**: headline bullets, segment tables and "Other Matters" for debt, buybacks and dividends. The brief counted 29; the corpus has 31, and all were read. Also read: the **seven shareholder letters (2019–2025**; dates are approximate, set to Mar 31 of the following year), **~28 deal, investment and divestiture releases**, and three Sprecher/Jackson podcasts. The earnings text stops before the financial tables, so there are **no adjusted EBITDA, segment D&A or amortization lines** here. Leverage below is a proxy built from stated debt and adjusted operating income.
- **Core claim:** ICE has posted record revenue every year since its 2005 IPO; 2025 was the **20th straight**. From 2018 to 2025 net revenue doubled from **$5.0B to $9.9B (+10.4% a year)** and adjusted EPS rose from **$3.59 to $6.95 (+9.9% a year)**. The adjusted operating margin stayed in a **58–60% band** every year. The "all-weather" growth is real, but it was bought in steps. Capital allocation alternates between **debt-funded mega-deals** and **deleveraging**: Ellie Mae ($11B EV, 2020), Black Knight (~$11.9B, 2023), then MarketAxess (~$5.7B EV, 2026). Debt went from **$7.4B to $23.3B and back to $19.6B**. Buybacks stopped for **~4 quarters after Ellie Mae and ~11 quarters after Black Knight**. Two things carry the story. Adjusted metrics exclude the amortization these deals create, so **Mortgage Technology** posts *GAAP* segment losses beside 35–43% *adjusted* margins. And a "technology and data" company still gets the swing in its results from **energy futures**: +46% in 1Q26, −13% in 2Q26.

## 2. The segment change (FY20) and the revenue mix

- **Through 3Q20** ICE reported **Trading & Clearing** and **Data & Listings** (FY18: $2,420M and $2,559M). Fixed-income execution, CDS clearing and ICE Mortgage Services sat inside a "Fixed income and credit" line.
- **From the FY20 release (2021-02-04)** there are three segments: **Exchanges** (futures plus the NYSE), **Fixed Income & Data Services** (FIDS: FI Execution, CDS Clearing, FI Data & Analytics, Other Data & Network Services, the last renamed "Data and Network Technology" in 1Q25) and **Mortgage Technology**. The re-cut gave the newly bought Ellie Mae its own line and moved listings into Exchanges (inference on motive).
- **Recurring vs transaction** splits appear from 1Q22. Recurring was **$3,721M / $3,571M (51%)** in FY22, $4,138M / $3,850M (52%) in FY23, $4,829M / $4,450M (52%) in FY24 and **$5,056M / $4,875M (51%)** in FY25. The 2Q22 release says recurring revenues “account for roughly 50% of our total revenue”. In 1Q26 the energy surge pushed recurring down to **44%** ($1,320M / $1,657M). It was back at 51% in 2Q26 ($1,353M / $1,313M).

**The hurdle rule.** The 2019 letter says ICE requires deals to “underwrite an internal rate of return, or IRR, of at least 10% and above our weighted average cost of capital, which in 2019 was 6%”. It adds: “Many of the opportunities we explore do not pass the test”. Ellie Mae (2020) and Black Knight (2022) were each underwritten at an **IRR of exactly 10%**, the floor. The 2020 deal release adds: “Expected Ellie Mae transaction IRR of 10%; ICE ROIC will remain above ICE WACC in year one, growing thereafter.” The MarketAxess release states no IRR at all, only adjusted-EPS accretion.

## 4. The leverage cycle, and how buybacks move with it

1. **Pre-2020: steady state.** Debt ~$7.5B, ~2.6x the proxy. Buybacks of $1.2–1.5B a year. Capital return was a record $2.1B in 2019 (letter).
2. **Ellie Mae (2020):** debt more than doubled to $16.5–17.3B. Buybacks ran through 3Q20, stopped, and resumed in **4Q21**. The Coinbase sale got ICE back to target leverage, per the 2021 letter: “timely moves like selling our Coinbase investment helped us reach our target leverage nearly a year ahead of schedule”.
3. **Black Knight (2022–25):** buybacks were suspended on **May 4 2022**. Debt peaked at **$23.3B** (3Q23). 2023 and 2024 had **zero buybacks**, dividends only. Gardiner (2Q25): ICE has managed to “successfully achieve our leverage target related to our 2023 acquisition of Black Knight”. Buybacks resumed in 1Q25 ($241M).
4. **MarketAxess (2026–):** buybacks of $1.2B in 1H26 and an authorisation of up to $4.0B from Jul 1 2026. Despite all-debt funding at 3.4x, buybacks are **raised**. Gardiner (2Q26): “Share repurchases remain a priority”. **This is the first time in the cycle that ICE has kept buying back stock through a mega-deal.**

Deleveraging math (derived): Black Knight debt fell from $23.3B (3Q23) to $19.6B (FY25), **−$3.7B in 9 quarters**, while adjusted operating income grew from $4.7B to $6.0B. From FY23 to FY25 the proxy ratio fell from 4.8x to 3.3x, **about two-thirds through earnings growth and one-third through repayment** (Rep, c).

## 5. GAAP vs adjusted: where the "all-weather" story lives

- **The cumulative gap.** Summed adjusted EPS for FY19–FY25 is **$37.48**; summed GAAP EPS is **$31.69**, a gap of **$5.79 (15%)**. That is with the Coinbase gain *helping* GAAP in 2021. The per-share gap widened from **$0.16 (2018)** to **$1.18 (2025)**.
- **Mortgage Technology carries most of it.** Segment margins, GAAP vs adjusted: FY20 25% / 61%; 1H21 ~29% / ~58%; 2Q22 **−2%** / 46%; FY22 5% / 45%; 3Q23 **−48%** / 39%; FY23 **−21%** / 39%; FY24 **−8%** / 36%; FY25 **1%** / 41%; 1Q26 −2% / 39%; 2Q26 8% / 43%. On FY25 revenue of $2,101M, the 40-point gap is **~$840M a year** of costs excluded from adjusted results. The adjusted definitions exclude “amortization of acquisition-related intangibles” (deal-release footnotes, 2022-05-04 and 2026-07-30), so most of this is very likely deal amortization (inference). Before Black Knight, the adjusted mortgage margin was ~56–60%. After it, the adjusted margin is ~36–43%.
- **The narrative leans on the adjusted numbers.** Sprecher (2023 letter): “ICE Mortgage Technology is spring-loaded for growth”. Gardiner on Black Knight (2022-05-04): “Black Knight’s high-growth, recurring revenue stream will further complement our 'all weather' business model”. Mortgage revenue then fell from $1,407M (FY21) to $1,129M (FY22), as originations fell nearly 50% (2022 letter). It took Black Knight to lift it to $2.0B+.
- **GAAP sometimes beats adjusted.** That happened in FY21 (the Coinbase gain) and again in **1Q26 (GAAP $2.48 vs adjusted $2.35)**. The 1Q26 release text does not explain the reversal; equity-method or investment gains (Polymarket, OKX) are a candidate (inference). Don't annualise it.

## 6. Energy concentration inside a "technology and data" company

- Energy revenue went from **$1,236M (FY21) to $2,182M (FY25)**, +15.3% a year. That is **22% of ICE's FY25 revenue** and 40% of Exchanges.
- **1Q26: $814M (+46%)**, about **51% of ICE's entire year-on-year revenue increase** ($257M of $504M; derived). It drove the record 65% adjusted margin and the +37% adjusted EPS. **2Q26: $518M (−13%)**, and adjusted EPS growth slowed to +5%.
- Earlier swings: FY22 energy −6% (Q4 −17%) in the European gas-crisis year, then **+29% (FY23)** and **+25% (FY24)**. In 1Q19 Sprecher credited Data & Listings, which “helped to offset a quarter of relatively muted trading activity”.
- **The read (inference):** the "all-weather" claim works at the annual level. Energy, rates and mortgage do partly offset one another. But the *quarterly* earnings path is set by energy volatility. That is the same volatility the MarketAxess debt plan relies on to fund $1.6B a year of buybacks.

## 7. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Record" is defined to always be true.** Every year is a record in net revenue, and in *adjusted* EPS. GAAP EPS fell in 2019 ($3.42 vs $3.43) and in 2022 (−64%).
2. **The metrics shift.** "Free cash flow" became "adjusted free cash flow" (3Q22). FIDS sub-lines were renamed (1Q25). The segments were re-cut (FY20). Each change may be justified, but together they make long series harder to rebuild.
3. **Deal accretion is always adjusted.** Every deal release promises accretion to adjusted EPS. None states a GAAP effect or a post-close ROIC. The **10% IRR** case for Ellie Mae and Black Knight is never revisited in the corpus. Black Knight's synergy delivery is described only qualitatively: “continuing to make progress towards achieving the leverage and synergy targets” (2Q24, 2024-08-01) and “accelerating expense and revenues synergies” (2025 letter).
4. **Multiples are on ICE's own terms.** Black Knight's "~15x" uses synergized, SBC-burdened EBITDA. MarketAxess's "~10.6x" uses full run-rate synergies against trailing EBITDA. Most small deals (MOVE, risQ, Urgentem, tZERO, BondLink, AFX) have no price at all.
5. **Remedy cost is hidden.** The Black Knight price cut ($13.1B → $11.7B) partly reflected ICE's own share price falling, since ~20% was paid in stock. ICE then *lent* Constellation $500M to buy Optimal Blue. The net cost of the FTC fight is not reconstructable here.
6. **Survivorship in the letters.** No letter mentions eBay or the CBOT loss, and only the 2019 letter mentions Bakkt's consumer app. The 2024 and 2025 letters drop ESG entirely ([[ice-mission-forces]]).

## 8. Strategic read (inference — not stated by ICE)

- **The pattern is a ratchet.** Lever up for a network asset at the trough of its cycle (Sprecher: “it's easier to start a new initiative and have market acceptance of your initiative during periods of stress.”, podcast 2024-08-12). Integrate it. Let energy and rates volatility grow earnings into the debt. Then resume buybacks. Each cycle lifts adjusted EPS and adds amortization that GAAP carries for years.
- **Price discipline is the variable.** Sprecher in 2018: “I've never been the low bidder.” The multiples fell from ~23x (Ellie Mae) to ~20x unsynergized (Black Knight) to ~13x (MarketAxess). The *cheapest* deal is also the most strategically central, which suggests ICE learned from the mortgage trough (inference).
- **Shareholders do have a veto.** eBay was abandoned within 48 hours, after investor conversations on the FY19 earnings call. In 2026, raising buybacks while re-levering looks like the price of investor consent for another debt-funded deal.
- **The 10%-IRR floor is a ceiling in practice.** Each mega-deal was underwritten *at* the minimum hurdle, which means the base case leaves no margin of safety. Upside depends on synergies or cycles the model doesn't count, such as a refinancing wave in mortgages or institutional share in bonds.

## 9. Canon lenses

- **Capital allocation as the CEO's main job** ([[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]]): ICE's letters state a hurdle (2019) and then never report back against it. Compare the per-share free-cash-flow scorecard Bezos asked to be judged on.
- **Agency and adjusted metrics** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): when management sets "adjusted" definitions that exclude the cost of its own deals, ROIC after amortization is the check owners need.
- **Margin of safety** ([[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]]): IRR at exactly the hurdle, with stock as part of the currency, is the opposite of a margin of safety.
- **Luck vs skill** ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]): split each record quarter into energy volatility (exogenous) and recurring growth (managed). 1Q26 is mostly the first.
- **Antifragility** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): futures and clearing *gain* from disorder. Mortgage software loses from rate shocks. "All-weather" is a portfolio of fragile and antifragile parts, not a single trait.
- **Industry structure** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): the FTC case shows that buying a near-monopoly (Black Knight MSP) next to a near-monopoly (Encompass) invites remedies that create a rival. Constellation now operates Empower and Optimal Blue, partly on ICE's credit.

## 10. Practitioner counterpoint

- **Bull:** ten-plus-percent compounding for two decades, a 58–60% adjusted margin through COVID, the 2022 rate shock and a mortgage depression, and FCF that has almost doubled since 2018. The deleveraging promises were kept: leverage hit target after Ellie Mae nearly a year ahead of schedule, and after Black Knight by 2Q25. Few acquirers can show that.
- **Bear:** GAAP EPS growth (+7.7% a year) trails adjusted (+9.9%). Mortgage Technology has earned close to zero GAAP operating profit since Black Knight. The 2026 plan stacks all-debt M&A, higher buybacks and an energy-dependent earnings path.
- **Synthesis:** judge ICE on GAAP ROIC per deal and on free cash flow per share, not on "record" adjusted EPS. The MarketAxess debt path (≤3.0x within 18–24 months) is the next test of whether the ratchet still works.
