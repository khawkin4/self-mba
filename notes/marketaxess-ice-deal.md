# Note · MarketAxess × ICE — targets in December, a sale in July: the standalone plan lost to a 33% cash premium

- **Source:** MarketAxess corpus `_ingest/raw/marketaxess/articles.jsonl`: the clean deal text *Intercontinental Exchange to Acquire MarketAxess, Creating a Premier Fixed Income Marketplace* (2026-07-30, copied from the ICE corpus because the MKTX PDF lost ligatures); the **2Q26** (same morning) and **3Q26** (2026-10-23) earnings releases; the **Dec 9 2025** targets/ASR release and Goldman Sachs deck; the **2024-08-05** ICE Bonds liquidity-link release; ICE's **2024-12-12** MSCI MarketAxess futures release; the **2024-10-31** S&P Global data partnership; board releases (Hernandez 2023/2024; Cifu and Schiciano 2026-01-26); Concannon's 2019 appointment bio. **Not in the corpus:** the merger agreement, proxy, fairness opinion, HSR filing, break fees, background-of-the-merger section, or any post-signing ICE commentary. MKTX's own LTM figures below are summed from its quarterly releases (derived).
- **Core claim:** On **Jul 30 2026** ICE agreed to buy MarketAxess for **$167 a share in cash**, a **33%** premium (unaffected ≈ **$125.56**, derived), **~$6.0B** equity and **~$5.7B** EV, *~10.6x LTM EBITDA pro forma for full run-rate expense synergies* of **$100M** — about **18.6%** of MKTX's own FY26 expense guide of $530–545M (derived). On MKTX's reported numbers the price is **~13.2x** unsynergised LTM EBITDA of **$431M**, **6.6x** LTM revenue and **~21.6x** LTM EPS ex-notables (derived). The sale came **7.5 months after** the board published 2026–28 targets (8–9% revenue growth, +75–125bp margin a year) and launched a **$300M ASR with JPMorgan funded with $220M of revolver**, and merger costs were already being booked in **1Q26** as *other legal expenses* (reclassified in 3Q26). A simple DCF of the targets (derived, 10% discount, 4% terminal growth) gives ~**$160** a share — so the offer roughly pays holders the full value of a plan the 2026 numbers were not delivering (revenue +4% YTD). ICE gets the institutional network, Open Trading and CP+'s flow data; MKTX gets the scale, retail/wealth reach and data distribution it lacked. The mirror deal is Tradeweb–Trumid (Oct 19 2026), with the same 1H 2027 close window.

## 3. Stated rationale

**ICE (Sprecher, Chair & CEO):** "take the largest, least-efficient corners of global finance and apply technology and network effects to improve transparency" · "That is what we did in energy, in credit default swaps, and in mortgage technology." · "Acquiring MarketAxess is the natural next step in that journey." The release frames bonds as a market that "remains disproportionately manual, bilateral, and information-asymmetric" and casts MKTX as the missing piece: "MarketAxess brings the institutional trading network and leading electronic execution capabilities that complete the picture."

**ICE (Gardiner, CFO):** "We are acquiring a high-quality, cash-generative business" · "our balance sheet strength allows us to finance this acquisition entirely in cash". The release headline adds "ICE reaffirms plans for ongoing share repurchases".

**MarketAxess (Concannon, CEO):** "What makes this combination so compelling is the complementary strengths each company brings." · "ICE brings additional retail and wealth trading protocols, strong data, connectivity, and a broader set of product capabilities" · "Together, we will have the scale to invest more deeply in the areas that matter most to our customers."

**How ICE describes the target:** "a preeminent electronic trading platform for global institutional fixed income markets". *Institutional* is doing work: retail, wealth, data and indices are ICE's half of the story. The three benefits ICE lists end with "Accelerates ICE’s data and analytics leadership." — the data case, not the execution case, is the one ICE puts in its own headline list (inference on emphasis).

**What is absent:** no MKTX quote about standalone value, the December targets, or why now. The 3Q26 CEO quote is entirely product roadmap and does not mention ICE.

## 4. Valuation arithmetic

**Premium and price.** Unaffected $125.56; aggregate premium ≈ 35.9M × $41.44 ≈ **$1.49B** (derived). At unaffected, MKTX traded at ~16.3x LTM EPS ex-notables and ~9.8x LTM EBITDA (EV ≈ $4.51B − $0.3B net cash ≈ $4.2B; derived) — a low multiple for a ~50%-EBITDA-margin venue, reflecting the shrinking US credit core.

**Synergy capitalisation (derived; 25% tax assumed).**

| Method | Value | Share of $1.49B premium |
|---|---|---|
| After-tax $75M × 10x | $0.75B | 50% |
| After-tax $75M at 8% (perpetuity) | $0.94B | 63% |
| Pre-tax $100M × 13.2x (the stand-alone multiple ICE paid) | $1.32B | 89% |

All three ignore the three-year phase-in and integration costs, which lower them. The premium is therefore **only covered by cost synergies at the most generous capitalisation**; the rest has to come from revenue synergies (none stated) or from ICE's view that the stand-alone price was too low.

**Synergies against MKTX's cost base.** FY26 expense guide $530–545M (midpoint $537.5M) → $100M is **18.6%**. Expenses ex-notables were $499.4M in FY25 and $380M YTD through 3Q26. Cutting ~a fifth of the cost base implies overlap in technology, data, sales and listed-company costs; MKTX's 858 headcount (3Q26) is the pool (inference).

**What the board turned down: a standalone DCF sketch of the December 2025 targets (derived, illustrative).** Base FY25 revenue $846.3M, operating margin 41.0% ex-notables, 25% tax, free cash flow ≈ NOPAT (capex ~$65–75M roughly offsets D&A), net cash ~$0.3B, 35.9M shares.

| Case | FY28 revenue | FY28 op margin | FY28 NOPAT | Discount / terminal g | Value per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Targets met** (8.5% growth, +100bp a year) | $1,081M | 44.0% | $357M | 10% / 4% | **~$160** |
| Targets met, cheaper capital | $1,081M | 44.0% | $357M | 9% / 4% | ~$190 |
| **2026 pace** (4% growth, flat margin) | $952M | 41.0% | $293M | 10% / 4% | ~$134 |
| 2026 pace, lower terminal growth | $952M | 41.0% | $293M | 10% / 3% | ~$118 |

**Read (inference):** the unaffected $125.56 sits between the two *2026 pace* cases, i.e. the market priced roughly no delivery of the targets. $167 is close to the *targets met* case at a 10% discount rate. The offer hands holders, in cash and without execution risk, about what the plan was worth if it worked. The answer is very sensitive to the discount rate (±1pt moves it ~$30), which is why a fairness opinion is the document to read.

**The ASR in hindsight (rough, derived).** About $240M of shares were delivered up front and 359,782 at final settlement. If the final tranche corresponds to the remaining ~$60M at the average price, the implied average is ~$60M / 359,782 ≈ **$167** — essentially the deal price. Treat this as an order-of-magnitude check (ASR pricing includes a discount and the actual VWAP is not in the corpus). If roughly right, MKTX borrowed to buy back ~5% of its shares (1Q26 cites an ~6% lower share count, mainly from the ASR) at about the price ICE paid seven months later, after the stock had fallen ~25% to $125.56 in between.

## 5. What ICE gets vs what MarketAxess lacked

| ICE gets | MarketAxess lacked (by its own account or numbers) |
|---|---|
| ~2,100 institutional investors and dealers, ~13,000 traders; Open Trading (1,000+ liquidity providers) | **Retail/wealth reach**: Axess IQ ADV ~$131–147M (2024), Moment (2026) and the ICE Bonds link were small; Concannon names ICE's "retail and wealth trading protocols" |
| CP+ and the inquiry/response flow behind it (400M+ responses a year), TraX post-trade data, Relative Liquidity Scores, the MSCI MarketAxess indices | **Data distribution and index scale**: CP+ reached clients via Refinitiv (2019), S&P (2024), Arcesium (2026) — partners, not owned channels |
| Pragma algos, Mid-X, closing auctions, RFQ-hub (ETFs/derivatives) | **Scale to fund the rebuild**: five years from the 1Q22 *redesigned trading experience* promise to the 3Q26 X-Pro RFQ, with US credit revenue shrinking |
| A credit RFQ franchise strongest in HY, EM and Eurobonds | **A growing US credit core**: (2%) CAGR 2020–24 per the Dec 2025 deck |

**Fit (inference):** ICE owns evaluated pricing, indices, a retail order book and clearing; MKTX owns the institutional RFQ network and a model price. The combination is a *price + index + venue* stack. For ICE the CP+ flow improves its evaluations; for MKTX's clients the question is whether data they generate is now monetised by a vendor that competes with their other vendors.

## 6. Antitrust, regulatory and the S&P feed

- **Horizontal overlap is real but small on ICE's side.** ICE Bonds is retail/odd-lot-heavy; MKTX is institutional. Context, from [[ice-marketaxess-deal]]: ICE's Fixed Income Execution line was ~$125M in FY25. The larger competitive question is *vertical*: ICE's evaluated prices and indices plus the largest corporate-bond RFQ network.
- **The S&P dependency.** Since 2024 CP+ feeds S&P Global's Evaluated Bond Pricing, which Sweeney said would "close the gaps between best execution, intraday trading decisions and end-of-day valuation". The GS deck calls S&P "our partner, SPGI" for evaluated prices of large index funds and ETFs. Context, from [[tradeweb-ai-price]] §5: after close, S&P's corporate evaluated pricing would rest on a feed owned by ICE, one of its two largest evaluated-pricing competitors. Change-of-control terms are not in the corpus. See [[spglobal-hub]], [[spglobal-pricing-fixed-income]].
- **Two consolidations at once.** Tradeweb–Trumid targets the same 1H 2027 close (context, from [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]]). A reviewer looking at US credit e-trading would see the #1 and #2 RFQ venues each absorbing a rival or adjacent player in the same window (inference; no regulator comment in the corpus).
- **Stockholder vote.** Required. The corpus contains no proxy, so holder composition, any activist position and the board's stated alternatives are unknown.

## 8. What the claims don't prove

1. ***~10.6x* is a synergised multiple.** It divides EV by trailing EBITDA plus *full run-rate* synergies that arrive by year three. On MKTX's reported LTM EBITDA it is ~13.2x.
2. ***Accretive to adjusted EPS* is a low bar.** ICE's adjusted EPS excludes acquisition-intangible amortisation and integration costs; debt at ICE's cost against a ~7.6% EBITDA yield (1/13.2) on the target makes accretion likely regardless of value creation (inference).
3. **Complementarity is asserted, not measured.** No cross-sell targets, no revenue synergies, no customer overlap data. The 2024 ICE Bonds link, the best test of retail-institutional complementarity, never reported volumes in the corpus.
4. **The premium is to a depressed price.** 33% over $125.56 still leaves $167 at about the level the board was buying stock in Dec 2025–Feb 2026 (rough, §4). A premium measured from the unaffected day is not the same as a premium to recent value.
5. **No process disclosure.** Whether other bidders were approached, whether the targets and ASR were set with a sale already in view, and what JPMorgan's fairness analysis assumed are all proxy questions. The 1Q26 cost reclassification proves money was spent on the merger by 1Q26, not when talks began.
6. **"Institutional" network size is a count, not a share.** ~2,100 firms says nothing about wallet share, which is what MKTX was losing.

## 9. Strategic read (inference — not stated by MarketAxess or ICE)

- **The board chose certainty over a plan its own numbers weren't confirming.** By 2Q26 US credit commissions were −9% and FPM was still falling; 2026 revenue growth was tracking ~4% against an 8–9% target. Against that, a 33% cash premium approximately equal to the *targets met* DCF value is a rational sale.
- **The December package looks like a last standalone test.** Targets + ASR + *comprehensive review* on one day, then deal costs within a quarter. Either the board was setting a public bar for bidders, or a bidder appeared after the stock failed to respond. The corpus cannot distinguish these.
- **Governance drift toward a transaction.** A 37-year JPMorgan banker as first independent Chair (2025), a market-maker founder and a PE adviser on the board (Mar 2026), JPMorgan as ASR counterparty and sell-side adviser. Each is defensible; together they describe a board equipped for a deal (inference; no conflict is alleged in the corpus).
- **For ICE it is a buy-after-build.** Context, from [[ice-marketaxess-deal]]: seven years of ICE Bonds produced ~$125M of FY25 execution revenue. Buying MKTX is the admission that institutional RFQ networks are not built from a retail order book.
- **For MKTX's clients the data question dominates.** Dealers and asset managers generate the inquiry flow behind CP+. Under ICE, that flow feeds the owner of evaluated prices and indices. That is where pricing power — and client pushback — is most likely (inference).

## 10. Canon lenses

- **M&A value creation** ([[wss-capital-markets-ma]]; E3): the acquirer pays the premium up front and earns synergies later. Here the sized synergies cover 50–89% of the premium, so the rest is a bet on revenue synergies ICE has not named.
- **Agency theory** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): managers and directors of a target with a shrinking core have incentives to prefer a sale (certainty, change-of-control terms) or a levered buyback (EPS optics). The stockholder vote and the proxy's background section are the monitoring tools. A debt-funded ASR months before a sale is the classic question for that section.
- **Diagnosis before policy** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): the Dec 2025 deck finally stated the diagnosis — "A return to U.S. credit revenue growth is fundamental to our future success". The guiding policy (three channels) and actions (X-Pro, blocks, Mid-X) followed, but the sale says the board judged the policy unlikely to work fast enough.
- **Margin of safety** ([[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]]): ICE's margin is the gap between stand-alone value and $167, plus synergies; MKTX holders' margin was the unaffected price. The DCF table shows how thin both are without the targets.
- **Network power and its owner** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]], [[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): the network economy in all-to-all liquidity is worth more to an owner of complementary information goods (prices, indices) than to a standalone venue losing share.

## 11. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For the deal (MKTX holders' view):** cash at a premium that roughly equals the best case of the standalone plan, from a buyer with the balance sheet to fund the rebuild. Concannon's scale argument is credible: technology rebuilds in this market take years, and ICE's data, index and retail franchises are complements MKTX had tried to rent through partners.
- **Against:** holders give up the option on a volatility rebound — 1Q26 showed what a volatile quarter does (revenue +12%, 52.1% EBITDA margin) — and the 33% premium is measured from a price well below where the company itself was buying stock in December (rough, §4). And the board asked holders to back a standalone plan in December, then sold before the plan could be tested.
- **Both:** the deal can be fair to MKTX holders and still be strategically better for ICE than for clients. The test after close is fee-per-million and data pricing, not the premium.
