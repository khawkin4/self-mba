# Note · S&P Global × IHS Markit — the $44B merger's real price was paid in remedies, a bad first year and four years of selling what came with it

> M&A case note · Maps to **E3** (M&A, Deals & Restructuring — remedies, synergy accounting, post-merger pruning), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — all-stock exchange ratios, accretion, ASR), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — scale vs focus, divesting into rivals' hands), **E4** (Corporate Governance — board seats for the target, chair continuity), **Course 07** (Decision-Making — base rates for large stock deals) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[spglobal-hub]]; companions [[spglobal-strategy-financials]], [[spglobal-pricing-fixed-income]], [[spglobal-people]]

- **Source:** S&P Global corporate press and IR releases, **Apr 2020 – Oct 2026**, plus the 2021 merger-vote slides, the 2022 and 2025 Investor Day decks, the 2021–2025 annual-report letters and the 2026 Mobility Global Investor Day deck (`_ingest/raw/spglobal/articles.jsonl`). This is the company's own record. There are no analyst transcripts, no regulator decisions and no third-party press in the corpus, so regulators appear only as S&P describes them.
- **Core claim:** The deal was sold as *scale plus fit*: "This merger increases scale while rounding out our combined capabilities" (Peterson, 2020-11-30). What actually happened is a sequence of **subtractions**. Regulators made S&P sell assets to direct rivals: CUSIP Global Services to **FactSet ($1.925B)**, LCD to **Morningstar ($650M)**, and OPIS, Coal/Metals/Mining, PetroChem Wire and Base Chemicals to **News Corp (~$1.15B + $295M)**. Twenty-two months passed between signing and close. The deal then closed into a Ratings slump: **guidance was suspended on 2022-06-01** and 2022 pro forma revenue fell **4%**. Revenue synergies ran at about **$16M** nine months after close, against a **$350M** target. After that S&P sold or spun off most of what IHS Markit had diversified into: Engineering Solutions (Jan 2023), Fincentric and PrimeOne (2024), OSTTRA (2025), EDM/thinkFolio (2026), upstream software (2026) and Mobility (Jul 2026). What S&P kept is the **financial-markets core**: iBoxx, CDX/iTraxx, evaluated pricing and reference data, Ipreo's primary-market workflow, the energy content folded into Platts, and private-markets software (iLEVEL, WSO, ClearPar). *In hindsight, the merger was a purchase of IHS Markit's financial-services unit, paid for with stock and with the disposal of everything else.*

## 2. The remedy map — who got what

| Asset sold | Buyer | Price | Why it had to go (as S&P reports it) | What the buyer gained |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OPIS, Coal/Metals/Mining, PetroChem Wire | News Corp (Dow Jones) | ~$1.15B | Overlap with Platts price reporting (DOJ, CMA) | A rival price-reporting agency, owned by a firm that also supplies news to MI |
| Base Chemicals | News Corp | $295M | CMA: UK petrochemical assessments | Same |
| **CUSIP Global Services** | **FactSet** | $1.925B | EC condition | The US securities identifier, which S&P had run for the ABA |
| **LCD + leveraged-loan indices** | **Morningstar** | $650M | EC condition | Leveraged-loan news, data and benchmarks |

Two of these four were **S&P's own assets, not IHS Markit's**. In January 2021 S&P was still praising CUSIP's renewed accreditation as the US standard identifier. By December 2021 it had agreed to sell it. Regulators made the acquirer pay with its own crown jewels. *(Reading of the remedy list; the regulators' reasoning is not in the corpus.)*

|---|---|---|---|

| EDM, thinkFolio | Markit | STG (2026) | Not material |

| **Mobility** | IHS Transportation (+ CARFAX) | Spun off (2026) | "Separating Mobility will allow us to continue to focus on our core businesses and pursue our growth strategy," (Cheung) |

**Pattern:** S&P kept the *data and benchmarks* and sold the *software and post-trade processing*. The exception is private-markets and loan software (iLEVEL, WSO, ClearPar, the new DataXchange), which S&P is building up.

## 4. What survived — and why it matters for market structure

- **iBoxx and CDX/iTraxx.** These now sit under S&P DJI's fixed-income indices. They are the credit-derivatives index franchise, and in 2026 they are the benchmarks S&P takes on-chain (tokenized iBoxx TRS, Oct 2026; see [[spglobal-digital-assets]]). IHS Markit's head of indices called them "the liquidity cornerstone of the global investment community" (2020-04-08).
- **Evaluated pricing, reference data and liquidity scores.** These went into MI and onto Capital IQ Pro (19.4M+ fixed-income securities added in 2024). This is the business that took MarketAxess CP+ in 2024; see [[spglobal-pricing-fixed-income]] and [[tradeweb-ai-price]] §5.
- **Ipreo / Global Markets Group.** InvestorAccess and Equity Bookbuild are the primary-market order and allocation utility, linked to Tradeweb (2022) and Bloomberg TSOX (2023).
- **IHS energy content.** It was merged with Platts into Platts Connect (2023), which is now S&P Global Energy.
- **Private markets.** iLEVEL and WSO became the base for With Intelligence ($1.8B, 2025).

## 5. Synergies — targets versus what was reported

| Item | Signing (Nov 2020) | Close (Feb 2022) | Through 3Q22 (Investor Day 2022) | Later |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cost synergies | ~$480M | **~$600M** | **~$311M** run-rate (~50% actioned) | Investor Day 2022 schedule: "~80% achievement in 2023" |
| Revenue synergies | $350M | ~$350M | **~$16M** run-rate; "~$8M achieved YTD" | ~$210M of the target sits in MI, $100M in Commodity Insights, by 2026 |
| EBITA margin | +200bp a year | +200bp a year | — | Investor Day 2025 targets 50–75bp a year |
| Merger costs | — | — | $619M (2022) | $236M (2023), $133M (2024) |

The FY2022 release headline said S&P "Achieved 2022 Merger-Related Synergy Targets Ahead of Schedule". That is true of the *2022* targets, which were back-loaded. The *revenue* side, which was the strategic case for the deal, started at about 5% of target. No later item in the corpus reports a cumulative revenue-synergy figure.

## 6. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Ahead of schedule" depends on which schedule.** The 2022 targets were a small share of the full plan ($255M cost and $15M revenue expected for 2022). Beating them says little about the $350M revenue promise.
2. **Synergy accounting is self-reported and has no end date in the corpus.** S&P never publishes a final scorecard, and after 2023 the topic disappears from releases. Reorganisations and disposals (OSTTRA, Mobility) make a clean count impossible.
3. **The divestitures muddy organic growth.** Pro forma figures are restated after every sale. "Organic" growth after 2022 is measured on a base that keeps shrinking.
4. **"Strong, profitable business" vs a 0.6–3.8% reported margin.** Engineering Solutions' reported 2022 segment margin (B2 findings) does not match the praise used at its sale.
5. **No counterfactual.** The corpus cannot show what S&P alone would have earned in 2022–25. Ratings drove the 2022 decline, and the merger cushioned it.
6. **The merger-vote slides list approvals, not outcomes.** "Other antitrust and regulatory approvals" in March 2021 became four forced sales by December.

## 7. Strategic read (inference — not stated by S&P Global)

- **It was a financial-services deal wrapped in a conglomerate.** Everything outside financial data, benchmarks and commodities has now left. S&P effectively paid ~$44B in stock for IHS Markit's Financial Services unit (iBoxx, CDX/iTraxx, pricing, Ipreo, loan platforms, MarkitSERV), plus energy content and Mobility. It then recovered value by selling or spinning off the rest. Mobility alone sent back a **$1.9B** cash dividend before it left.
- **The remedies seeded competitors.** FactSet now owns CUSIP, the identifier S&P's own data products key off. Morningstar owns LCD. News Corp owns OPIS. In response S&P pushes **its own free identifiers**: Capital IQ IDs on DUNL.org (2025) and LXIDs for private credit (2025). *Read this as rebuilding the identifier position the remedies took away.*
- **The acquirer ceded MI to the target, then took it back.** Kansler (IHS) ran MI from 2022 to 2024. Saha, an S&P insider, ran it 2024–26. Since July 2026 MI has been split between Sally Moore (from Markit) and Darren Thomas. Leadership churn at MI is the visible cost of an integration that never fully settled.
- **The base rate was in S&P's own research.** Its Quantamental report six weeks before signing describes this deal's failure mode. The deal partly escaped that base rate because the target's best assets were *benchmarks*, which carry pricing power, and the rest could be sold.

## 8. Canon lenses

- **Base rates** ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]], [[canon-tetlock-superforecasting]]): start from *large stock deals underperform* (S&P's own finding, paraphrased), then adjust for asset type. Benchmarks and indices are the reason to adjust upward.
- **Strategy as subtraction** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]], [[canon-lafley-martin-playing-to-win]]): *where to play* was settled only after the deal, through disposals. A good strategy would have named the core before buying.
- **Power** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): iBoxx, CDX/iTraxx and S&P 500 licensing are *cornered resources* with network effects in derivatives. Engineering documents and auto data are not. The kept and sold lists line up closely with this distinction.
- **Information goods and lock-in** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): identifiers such as CUSIP, LXID and Capital IQ IDs are standards. Losing one and seeding another is a standards war.
- **Agency** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): all-stock consideration plus a $12B ASR soon after close. Management could buy scale without spending cash, then use the new cash flow to shrink the share count.

## 9. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For the deal:** by 2025 revenue was **$15.3B** and adjusted EPS **$17.83**, against $11.8B and $11.19 in 2022 (Investor Day 2025 shows a 9% revenue CAGR and 17% EPS CAGR, 2022→2025). The adjusted margin reached **50.4%**. Recurring revenue rose to ~76%. The 2022 slump was a cyclical Ratings problem the merger *softened*; Peterson wrote that the "diversified business portfolio helped counterbalance a steep decline in revenue from S&P Global Ratings". Disposals returned real cash: $975M, ~$1.55B (S&P's half of OSTTRA, derived) and the $1.9B Mobility payment.
- **Against:** S&P had to hand rivals its identifier franchise and its leveraged-loan data. Revenue synergies were tiny early and never reported later. Four years of reorganisation followed, with three MI leaders and repeated recasts. Since 2022, 145% of adjusted FCF has been returned, partly funded by sales of businesses that were bought with stock (see [[spglobal-strategy-financials]]).
- **Both:** judge the deal on *per-share* value with all disposals and the Mobility distribution counted. Headline revenue is distorted by the remedies and the spin.

## 10. Open questions to track

1. **Cumulative revenue synergies.** Did S&P ever disclose reaching $350M? Check the FY2023–25 10-Ks and earnings decks, which are not in this corpus.
2. **Identifier strategy.** Does FactSet's CUSIP pricing or licensing change S&P's costs, and do DUNL/LXIDs gain adoption?
3. **iBoxx/CDX economics.** How much of S&P DJI's $1.85B FY25 revenue (per [[tradeweb-ai-price]] §5) comes from the IHS Markit fixed-income indices? It isn't broken out.
4. **What is left to sell?** MI's Enterprise Solutions was told to focus "on the most differentiated software assets". Which software comes next?
5. **Mobility Global after the spin.** Does it trade above the value implied inside SPGI, which would show the conglomerate discount was real?

Three computations, using only numbers from this note.

**(a) The exchange ratio.** SPGI holders own ~67.75% of the combined company. If S&P had ~241M shares before the deal, how many new shares went to IHS Markit holders, and what does 0.2838 imply about the INFO share count? *(Answer: new shares = 241 × 32.25/67.75 ≈ **115M**. INFO shares ≈ 115/0.2838 ≈ **405M**.)* Explain in two sentences why an all-stock deal moves the risk of a 2022-style Ratings slump onto the target's shareholders as well.

**(b) Remedy proceeds vs the price.** Add up the forced sales (OPIS ~$1.15B, Base Chem $0.295B, CUSIP $1.925B, LCD $0.65B) and the voluntary ones with disclosed values (Engineering Solutions $0.975B, half of OSTTRA $1.55B, Mobility cash payment $1.9B). What share of the $44B IHS Markit EV is that? *(Answer: $3.92B forced + $4.43B voluntary = **$8.4B**, about **19%** of $44B. Note that CUSIP and LCD were S&P's own assets, so they were not "recovered" deal value.)*

**(c) Synergy pace.** Cost synergies were $311M run-rate at 3Q22 against $600M, and revenue synergies $16M against $350M. Compute each as a percentage. If revenue synergies grew linearly from $16M (Q3 2022) to $350M by end-2026, what would the run-rate have been at end-2024? *(Answer: **52%** and **4.6%**. Linear: (350−16)/17 quarters ≈ $19.6M a quarter, so end-2024 (9 quarters on) ≈ 16 + 177 ≈ **$193M**.)* Then write the question you would ask the CFO on an earnings call, since the corpus never reports the actual figure.
