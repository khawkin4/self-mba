# Note · Bloomberg Indices — a bought benchmark (the Agg) turned into a regulated factory that sells licences, then the products built on them, then the tools to trade those products

> Market-structure / benchmark-power note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — standards, bundling, counter-positioning vs committee indices), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance — benchmarking, passive vs active, credit beta), **Course 11** (Global Strategy & Macro — China and India inclusion as capital-flow policy), **Course 07** (Data — who prices the ruler), **E4** (Governance of benchmarks — BMR, IOSCO, ESMA), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro), **E3** (M&A — BRAIS 2016, BCOM 2020) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[bloomberg-hub]]; companions [[bloomberg-pricing-bval-ibval]], [[bloomberg-electronic-trading]], [[bloomberg-enterprise-data]], [[bloomberg-private-markets]]; compare [[spglobal-indices-benchmarks]], [[lseg-ftse-russell-indices]], [[ice-fixed-income-data]], [[ice-benchmarks-iba]]

- **Source:** Bloomberg corpus `_ingest/raw/bloomberg/articles.jsonl`: about 70 index items, **Jan 30 2019 – Aug 26 2026**. These are BISL press releases (bloomberg.com and PR Newswire), Bloomberg Indices Insights pieces by product managers, the 2020 Indices Engineering profile, and BCOM target-weight notices for 2020–2026. Reader notes P1–P8 and DELTA were used for triage. Bloomberg is private. **There are no index revenues, margins or benchmarked-AUM totals in the corpus**, and none are given here. Competitor figures are labelled *(context, from …)* with the sibling note linked.
- **Core claim:** Bloomberg did not build the world's main bond benchmark. It bought it: Barclays Risk Analytics and Index Solutions (**BRAIS**), in Aug 2016, under a five-year co-brand that ended on **2021-08-24**. It then wrapped the asset in regulation: an **FCA/EU BMR** authorisation (2019-10-18), IOSCO reviews, and **ESMA Art. 32 recognition** from 2026-01-01. It has used that licensed core in three ways:
  1. **Widening the perimeter.** China into the Global Agg (2019–20), India FAR bonds into EM Local (2025), leveraged loans (2024–25), TOTALFI (2025).
  2. **Entering rivals' asset classes on price.** Equities (B500, 2019) and commodities (BCOM, bought 2020).
  3. **Turning indices into things you trade.** Tradable Trackers, CME/Eurex credit futures (~$300bn notional by Dec 2025), QIS/annuity/covered-call indices.

  The 2026 leadership signals point the same way. A QIS structurer, **Emanuele Di Stefano**, is CEO, and the unit reports to **Tony McManus**, Global Head of *Enterprise Data and Indices*. The index is being run as a data product and a product shelf, not as a neutral yardstick alone.

**Rules worth knowing:**
- **BCOM caps:** group ≤33%, a commodity and its derivatives ≤25%, a single commodity ≤15%, floor 2% where liquidity allows. Weights are set once a year in January, so observed weights drift between resets (gold at 20.48%).
- **Agg mechanics:** month-end two-day membership lockout from Mar 2025. A daily Projected Universe. 2pm/4pm pricing snaps planned for early-close days (Atha, 2025-01-24). Index Research says the sub-1Y maturity exit is "on average, the reason about 2/3 of the securities leave a flagship index on a given month" (2024-08-02).
- **The benchmark / non-benchmark line:** the Second Measure consumer-spend index is "not administered by Bloomberg’s benchmark administration business and is not intended for use as a financial benchmark" (2024-09-09). The same disclaimer appears on Hamilton Lane's indices on the Terminal. BMR scope is an explicit product-design variable.

## 3. How the narrative moved

1. **2019–2021: regulated, objective, inherited.** Berkley's vocabulary is "accuracy and integrity", "independent, transparent approach to indexing". Equity is a "natural extension". The selling points are heritage (Kuhn Loeb → Lehman → Barclays) and the BMR licence.
2. **2021–2022: challenger on price.** Equity and multi-asset are pitched as a "cost-effective alternative". The wins all come from existing Terminal/AIM clients. Canada Life's Willans praises the "well-designed and cost-effective Equity indices". BCOM's "most widely used" claim disappears. The FI line keeps it ("the most widely used fixed income indices in the world", Berkley, 2022-06-23).
3. **2023–2025: the index as underlier.** IBVAL makes intraday trackers possible, and the trackers make futures fair value possible. "Harmonized analytics mean that an ETF, an index and a futures contract can now be evaluated on an apples-to-apples basis in real time." (Sharaby, 2026-02-18). See [[bloomberg-pricing-bval-ibval]].
4. **2025: the flagship gets a sibling.** Bloomberg's own researchers write that "Many active funds are benchmarked to the Bloomberg U.S. Aggregate Index yet take on significantly more credit risk." Seven days later TOTALFI launches, and Gendron defends the Agg as "the core investment-grade fixed income markets, which is the most sizeable segment".
5. **2026: benchmark as product.** Di Stefano's line on "integral components of the investment process", a QIS résumé in the CEO seat, and indices folded under Enterprise Data.

## 4. What the claims don't prove

1. **‘Most widely used’ is self-asserted.** No AUM-benchmarked total, market share or third-party ranking appears in the corpus. BCOM dropped the phrase after 2019, and the FI line kept it. The ">500 ETFs" figure is a count, not assets.
2. **The research behind TOTALFI comes from the seller.** The study that finds Agg funds mismatched and the paper showing TOTALFI's "outperformance and lower volatility" are both by Bloomberg's index research team. The Agg-fund numbers don't add up: 15bp alpha + 47bp credit = 62bp against 50bp of excess return. That implies a negative curve contribution of about 12bp, which is not stated. The ‘Universal beat Agg six years running’ result also covers a rising-rate window that favoured shorter duration and more credit.
3. **Futures notional is cumulative turnover, not open interest.** About $300bn traded through Dec 2025 against peak OI of ~$4.5bn. The Jan 2025 text said ~$100bn, so roughly **$200bn** traded in 2025. That is real growth from a small base.
4. **Stale boilerplate.** The B500 was "$58.19 trillion" in both Aug 2025 and Mar 2026, then "more than USD 60 trillion" in Aug 2026. BCOM AUM moves from >$100bn to >$110B, $102B and $108.8B without an explanation of method.
5. **The wording softens under scrutiny.** "eliminates potential biases" (Mar 2025) became "mitigates" (Aug 2025). The jab at committees stays in.
6. **BSBY silence.** The corpus has launch, IOSCO and loan-market releases (2021), a mention in a 2022 LIBOR survey, then nothing. (Context, not from Bloomberg: regulators publicly questioned credit-sensitive rates in 2021, and BSBY is generally reported to have ceased publication in Nov 2024. Verify before citing.)
7. **The vertical stack raises an independence question that the releases don't address.** BVAL/IBVAL (an affiliate) prices the indices. The indices underlie futures that execute on Bloomberg's EMSX/RFQe. BISL's BMR conflict-of-interest controls are asserted ("robust governance and oversight", 2021) but never described.
8. **Wins are hand-picked.** Canada Life ($3bn), Triodos, Canaccord, Metrobank, Touchstone, TOBAM and KoCAA are all announced switches toward Bloomberg. No loss is ever reported. FTSE Russell, by contrast, disclosed a £15–20m ETF mandate loss (context, from [[lseg-ftse-russell-indices]]).

## 5. Strategic read (inference — not stated by Bloomberg)

- **Index first, venue second.** In China, Bloomberg set the Agg weight, supplied the CFETS-linked trading pipe and showed BVAL on CFETS. In India, FAR inclusion (2025) came before Bloomberg's IGB e-trading on NDS-OM (2026-07-09). In South Africa, the JIBAR fallback role puts Bloomberg inside ZAR derivatives plumbing. The benchmark decision creates the flow, and Bloomberg's venues are already standing where the flow arrives. See [[bloomberg-electronic-trading]].
- **The Terminal bundle is the distribution moat for equity.** Every equity win cites existing Bloomberg services. Equity indices are priced as an add-on to an installed base, which is low-end entry against S&P/MSCI. Bloomberg does not need to beat the S&P 500 brand. It needs a CIO who already pays for AIM to stop paying a second vendor.
- **The rival models differ.** S&P DJI is a toll booth whose liquidity is owned by venues (CME holds 27%) and whose brand rests on its committee. FTSE Russell uses gatekeeping (WGBI) plus a parent-owned price source (Tradeweb FTSE closes). ICE runs index → NYSE listing → ETF Hub → iNAV. Bloomberg is the only one that owns **price + index + execution front-end** with no exchange parent. Instead it licenses its futures to CME, Eurex and Cboe while routing the orders through EMSX (context, from [[spglobal-indices-benchmarks]], [[lseg-ftse-russell-indices]], [[ice-fixed-income-data]]). FTSE Russell's CEO has framed the contrast this way: Bloomberg as a *walled garden*, FTSE as the *open meadow* (context, from [[lseg-ftse-russell-indices]] §3).
- **MSCI is a partner in bonds and a rival in stocks.** Bloomberg MSCI ESG FI indices, Paris-aligned co-brands, MSCI ESG ratings on the Terminal (2020-10-13) and a Eurex contract on the Bloomberg MSCI Euro Corp SRI index sit alongside an equity suite built to take MSCI's benchmark mandates. Bloomberg also uses its own **BICS** classification, not GICS, which S&P and MSCI co-own. KoCAA's custom benchmark was built on BICS. That is coopetition with a standards edge.
- **TOTALFI hedges against the Agg's narrowness.** Loans, structured credit and BDC debt are growing, and active managers already hold them. If mandates migrate to whole-market or core-plus benchmarks, Bloomberg wants to own the successor. Its own research makes the case first.
- **The QIS turn shifts the revenue mix.** VolMax, Versa 10 and covered-call indices put bank-engineered strategies inside a regulated index wrapper. The bank hedges and the insurer or ETF licenses. Fees then track product AUM (>$30bn FIA/life) more than terminal subscriptions. That is better growth and higher reputational risk: leverage up to 500% sits under the BISL name.
- **Regulation is the moat and the cost.** BMR authorisation (2019), Art. 32 recognition (2026), UK BMR for BFIX, and ISDA/SARB calculation-agent roles keep BISL's benchmarks usable in contracts. A non-authorised builder cannot sell into EU-labelled products. Bloomberg kept EU access through recognition from London. FTSE stood up an EU entity instead (context, from [[lseg-ftse-russell-indices]] §4).

## 6. Canon lenses

- **Standards and lock-in** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): a benchmark is a standard. Mandates, ETF prospectuses, futures specs and fallback clauses are the switching costs. The Barclays co-brand period was a five-year brand migration designed to avoid breaking the standard.
- **Power** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): the Agg is a *cornered resource* (50 years of history, embedded in mandates). The equity push is *counter-positioning*: rules-based and bundled against committee-based and standalone. Incumbents can't copy the bundle without a terminal.
- **Low-end entry** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): the "cost-effective alternative" equity indices start with mid-size managers (Canada Life $3bn, KoCAA $29.5bn) whom the big providers serve least well.
- **Focal points** ([[canon-schelling-strategy-of-conflict]]): fallback rates (ISDA, JIBAR) and BFIX succeed because everyone must coordinate on one number. Calculation-agent roles go to whoever already looks neutral.
- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): MSCI as co-brand and rival. EBS (CME) and Euronext FX feed BFIX. CME and Eurex list Bloomberg's futures while S&P's part-owner competes.
- **Bundling** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): data + price + index + OMS + execution, so a client's switching cost is the whole stack.
- **Noise in measurement** ([[canon-kahneman-sibony-sunstein-noise]]): benchmark choice decides what counts as skill. Behind the Benchmark is a lesson in how the ruler creates the "alpha".

## 7. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For Bloomberg:** the Agg's rules (projected universe, lockout, replicability) are what passive managers need. The China and India phase-ins were telegraphed months ahead with ex-country versions, which is good governance by any standard. Calculation-agent selection by ISDA and the SARB is an outside vote of confidence. Futures reaching $300bn cumulative in 18 months shows real demand for a listed credit-beta tool.
- **Against:** equity wins are modest and come through the bundle. The S&P 500 brand and MSCI's EM franchise are not at risk from anything in this corpus. QIS indices with leverage and bank co-design blur the line between administrator and product sponsor. Index providers that also own the evaluated price and the execution ticket concentrate a lot of judgment in one firm.
- **Both:** a manager choosing a benchmark should test tracking against **two** price sources, check whether the benchmark's risk profile matches the mandate (Bloomberg's own Agg finding), and read the BMR conflict disclosures, not the press release.

### Rep
Four computations with numbers from this note.

**(a) The India phase-in is not linear.** At full weight India is 7.26% of a $6.18tn index, so India's market value is $0.0726 × 6.18tn ≈ **$448.7bn** and the rest of the index is ≈ $5,731bn. In step *k* (k = 1…10), India enters at 10k% of its market value. Compute India's index weight after steps 1, 5 and 10: w_k = 0.1k·448.7 / (5,731 + 0.1k·448.7). *(Answer: 44.9/5,776 ≈ **0.78%**; 224.4/5,955 ≈ **3.77%**; **7.26%**.)* Each step adds slightly less weight than the one before, about 0.78pp at first and about 0.68pp at the end. Now **assume** (not in the corpus) $40bn passively tracks the index. What is the cumulative passive buying, and the buying in month 1? *(≈ 0.0726 × 40bn = **$2.9bn** in total; ≈ $0.31bn in month 1.)* Why would active managers who are benchmarked but not passive move earlier?

**(b) China in the Global Agg, then and now.** In 2019 the full-weight target was 6.03% of $54.07tn ≈ **$3.26tn**, phased over 20 monthly steps of 5% (about 0.30pp per month). At end-2024 CNY was 9.9% of $66.9tn MV ≈ **$6.62tn**. Roughly how much of that doubling is index growth versus China's share rising? *(Share 6.03% → 9.9%, ×1.64; index MV basis ×1.24, though the 2019 figure may not be MV. Discuss what you can't separate.)* Explain why China passing JPY matters for a Japanese life insurer benchmarked to the unhedged Global Agg.

**(c) The BCOM gold reset as a flow.** Gold's observed weight peaked at **20.48%** against a 2026 target of **14.90%**. Take benchmarked AUM of **$108.8bn** and suppose the peak weight held into the January roll. What notional of gold futures must index trackers sell? *(Answer: (0.2048 − 0.1490) × 108.8bn ≈ **$6.1bn**.)* Then say why the true figure is smaller: prices moved after Oct 20, the AUM estimate covers more than strict trackers, and some trackers use swaps. Say why a once-a-year reset with 2:1 liquidity:production rules is a deliberate choice for "continuity".

**(d) Credit futures in numbers.** "almost $300 billion in notional terms" over "more than 3 million contracts" gives an average contract notional of about **$100k**. Peak OI was $4.5bn. Turnover in 2025 alone was about $300bn − $100bn = **$200bn**. Compute the 2025 turnover-to-peak-OI ratio *(≈ 44×)* and compare it with a liquid equity-index future (look up E-mini S&P figures yourself). Fair value: with a Tradable Tracker showing +0.40% since the last index close, a benchmark at 2,500.00, and net carry of +0.35 index points to expiry, what is the futures fair value? *(2,500 × 1.004 + 0.35 = **2,510.35**.)*
