# Note · Goldman Sachs strategy & financials — durability came from the old dealer's balance sheet, not the new businesses

> Corporate-finance / strategy note · Maps to **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — ROE bridges, efficiency ratio, what "durable" revenue is worth), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — where a dealer's moat lives once trading is electronic), **E2** (Capital Allocation — SCB, dividend, buybacks, principal-investment run-off), **E3** (M&A — the AWM deal spree versus the consumer exits), **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations — public targets and how the goalposts moved) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[goldman-consumer-retreat]] and [[goldman-hub]]; dealer-side counterpart to [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]]

- **Source:** Goldman's own channels, `_ingest/raw/goldman/articles.jsonl`: **32 earnings releases** (Jan 2019 – Oct 2026; the web text plus PDF pages 1–4 only, so no full segment tables, opex lines or capital-return detail for most years), **7 shareholder letters** (FY2019–FY2025, dated to fiscal year-end but published ~Mar–Apr of the following year), the **Investor Day decks** (Jan 29 2020 consolidated; eight decks from Feb 28 2023), **10 capital/SCB statements** (Jun 2019 – Jun 2026) and the Solomon/Waldron *Exchanges* transcripts. Everything here is Goldman-authored or Goldman-hosted. **Missing:** earnings-call Q&A (not public), 10-K segment detail, and any analyst or regulator voice. Deck text is PDF-extracted and some tables are garbled; numbers read from those are flagged.
- **Core claim:** The Jan 2020 Investor Day promised **>13% ROE within three years** and said that the *longer-term* lift to mid-teens would come "as certain of its newer businesses, such as Transaction Banking and Consumer Banking, mature." ROE then ran **10.0% (2019) → 11.1% → 23.0% → 10.2% → 7.5% → 12.7% → 15.0% (2025)**. The target was met **five years late**, and the mix that met it was not the one in the plan. Between 2022 and 2025, FICC+Equities **financing** (prime, repo, structured and mortgage lending) grew from **$7.1bn to $11.4bn, 37% of markets revenue**, and AWM **management and other fees** from **$8.8bn to $11.5bn**. The consumer bank was sold off in pieces and Transaction Banking stalled at roughly **$0.3bn of revenue** against a $750m 2024 target. When the 2025 letter boasts it has "doubled our more durable revenues", the footnote defines *durable* as exactly those two lines (financing + AWM fees/private banking). The new businesses that were supposed to deliver durability are not in the definition. The efficiency target (~60%) has still not been met on a reported basis (**~64% in 2025**, derived).

## 1. Targets vs actuals, year by year

**Targets as set.** 2020 Investor Day (Jan 29 2020): ROE **>13%**, ROTE >14%, efficiency **~60%**, CET1 13–13.5%, over three years. Five-year "opportunities" (2019 letter / ID20): alternatives **"$100 billion in net alternative asset inflows"**, Transaction Banking **$50bn deposits and $1bn revenue**, consumer **$125bn deposits and $20bn loans/cards**. Raised in Jan 2022 (2021 letter) after the 23% year: ROE **14–16%**, ROTE 15–17%, **$225bn** gross alts fundraising 2020–24, **>$10bn** management and other fees in 2024, **~$750m** TxB revenue in 2024 and **>$4bn** consumer revenue in 2024. Investor Day 2023: Platform Solutions **pre-tax breakeven in 2025**, AWM mid-20s margin, TxB deposits target **$100bn**.

**Reading the scorecard.**
- **ROE.** The 2020 target was hit in 2021 (23.0%) on the post-COVID capital-markets surge, which Solomon calls "a big tailwind." It was missed in 2022–23, when the target was 14–16%, and met in 2025. The 2025 figure includes a **+10bp** benefit from the Apple Card exit (L25 footnote). Excluding it, ROE is **~14.9%**: inside 14–16%, but only just.
- **Efficiency.** The ~60% target was met once, in 2021 (53.8%). On reported numbers 2025 is ~64.4%. Stripping out the Apple Card markdown (revenue $60.54bn, opex $37.51bn, from the L25 reconciliation table) gives **~62.0%** *(derived)*. The 2020 plan's $1.3bn of run-rate expense saves happened, but they never got the ratio to 60% in a normal year.
- **Fees and alts** are the clean wins. The $10bn fee target was met in 2024, and alts fundraising beat every version of its target. The next targets are already set: **$75–100bn a year** and **$750bn of fee-paying alts AUS by end-2030** (L25).
- **TxB and consumer** are the misses. Neither revenue target ($750m TxB, >$4bn consumer in 2024) is reported as met anywhere in the corpus. After the 2023 Investor Day both disappear from the letters' scorecards.

- **The crossover.** In **Q3 2026, Equities financing ($3.37bn) exceeded Equities intermediation ($3.08bn)** for the first time in the corpus. Q2 2026 Equities revenue of $7.42bn was the record. In the release's own wording, FICC financing growth is driven by "mortgages and structured lending" quarter after quarter.
- **Waldron's statistic (Jun 5 2025).** "In the last five years, the standard deviation of our results is 6%. In the prior 10 years, it was 24%." He says this was measured on "risk, intermediation revenues in our global markets business".   2. **The five-year window (≈2020–24) was a volatile but rising stretch for trading.** A trending series looks either stable or unstable depending on whether the method removes the trend, and we aren't told which.
  3. **It's a self-reported number on a Goldman podcast.** No 10-K figure in the corpus matches it.

- **The volume tell.** The 2020 GM deck put electronic share of tickets at ~99.9% of U.S. cash equities and ~75% of U.S. credit. Those statistics **never reappear** in the letters. From 2021 the shareholder story is told in *intermediation*, *financing* and *wallet share*. Electronic execution became table stakes; the balance sheet became the product sold to investors *(inference)*.

- **Arithmetic.** The SCB fell **330bp from the 6.7% start** (the 2025 letter says "a cumulative 320 basis points since 2020", measuring from 6.6%). Because SCB is part of the CET1 requirement, each point is roughly 1% of RWAs that can be paid out or redeployed. The dividend went **$1.25 → $5.00 (4×) from 2019 to 2026**, and +67% in the two years after the 2024 dispute ($3.00 → $5.00).
- **Two requirement figures don't reconcile.** (1) The 2022 letter cites a "new capital requirement of 13.8 percent in the beginning of 2023", while the SCB statement implied 13.3%. (2) In 2026 the requirement is **11.4%** at the same 3.4% SCB that gave **10.9%** in 2025. Both gaps are most likely G-SIB surcharge changes, but the corpus doesn't say *(ambiguity; check the 10-K)*.
- **The causal claim.** Solomon ties the relief to strategy: "Today's announcement is a reflection of the work we have done over the years to reduce our capital intensity" (Jul 2025). The 2025 letter adds the evidence: historical principal investments cut "from roughly $64 billion to $6 billion". The stress test punishes on-balance-sheet equity and consumer card losses most. Exiting principal investments and consumer is what lowered the SCB, and the falling SCB is what funded the dividend. The fee pivot and the capital return are the same decision *(inference, consistent with the firm's own framing)*.
- **Buybacks.** Annual repurchase totals appear only for 2019 ($5.34bn) and 2022 ($3.5bn). The rest sit in pages of the releases that weren't scraped. *Gap.*

## 5. Moving goalposts

1. **Alternatives: net became gross.** The 2019 letter and ID20 slide target "$100 billion in net alternative asset inflows". The 2020 letter reports progress toward "our goal of $150 billion in gross fundraising over five years". Gross fundraising was then the only series reported ($225bn → $438bn). Net inflows, which subtract realisations, never return.
2. **Top clients: 100 became 150, with a confusing base.** In the 2022 letter Goldman is top-3 with "77 of the top 100" clients, "up from a base of 51". In the 2023 letter it is "117 of the top 150 clients — up from 77 in 2019". The same number, 77, is the 2022 top-100 count *and* the restated 2019 top-150 base. The series can't be compared across the change *(flag; may be coincidence or conflation)*.
3. **ROE: target, raised target, then "through the cycle".** >13% (2020) became 14–16% after the 23% year (Jan 2022). Once 2022–23 came in at 10.2% and 7.5%, the 2023 letter rephrased the goal as "mid-teens returns through the cycle". A through-the-cycle target is never missed in any given year.
4. **"Durable revenues" were redefined around what worked.** In 2020, durability was to come from TxB, consumer, alts and wealth. The L25 footnote defines "more durable revenues" as **FICC financing, Equities financing, Management and other fees, and Private banking and lending**, with no TxB or consumer line. In ID23's "why invest" deck, the footnote to a slide-9 revenue series (most likely the durable-revenue chart; the extraction is garbled) still included "Consumer platforms net revenues and Transaction banking and other net revenues". By 2025 they had been dropped.
5. **"Met or exceeded almost all."** The Jan 2025 release: "I'm encouraged that we have met or exceeded almost all of the targets we set in our strategy to grow the firm five years ago". *Almost all* excludes the two businesses the long-term return case was built on.

## 6. The narrative and how it shifted

| Period | Self-description | Representative line |
|---|---|---|
| 2019–20 (build) | Diversify away from a capital-markets-heavy mix; four growth bets; the first public targets | "We are taking real and significant steps to make Goldman Sachs more transparent and easier to understand." (Jan 29 2020) |
| 2020 (COVID) | The old business vindicated | "there were calls to downsize our Global Markets business, but we believe its performance in 2020 shows it was wise to stay the course" (L20) |
| 2021 (peak) | Records, acquisitions for durability | GreenSky and NN IP bought to drive "higher, more durable returns" (Q3-21 release) |
| 2022–23 (refocus) | Two core businesses; Platform Solutions as a small set of "emerging businesses" | "our actions over the past several years have raised the floor of our returns, while retaining the upside in more conducive markets" (L22) |
| 2024–26 (scale) | "World-class, interconnected franchises", financing as "ballast", private credit as the "fulcrum" | "Since our first Investor Day where we laid out our comprehensive strategy, the firm has grown its revenues by 60%" (Jan 2026) |

**The shift:** the 2020 story was *'we will become less like a trading house by building new businesses.'* The 2026 story is *'we became less volatile by using the trading house's balance sheet differently, and by selling other people's alternatives.'* The revenue growth is real: **$36.55bn → $58.28bn, +59%** *(derived)*. What changed is the explanation of where durability comes from.

## 7. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Durable" is a label, not a measured property.** Financing revenue is secured lending to hedge funds and structured borrowers. It is sticky while balances and leverage are high, and the 2026 private-credit and BDC stress (see [[goldman-awm-private-credit]]) shows how quickly that can change. Prime balances fall when hedge funds de-gross. The definition is Goldman's, and it is chosen after the fact.
2. **Waldron's 24% → 6%** has no stated method and comes from a firm-hosted podcast. It may be right, but it isn't auditable from the corpus.
3. **ROE met, efficiency not.** A 15.0% ROE alongside a ~64% efficiency ratio means the return came from revenue and lower capital requirements (SCB down 330bp), not from the cost discipline promised in the 2020 bridge.
4. **'+60% revenue, +500bp ROE, TSR >340%' uses 2019 as the base.** By the release's own account 2019 was a weak year (ROE 10.0%, Q4 annualised 8.7%). 2025–26 sit at the top of a capital-markets cycle. Neither end is "through the cycle".
5. **The consumer cost is never totalled.** Selected items cut 2023 EPS by **$8.04**, and the GreenSky goodwill impairment was **$504m**. Apple Card brought **$2.26bn** of markdowns, offset by a $2.48bn reserve release. Seven years of build-phase losses (ID20: TxB + Consumer at "$1bn of pre-tax loss including reserve build" in 2019) are never added up. See [[goldman-consumer-retreat]].
6. **Excluding Apple flatters 2025 in one direction and hides it in another.** The Apple Card release produced a **net +$0.46 EPS** gain because the reserves built over six years were released. Those reserves had depressed earlier years' ROE. The exit 'earns' back what the build had cost.

## 8. Strategic read (inference — not Goldman's words)

- **The diversification thesis was right about the problem and wrong about the answer.** Goldman needed less volatile, less capital-intensive revenue. The diversifiers it chose (deposits, cards, cash management) were businesses where it had no distribution or cost advantage. What it did have was a client franchise among hedge funds, sponsors and wealthy families, plus the risk-management machinery to lend to them against collateral. Prime, repo, structured lending and alternatives fundraising all monetise that franchise. Marcus monetised the brand.
- **Same capability, two directions.** Solomon in 2019 held both views at once: "And capital is a commodity", and Goldman "still excel[s]" at "bespoke risk intermediation". Financing is where those views meet: the balance sheet is a commodity, the judgment about whom to lend it to is not. Consumer lending inverted that. It needed scale underwriting of millions of small, uncorrelated credits, which is a different machine.
- **The SCB is the hidden P&L line.** Every exit lowered stress losses, every SCB cut freed capital, and the capital went to dividends and to the financing book. The 2024 public fight with the Fed (6.4%, then 6.2% on reconsideration) shows management treating the stress test as a negotiable input to strategy.
- **From the dealer side, Tradeweb's 'automation is the product.'** Tradeweb's thesis is that the *venue* owns the workflow, the data and the reference price, and that electronification compresses dealer spreads ([[tradeweb-hub]], [[tradeweb-tech-ai-data]]). Goldman's numbers show the dealers' answer. Concede the flow (99.9% of U.S. cash-equity tickets electronic by 2019), keep making markets on everyone's venues, and move the margin into **balance sheet**, where a venue can't follow. Varadhan (Nov 2023): "so much of our workflow has migrated through technological automation". Waldron's volatility statistic is the payoff. The two strategies are complements, not rivals, and they are financially entangled:
  - Dealers' stake in Tradeweb fell **from 46% to 17.3%** at the 2019 IPO.
  - Goldman joined Digital Asset's $135M round co-led by Tradeweb ([[tradeweb-outside-voices]]).
  - Goldman advised **Trumid** on its sale to Tradeweb (Oct 19 2026; [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]]).

  The dealer sells liquidity on the venue and financing off it.
- **TxB's quiet move into GBM is the right end-state.** Cash management for corporates and sponsors is a GBM client product (over 90% of TxB clients "already had relationships with the firm", L21). As a standalone 'platform' it had no scale. As part of the coverage bundle it doesn't need to *(inference)*.

## 9. Canon lenses

- **Good strategy names the crux** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): the 2020 plan was a list of four growth bets plus targets, which is closer to Rumelt's "fluff + goals" than to a diagnosis. The diagnosis that actually worked, *use the franchise's balance sheet where clients pay for judgment*, was articulated only after 2022.
- **Where to play / how to win** ([[canon-lafley-martin-playing-to-win]]): consumer was a where-to-play choice without a how-to-win. Financing and alts had both.
- **Power** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): financing earns on *counter-positioning* (venues can't hold risk) and on *switching costs* (prime relationships are sticky). Marcus had none of Helmer's powers against JPMorgan or Capital One.
- **Targets as commitment devices** ([[canon-doerr-measure-what-matters]]): public KPIs (~35 of them, per Solomon) built accountability. The moved goalposts in §5 show the cost of fixing outcome metrics before the strategy was proven.
- **Luck vs skill** ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]): 2021 and 2025–26 ROE coincide with capital-markets booms. Before crediting strategy for the ROE, separate the financing trend (skill, structural) from the intermediation tailwind (luck, cyclical).
- **Agency and incentives** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): Management Committee pay moved to 100% PSUs (L22), tied to the same targets management sets and re-scopes.

## 10. Practitioner counterpoint

- **Bull view (management):** revenue is up ~60% and ROE up ~500bp since 2019. Book value per share went $218.52 → $357.60 (L25), and TSR was "over 340 percent", which the letter calls the best in its peer group. The dividend quadrupled, the SCB nearly halved, and the markets franchise is now financing-anchored. "We have materially improved the risk profile of the firm and enhanced the resilience of our earnings." (L25)
- **Skeptic's view:** the ROE target was met only at a cycle peak, with an efficiency ratio still ~4pp over target. The 'durable' businesses are leveraged lending to hedge funds and private-credit vehicles, which is exactly where 2026's stress appeared. Solomon himself warns the softness "are a reminder that the credit cycle has not been repealed." The consumer write-downs were real shareholder money, and their total is never disclosed.
- **Synthesis:** the operating turnaround is genuine and well documented in Goldman's own numbers. The *explanation* was rewritten to fit the outcome. Judge the next cycle on (a) financing revenue in a de-grossing year, and (b) whether AWM's fee growth survives a private-markets drawdown. The question is not whether ROE stays above 15% in a boom.

(d) **Capital release.** The SCB fell from 6.7% to 3.4%. If standardized RWAs were ~$700bn (an assumption for this exercise; look up the real figure in the 10-K), how much CET1 does 330bp free? Compare it with a year of dividends at $5.00/quarter on ~300m shares (also an assumption).

*Answers:* (a) Totals: 25.67, 23.61, 26.63, 31.06 → mean 26.74, population SD ≈ 2.72 → **CV ≈ 10.2%**. Intermediation: 18.55, 15.81, 17.50, 19.61 → mean 17.87, SD ≈ 1.40 → **CV ≈ 7.8%**. Financing: 7.12, 7.80, 9.13, 11.45 → mean 8.88, SD ≈ 1.65 → **CV ≈ 18.6%**, nearly all of it trend growth. Dispersion around a mean is the wrong test for a growing line: financing never *fell* year on year, while intermediation dropped 15% in 2023. Waldron's figure is plausible as a statistic on a de-trended series, but it can't be reproduced from these totals. (b) **14.9%**, 0.9pp above 14%. (c) 60% × 58.28 = $34.97bn vs $37.54bn actual → **~$2.57bn** too high. On the ex-Apple basis, 60% × 60.54 = $36.32bn vs $37.51bn → **~$1.19bn**. Stripping out Apple more than halves the gap. (d) 3.3% × $700bn ≈ **$23bn** of CET1, against ~$6bn a year of dividends ($20 × 300m). The SCB cut alone pays for roughly four years of the current dividend.
