# Note · Goldman Sachs through outside eyes — a hosted chorus: skeptics invited to argue the theme, partners paid in publicity to praise the rail, and almost nobody who could cost Goldman money

> Stakeholder / outside-view note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy: coopetition with alternatives managers, market makers and venues who are clients, partners and rivals at once), **Course 08** (Negotiation: reading the other side's incentives in a testimonial or a podcast answer), **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations: whose voice gets printed on the firm's own channels, and why) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[goldman-hub]]; companions [[goldman-people]], [[goldman-mission-forces]], [[goldman-awm-private-credit]], [[goldman-digital-assets]]; Tradeweb counterpart [[tradeweb-outside-voices]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/goldman/articles.jsonl` (262 items, Jan 2019 – Oct 2026), searched for every non-GS speaker, then checked against the reader notes A–G. By my count about **17 hosted items** carry external guests: *Top of Mind* issues and their podcasts (crypto May 2021, crypto winter Dec 2022, gen-AI Jun 2024, stablecoins Aug–Sep 2025, private credit Apr–May 2026) and *Exchanges / Great Investors* episodes (Rowan 2023, Arougheti 2024, Aronson 2025, Craver, Zelter and Griffin 2026). About **15 press releases** carry partner or counterparty quotes (CCP paper ×2, AWS, AmEx, Datonomy, Visual Structuring, GS DAP, BNY, GreenSky sale, T. Rowe Price, QIA, plus the AWM targets). **Gaps:** the Dec-2022 *Top of Mind* is a redacted scrape, so Clayton, Massad, Kasumovich and Gorton survive only as cover pull-quotes. There is no earnings-call Q&A, so no sell-side analyst ever speaks. No 10-K or press coverage was used.
- **Selection-bias warning (it applies to every line below):** every voice here sits on a **Goldman-owned channel with a Goldman host**: Allison Nathan (Research), Jake Siewert (Communications), Alison Mass (chairman of Investment Banking), Tony Pasquariello, Raj Mahajan, George Lee, Michael Brandmeyer. Guests are chosen, edited and published by the firm, and transcripts carry the disclaimer that views are the interviewee's own. The skeptics are **invited**: most argue about a *theme* (AI capex, crypto, stablecoins, private credit), almost never about Goldman. Partner quotes are co-written releases. Read every line as *what this speaker was willing to say on Goldman's microphone*. The few lines that do cut against the house view count for more because they got through.
- **Core claim:** The outside voices fall into three bands. (1) **Invited skeptics** (Acemoglu, Roubini, Eichengreen, Gorton, plus in-house Covello) let Goldman *own both sides* of every theme it also earns fees from. Their objections are real and, read in order, they **pre-critique Goldman's later moves**: Roubini's *blockchain in name only* (2021) before GS DAP's "permissioned distributed technologies" (2024); Gorton's interoperability warning (2022) before the BNY tokenized-MMF bridge (2025); Covello's "$1tn problem" (2024) before OneGS 3.0 (2025). (2) **Alternatives managers and market makers** (Marks, Arougheti, Zelter, Rowan, Aronson, Griffin, Craver) are clients, borrowers' financiers and competitors at once. They talk their own book, and the sharpest strategic line in the corpus is Rowan's: the "new banks" are investors. (3) **Partners and co-signers** (Apple by silence, BNY, Tradeweb, AWS, MSCI, AmEx, Sixth Street, T. Rowe, QIA; BlackRock, JPM, Vanguard, Guardian on CCPs) praise the *rail or the deal*, never Goldman as indispensable. My reading of the power map *(inference)*: the **Federal Reserve** (sets the stress capital buffer that sets the dividend) and the **large asset managers and alternatives giants** hold the most leverage. Then come **Citadel Securities–type market makers** and the **rail partners** Goldman needed to mutualize GS DAP. Goldman holds power mainly over the founders it buys (Industry Ventures, Innovator, NEOS, LCN) and the managers it launches. **Absent:** regulators in their own voice, unhappy clients, Marcus customers, rival dealers and venues.

## 3. Alternatives managers and market makers — clients, competitors, the "new banks"

**What they agree on.** Every alternatives guest in 2026 argues the stress is *technical, not fundamental*. Zelter: "I think the market is missing what it really is." (Apr 16 2026). Arougheti: "So there is no asset liability mismatch, there is no run on the bank in these funds." (Apr 29 2026). Marks, the nearest to a skeptic, still calls the software hit bad rather than systemic. Goldman's own voices say the same. Lynam on 5%-a-quarter redemption limits: "they are a feature, not a bug" (Apr 29 2026). There is **no internal or external dissent** on whether private credit is systemic. That should make a reader more cautious, not less.

**What they reveal about the dealer's position.**
- **Rowan (Feb 10 2023)** gives the dealer's core strategic problem in two lines: "we have roughly the same amount of dealer capital, our market making capital today as we did in 2008." Markets, he says, are three times bigger. And: "So, who are the new banks? Investors." From a dealer's seat this is a threat *and* a business. Capital Solutions Group (Jan 2025) is Goldman's attempt to originate for the new banks rather than compete with them ([[goldman-awm-private-credit]]).
- **Arougheti (Jul 23 2024):** "we say no to our clients 95 percent of the time." Origination discipline is the edge. Zelter makes the same point about origination as the bottleneck. Both are describing the scarce input that Goldman's banking franchise supplies.
- **Aronson (Sep 3 2025):** "But I always leave the last nickel on the table. Always." A seller's maxim on Goldman's *Great Investors* show, from a manager whose structured-equity product competes with bank financing.
- **Griffin (Jul 9 2026)** is the only voice from a non-bank rival to Goldman's markets business. He says compute is now a core input to market making: "people who have lower margin businesses won't be able to bear that cost impact". That is a scale argument that cuts toward Citadel Securities and the largest dealers alike. He also ribs the host firm on its own podcast.
- **Craver (Feb 12 2026):** "I used to be able to read a press release and tell you what the stock was going to do the next day." Passive and multi-strategy flow have hollowed out fundamental price discovery. For Goldman this is a *tailwind*: it finances the multi-strats and makes markets in the ETFs ([[wss-trading-hedge-funds]]).

**Incentive read *(inference)*:** each of these guests is simultaneously a client of GBM (financing, prime, coverage), a counterparty to GSAM (co-investor, competitor for LP dollars) and, for Griffin, a direct rival for market-making share. None has an incentive to criticise Goldman on air, and none does.

## 6. The CCP co-signers — the clearest dealers-and-users vs infrastructure stance

The Oct 24 2019 paper was signed by BlackRock, Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan, Allianz, Citi, Societe Generale, State Street, T. Rowe Price and Vanguard. Ten more joined on Mar 10 2020 (ABN AMRO Clearing, Barclays, Deutsche Bank, CBA, Franklin Templeton, Guardian Life, Ivy, Nordea, TIAA, UBS). It asks CCPs to put their own capital into the default waterfall in two tranches and caps assessments at one times a member's default-fund contribution. Goldman's Nicolas Friedman frames it as "aligning incentives and enhancing financial stability through even stronger CCPs". BlackRock's Kiely wants CCPs "optimally structured". Vanguard's Thum asks for "additional protections to strengthen margin calculations and default fund components".

**Read *(inference)*:** this is the one place where buy-side and sell-side speak with one voice, and the target is market infrastructure owned by exchange groups (the CCP owners are never named). **No CCP is quoted.** For a dealer, CCP rules are a cost line and a tail risk. Pushing loss-absorption onto the CCP's own capital moves risk from members to exchange shareholders. Compare Tradeweb's position on Treasury clearing ([[tradeweb-derivatives-regulation]]): the venue warns about margin stress, while the dealers here want the CCP to carry more of it.

## 7. The institutional counterweights — heard only through Goldman

- **Federal Reserve.** It appears as the source of a number, never as a speaker. The 2020 SCB of 6.7% was revised to 6.6% after a Fed calculation error (Sep 4 2020). Solomon publicly disputed the 2024 figure: "This increase does not seem to reflect the strategic evolution of our business" (Jun 28 2024). Then, after the SCB fell to 3.4%, he praised the Fed's intent "to institute a more transparent and fair approach to these tests" (Jul 1 2025). The Fed's own reasoning is absent.
- **DOJ and US/UK/Singapore/Hong Kong regulators (1MDB).** Heard through Solomon's memo ("we recognize that we did not adequately address red flags", Oct 22 2020) and the Board: "The Board views the 1MDB matter as an institutional failure, inconsistent with the high expectations it has for the Firm."
- **Government of Malaysia.** Heard through the settlement terms: $2.5bn paid plus a guarantee of at least $1.4bn in asset recoveries, and "the Government of Malaysia agreed to withdraw the pending criminal charges" (Jul 24 2020).

**Read:** these are the only actors who imposed costs on Goldman without its consent: $3.42bn of 2020 litigation provisions, about $174m of compensation clawbacks and cuts, a capital buffer that moved 330bp from its first print *(derived: 6.7% → 3.4%; the 2025 letter says 320bp, measuring from the revised 6.6%)*. They are also the only ones with no microphone. That is the clearest measure of what the channel is for.

## 8. Power map *(inference)*

Dependence = importance × lack of alternatives ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]).

| Actor | What Goldman needs from them | Alternatives Goldman has | Leverage over GS |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Federal Reserve** | Capital headroom: SCB sets the CET1 floor, and the floor sets buybacks and the dividend ($0.85 in 2019 → $5.00 in 2026) | None | **Highest** |
| **Large asset managers** (BlackRock, Vanguard, T. Rowe) | Flow, mandates, co-signers on market-structure fights, a 401(k) channel | Few at this scale | **High.** They sit on both sides: clients and allies on CCPs, competitors in asset management |
| **Alternatives giants** (Apollo, Ares, Oaktree, Sixth Street) | Sponsor and financing fees; buyers for assets GS exits (GreenSky) | Some, but the sponsor wallet is concentrated | **High and rising**: they are the "new banks" |
| **Non-bank market makers** (Citadel Securities) | Nothing directly; they compete for the same flow and are also prime clients | — | **Medium.** Griffin's compute argument says only the scaled survive, which includes GS |
| **Rail partners** (Tradeweb, BNY, Digital Asset) | Neutrality and adoption for GS DAP | Few: a GS-only rail failed the adoption test | **Medium**, on the digital-asset bet only |
| **Big Tech** (Apple, AWS) | Distribution (Apple), cloud and talent (AWS) | Apple: none once it left. AWS: other clouds | **Medium–high** in consumer (now exited); medium in tech |
| **Sovereign anchors** (QIA) | $25bn of capital | Other sovereigns | **Medium**; priced in Doha headcount |
| **Acquired founders** (Industry Ventures, Innovator, NEOS, LCN) | Product and wrappers | Build instead of buy | **Low**: earn-outs and GS stock tie them in |

**Read:** Goldman's leverage is greatest where it is buyer or host and weakest where it needs permission (Fed), distribution (Apple, T. Rowe, BNY) or neutrality (Tradeweb). The consumer exit is a case of that map: every party it depended on had alternatives, and Goldman had none.

## 9. What is conspicuously absent

- **Marcus customers.** "Marcus" appears in 21 items, always in management's voice. No depositor, borrower or Apple Card holder speaks. The customer-assistance program of 2020 is described, not heard.

## 10. Canon lenses

- **Power and dependence** ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]): §8. Goldman's leverage is as buyer and host. The Fed, Apple and the big asset managers each score high on importance and on Goldman's lack of alternatives.
- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): Apollo, Ares and Citadel are clients, competitors and complementors at once.
