# Note · MarketAxess's mission and the forces on it — the identity line never moved ("preferred all-to-all"), the mission migrated from *open* to *intelligent*, and the forces that mattered most were calm markets, curated rivals and fee mix — until an exchange group priced the answer

> Strategy / environment-scan note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy: five forces, counter-positioning, consolidation), **Course 11** (Global Strategy & Macro: EM index inclusion, China, India, Brazil, MENA), **E5** (Transformation: a five-year protocol and platform rebuild), **E6** (Crisis Leadership: COVID, 2022, SVB, tariffs, event credits), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro: rates regimes, tariffs, Argentina), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation: CP+, automation, AI routing) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[marketaxess-hub]]; companions [[marketaxess-people]], [[marketaxess-outside-voices]], [[marketaxess-strategy-financials]], [[marketaxess-ice-deal]]; mirror note [[tradeweb-mission-forces]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/marketaxess/articles.jsonl` (359 items, Jan 2019 – Oct 23 2026). I read the seven CEO letters (FY2019–FY2025), the 2021 Investor Day and Dec 2025 Goldman Sachs decks, all 32 earnings-release narratives (text cut at ~20k characters; many tables truncated), every *About MarketAxess* boilerplate in the IR releases (scripted extraction of firm counts and key phrases by first-seen date), and the Knowledge Center research and event pieces (many dates estimated). Financial figures are as MarketAxess states them; *derived* marks my arithmetic. No call transcripts; no 10-K risk-factor sections beyond the forward-looking-statement boilerplate.
- **Core claim:** MarketAxess has had a **purpose** (2019–21: "open global markets"), a **mission** (2024–25: an "intelligent, global, electronic fixed-income marketplace that works better for everyone") and one **identity sentence** that never changed from April 2019 to the sale: Open Trading is "the preferred all-to-all trading solution in the global credit markets". In practice the company optimised for **credit fee revenue from institutional RFQ in a few big markets, defended by all-to-all breadth and proprietary data** — which made it a **volatility derivative** (records in 2020 and 2022, a slump in 2021, "dampened" share in calm 2025). Tailwinds were real and mostly outside US credit: EM ($1T+ volume in 2025), index-driven flow, data and services ($112M in FY25), automation. The decisive headwinds were **US credit share loss** (HG 20.9% → 18.4%, 4Q23 → 4Q24), **fee-per-million compression** ($184.78 FY21 → $127 3Q26) and a **platform rebuild** that took five years. The forces map turned on rivalry and buyer power; ICE's bid (Jul 30 2026, $167/share, ~10.6x synergised LTM EBITDA) resolved the standalone question. **MarketAxess admitted volatility, fee mix and US-credit "challenges"; it never named a competitor, never explained the sale, and stopped publishing several of the metrics that would have shown the turn.**

**What the boilerplate shows (inference unless noted):**
1. **Clients grew ~50% (1,400 → 2,100, derived) while the identity sentence stayed fixed.** Unlike Tradeweb's notional counter ([[tradeweb-mission-forces]] §1b), MKTX's boilerplate never states volume or share — it counts firms.
2. **Trax left the boilerplate in 2020, then came back as a product name in 2026 (TraX Tape).** Post-trade was demoted from identity to service line, then repurposed as data.
3. **Data creeps in by adjective:** *data products* → *intelligent data products* → *intelligent data and index products* → *integrated and actionable data offerings*. AI appears in the letters (CP+ "AI-based", FY19) long before the boilerplate.
4. **The counts are marketing numbers:** "Nearly 1,900" and "Over 2,000" ran side by side in 2023.

### 1c. Values and culture claims
- **Collaboration and transparency:** "we prize collaboration with our clients and with our colleagues" (FY19); "principles of transparency and collaboration" (DeLise release, 2021).
- **Public function in crises:** NY Fed SMCCF eligible seller (Sep 2020); "essential to the functioning of credit markets" (1Q20).
- **ESG:** Trading for Trees, green-bond flags, the Diversity Dealer Initiative, ESG pay goals (FY21). Present in every letter through FY23; absent in FY24–25.
- **Regulatory self-image:** first movers to an EU MTF post-Brexit; APA/ARM reporting; *give-to-get* data in Asia; US policy asks for a modern best-execution framework (paraphrase) (est. 2026-06), with caution that leveraged retail products "sit uncomfortably close to gambling-style behavior".

| Stated | Revealed by action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| *Open global markets* | **Institutional credit RFQ fees**, with all-to-all as the differentiator | US credit was the revenue core; the Dec 2025 deck: "A return to U.S. credit revenue growth is fundamental to our future success" |

**The tension (inference):** the identity says *all-to-all*; 2024–26 practice says *targeted*. MarketAxess converged on the same *select smart* answer as Tradeweb ([[tradeweb-a2a-information-leakage]]), from the opposite starting point, without changing its boilerplate.

## 4. Forces map, 2026 (five forces + regulation + technology)

Pressure score: 1 = benign, 5 = severe (judgement). Arrows show change since 2019.

| Force | Score | Trend | One-line evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rivalry** | **5** | ↑ | Share flipped to Tradeweb (context); Tradeweb–Trumid; Bloomberg; IDBs targeted by Mid-X/DealerAxess. Now resolved by stacking: ICE+MKTX vs Tradeweb+Trumid (LSEG) vs Bloomberg |
| **Threat of entry** | 2 | → | Licences, network and data are high barriers; entry happens at the edges (Brazil: "one of four accredited platforms") |
| **Supplier power (dealers, algos, non-banks)** | 3 | → | Algos dominate the pool; the 2024–26 dealer suite courts banks; non-banks now on the board (Cifu) |
| **Buyer power** | 4 | ↑ | BlackRock/Aladdin gatekeeping; big managers multi-home and shape protocols (auction advisory group); fee per million falling |
| **Substitutes** | 3 | ↓ slowly | Voice for blocks (blocks est. 10–20% electronic), OMS/EMS aggregation, PT desks at dealers |
| **Regulation** | 2 (net tailwind) | ↔ | EU/UK tapes create data demand but also free public data; US all-to-all advocacy for Treasuries |
| **Technology (AI / automation)** | 4 (double-edged) | ↑ | Automation raises stickiness; AI routing commoditises the RFQ unless you own the price (CP+) |
| **Ownership (ICE)** | — | new | From 1H 2027: MKTX inside a stack with ICE's retail ATS, pricing, indices and futures. Partners (S&P) become rival-dependent |

**Read:** the industry stayed attractive (barriers, automation stickiness); MarketAxess's *position* within it weakened where it earned most (US credit). The two worsening forces — **rivalry** and **buyer power** — both pressed on the same number, US credit fee capture.

## 5. Crisis tests — what each one revealed

|---|---|---|---|
| **COVID, Mar 2020** | "essential to the functioning of credit markets" (1Q20); 10,000+ remote users onboarded | Open Trading 35% of credit volume in Mar 2020 vs 27% in 2019; 1Q20 revenue $169.0M (+36%); FY20 savings $1.1B > revenue; Euro IG trades within 10c of CP+ fell from 74% to 25% | **All-to-all's best hour.** Volatility became revenue; the brand was made here |
| **Low volatility, 2021** | "heavily impacted by low levels of credit market volatility" | 2Q21 revenue −5%, EPS −20%; FY21 operating income −10%; Open Trading savings halved to $570M | **The business is a volatility derivative.** The 13-year earnings-record streak ended without comment |
| **Rate shock / Ukraine, 2022** | "incredibly difficult and volatile fixed-income trading conditions" (3Q22) | Record composite share 20.6% (3Q22); Open Trading record 37%; PT $25B a quarter; HG FPM down on duration; IG indices −14% | **Last share-gain year** — and the start of fee compression |
| **SVB / Credit Suisse, Mar 2023** | automation persisted: overrides "sat at 21%" | No-touch rate 7% (COVID) → 36% (SVB/CS); 3Q23 Open Trading share fell to 33%, HY share 16.1% (−320bp) | **Automation survived stress; share did not grow from it** |
| **Tariffs, Apr 2025** | dealers "stayed active and algos continued to provide liquidity" | Record $102.3B UST day (Apr 9); US HG bid-ask 9.11bp vs 44.62bp at COVID peak; prices per RFQ 13.2 → 6.6 → 11.3 in a week | **Market structure had hardened** — but the FY25 letter still blamed calm for the year |
| **Argentina, Sep–Nov 2025** | event-driven volume showcase | ADV $456M the day after the Sep 7 election vs $195M YTD; October Open Trading $255M vs $176M | Event credits pay; they are episodic |
| **Oracle / hyperscalers, 2025–26** | "investors are already treating Oracle as high-yield risk" | ORCL Open Trading share of trades 20% → 30–40%; hyperscalers' responses per inquiry fell from 14–16 to ~12; "the era of effortless liquidity for hyperscalers appears to be moderating" | **Open Trading as a stress indicator** for single names |
| **Oil / HY energy, est. 2026** | "volatility does not eliminate liquidity. It raises the price of accessing it." | MKTX HY energy ADV $75M → $100M+ while TRACE fell $5.6B → $4.2B | The house thesis in one sentence |

**Pattern:** each shock raised MarketAxess's *behavioural floor* (automation, Open Trading usage) but did not change the *calm-market* trend in US credit share. The blind spot mirrors Tradeweb's: the shock month is reported; the quarter after is not ([[tradeweb-mission-forces]] §5).

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Industry structure** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): §4. The defining move is rivalry collapsing into three vertical stacks; MarketAxess chose a stack rather than stay a standalone node.
- **Counter-positioning and its expiry** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): all-to-all counter-positioned against dealer RFQ; once rivals added anonymous pools (Tradeweb AllTrade, Trumid) and MKTX added curation, the counter-position eroded and the remaining power was **cornered resource** (inquiry data → CP+).
- **Disruption and the incumbent's dilemma** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): the 2013 disruptor became the 2024 incumbent defending fee per million; Tradeweb won on workflow, not price ([[tradeweb-credit]]).
- **Antifragility** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): MarketAxess *gained from disorder* and bled in calm. The strategy problem was to earn in calm.
- **Luck vs skill** ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]): separate the 2020 and 2022 records (volatility) from share (skill). The skill line peaked in 2020–22.
- **Systems** ([[canon-meadows-thinking-in-systems]]): reinforcing loop — inquiries → CP+ → automation → more inquiries; balancing loop — fee mix and leakage. Leverage point: who owns the reference price, which ICE bought.

### Rep
**Write Rumelt's kernel for a standalone MarketAxess in Jan 2025 (≤150 words), then do the arithmetic ICE did.**

*Draft kernel (one possible answer):*
- **Diagnosis:** US credit — the profit core — is losing share (HG 20.9% → 18.4% in a year) and fee capture ($184.78 → ~$150 FPM, FY21 → 4Q24) in calm markets; all-to-all wins only in stress.
- **Guiding policy:** earn in calm by owning the *reference price and the close*, not the broadcast.
- **Coherent actions:** (1) CP+ as the strike for Mid-X, auctions and PT; (2) targeted blocks for the phone tail; (3) win back dealers (DealerAxess); (4) price new protocols to protect FPM; (5) publish share on one stable denominator.

*Arithmetic:* ICE paid ~$5.7B EV at ~10.6x LTM EBITDA including $100M synergies. Implied synergised EBITDA ≈ $5.7B / 10.6 ≈ **$538M**; ex-synergies ≈ **$438M** (derived). Against FY25 revenue of $846M, that is roughly a 52% pre-synergy margin (derived and approximate: LTM-to-June-2026 EBITDA against calendar FY25 revenue; ICE's EBITDA basis is not stated). *Test:* would actions (1)–(5) have produced a value above $167/share before 2028 — given the 8–9% revenue targets abandoned in 7.5 months?
