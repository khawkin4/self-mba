# Note · ICE's NYSE, listings and digital assets — a tenth of the revenue, all of the front door: used to swing ICE from "regulated-only" crypto to on-chain market infrastructure, and from ESG to Texas

- **Source:** 63 items from `_ingest/raw/ice/articles.jsonl`, **2019-10-04 → 2026-10-19**, all read in full: 41 IR press releases (NYSE, NYSE Texas, Bakkt, Polymarket, OKX, Circle, Securitize, CoinDesk, event futures, earnings), 6 ICE Vox blogs, 11 *Inside the ICE House* podcast transcripts and 5 shareholder letters (2019, 2022–2025). The corpus is ICE's own voice: no SEC comment-file text, no press coverage, no Nasdaq or Texas Stock Exchange material. Segment revenue comes from earnings-release tables; "NYSE share of revenue" is my derivation, not an ICE figure.
- **Core claim:** The NYSE is about **10% of ICE's revenue** (FY25: listings $495M plus cash equities and equity options $467M, of $9.9B net) but it is the company's **brand and political front door**. Between 2019 and 2026 ICE used it to make two reversals. **Crypto:** from a "fully-regulated, physically-delivered" bitcoin future (Bakkt, 2019) that found no market, through a 2023 blog titled "The failed promise of unregulated crypto", to an NYSE 24/7 tokenized-securities venue (Jan 2026), up to $2B in Polymarket, a stake in OKX at a $25B valuation and its own cleared event futures (Jun 2026). **Politics:** from "ESG is a primary focus" (2022) to NYSE Texas, Governor Abbott and Trump Media as the first listing (2025). Underneath, the NYSE's real economic levers are technology (Pillar), the floor and the closing auction, and the timing rules around clearing, which ICE argues should cap trading hours. Listings revenue itself is flat ($497M FY23 → $495M FY25) while ICE talks about a shrinking public-company universe.

## 2. NYSE technology and the floor

**Pillar over the ICE platform.** After buying NYSE Euronext (2013), ICE chose not to port the NYSE onto its own futures engine. CIO Mark Wassersug explains why (2023-01-03): “The ICE trading platform, robust trading platform, but it's specific for derivative markets, it's not specific for equities markets.” ICE says the rollout covered every NYSE equity and options market by late 2023. One release dates completion to October 2023 (2024-05-15); the Vox blog dates the last migration to 2023-12-21.

**What ICE says Pillar bought (all self-measured):**
- NYSE American Options median latency fell "by a stunning two-thirds" to under 30 microseconds, and message traffic more than doubled (ICE Vox, 2024-07-23).
- “NYSE Group has grown market share over 250 basis points” in options (1H24 vs 2H23). Over 200bp of that came from electronically matched trades.
- Peak message volume: more than 600 billion in a day (2023 letter) → more than 1.1 trillion (2025-12-15), at "around 30 microseconds".
- April 2025 tariff shock: 6.2B shares on Apr 7 and 6.4B on Apr 9, both NYSE Group records. NYSE-listed stocks traded with "45% tighter spreads and over 20% lower volatility" (2025-07-01). No benchmark is named.

**The floor defence.** ICE's claim is that people plus machines beat machines alone, and the COVID closure is its main evidence.
- Sprecher (2020-05-08): “until you actually close the floor, there was no way to have actual empirical data.” King quotes the Q1 2020 call: “our data shows that market participants save millions of dollars a day when the floor is operating thanks to tighter spreads”.
- Martin (2022-03-08), on 24 Jan 2022: “we saw two times less volatility at the open and the close for NYSE-listed stocks, directly attributable to our floor community.”
- Tuttle (2022-11-07): “it still wasn't as good as when we had this building and this floor open as well.”
- **The closing auction** is the floor's cash product: a record 3.57B shares and $230.5B notional on 2026-03-20, "the single largest daily liquidity event in U.S. cash equities trading" (2026-03-27).

## 3. Trading hours: 22 not 24, and clearing as the brake

- **The plan (2024-10-25):** NYSE Arca trades 1:30am–11:30pm ET on weekdays. Trades are cleared by DTCC, "which recently announced plans to extend its hours". Kevin Tyrrell casts the NYSE “As the steward of the U.S. capital markets”.
- **The argument (ICE Vox, 2025-02-26).** Three reasons for stopping at 22 hours:
  1. Everyone needs a daily break.
  2. Clearing: “We strongly believe exchanges should not match buyers and sellers when centralized clearing is not available”. When DTCC is shut, counterparty risk falls on the clearing firm, and exchanges are not clearing firms.
  3. Corporate actions are processed overnight.
  - It adds: “we proposed 22 hours, rather than 24, not because we don’t think the industry can or will get there”.
  - It also hoped hours could be extended "in 2025". By Oct 2026 the extension had reached **options only**, with equities promised "in December".
- **Read (inference).** The clearing argument doubles as a competitive one. It is aimed at overnight ATSs, which match without CCP cover. It fits a group that owns six clearing houses. And it turns ICE's slower start into a principle. Once DTCC's NSCC went 24x5 on 2026-06-29 ([[market-structure-24-5-trading]]), the stated brake weakened. ICE's own 24/7 answer is the tokenized venue, with atomic on-chain settlement in place of DTCC. See [[market-structure-24-5-institutional-response]] for the operational cost of having no maintenance window.

## 4. Listings: droughts, transfers, Texas

**The cycle, in ICE's words:**
- 2022: “a quiet year for new listings due to market volatility” (2022-12-21).
- 2022–23: “two somewhat subdued years for new issuance” (Harris, 2024-07-01).
- 2024: 53 new companies and >$17B raised (2024 letter).
- 2025: $25B in IPO capital and 7 of the top 10 IPOs. The 2025 letter adds “only ~40% of global IPOs met our rigorous listing standards”.
- Martin's 2026 priority is “reversing the decline in the number of public companies. The key will be streamlining disclosures” (2025-12-15).

**Transfers are the competitive scoreboard against Nasdaq,** which is never named.
- 2022: 34 transfers (2022 letter).
- 2023: 32 transfers, of which 20 were operating companies, “nearly triple the number of operating companies transferring to competing exchanges”. Median transfer market cap was 5x. ICE also said “70% of the year’s U.S. IPOs did not qualify to list on the NYSE” (2023-12-18).
- Since 2000: more than 350 transfers. AstraZeneca's planned move would be the largest in NYSE history (2025-12-15).
- **Yet listings revenue is flat:** $497M (FY23), $489M (FY24), $495M (FY25). The scoreboard wins share of a pool that is not growing (inference).

**NYSE Texas** is a rebrand, not a new build. NYSE Chicago was reincorporated in Texas and renamed (2025-02-12).
- Opening day (2025-03-31): Trump Media was the first listing, but its "existing primary listing" stays where it is. Devin Nunes: “part of a growing movement to take our business to states that value free enterprise and personal freedom.”
- ICE dual-listed itself (2025-06-17). Sprecher: “Supporting the momentum in Texas as Governor Abbott and his team create an environment where companies can thrive”.
- Bryan Daniel was named President (2025-08-04). Daniel previously led economic development on Governor Abbott's senior staff and chaired the Texas Workforce Commission.
- Growth: 16 secondary listings (Jul 2025) → 100 dual listings (Dec 2025) → >120 issuers and a Dallas HQ (Aug 2026).
- Texas NYSE-listed market value: $3.7T → $3.9T → $4.8T.
- Primary listings are the stated next step. Daniel (2025-12-01): “At some point soon we'll have the ability to do primary listings.”
- Inference: the move pre-empts a Texas-based rival exchange (context, not from ICE corpus: the Texas Stock Exchange). A dual listing earns little and costs the issuer little.

## 5. The NYSE as policy voice

- **SEC equity package (2023-03-06).** Filed jointly with Citadel Securities (“the U.S. equity markets’ largest market maker”) and Charles Schwab. The letter says “we recommend pressing forward with two of the package’s four proposed rule changes”: tick-size harmonisation and execution-quality disclosure. It asks to withdraw the other two (context, not from ICE corpus: the order-competition auction and best-ex rules, which threatened payment-for-order-flow economics). An exchange siding with the largest wholesaler and the largest retail broker is a coalition of incumbents (inference).
- **Buyback tax (2023-03-16).** An informal poll of NYSE-listed leaders found they would shift to dividends, and that capex, hiring and R&D would not change. The post's line: “We need more public companies to invest in, not fewer.”
- **NYSE Institute (2022-07-19).** First-year tally (2023-08-22): delegations from more than 30 countries, seven heads of state, a quarter of the US Senate, and “commented on six SEC or PCAOB rulemakings”.
- **Politics.** In 2024 the "My Vote" campaign was neutral: “Financial markets rely heavily on the free flow of capital and other freedoms that our great nation has long championed.” (Sprecher, 2024-10-25). By 2025–26 the tone is explicitly aligned: Texas and Abbott, then Martin in the Trump–Xi delegation (2026-05-15). There Martin praised the administration's AI policy, “we are doing it by getting out of the way of the people building it.”

## 6. Crypto, round one: Bakkt (2018–2023)

- **The thesis.** In 2019 the shareholder letter's section heading was "Building a Regulated Ecosystem for Digital Assets": regulated custody plus physically delivered futures. Sprecher (2020-02-05): “Bakkt was created to help bring transparency and trust to the previously unregulated digital asset markets”.
- **The drift.** A consumer app and loyalty points (Bridge2), a $300M Series B, then three CEOs in roughly five months (Loeffler → Blandina → Clifton).
- **The SPAC.** Expected ICE stake 65% at signing, 68% at close. Deconsolidation moved Bakkt's losses into "net losses from unconsolidated investees", which ICE excludes from adjusted EPS. FY22 GAAP EPS fell 64%; adjusted EPS rose 5%.
- **The verdict, in ICE's own words.**
  - The Vox post (2023-08-02) says the market “did not then - and we believe evidence continues to show still does not” value regulated, physically delivered crypto.
  - Its conclusion: “Crypto can only develop as a true asset class if it follows existing regulation under which markets in the real world operate.”
  - Sprecher had warned of direct-clearing models (2022-06-27): “would we just create a global crash with an algorithm?”
  - Terry Duffy (CME, 2023-03-09): “Everything needs to have a use case.”

## 7. Crypto, round two: on-chain infrastructure (2024–2026)

**What changed.** The stance moved from "regulated-only" to "regulated wrapper around crypto-native distribution":
- Sprecher calls Polymarket “a forward-thinking, revolutionary company pioneering change within the Decentralized Finance space” (2025-10-07).
- Martin backs stablecoins “as digital currencies become more trusted by market participants as an acceptable equivalent to the US Dollar” (2025-03-27).
- Blaugrund frames the tokenized venue as “a pivotal step in ICE's strategy to operate on-chain market infrastructure for trading, settlement, custody, and capital formation” (2026-01-19).

**The Blaugrund/Herrick design (podcast, 2026-02-18):**
- **Engine and ledger.** Pillar matching sits in front of “a privately permissioned blockchain to be able to facilitate the settlement of a transaction in an atomic way”. Trades are tokenized equities against stablecoins, pre-funded, with LULD bands and trading halts carried over.
- **Regulatory posture.** Herrick wants to “avoid unnecessary regulatory exemptions until we have use cases that drive it”. Blaugrund: “we don't require any meaningful regulatory exemptions. We don't require any legislative action.” Target: "the back half of this year" (2026).
- **The critics, as Blaugrund relays them.** Crypto natives: “This is just kind of window dressing.” Traditional institutions: “This is an outrageous rate of change.”
- **Partners.** Securitize as the first digital transfer agent (2026-03-24). The DTCC Russell 1000 tokenization pilot as the bridge for existing shares. BNY and Citi on tokenized deposits across ICE's clearing houses (2026-01-19).
- **Distribution.** OKX is to give its 120M accounts access to “ICE's U.S. futures and NYSE tokenized equities markets” (2026-03-05). That is offshore retail distribution for an onshore venue, and it is still "subject to regulatory approval".
- **An outside voice (guest on ICE's podcast).** CoinDesk's David LaValle said that a year earlier LaValle would have called tokenization “a solution looking for a problem” (2026-02-04).

## 8. Prediction markets: data first, then ICE's own contracts

1. **Equity plus data (2025-10-07).** Up to $2B at ~$8B pre-money: $1B in Oct 2025, $600M in Mar 2026 and up to $40M of secondaries. Each release calls it "not material" to results.
2. **Data product (2026-02-11).** ICE normalises Polymarket prices into feeds mapped to securities, as the exclusive institutional provider. Edmonds: “It’s so rare for new datasets like this to become available at such a quick pace”. Coplan calls the data “a credible input alongside traditional data sources”.
3. **Hedge (podcast, 2026-01-14).** Edmonds says the stake lets ICE join the disruption story: “it gives us an opportunity to be a part of that story, while not necessarily doing anything to our traditional markets.”
4. **Own contracts (2026-06-29).** Cash-settled, centrally cleared futures on Fed, ECB and BoE decisions and EIA gas storage. Bland points to “demand for regulated onshore products that allow customers to take positions on economically relevant risks that shape markets”.
- **Contrast with Tradeweb** ([[tradeweb-tech-ai-data]]). Tradeweb took a minority stake in **Kalshi** (Feb 2026) to pipe event probabilities into its rates and credit screens, and it supplies execution on **Canton**, a ledger it does not own. ICE paid far more, became the data distributor, *and* built a competing cleared venue. Tradeweb is positioning as the execution layer; ICE is positioning as data plus venue plus clearing.

## What the claims don't prove

- **Floor value is self-measured.** "Millions of dollars a day", "two times less volatility" and "45% tighter spreads" are all ICE's numbers, with no method, control group or benchmark published. The 2020 "experiment" coincided with the most volatile weeks in decades, which confounds any spread comparison.
- **The +250bp options share** compares two half-years in a fast-growing market. ICE does not say what happened next.
- **"Leads in transfers"** counts companies, not revenue. Flat listings revenue suggests the wins replace attrition rather than grow the pool.
- **NYSE Texas metrics** ($2T combined cap, 100+ issuers) count dual listings of companies whose primary listing pays NYSE already. Incremental revenue is undisclosed and probably small (inference).
- **Polymarket and OKX** are both "not material", and OKX's investment terms are not disclosed. The valuation of the $600M Polymarket tranche was to be disclosed later and is not in the corpus. The returns case cannot be checked.
- **Tokenized venue:** no launch, no approval and no volume as of the corpus end (2026-10-22). "Back half of 2026" is a target.
- **Survivorship in narrative.** The Vox post credits ICE with being early and right on regulation. It does not mention that Bakkt's losses, under ICE's majority economic ownership, cut FY22 GAAP EPS by 64%.

## Strategic read (inference — not stated by ICE)

- **The NYSE is ICE's licence to talk.** At ~10% of revenue it is not where the cash is, since energy alone was $2.18B in FY25. It is where ICE meets regulators, heads of state and retail investors. Every pivot (ESG, Texas, tokenization) is announced through Lynn Martin, not through the futures business.
- **Crypto round two is a different bet, not the same bet retried.** Round one tried to make crypto obey exchange rules, and buyers did not come. Round two buys distribution (OKX's 120M accounts), data (Polymarket) and settlement rails (Circle, Securitize), and keeps the matching engine and clearing in-house. If on-chain trading wins, ICE owns a piece of the winners. If it loses, the exposure is a few billion in minority stakes against a ~$100B market cap.
- **The clearing argument is a moat dressed as prudence.** "No matching without central clearing" is true on risk grounds. It also puts ICE's strongest asset, CCPs, at the centre of the hours debate. The tokenized venue's atomic settlement is how ICE gets to 24/7 without conceding the point.
- **Texas is cheap optionality and political hedging.** Reincorporating an existing licence costs little. It aligns the NYSE with the administration and a pro-business state, and it pre-empts a local rival. The cost is reputational with the ESG constituency ICE courted until 2022.

## Canon lenses

- **Counter-positioning and cornered resource** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): the floor plus the closing auction is a resource that Nasdaq and the ATSs cannot replicate cheaply. Six clearing houses let ICE argue "no clearing, no trading" in a way pure venues cannot.
- **Disruption from below** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): overnight ATSs and crypto venues served retail customers the incumbents ignored. ICE's response follows the textbook: buy into the disruptors (Polymarket, OKX) while building a sustaining version (the tokenized NYSE venue).
- **Multi-sided platforms** ([[canon-hagiu-wright-multi-sided-platforms]]): listings, trading and data are three sides of one platform. The NYSE brand subsidises the listing side, and the trading and data sides monetise it.
- **Strategy of conflict** ([[canon-schelling-strategy-of-conflict]]): the joint SEC letter with Citadel and Schwab is a coalition that makes the regulator's preferred outcome costlier. Committing to 22 hours, not 24, reframes the debate before rivals move.
- **Antifragility** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): the barbell is visible. A core of regulated, cleared infrastructure sits on one side; small, capped stakes in volatile crypto-native firms sit on the other.

## Practitioner counterpoint

- **For ICE:** the regulated-first stance aged well after FTX, and the 2023 Vox post's warnings were vindicated. Pillar's latency numbers are plausible and fit the options share gain. Dual listings are cheap for issuers and keep them in the NYSE orbit.
- **Against:** the NYSE is betting on the two things retail cares least about, the floor and regulatory form. Tokenized NYSE equities on a permissioned chain may be the "window dressing" its critics say, with no composability with DeFi, which is where retail activity is. The political turn exposes ICE to the next election, and the "ESG then Texas" swing reads as positioning rather than conviction.

### Rep

**1. How big is the NYSE inside ICE?** FY25 listings were $495M, cash equities and equity options $467M, and net revenue $9.9B. What share is the NYSE, and how does it compare with energy ($2,182M)?
*(495 + 467) / 9,900 = **~9.7%**. Energy alone is 2,182 / 9,900 = **~22%**, about 2.3x the NYSE lines. This understates the NYSE slightly, because NYSE market data sits inside "Data and Connectivity Services" ($1,031M) and is not broken out. The brand does far more work than the revenue.*

**2. What did ICE pay for its Polymarket stake?** ICE invested $1B (Oct 2025) + $600M (Mar 2026) + up to $40M of secondaries, against an ~$8B pre-money valuation for the first tranche. Estimate ICE's ownership if all of it went in at that valuation, and the stake value if the later tranche was priced at double.
*Total ≈ $1.64B. At $8B pre-money, $1B buys 1 / 9 = 11.1% post-money. If the $600M went in at a $16B pre-money, it adds about 0.6 / 16.6 = 3.6% of the enlarged company, and the first stake is diluted to about 11.1% × (16 / 16.6) = 10.7%. That gives roughly **14–15%** in total. At a $16.6B post-money the stake is worth about $2.4B against $1.64B paid, so it is marked up only if the later round really did double the price. ICE disclosed neither number, which is why the "not material" language matters.*

**3. 22 hours or 24?** NYSE Arca's plan leaves a 2-hour daily gap (11:30pm–1:30am ET). Name one risk the gap removes and one flow it loses, then say which side DTCC's June 2026 move to 24x5 strengthens.
*Removes: matching without CCP cover (counterparty risk falls on clearing firms) and corporate-action processing during live trading. Loses: the Asia-Pacific retail flow that overnight ATSs serve, where that window is daytime ([[market-structure-24-5-trading]]). DTCC at 24x5 weakens ICE's clearing argument for the gap. What remains is the corporate-actions and maintenance case, and ICE's own Vox post admits that one is about cost and complexity rather than principle.*
