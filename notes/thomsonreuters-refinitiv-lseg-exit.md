# Note · Thomson Reuters' exit from markets — it sold the trading floor in three steps and kept one wire into it: Reuters News, sold to traders only through LSEG until 2048

> Market-structure / deal-mechanics note · Maps to **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — stake accounting, lock-ups, tax leakage, realized vs. marked value), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — exchange/data consolidation, exclusive distribution), **E3** (M&A, Deals & Restructuring — carve-out, all-share sale, staged sell-down), **E2** (Capital Allocation — when to monetize a non-core stake), **E4** (Corporate Governance — co-ownership with a PE sponsor, board seats at the acquirer) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[thomsonreuters-hub]]; companion [[thomsonreuters-capital-allocation]]; lineage link to [[tradeweb-hub]] and [[lseg-hub]]

- **Source:** Thomson Reuters press releases and IR documents, 2019-01 → 2026-10 (`_ingest/raw/thomsonreuters/articles.jsonl`, 293 full texts). The relevant items: the Refinitiv–LSEG talks (Jul 26 2019), every quarterly results release 2019–2026 (each has an "Ownership Interest" section until Aug 2024), the closing release (Jan 29 2021), the Microsoft sale (Dec 12 2022), three placement releases (Mar, May, Sep 2023), the final sale (May 15 2024), the LSEG–TR news pact (Jan 17 2023), the 2019 AGM transcript and the 2021 Investor Day transcript. Nothing from LSEG, Blackstone or Microsoft directly; their side of each trade is only what TR chose to print.
- **Core claim:** Thomson Reuters left trading and market data in three moves:
  - **2018: carve-out.** 55% of Financial & Risk (F&R) went to Blackstone-led funds for **~$17B cash**. TR kept 45% of what became **Refinitiv**, which in 2019 owned the controlling stake in Tradeweb.
  - **2019–21: all-share sale.** Refinitiv went to LSEG at **~$27B EV**. TR's 45% became **82.5M LSEG shares**, worth **~$9.8B at the Jan 2021 close**.
  - **2021–24: staged sell-down.** TR sold through lock-up windows, sales to Microsoft and to LSEG itself, and London placements at **£71.50–£91.50**. It exited on **May 15 2024**.
  - Roughly **$8.3B** came back in sales (sum of TR's stated per-tranche figures, which mix gross and pre-tax net; derived). That is *below* the closing mark of $9.8B. So the patient sell-down did not beat the day-one value. **Tradeweb's IPO** helped build that value: Jim Smith said so in Aug 2019.
  - What TR kept is a **30-year Reuters News supply contract (2018–2048)**. Under it, LSEG is the *exclusive* channel for Reuters news to financial professionals, with contractual price increases every year. That contract is TR's only remaining tie to market structure.

## 2. What came back, and what it cost

| Tranche | TR shares (M) | Proceeds to TR | Implied $/share (derived) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 2021 (tax sale) | 10.1 | $994M pre-tax net | ~$98 |
| Jan 2023 (Microsoft) | 10.5 | ~$1.0B gross | ~$95 |
| Mar–Sep 2023 (three placements) | ~43.9 | ~$4.1B pre-tax net | ~$94 |
| FY 2023 as restated by TR | 56.0 | "nearly $5.5 billion" gross | ~$98 |
| Q1 2024 | 10.1 | $1.2B | ~$119 |
| Q2 2024 (LSEG buyback + final placement) | 5.9 | $0.6B | ~$102 |
| **Total 2021–24 (derived)** | **82.5** | **≈ $8.3B** | **≈ $100** |

- **Against what benchmark?**
  - Value at signing (Jul 2019): **$6.7B**, about $81 a share.
  - Value at close (Jan 2021): **$9.8B**, about $119 a share.
  - Realized: **≈ $8.3B** in sales (mixed gross and pre-tax net), plus **~$0.2–0.3B of LSEG dividends** received 2021–23 ($51M + $24M in 2021, $87M through Oct 2022, 2023 not itemized; derived).
  - So the exit captured roughly the midpoint between signing and closing values. A buyer of LSEG on the closing day did better than TR's staged exit. *(Derived; ignores FX hedges, which TR used on the sterling exposure in 2022, and the time value of earlier cash.)*
- **Tax leakage was real and front-loaded.**
  - ~$700M was due at close; the Q1 2021 release restates it as ~$640M.
  - ~$225M was due on the Mar 2021 sale.
  - $444M was paid in H1 2021 alone.
  - $317M was paid in 2024 and $62M in 2025 (cash-flow statements).
  - The F&R chain was "predominantly tax deferred" (TR's words), not tax free.
- **Accounting noise.**
  - **2018–20:** Refinitiv was an equity-method investment. Its losses (and warrant revaluations) hit IFRS earnings but were excluded from adjusted EPS.
  - **From 2021:** the LSEG stake was marked at fair value, and IFRS EPS moved with LSEG's share price. Diluted EPS was **$11.50 in 2021** (the $8.1B gain) against adjusted EPS that never moved with it. Q4 2021 showed a diluted *loss* per share of $(0.36) because LSEG fell.
  - For four years, TR's headline EPS was partly a proxy for LSEG.

## 3. The wire that stayed: Reuters News inside LSEG

- **The contract.**
  - 30 years, signed 2018 with Refinitiv. "Reuters News’ 30-year agreement with Refinitiv will continue following closing and is scheduled to run to 2048." (Jan 29 2021)
  - LSEG is described from 2023 as the channel to "financial market professionals exclusively via LSEG products".
  - Revenue grows each year through a "contractual price increase from our news agreement with the Data & Analytics business of LSEG" (results releases, 2022–2026).
- **The Jan 17 2023 pact**, announced while TR was selling LSEG shares:
  - 100 new editorial roles focused on markets, M&A, the energy transition and data visualization.
  - The India reporting team more than doubled; China equities coverage expanded.
  - **PLX AI**, an AI breaking-news service TR bought in 2022, made available *exclusively through LSEG*.
  - A "path forward" for Reuters consumer subscriptions.
  - David Schwimmer (LSEG CEO): "This collaboration will broaden our news offering, enhancing the speed and analysis of breaking news".
- **Economics** (from the results releases):
  - Reuters News revenue was $155M in Q4 2018 (+107%, the first quarter of the Refinitiv contract) and $853M in 2025.
  - Reuters' adjusted EBITDA margin was 23.6% in 2024 and 20.4% in 2025. That is the lowest of TR's segments; Legal runs at ~47%.
  - In 2023–24 Reuters also sold generative-AI training licences, "largely transactional in nature". They lifted Q1 2024 organic growth to +17%, then reversed (−7% in Q1 2025).
- **Why it matters for the market-structure lens (inference):**
  - TR is no longer a market-data vendor, but its newsroom is a **component of LSEG Workspace**, the same distribution channel that carries Tradeweb data and Ai-Price ([[tradeweb-ai-price]]).
  - LSEG holds the pricing power: it is the exclusive buyer for the financial channel, with fixed escalators until 2048.
  - TR's ability to sell Reuters *directly* to traders is contractually closed. It can only grow Reuters through media agencies, events, Reuters.com and AI licensing.

## 4. The Tradeweb connection

- **Lineage:**
  - Thomson buys Tradeweb (2004–05).
  - Tradeweb sits inside F&R.
  - 55% of F&R goes to Blackstone (Oct 2018) and becomes Refinitiv, which keeps ~54% of Tradeweb after the Apr 2019 IPO.
  - LSEG agrees to buy Refinitiv (Aug 2019) and closes (Jan 2021), becoming Tradeweb's controlling shareholder (see [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]] §2).
  - TR exits LSEG (May 2024).
- **The value link, in TR's own words.**
  - Smith (Aug 1 2019): "The value creation at Refinitiv since our partnership with Blackstone has largely been driven by operational enhancements, cost savings and the IPO of Tradeweb."
  - TR's 45% Refinitiv stake was worth ~$3B in Jan 2018 and $9.8B at the 2021 close (Investor Day figures).
  - Part of that tripling came from a market re-rating Tradeweb at its IPO price and above. The rest came from Refinitiv's $650M cost program and the LSEG premium.
  - Inference: some of the money TR later spent on Casetext, Pagero and two returns of capital was, in effect, Tradeweb equity value created in 2019–20.
- **The thesis line for consolidation.** Smith, same release: "There is a trend toward consolidation of the global financial markets infrastructure and data segments". The TR exit is the *seller's* view of the trend that produced LSEG–Refinitiv, and later ICE–MarketAxess and Tradeweb–Trumid.
- **The irony worth remembering.** TR's own 2021 Investor Day transcript was produced and copyrighted by *Refinitiv StreetEvents*. TR's record of its own strategy is a product of the business it had just sold.

## 5. What the claims don't prove

1. **Monetized is not maximized.** TR never compares realized proceeds with the closing mark or with a sell-at-close counterfactual. On TR's own numbers the staged exit realized less than the $9.8B mark (§2). Lock-ups forced some of that, but TR chose the pace after Jan 2023.
2. **The $15B "capacity" (2021) was mostly LSEG's share price.** It moved with a stock TR didn't control: the stake was $9.8B in Jan 2021 and $6.3B in Oct 2022. Treat any firepower figure that includes a listed stake as a range, not a number.
3. **Proceeds are reported inconsistently** (gross for Microsoft and FY 2023, pre-tax net for placements). The quarterly sums (55.0M shares, $5.4B) don't quite match the FY 2023 restatement (56.0M, ~$5.5B). The gap is unexplained.
4. **TR doesn't disclose the Reuters–LSEG contract's price escalator or minimums.** "Contractual price increase" appears every quarter with no size attached.
5. **Use of proceeds is asserted, not traced.** "Funded with LSEG sale proceeds" covers the 2023 return of capital, Casetext ("funded by proceeds from recent sales in London Stock Exchange stake") and, in 2026, a $605M return of cash received two years earlier. Money is fungible. The labels say which story TR wanted to tell for each use (see [[thomsonreuters-capital-allocation]]).

## 6. Strategic read (inference — not TR's words)

- **Why sell markets at all?** The 2021 Investor Day answer is implicit. Legal, tax and corporates software grew 4–6% with high retention and pricing power, while trading terminals faced Bloomberg and fee pressure. A **portfolio of different businesses** under one holding company (Hasker's "portfolio orientation" critique) can't run one operating model. Selling F&R let TR become one company. Smith's line, "the most important part of transaction was that it gave us the opportunity to reposition the new Thomson Reuters", says the purpose was focus, not price.
- **Why take shares, not cash, in 2019?** Smith said the deal gave holders "a more certain path and timetable to liquidity". A Blackstone-led private company has no listed exit, while LSEG shares do. TR traded control of the timing (lock-ups) for a liquid currency and exposure to the consolidation premium.
- **Why keep Reuters tied to LSEG?** The 30-year contract was the price of the 2018 carve-out: Refinitiv needed the news feed. TR got steady, escalating revenue and kept editorial independence (the Trust Principles), at the cost of strategic optionality in financial news.
- **Who won?**
  - Blackstone's consortium owned 55% and sold alongside TR in each placement.
  - LSEG got Refinitiv's data and Tradeweb, and later sold a stake to Microsoft as a partner.
  - Microsoft bought in at roughly $95 a share.
  - TR got focus and roughly $25B over 2018–24 ($17B cash plus ~$8.3B of LSEG sales). It returned most of that to shareholders.

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Focus vs. conglomerate** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): the F&R sale is a coherent-action choice. It concentrated resources on the legal, tax and compliance segments, where TR had the stronger content position. The diagnosis was implicit in Hasker's "portfolio orientation" complaint.
- **Margin of safety in exit timing** ([[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]]): a seller with a lock-up and a tax bill is a forced seller for part of the stake. TR used the lock-up exception only for the tax, and otherwise sold into strength (2023 placements at £71.50–£80.50, final sale at £91.50).
- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): LSEG was at once TR's investee, its exclusive news buyer, a buyer of its shares and a competitor for AI-era content licensing. The 2023 news pact is a value-creating move inside a relationship that had become value-*claiming* on the share side.
- **Agency and the co-owner** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): TR held its LSEG shares *through an entity jointly owned with Blackstone*, so every sale was a joint decision. Lock-up amendments (Microsoft 2022, LSEG buyback 2024) needed LSEG's consent. A minority holder's exit is a negotiation, not a button.

## 8. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For the exit:** it removed a volatile, non-core asset from the EPS line and funded ~$2.8B of direct returns ($2.2B in 2023, $605M in 2026) plus the AI and tax deals. It also let management talk about one business. The P&L supports the result: organic growth went from ~1–4% (2019–20) to 7% (2024–25).
- **Against:** the stake fell from a $9.8B mark to ≈$8.3B realized, and TR paid ~$1B+ in tax along the way. Investors who wanted LSEG exposure could have bought it themselves. The holding period added accounting noise and no strategic control: one board seat out of a ~15% stake, with voting agreed to follow LSEG's board.
- **Synthesis:** the exit's value was in **separation**, not in the sell-down's execution. The sell-down was competent but unremarkable.
