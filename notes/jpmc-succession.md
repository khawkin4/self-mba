# Note · JPMorganChase succession — seven years of rotations built a bench, then a banker/trader co-president race; the date was never named

> Governance / leadership note · Maps to **E4** (Corporate Governance & The Board — CEO succession as a board process), **E1** (Leading at Scale — developing leaders who lead leaders), **Course 09** (Leadership & Organizational Behavior — talent rotation, power), **E8** (Investor Relations — how a tenure question gets answered) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[jpmc-people]] and [[jpmc-hub]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/jpmc/articles.jsonl`. Used here: the leadership releases (2019-04-17, 2020-03-05, 2024-01-25, 2025-01-14, 2025-12-08, 2026-06-25); the signature blocks of the line-of-business letters AR2019–AR2025; Dimon's AR2022 letter (the only one with a succession paragraph); and the succession Q&A on the 4Q24 and 2Q26 calls and at the 2025 Investor Day. The May 2021 CCB hand-over (Smith → Lake and Piepszak) and Barnum's 2021 move to CFO have **no press release** on JPMC's sites. They are dated here from the AR2021 letters and the call transcripts.
- **Core claim:** JPMorganChase never named a successor or a date. Instead the Board ran a **rotation programme**. Each contender ran at least two different businesses, and the top jobs were split into pairs (Co-Presidents 2018–20, CCB co-CEOs 2021–23, CIB co-CEOs 2024–26, Co-Presidents again from June 2026). The final structure crosses the candidates' backgrounds: **Doug Petno, a corporate banker, runs the wholesale bank; Troy Rohrbaugh, an options trader, runs the consumer bank.** The one contender who had run both a finance function and a business, Marianne Lake, retired when the plan was set. Dimon's public line moved from "multiple successor candidates" (2023) to "intent... not a promise" (2025) to "Several years... plus or minus" (2026). The vagueness is deliberate and consistent.

## 3. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Several exceptional people" is unverifiable.** The Board's slate, its criteria and its timetable are never disclosed. The proxy statement (DEF 14A), not read here, is where any process detail would sit ([[sec-filing-intelligence]]).
2. **"Not a promise" cuts both ways.** It lowers expectations of an exit and keeps the option to stay. Each year from 2025 the answer is the same as last year. That fits a rolling horizon, not a fixed one.
3. **Rotation isn't evidence of readiness.** Rohrbaugh has run CCB since June 2026, about four months before this note. Petno has run a combined CIB alone for the same period. Track records in the new seats don't exist yet.
4. **The 2020 interim worked for weeks, not years.** It shows the Board can act on a key-person event. It doesn't show how the firm runs without Dimon's capital, regulatory and political roles (§4).
5. **Pronouns and motives.** On the 2Q26 call Dimon said Lake chose to retire "when she knew about the plan". The corpus gives no statement from Lake. Treat the "No mystery" framing as Dimon's.

## 4. Strategic read (inference — not JPMC's words)

- **The design is a tournament with crossed assignments.** Each finalist runs the business the other grew up near: the banker gets trading, payments and Global Banking; the trader gets 94 million consumer relationships, branches and cards. The Board tests breadth, which is exactly the trait list Dimon gave. The trade-off is that both units get a new leader in the same month.
- **Pairs are the house habit, not an exception.** Co-Presidents (Pinto and Smith), CCB co-CEOs, CIB co-CEOs, and Payments co-heads (Farooq and Neukirchen) all appear in this corpus. Pairs keep options open and reduce single-point dependency. Dimon's reprinted 2009 essay in the AR2020 letter warns that "When tri-heads report to co-heads, all decisions become political". The firm runs co-heads anyway.
- **Contrast with Tradeweb.** Tradeweb abolished its No. 2 seat after Pluta's 21 months ([[tradeweb-people]]). JPMorganChase doubled it. One CEO (Hult) wants two operators beneath; the other (Dimon) wants two heirs beside.
- **The COO seat is a "non-candidate" power role.** By saying Piepszak "does not want to be the CEO", Dimon makes the COO a stable centre for technology, data and AI that the next CEO inherits rather than fights over. "All Corporate functions will be managed jointly by Piepszak and Dimon." (2025-01-14).
- **Key-person risk is broader than CEO.** Dimon is also the regulatory voice ([[jpmc-capital-regulation]]), the political voice ("From the desk of", SRI council chair) and the capital allocator ([[jpmc-capital-allocation]]). The successor inherits all three roles or must rebuild them.

## 5. Canon lenses

- **Power and politics** ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]): a co-president race concentrates attention and resources on two people and makes everyone else a kingmaker or a bystander. Watch who reports to whom after the race ends.
- **Leaders who build leaders** ([[canon-grove-high-output-management]]): Grove's "task-relevant maturity" argues for rotating managers through unfamiliar work before giving them more leverage. The JPMC sequence is a textbook version.
- **Culture as the succession asset** ([[canon-schein-organizational-culture]]): Dimon's answer is "the disciplines and the culture of the place", meaning the firm should outlast the CEO. Schein would ask whether those norms are embedded or carried by one person.
- **Decision under uncertainty** ([[canon-duke-thinking-in-bets]]): "even if you thought you knew today, you couldn't be completely sure" is a bet-sizing answer. Keep options open until the information arrives.

## 6. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For the design:** analysts asked about succession on three of the five 2024–26 calls read, and the Board answered with structure rather than a date. That kept the narrative steady through Pinto's retirement and Lake's exit without a share-price event in the corpus.
- **Against:** Mayo's critique ("we have a FX trader now selling mortgage, credit cards and deposits") is the honest risk. A tournament can push the loser out, as Lake's exit suggests, and leave the firm with one experienced business head instead of two.
- **Synthesis:** the process is visible, the endpoint is not. The test is whether both finalists still run their units in 2028.
