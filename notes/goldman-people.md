- **Core claim:** Goldman's public voice is **unusually concentrated**. Solomon carries every earnings quote, every letter and an annual podcast. His arc runs builder (2019–21: "digital consumer bank of the future"), then refocus (2022–23: "no ambiguity about who we are"), then scale and AI (2024–26: One GS, OneGS 3.0, "I'm not in the job apocalypse camp"). **Waldron** is the operator and the visible heir: President/COO since 2018, on the Board from Feb 2025. He supplies the self-diagnosis in 2019 ("Balkanized") and the metrics in 2025 (σ 24% → 6%; "We kind of obsess over those rankings"). **Business seats are filled from inside** — Golten after 27 years in risk, Coleman a 1996 analyst, Esposito, Dees and Varadhan in their second and third decades. In Jan 2025 they were **split into triumvirates** "to create more operating leverage for our senior leadership". **Engineering and AI seats are bought from Big Tech** (AWS, Google ×2, Amazon AGI, AMD/AWS) at *partner* level, and Bellevue opened next to their campuses in Sep 2026. The firm also **stages its own disagreements** — Lee the AI bull against Covello the skeptic, Bantwal's "not semiliquid" against Brandmeyer's negative PE alpha — and these are the most candid lines in the corpus.

**What the chart says.**
- **Two people at the top for eight years.** The CEO and President/COO have not changed since Oct 2018. The No. 2 seat accumulated weight: President/COO, then a Board seat in Feb 2025. That is the standard heir signal at US banks *(inference; see §5)*.
- **The CFO seat turned over once, and the turn told the strategy.** Scherr, "integral to the development of key growth initiatives at the firm, including Consumer and Transaction Banking", gave way to a leveraged-finance banker in the same month (Sep 2021) GreenSky was announced. A year later consumer was being narrowed ([[goldman-consumer-retreat]]).
- **Co-heads everywhere, and in threes from 2025.** IB, FICC and Equities each have three heads. GSI has two, who also hold global business jobs. One GS has three. Solomon gives the reason: "to create more operating leverage for our senior leadership" and "to ensure we have a strong group of leaders who have been tested" (L24).
- **Control functions are filled by career insiders.** Golten (27 years), Richman (nearly three decades) and Coleman (1996). Outsiders come in to build **regulatory credibility** (Ruemmler, Apr 2020, the year of the 1MDB settlement), **geography** (Sneader), **culture and mentoring** (Kaplan, a boomerang) and **engineering** (§6).
- **The IR chair became a strategy chair.** Halio ran the bank (2020), then IR (2021), then IR plus CSO (2023), the year the firm reorganised and reset targets. Goldman treats the investor narrative as a strategy function ([[goldman-strategy-financials]]).

## 2. Profiles

### David Solomon — Chairman & CEO (Oct 2018–)
**Background:** came to GS about 20 years before 2019, after other firms. He is still "to my face, called the lateral hire" and answers "Yeah, I'm bleeding Goldman Sachs at this point." (2019). He was turned down twice in his twenties (2022). **Talks about:** client-centricity, plans and targets, durable revenues, regulation, AI, culture.
- **Plan over nimbleness.** "This organization has always been very adaptive, it's always been very flexible." Now "you have to have a plan" (2019). He says he is "the first CEO" to "lay out publicly a clearly articulated strategy" (Jun 2022).
- **Performance buys permission.** "If you're not performing, you have a lot less latitude to accomplish the things as an organization you want the organization to accomplish." He also says "private enterprise can't solve big problems in society." (both Jan 2022).
- **Capital versus judgment.** "And capital is a commodity." But "one of the things that, as an organization, we still excel at, is bespoke risk intermediation." (2019).
- **Consumer, in four sentences over seven years:** "We are building the digital consumer bank of the future." (L19) → "In 2022, we decided to significantly narrow our ambitions for our consumer strategy." (L22) → "Our consumer ambitions we narrowed materially, and then we went and we executed on those decisions very effectively." (Jan 2024) → "Other than the pivot on digital consumer banking where we decided it just wasn't the right path for us at this time" (Jan 2026).
- **Self-criticism, rationed.** "We haven't executed perfectly on all of them. And there are places where we certainly could have executed better." On the 10.2% ROE: "Of course, that's not what Goldman Sachs aspires to." (Feb 2023). On 1MDB: "we did not adequately address red flags" (Oct 2020).
- **Regulator-facing.** On the 2024 SCB: "This increase does not seem to reflect the strategic evolution of our business". On Basel III endgame: it "would hurt economic activity without improving financial stability." (L23).
- **AI, cooling his own story.** "There's no question that generative AI is going to disrupt a wide range of industries. But I believe it's important to keep perspective." (L23); "Euphoria cools." (Jan 2024); "This is not a place where you necessarily have to be first" (Jan 2024); on engineers, "We would have more if we weren't constrained by our desire to drive returns in our business." (Jan 2025); "I'm not in the job apocalypse camp." and OneGS 3.0 is "narrower than that grand vision of reimagining the firm." (Jan 2026).
- **Optimistic about** M&A ("2026 could be one of the best M&A years ever"), private credit (the "fulcrum", L24) and the US growth gap over Europe. **Worried about** exogenous shocks, cyber and geopolitics (the same list in 2025 and 2026), and that "the credit cycle has not been repealed." (L25).

### John Waldron — President & COO (2018–), director (Feb 2025–)
**Background:** Bear Stearns in the early '90s (leveraged finance), then GS in 2000, recruited by Solomon. Co-head of IBD. English major at Middlebury; father of six. **Talks about:** execution cadence, de-siloing, risk culture, durability metrics, geopolitics.
- **COO first.** "I think about the job as chief operating officer first and kind of president second." He runs a biweekly cross-divisional meeting and split his time half on existing businesses, half on new initiatives (2019).
- **The self-diagnosis.** "we have gotten more Balkanized. We do operate in more individual units."; "the organizational structure of the firm was getting in the way of the ethos of the firm" (2019). Full arc in [[goldman-one-gs-culture]].
- **Unity rule.** "You can kick and scream and fight behind closed doors, but when you come out, you set a direction." (2019).
- **Durability as a number.** "In the last five years, the standard deviation of our results is 6%. In the prior 10 years, it was 24%." He gives the reason too: "financing revenues tend to be stickier" (2025).
- **Risk parity.** "So many firms struggle to have risk management be equivalent to the business side." Also the mark-to-market test: "What's the third-party bid for that asset?" and "We have moderated our risk positioning since April 2nd." (2025).
- **How his view changed.** In 2019 technology was a confessed gap: "That more spoke to my insecurity of not understanding all the platform work". By 2025 it was the test of the firm: "I think the definitional success of our firm over time will be how well do we deploy technology to make us better." He adds, "that's not my historical area of expertise". On leadership: "I communicate more and better now than I used to." and "email is not enough". On people: "It's a people firm. It's a human capital driven firm."
- **Macro voice:** six-plus percent deficits "unsustainable" (2025). In the FT (Aug 2025), China's share of US imports fell from 22 per cent (2017) to 13.4 per cent (2024).

### Denis Coleman — CFO (2022–) and Stephen Scherr — CFO (2018–21)
- **Scherr** built consumer and TxB. The ID20 roadmap bridged ROE from 10.0% to >13%. On retiring: "Goldman Sachs is an incredible organization and having participated in its growth over my 28-year career, I am proud of what we have accomplished". Solomon's farewell pinned consumer to him.
- **Coleman** joined in 1996 in the Bank Loan Group and became partner in 2008. On appointment he promised to "ensure strong risk and capital management, and safeguard our unique culture". His ID23 roadmap reached 14–16% ROE through management fees, alts balance-sheet reduction and reversing the Platform Solutions loss. His SCB line is the technocrat's: "We will continue to engage with our regulator to better understand their determinations and to advocate for a more transparent process." (Aug 2024). The corpus has no Coleman interview; his conference remarks survive only as forward-looking boilerplate.

### Ashok Varadhan, Dan Dees, Jim Esposito — the markets and banking co-heads (2020–24)
- **Varadhan** (joined 1998 as an associate) on the COVID quarter: "returns will be an output, not an input." On talent stories: "there's always a newspaper article about the person that left not a newspaper article about the quality of the team that stays behind." (Apr 2020). **His shift:** by 2023 his top fear is operations, not markets: "so much of our workflow has migrated through technological automation", and errors are found "after the fact". On crises: "the pandemic, subsequent to the financial crisis, frankly, from, like, a markets perspective felt like nothing." He remembers Corzine walking the floor: "a culture of things being very flat".
- **Dees** ("Raising the floor", ID23: GBM financing $2.0bn in 2013 → $7.1bn in 2022): "For investment banking, it is investing in our clients." His worry: "the deteriorating fiscal position of the United States is concerning" (Nov 2023).
- **Esposito** (London-based; ran capital markets in 2008 alongside CFO Viniar) on TxB: "Goldman Sachs is uniquely positioned to disrupt this market building on over 150 years of financial and risk management experience" (Jun 2021). On leading: "putting the right person into an important seat at Goldman Sachs is the most leveragable asset you have as a leader" (Nov 2023). He notes private equity rose from ~20% to "just shy of 40%" of global merger volume.

### Kim Posnett — co-head One GS (2023) → global co-head IB (2025)
Posnett presented the 2023 One GS scorecard (IB fee share +445bp, FICC/Equities wallet share +255bp, $31bn alts raised from One GS clients). On *AI Exchanges* (Jan 2025): "I think maybe a year ago I would have said data. I think today I would say power." and "I think this year is the year of true enterprise adoption and scaling." The career path — from the One GS seat to an IB triumvirate seat — is itself a signal of what the firm rewards *(inference)*.

### Anthony Gutman & Kunal Shah — GSI co-CEOs (2025–), co-heads of IB and FICC
Both hold a regional and a global job at once. Gutman: Europe is "a third to a quarter of the firmwide revenues, firmwide head count", and the pair are trying "to change the default setting" on Europe. On AI and M&A: "what AI is doing is driving a view that scale is critical". Shah (intern in 2003) was warned "Don't become a trader. And especially don't go into fixed income." Now: "But we still have thriving teams of humans aided by technology." On software sell-offs: "someone being able to vibe code is not just going to be able to recreate their business models in any short order" (Mar 2026).

### Marc Nachmann and Julian Salisbury — AWM (2023–)
Nachmann (Global Head of AWM) framed the 2023 PFM sale as margin discipline: "It is margin accretive to Asset & Wealth Management". Salisbury (AWM CIO) appears only through slide text: ~1,100/600/300 investment professionals in the Americas/EMEA/APAC and "Average partner tenure of ~20 years" (ID23). Salisbury has no quoted view in the corpus.

### Stephanie Cohen — Chief Strategy Officer (2019) → Platform Solutions (2023)
Solomon moved her from IBD to CSO as one of his first rotations (2019). The ID20 innovation deck (with Argenti) is pure platform: "Developers are first class citizens"; "Simplify the complex services we provide to clients into component pieces". By ID23 she was running the profitability path for the consumer wind-down: "Manage through a complicated credit environment", breakeven by 2025. **The sharpest personal arc in the corpus:** the platform strategist became the person managing its retreat.

### Carey Halio — CEO GS Bank USA (2020) → IR (2021) → IR + CSO (2023)
In IR videos: "We produced solid results in a complicated market backdrop, reflecting the diversity and quality of our franchise" (Jul 2022). On the realignment: "our business realignment will help evolve the firm and further unlock the power of One Goldman Sachs" (Oct 2022). Her ID23 deck sold *transparency, accountability, alignment* and the people numbers (300:1 applicants, 20-year median partner tenure). The IR transcripts are not in the corpus.

### Marco Argenti — co-CIO (2019) → CIO
From AWS (VP Technology since 2013), Nokia and Dada. The 2019 hiring email framed tech as "digital platforms that seamlessly integrate execution, analytics and content". His views:
- "We need to think as big as we can…before AI thinks bigger than we do." (May 2023).
- "The revolution here is the revolution of knowledge" (Apr 2023).
- **Adoption is a people problem:** "the biggest friction point to adoption of AI in the enterprise is people, is behaviors"; developers adopted first (Mar 2025).
- **Culture inside agents:** "Goldman is very peculiar. Amazon, where I was before, was very peculiar." Agents arrive like new hires without the culture.
- **Juniors matter more, not less:** "Everybody becomes a manager." "The failure mode here is not technological, it's organizational." "Supervision is key, which requires experience, and experience requires time" (Fortune, Jul 2025).

### George Lee — co-CIO (2019) → co-head GS Global Institute; *AI Exchanges* host
The house AI bull. "There's an extraordinary level of unanimity about the importance and the potential of the AI revolution." (Mar 2024). On DeepSeek, aimed at Covello: "I think this answers some of Jim's principal concerns. Not all of them but some of them." He sees token costs that "trend towards marginal zero". He launched the GS AI Assistant: "AI is, in many ways, prolific throughout the firm, but this is the broadest and most general purpose offering we've made." (Jan 2025).

### Jim Covello — Head of Global Equity Research: the in-house skeptic
Covello is the most quotable dissent the firm publishes against its own AI narrative: "So, the crucial question is: What $1tn problem will AI solve?"; "The idea that technology typically starts out expensive before becoming cheaper is revisionist history."; and from GS's own use, "we've found that AI can update historical data in our company models more quickly than doing so manually, but at six times the cost." He still advised staying long, because "I've never seen a stock decline only because it's expensive" (Jun 2024). *Read:* Covello is Goldman's licensed skeptic. His timing (12–18 months of enthusiasm from Jun 2024) proved wrong so far, but his ROI question is the one Solomon's "narrower" OneGS 3.0 quietly accepts ([[goldman-ai]]).

### Mathew McDermott — Global Head of Digital Assets
Three phases. In 2021, access desk: "Client demand, pure and simple."; "we've crossed the Rubicon in terms of institutional buy-in", with a warning: "we're only one big fraud away from a very negative impact on the market". In 2022 (post-Terra), infrastructure: "I think it's important not to group all stable coins in the same bucket." In 2024–25, permissioned rails: "We view permissioned distributed technologies as the next structural change to financial markets" (GS DAP spin-out, Tradeweb first partner). Tokenized MMFs "would enable us to unlock their utility as a form of collateral" (Jul 2025). See [[goldman-digital-assets]]; Goldman's role in Digital Asset's $135M round is in [[tradeweb-outside-voices]].

### Chris Churchman — Head of Marquee
Product-first and sceptical of demos. "Visual Structuring aims to create a new paradigm in options trade idea generation" (2022); MarketView is "an open ecosystem" (2023). By 2026: Marquee's "mission is to help our clients make decisions under great uncertainty"; "But like a demo is judged on its best day, whereas a product is judged on its worst day, right?" On juniors, he warns of "cognitive atrophy" and wants to protect "the apprenticeship culture that we have to proactively push" (Aug 2026). Marquee revenue is never disclosed ([[goldman-markets-platforms]]).

### Eleanor Beasley and David Jeria — the market-structure voices
**Beasley** (Head of Equity Market Structure, EMEA) defends internalisation and backs the consolidated tape: "in 2024 alone we provided over €1 trillion of principal liquidity in European equities to our clients." and "This transparency will show that our markets are far more liquid than people realize" (2025–26). **Jeria** (Head of Electronic Trading Strats) is the candid dealer-side critic of retail internalisation: "Because these trades happen on a bilateral basis, they are non-participable from other market participants." and "Trading costs skyrocketed across the broad spectrum of all stocks that our clients were trading." (recorded Jun 2022; the corpus files it as 2020-06-14).

### Vivek Bantwal and Michael Brandmeyer — private credit and external investing (both to MC, Jan 2026)
**Bantwal** (co-head Global Private Credit, from GBM in Jan 2025): "But the reality is that it's not semiliquid, it's illiquid."; "There are a lot of new players in private credit that haven't been tested through cycles."; and a dated claim of foresight, "going back to 2023 when we turned down our first deal due to concerns around AI disruption" (Mar 2026). **Brandmeyer** (XIG CIO) gives the bluntest admission in AWM: "private equity has actually gone negative in terms of alpha to the public markets for the first time over the last two years." and "So, the system is gummed up. The circulatory system is not working." (Jun 2026). Details in [[goldman-awm-private-credit]].

## 4. Communication style

**Solomon — the annual narrator.** He sets a ritual (a January podcast, "what has become an annual tradition"; a spring letter) and uses fixed slots: macro, then CEO sentiment, then the firm, then "what keeps you up at night". He likes round historical claims ("It's not different this time") and baseball innings. He concedes with modifiers (*not executed perfectly*, "narrower") rather than nouns. He corrects his own forecasts in public (Omicron in 2022; the 2023 recession that didn't come). He separates the firm from politics ("private enterprise can't solve big problems in society").

**Waldron — the operator-explainer.** He uses plain-English glosses ("What does that really mean in plain English?"), triads (people, preparation, process; three strategic pillars) and first-person learning ("I communicate more and better now"). He is the only senior leader who names his own weakness on air, twice, six years apart.

**The co-head chorus.** "We all speak off the same hymn sheet" (Dees, Nov 2023). Co-head episodes are ensemble banter, not debate. Real disagreement lives in Research (Covello vs Lee) and in AWM's private-credit candour.

**Recurring house phrases** (first sightings): *One Goldman Sachs* (memo, Oct 2018) · *client-centricity* (2019) · *durable revenues* (Solomon, 2019; repeated in every AWM deal release) · *raising the floor* (L22, ID23) · *no ambiguity about who we are* (L23) · *operate at scale* (2024 strategy) · *interconnected franchises* (2024) · *fulcrum* (L24) · *OneGS 3.0* (L25) · *apprenticeship culture* (L21, L23, Churchman 2026).

**What they never talk about:** named venue competitors (Tradeweb appears twice; Trumid, MarketAxess and ICE never). Marquee revenue. Partner exits by name after 2019. The proxy pay numbers. Any regulator in its own voice. Lemkau's 2020 reflections on 28 years (a blurb, no transcript).

## 5. Succession read *(inference throughout)*

- **The stated template is Blankfein's handover to Solomon:** private word in March 2018, public announcement in July, effective Oct 1. Solomon calls it "a more thoughtful transition, to my benefit and I think, candidly, the firm's benefit", against Blankfein's own two-day takeover from Paulson in 2006. If Solomon repeats it, expect a public announcement months before an effective date.
- **Waldron is positioned as heir, not named as one.** He has been President/COO since 2018 and became a non-independent director in Feb 2025. Solomon's reason: "he will provide important insights into how we are driving execution priorities". He is the second voice on strategy and the one who names the metrics. The corpus never uses the word *successor*.
- **Solomon's horizon talk points past 2026.** "Now we are focused on setting up the firm for the next five years." (L24). In Jan 2026 he cites eight years of results ("we've taken the market cap from kind of $70 billion to $300 billion"). That reads as a CEO describing a legacy while still planning *(inference)*.
- **The bench below Waldron was deliberately widened.** The 2025 triumvirates add 18 Management Committee members "who have been tested". Gutman and Shah hold a regional CEO job plus a global business job. Posnett has run One GS and IB. Coleman holds the CFO seat. Nachmann runs AWM, the growth engine, and seven AWM leaders joined the MC in 2026. This looks like a deliberate pipeline for the generation after Waldron, not a contest for Solomon's chair.
- **Contrast:** JPMorgan's long co-president race, where the date was never named ([[jpmc-succession]]), and Tradeweb's choreographed, 10-month founder handover ([[tradeweb-people]]). Goldman sits between them: a single obvious No. 2, and no dated plan in public.

## 7. Leadership lenses

- [[canon-grove-high-output-management]] — Esposito's "putting the right person into an important seat" is Grove's managerial leverage. The 2025 triumvirates are leverage bought with span: three heads per business so the top two can work on the firm.
- [[canon-pfeffer-power]] — power accrues to the No. 2 through **position plus voice**: President/COO, the metrics, and the Board seat. The co-head structure diffuses power below that level. Nobody under Waldron controls a whole division alone.
- [[canon-schein-organizational-culture]] — Varadhan's flat-floor memory (Corzine, 1998) and Solomon's "lateral hire" joke are basic assumptions about status, voiced as stories. The Big Tech partners test them, as Argenti says: "Goldman is very peculiar."
- [[canon-kotter-leading-change]] — the three signatures on the day-one memo were the guiding coalition. Scherr's exit and Coleman's arrival are what happens to coalition members when the vision changes.
- [[canon-tetlock-superforecasting]] — Covello's 12–18-month call and Solomon's public forecast corrections are scoreable. Score Solomon's "2026 could be one of the best M&A years ever" against FY2026 league tables.
- [[canon-scott-radical-candor]] — Waldron's "kick and scream… behind closed doors" and the staged Lee–Covello debate are candor about the business. Candor about people is absent (no named exits after 2019).
- [[canon-badaracco-defining-moments]] — 1MDB is the defining moment for this leadership team. Solomon's memo ("we are responsible for each other's actions") and the $31m cut to current leaders' 2020 pay are the dated test of whether they lived the value or only stated it.
