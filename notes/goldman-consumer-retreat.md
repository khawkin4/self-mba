# Note · Goldman Sachs consumer retreat — a greenfield bank inside an investment bank, built 2016–21 and unwound in five dated steps

> Strategy / reversal case note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — entering a market with no right to win), **E3** (M&A, Deals & Restructuring — GreenSky bought for $2.24bn in stock, impaired, sold within two years; PFM; Apple Card to Chase), **E5** (Transformation & Change — killing your own flagship initiative), **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations — narrating a failure in one sentence), **Course 02** (reserve accounting: why exits *add* to EPS) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[goldman-strategy-financials]] and [[goldman-hub]]; counterparty side in [[jpmc-hub]]

- **Source:** Goldman's own channels, `_ingest/raw/goldman/articles.jsonl`: the **2019–2025 shareholder letters** (dated to fiscal year-end, published ~Mar–Apr of the next year), the **2020 Investor Day deck** and the **2023 Platform Solutions deck** (Stephanie Cohen; PDF-extracted, tables partly garbled), **12 earnings releases** that carry consumer items (Q1 2023 – Q3 2026), the deal releases (United Capital May 16 2019; GreenSky Sep 15 2021 / Mar 29 2022 / Oct 11 2023; PFM Aug 28 2023; Apple Card Jan 7 2026), the CFO succession release (Sep 14 2021), the corporate boilerplate across **~110 press releases**, and Solomon's *Exchanges* episodes (Oct 2019, Jan 2022, Feb 2023, Jan 2024, Jan 2026). **Missing:** any Marcus customer, any Apple or JPMorgan voice, the GreenSky sale price, cumulative consumer losses, and the 10-K segment P&L. Every line here is Goldman's own account.
- **Core claim:** Goldman launched Marcus in 2016 and won Apple Card in 2019 because, in Solomon's words, "we had no legacy business". Apple wanted a partner that would let it stay outside "the financial regulatory moat". The 2019 letter promised "the digital consumer bank of the future", sized at **$125bn of deposits and $20bn of loans in five years**. By 2021 it had **>10m customers and $110bn of deposits**, and it paid **$2.24bn in stock for GreenSky** to save "7-10 years" of building a merchant network. Then credit costs arrived on a book that was scaling fast, in a firm whose stress test punished exactly that. The bank was unwound in five dated steps:
  1. **Marcus loans** (Q1–Q2 2023)
  2. **PFM** (Aug 2023)
  3. **GreenSky** (Oct 2023, after a $504m goodwill impairment)
  4. **The GM card** (Q3 2024)
  5. **Apple Card to Chase** (Jan 2026; >$20bn book; +$0.46 EPS)

  Only the deposit franchise was kept, as funding inside AWM. The shareholder letters give the reversal **one sentence** (2022), a euphemism (2023), silence (2024) and a footnote naming "another issuer" (2025). The corporate self-description dropped "and consumer banking" between **Feb 21 and Apr 17 2023**, the first press releases after Investor Day 2023.

## 3. The numbers that drove it

**Platform Solutions, as Goldman presented it (ID23 deck, Feb 28 2023; extracted table text):**
- **Enterprise partnerships** (Apple, GM) average loan balances rose **$1bn (2019YE) → $3bn → $6bn → $13bn (2022YE)**, with plans for **>$15bn (2023) and >$20bn (2025)**. The "back-book" share was planned to rise from 0% to ~60% by 2025: growth first, profits once vintages season.
- **Consumer platforms** 2022 revenue **~$1.2bn**, against a **>$4bn 2024 target** set thirteen months earlier. Transaction Banking 2022 revenue **~$0.3bn**.
- **Merchant point-of-sale (GreenSky)**: average FICO ~770, $7.5bn originations in 2022, and a plan to grow *on-balance-sheet* loans from **0 → ~4 → >$10bn by 2025YE**. Seven months later the business was sold.
- **Sensitivity:** "A 50bps increase in the unemployment rate" could raise provisions by **~$150mm**. One of the slide's listed profitability drivers was 'Adjusted contract terms' (from 2H23) for card partnerships, i.e. renegotiating the Apple and GM economics.
- **Efficiency ratio (trailing):** **189% (FY2020) → 155% → 142% → 134% → 133% (FY2022)**, with a target of **<100% in 2025**. At 133%, the segment spent **$1.33 of expense for every $1 of revenue** *before* credit losses.

**Scale versus the firm** *(derived)*: FY2022 Platform Solutions revenue of $1.50bn was **3.2% of firm revenue** ($47.37bn). The bet consumed far more management attention, capital (through the SCB) and narrative than its revenue share.

**GreenSky, in and out** *(derived from stated figures)*:
- Paid ~$2.24bn in stock.
- The $504m goodwill impairment wrote off ~22% of the price within 16 months.
- The sale price is undisclosed and the Q3 2023 EPS hit was −$0.19.
- The full loss can't be computed from the corpus: marks on the loan book flowed through "selected items".

**Apple Card, the exit arithmetic** (L25 reconciliation table, $m):

| Line | Impact |
|---|---|
| Net revenues | −2,258 |
| Provision for credit losses | **−2,481** (a release) |
| Operating expenses | +38 |
| Pre-tax earnings | +185 |
| Taxes | +40 |
| EPS / ROE | **+$0.45 / +10bp** (release: +$0.46) |

The exit *added* to 2025 earnings. The reserves Goldman had built (under lifetime-loss accounting; context, not from the corpus) exceeded the markdown on selling the book. That cost had already been paid, in the provision lines of 2019–25.

## 4. The Apple Card origin: why a bank with "no legacy business" won

From *Exchanges* Ep. 138 (recorded Sep 23 2019), Solomon to Jake Siewert:
- **Apple's motive:** "They did not want to jump inside the financial moat, the financial regulatory moat." Apple wanted the card, the phone experience and the customer, without the charter, capital or supervision.
- **Goldman's edge:** "we had a very nascent consumer business that was really digitally oriented, but we had no legacy business". It had greater flexibility "because we weren't married to an existing platform or existing tools", so it could "take out a white piece of paper". Second, a relationship with Apple of "25-plus years".
- **The framing at the time:** for individuals there was "a very, very open field, because we've had very, very little of a consumer focus".

**Read it backwards** *(inference)*: each advantage became a liability.
- **"No legacy business"** also meant no installed card-servicing scale, no collections machinery and no loss history to price from.
- **Apple's wish to stay outside the moat** meant Goldman carried all the regulated, capital-consuming parts: credit risk, CECL reserves, consumer compliance and stress-test losses. Apple kept the customer and the brand.
- **A blank sheet of paper** lets the partner design the product, which includes the terms Goldman later wanted to "adjust".

The moat Apple wouldn't jump into is the one Goldman's stress capital was paying for. When the business moved, it went to Chase, the incumbent with the legacy business. In the deal Chase gains a >$20bn book and the Apple relationship; JPMorgan's side is for [[jpmc-hub]].

## 5. People: who built it, who unwound it

- **Stephen Scherr** (CFO 2018–21) presented the TxB + consumer ROE bridge at ID20 ("$1bn of pre-tax loss including reserve build"). He retired in Sep 2021, a day before the GreenSky announcement, and was replaced by **Denis Coleman**, a leveraged-finance banker. The release credits Coleman's "strong foundation across capital markets and risk management". By 2023 Coleman's financial roadmap modelled the Platform Solutions pre-tax loss *reversing* as a source of ROE (ID23).
- **Stephanie Cohen** went from Chief Strategy Officer, pitching platform-as-a-service at ID20 ("Simplify the complex services we provide to clients into component pieces"), to Global Head of Platform Solutions presenting the path to breakeven in 2023. Her ID23 executive summary: "Manage through a complicated credit environment".
- **David Solomon** narrates every stage himself (§6). **John Waldron** in 2019 described the new businesses on a J-curve. In 2025 he never mentions consumer: durability is "more management fees. More financing revenues".
- **Marc Nachmann** (AWM) sells PFM as focus on "premier ultra-high net worth wealth management". **Alan Waxman** (Sixth Street) buys GreenSky to "continue the company's legacy of driving growth through enhanced technology and great user experiences". The specialty-finance owner sees value where the G-SIB did not.

## 6. How the narrative was managed

**Mentions in the shareholder letters** (case-insensitive word counts, from `articles.jsonl`):

| Letter | Marcus | Apple | GreenSky | "consumer" | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2019 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 6 | "digital consumer bank of the future"; $125bn / $20bn |
| FY2020 | 3 | 1 | 0 | 12 | partnerships; $97bn deposits; GM card |
| FY2021 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 9 | 10m customers; checking in 2022; >$4bn target |
| FY2022 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 6 | **the one sentence**: "In 2022, we decided to significantly narrow our ambitions for our consumer strategy." |
| FY2023 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 (both macro) | "swift, decisive action to refocus the firm's strategy"; "streamlined strategy" |
| FY2024 | 1 (a footnote defining AUS) | 0 | 0 | 0 | silence |
| FY2025 | 1 (same footnote) | 7 (all in the footnote and reconciliation) | 0 | 1 ("post-consumer recycled paper") | Apple exit as footnote 4: "transition the Apple Card program to another issuer" |

**The boilerplate edit** (every corpus press release's "About Goldman Sachs" paragraph):
- **To Dec 18 2020:** "a leading global investment banking, securities and investment management firm".
- **From Jun 21 2021 (first seen):** "delivers a broad range of financial services across investment banking, securities, investment management and consumer banking".
- **Last seen Feb 21 2023; replaced by Apr 17 2023:** "delivers a broad range of financial services to a large and diversified client base". The phrase was cut **within seven weeks of Investor Day 2023**, before any of the five exits were announced. *(The DIGEST and reader notes date the change to the Oct 2023 GreenSky release; the corpus shows it earlier.)*

**Other techniques visible in the corpus:**
1. **Losses as "selected items related to the execution of strategic goals as outlined at Investor Day".** Each write-down is presented as progress on a plan, not as a loss on a bet (Q2 and Q3 2023 releases).
2. **Credit for decisiveness.** "Our consumer ambitions we narrowed materially, and then we went and we executed on those decisions very effectively." Also: "it's important for companies to be nimble", and, if things aren't working, to adjust (Jan 2024).
3. **Ranking the drag below something else.** In Feb 2023 Solomon listed consumer provisions as the drag that "gets a lot of attention", then added: "The biggest drag was actually the asset management performance of that balance sheet". This is true by the numbers (a ~$10bn revenue swing), and it also moves the spotlight.
4. **Renaming before retreating.** "Consumer & Wealth Management" (2020) → "Consumer platforms" inside "Platform Solutions" (2022). Partnerships rather than a bank. The 2022 letter calls them "a smaller set of emerging businesses".
5. **The 2026 retrospective.** The four 2020 growth avenues were executed, "Other than the pivot on digital consumer banking where we decided it just wasn't the right path for us at this time". Seven years of build reduced to one subordinate clause.

## 7. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Executed on those decisions very effectively"** describes the *exit*, not the *entry*. The corpus never totals the cost of entering. Reserve builds sat inside provisions, GreenSky marks inside selected items, and the build-phase operating losses (189% efficiency) are never disclosed as a sum.
2. **"+$0.46 EPS"** is accounting, not value creation. It reverses reserves charged in earlier years. The economic loss is the cumulative provisions plus the markdown, minus the interest earned over 2019–25. That figure is absent.
3. **"Narrowing" implies a strategy that got smaller.** The record shows one that was abandoned: every lending and card asset was sold. What remains (deposits, TxB) was absorbed into the core segments.
4. **"No legacy business" was presented as an advantage** in 2019 without the other side: it meant no scale, no data history and no servicing. Nothing in the corpus revisits that claim.
5. **Partner economics are unstated.** 'Adjusted contract terms' (from 2H23) for card partnerships suggests the original Apple and GM terms were unprofitable for Goldman *(inference)*. The terms themselves never appear.

## 8. Strategic read and lessons for diversification (inference)

- **Diversify into businesses that use your scarce asset, not your brand.** Goldman's scarce assets are risk judgment on complex, concentrated exposures and access to institutions and the ultra-wealthy. Mass-market card lending needs the opposite: actuarial underwriting of millions of small credits, low-cost servicing, collections and marketing scale. The diversifiers that worked (FICC/Equities financing, alternatives, the private bank; see [[goldman-strategy-financials]]) all use the scarce asset.
- **A partner that avoids the moat hands you its costs.** Embedded-finance deals (Apple, GM, Walmart, Amazon) give the brand owner the customer and the bank the balance sheet and the supervision. The bank needs pricing power over those terms. Goldman had a partner with more bargaining power than itself.
- **Count the capital cost from day one.** A G-SIB's consumer losses flow straight into the stress test, so the SCB effectively prices every consumer dollar at a stressed loss rate. The SCB fell from 6.2–6.4% in 2024 to 3.4% in 2025 as consumer and principal investments ran off. Part of the exit's value lies in that lower buffer, which a standalone consumer P&L never shows.
- **Buying distribution to save time costs most when the thesis is weakest.** GreenSky was bought to save "7-10 years" of merchant-network building, at a 2021 fintech valuation, eleven months before the narrowing. Reversibility was low: an all-stock deal, a new Partner, a public "platform of the future". It was sold about 18 months after closing.
- **Separate the bet from the people.** The architect retired the day before the GreenSky deal. The unwind was led by a CFO from the financing side and a strategist reassigned to run the segment. Accountability came through successors, and the public record never names a decision as wrong *(inference)*.
- **Dated, small, reversible steps make a better exit than a single announcement.** Five sales over three years let each one book a selected item without a single headline write-off, and without forcing buyers to take everything at once. The Apple step waited for a buyer with scale. That is good exit execution, even for a bet that should have been smaller to begin with.

## 9. Canon lenses

- **Kill criteria and quitting** ([[canon-duke-thinking-in-bets]]): no public kill criteria were set in 2020. The breakeven target arrived only in 2023, after the decision to narrow. Duke's question is what signal, set in advance, would have triggered the exit in 2021 rather than 2022.
- **Escalation and sunk cost** ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]): GreenSky (Sep 2021) was an escalation, bought after five years of losses to accelerate the same thesis.
- **Power and right to win** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]], [[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): no scale economies, no switching costs, a supplier (Apple) with power over terms, and incumbents (JPMorgan, Capital One, Amex) with all three advantages.
- **Disruption read the wrong way round** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): Goldman entered as a digital newcomer against incumbents, but with an incumbent's cost of capital and regulation. A cloud-native stack is not a disruptive business model when unit economics are set by credit losses.
- **Good strategy** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): "the digital consumer bank of the future" is an aspiration without a guiding policy on *why Goldman wins*.
- **Change management** ([[canon-kotter-leading-change]]): the reversal ran through structure (the 2022 resegmentation), people (the CFO change) and language (the boilerplate edit), before the asset sales. That is classic sequencing, done quietly.
- **Thinking in time** ([[canon-neustadt-may-thinking-in-time]]): the 2016 decision needed a base rate, namely how earlier investment-bank moves into mass retail had fared. The corpus offers none; that history has to come from outside sources.

## 10. Practitioner counterpoint

- **Bull view (management):** the build delivered a $100bn-plus deposit franchise that Goldman kept as low-cost funding. It also proved Goldman could launch digital products, and it was exited without a single crippling loss: Apple even produced a gain. The core businesses compounded while consumer shrank, and ROE reached 15% in 2025 and ~21% in 9M 2026.
- **Skeptic's view:** six years and several billion dollars of provisions, an impaired $2.24bn acquisition and a damaged reputation for strategic judgment, to end up with an online savings account. The narrative never admits the bet was wrong, only that it was "narrowed". The same management team now asks investors to trust its "fulcrum" bet on private credit.
- **Synthesis:** the exit was competently run. The entry was a category error, putting a bank built for concentrated institutional risk into diffuse retail credit. The deposit funding the bet created is the one durable residue.
