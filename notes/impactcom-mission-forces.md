# Note · impact.com's mission and the forces on it — eight identity labels in sixteen years, one constant rule ("not another affiliate network"), and a market that rewarded the rule until the rule's target became its biggest partner

> Strategy / environment-scan note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy: five forces, SaaS vs network, coopetition), **Course 05** (Marketing & Brand: category creation and naming), **Course 10** (Entrepreneurship & Venture: founding thesis vs pivots), **E5** (Transformation: tag-and-tracking suite → partnership platform → commerce infrastructure), **E6** (Crisis Leadership: COVID 2020, the 2022 reset, SVB 2023), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation: cookie loss, AI search, agentic commerce) · Written 2026-11-01 · Part of [[impactcom-hub]]; companions [[impactcom-strategy-financials]], [[impactcom-people]], [[impactcom-outside-voices]]; mirror of [[bloomberg-mission-forces]] and [[tradeweb-mission-forces]]

It publishes no revenue, margin, retention or audited numbers, so every scale figure here is self-reported. Funding, ARR and KPI tables live in [[impactcom-strategy-financials]]; the people in [[impactcom-people]].
- **Core claim:** the label changed eight times, but the founding rule never did. In Jan 2008 the founders agreed on a whiteboard that they were "not building another affiliate network" (Crawford, 2021). Every later identity is a new answer to *what do we sell instead of a network's cut*: a directory (2010), a tag-and-tracking suite (2012), attribution (2014), a fraud-proof 'system of record' (2016), 'Partnership Automation' (2019), a 'partnership management platform' (2021), a 'commerce partnership marketing platform' (2025) and, from May 2026, "the global infrastructure for partnership-driven commerce". Judged by actions, it optimises for **owning the contract, tracking and payout record of every partnership**, whoever finds the partner or sells the service. That is why it could end up as the rails under Rakuten, the network it was founded to replace.
  - **Tailwinds** come from the decay of paid media: ad distrust, cookie and device-ID loss, rising acquisition costs, the creator economy and B2B partner programs. Each is a reason to pay partners only for results.
  - **Threats** come from whoever owns the demand: Apple, Google and Meta set the tracking rules, Shopify, YouTube and retail media own the shelf, and AI assistants may become the 'publisher' that sets its own affiliate terms. The anti-network argument also softened as the company drifted toward a network's price shape and alliances.

### 1b. How the identity moved

1. **2010–11: open marketplace.** A multi-channel directory for TV, radio, print and online performance deals, with 'Radius Pricing'. Crawford later admits the offline bet failed: "It was not really getting traction." (2021).
2. **2012–15: the measurement vendor.** A SaaS tag, tracking and attribution suite sold on 'unbiased data'. Networks were the enemy: "the inherent biases and conflicts of interest within the traditional affiliate network providers" (2014-09-16).
3. **2016–18: the fraud-proof system of record.** Forensiq (2016) and the three-brand 2018 rebrand (Forensiq, Altitude, Radius) sold *confidence in paid media spend*. Both brand names disappeared within two years.
4. **2019–24: the category creator.** The $75M PSG round, a commissioned Forrester study and the 'Partnership Automation' label arrived in the same quarter (Mar–Jun 2019). The CEO's book *The Partnership Economy* followed (Feb 2022). Partnerships became a 'third' growth channel beside sales and marketing.
5. **2025: back toward commerce and marketing.** 'Commerce' (Dec 2024), then 'marketing platform' (Aug 2025), Retail Media Solution (Dec 2025). The company now sells in the advertiser's budget language, not a new category's.
6. **2026: infrastructure.** The word changed fifteen days after the Rakuten alliance (Apr 28 → May 13). *Inference:* once a network runs on your rails, 'infrastructure' is a more accurate word than 'alternative'.

### 1c. The founders' rule: 'not another network'

- **Origin.** Crawford (Jan 2021 podcast): "the industry did not need another affiliate network" and "All you’re doing when you create another affiliate network is just going after the same pie." The 2016 blog: "Our founders did not want to build another network, they wanted to disrupt the affiliate market and deliver unbiased data marketers can trust."
- **What it meant in practice.** Software, not services. Crawford: "we’ve had clients that would not want business because we won’t provide service". The price was a fee, not a take: "instead of paying the 20-30% commissions common with major networks" (2013). Agencies supplied the service layer (Agency Advisory Board 2019; tiered Agency Partner Program 2022 and 2025).
- **The cost the founders admitted.** "we don’t have a hand in the success of the affiliate program directly" (Crawford, 2021). SaaS income follows the licence, not the result.
- **Where the rule bent.** The same 2016 post concedes "we’re listed on several “Best Affiliate Network” lists" and has a marketplace of media partners. By 2024 the company advertised being "ranked the number one Affiliate network by the Blue Book". On the 2026 B2B rate card (per Rework, outside) a 2.5% transaction fee sits on top of the subscription. That is a network's *price shape*, though not its 20–30% *level* (see Rep).

## 4. Five forces for partnership-management software, 2026

Pressure score: 1 = benign, 5 = severe (judgement). Arrows show the change since about 2019.

| Force | Score | Trend | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rivalry** | 4 | ↑ | Enterprise peers (Partnerize, PartnerStack, Everflow) quote privately. Networks bundle software with services. Cheap tools (Tapfiliate, Refersion, Rewardful) undercut the low end, which pushed impact.com to publish a $30 Starter tier. Consolidation: Partnerize + Pepperjam (2020), Awin + ShareASale (2025), impact.com + Rakuten (2026) |
| **Buyer power (brands, agencies)** | 4 | ↑ | Multi-platform programs doubled to ~20% (PMA). Agencies drive "as much as 50% of partnership activity" (impact.com, 2022) and pick the platform. Migration is the sales motion, so it also works in reverse |
| **Supplier power (data and demand gatekeepers)** | 5 | ↑ | Apple (ITP, ATT), Google (Chrome, Search, Pay), Meta and Instagram, YouTube, Shopify, Amazon. They set tracking rules and own creator data. impact.com is an early adopter of their APIs, never the rule-setter |
| **Supplier power (publishers, creators)** | 2 | → | Fragmented and free to join. Concentration inside programs is high ("5% of affiliates drive 95% of the channel revenues", 2014). A dominant publisher such as Honey can be removed |
| **Substitutes** | 4 | ↑ | In-house builds, native platform affiliate tools (context: Shopify Collabs, Amazon's creator programs), retail media, and AI assistants with their own offer and checkout layers. Each brings partners without a partnership platform |
| **Threat of entry** | 3 | → | Basic tracking is cheap to build. Payments in 60–70 currencies, tax forms, fraud history, 20 years of contracts and integrations are not. AI agents lower the cost of partner recruiting for everyone, including entrants |
| **Complementors** | — | ↑ | Agencies (Acceleration Partners, Gen3, DMi, LT Partners), commerce platforms, CLO firms (Fidel, Cardlytics), GEO (Evertune), and now Rakuten. Coopetition is the default ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]) |

**Read:** the industry structure is mediocre for a pure software vendor. Buyers multi-home, suppliers of data are giants, and substitutes multiply. The defensible piece is the **payments-and-contract ledger**: brands rarely move years of partner contracts and payout history. That is why the company drifted from 'software' to 'infrastructure'.

## 7. Scenarios to 2030 (inference, not impact.com's)

Rough weights are judgement, to be revised as signals arrive.

1. **The rails of partnership commerce (base case, ~40%).** The Rakuten template spreads: more networks and agencies license contracting, tracking and payouts and keep the service and publisher relationship. impact.com becomes the back office of the channel, and growth tracks payout volume. *Signal:* a second network alliance; GTV disclosed again and growing faster than brand count; the advertiser contract stays with impact.com.
2. **Agent-commerce toll booth (~20%).** AI assistants become the main discovery layer. impact.com's contract and payout ledger becomes how agents get paid for referrals (MCP connector, AI Search Visibility, Evertune, Minty-style offer syndication). *Signal:* an assistant or agent platform announces affiliate payouts through impact.com; 'partner-referred GMV' from AI sources broken out.
3. **Squeezed by the demand owners (~25%).** Shopify, Amazon, YouTube, retail-media networks and AI checkouts offer native affiliate and creator payouts. impact.com is pushed to mid-market software with a network-like price. *Signal:* falling ARR per brand, more free bundling, more Starter-tier marketing, partnership counts quoted as 'total' rather than 'active'.
4. **Exit reshapes the map (~15%).** An IPO, a sale to a strategic (Rakuten, an ad-tech or marketing-cloud firm) or a sponsor take-private. *Signal:* audited numbers, an S-1, or Rakuten equity.

## What the claims don't prove

1. **Labels are not strategy.** Eight identities in sixteen years may be one strategy described eight ways, or eight resets. The product line history (two brands retired by 2020) points both ways.
2. **The tailwind statistics are mostly unsourced or reused**: 69% distrust, 222% CAC, $480B creator economy, 28% of revenue from partnerships.
3. **'Partner-referred' is not 'incremental'.** No independent incrementality test of impact.com programs appears in the corpus. The one ROI study (314%) is a commissioned composite of five customers.
4. **'More than our closest competitors combined'** (payouts and referred revenue, Dec 2025) names no competitor and gives no number.
5. **The Rakuten alliance has no disclosed terms**: no revenue share, equity, exclusivity or migration path (PPC Land).

## Strategic read (inference — not stated by impact.com)

- **One rule, many labels.** The constant is the founders' refusal to take a network's cut. Each label was the best available story for why a brand should pay a fee instead: data (2012), confidence (2016), a new category (2019), commerce and AI (2025–26).
- **The category play worked, then became a liability.** 'Partnership economy' won attention and capital in 2019–21. As growth slowed, the company moved back into budgets buyers already have: 'marketing', 'retail media', 'performance'. It is the [[canon-thiel-zero-to-one]] path in reverse: claim a new market to raise money, then rejoin the old one to sell.
- **Disruption ended in absorption.** In [[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]] terms the networks could not match a ~5% take without wrecking a 20–30% override. The networks' answer was not to die but to rent the disruptor's rails and keep the service margin.
- **The real moat is the ledger, and AI is a test of it.** If AI agents start finding, contracting and paying partners, whoever holds the contract and payout record is the default settlement layer. That is why the company says 'infrastructure', not 'platform'.

## Canon lenses

- [[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]] — Five forces explain why a software vendor shades into infrastructure: buyers multi-home, suppliers of data are giants, so the defensible position is the payments ledger.
- [[canon-hagiu-wright-multi-sided-platforms]] / [[canon-parker-platform-revolution]] — Free publishers, paying brands, and agencies as a subsidised channel. 'Not a network' is a claim about *who pays*, not about whether it is multi-sided.
- [[canon-helmer-7-powers]] — *Switching costs* (contracts, payout history, tax data) and *counter-positioning* (a network cannot copy a fee model without cannibalising its override).
- [[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]] — A low-price model that incumbents could not copy, ending in an alliance rather than an exit.
- [[canon-akerlof-market-for-lemons]] — Affiliate fraud, coupon leakage and attribution games are a lemons problem. Every identity since 2013 sells some form of quality certification.
- [[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]] — Versioning (Starter → Enterprise), then bundling (Creator and Advocate free), then standards (MCP, YouTube API).
- [[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]] — Rakuten, agencies and Trackonomics' cross-network reporting: grow the pie with rivals, then fight over the ledger.

## Practitioner counterpoint

- **For impact.com:** it kept one coherent economic idea for sixteen years, adapted the story to each wave (cookies, creators, B2B, AI), and turned its main rival into a customer of its rails without raising primary money since 2021.
- **Against:** the label churn looks like a company still searching for a category buyers recognise. Its tailwinds rest on unsourced statistics. Its moat depends on rules written by Apple and Google. And the 'not a network' story now runs through a network and a transaction fee.

Compute impact.com's bill if the fee applies (i) to payouts, (ii) to sales. *(Answer: (i) $30k + 2.5% × $420k = $30k + $10.5k = **$40.5k**, ~39% of the network's take.
