- **Core claim:** ICE is the opposite of Tradeweb's choreographed handover. One founder has been Chair and CEO for 26 years and wrote in Dec 2021, “I’m not going anywhere, but for many years, we’ve been working to develop the next generation of ICE leaders” — and no successor is named anywhere in the corpus through Oct 2026. Development happens by **rotation, not by heir-designation**: a CDS-clearing builder (Edmonds) runs Fixed Income & Data; the LIBOR administrator (Bowler) runs mortgage technology; the IR head (Gardiner) becomes CFO; the NYSE COO (Blaugrund) becomes the tokenization lead; the FIDS president (Martin) runs the NYSE. Ben Jackson stays President throughout, so the obvious No. 2 is a fixture rather than an heir. The connective tissue is a **founder's script** that every level repeats — "analog to digital," "all-weather," "connect people to opportunity," "neutral" — which is how a centralized, founder-led firm keeps a 10,000-person, five-segment company sounding like one company. Where leaders differ, it is on *timing and politics* (crypto, ESG, cloud, Texas), not on the playbook.

**What the chart says.**
- **No heir, by design (inference).** Every top seat except CFO and COO changed hands in 2019–26; the CEO and President seats did not. The Dec 2021 reshuffle is said to “draw entirely from deep and diverse talent within the organization” (release text) and is framed as "good governance," not succession.
- **Rotation is the development system.** Edmonds: clearing → sales → CDO (with marketing) → FIDS. Bowler: IBA → clearing → mortgage. de Ruig: futures COO → IBA. Martin: FIDS → NYSE (still FIDS Chair). Blaugrund: NYSE COO → ICE strategic initiatives. Bland: one exchange → all exchanges. Bland's own line: “ICE has always benefitted from creating and cultivating a deep bench of talent across our global operations” (2022-06-29).
- **Gravity pulls to Atlanta.** Edmonds moved from Chicago because the FIDS president should “sit in Atlanta with the rest of senior leadership” (profile, Apr 2024, in-text); Hindlian left because Hindlian “has elected to remain rooted in the New York area” (2023-10-26). Location is a selection criterion.
- **Outside hires come in for regulation, not for running a segment:** Klima (CFTC), Serafini (JPM/Goldman risk), Bowen and Lord Hill (board). The acquired-company heads (Tyrrell from Ellie Mae; Black Knight's Grey and Walden) stay a level down or move on.
- **Subsidiary boards feed the parent board.** Silver also joined ICE Clear Europe's board; Mulhern sat on Ellie Mae's; Tirinnanzi came from the IMT and MERS boards; Lord Hill from ICE Endex. Directors are pre-vetted inside the group.

## 2. Profiles

### Jeffrey Sprecher — founder (2000), Chair & CEO
**Background:** bought a failing Atlanta power-trading firm (Continental Power Exchange); not from Wall Street: “I suspect I'm the only chairman of the New York Stock Exchange that never has traded, never has worked at a bank” (2018-04-03). **Talks about:** the playbook, deal discipline, neutrality, crises as launch windows, culture as productive conflict.
- **Deal doctrine.** “I've never been the low bidder.” Customers as owners: “I gave away, actually, 80% of the equity to the customers in exchange for a commitment that they would try it and use it.” (both 2018-04-03).
- **Neutral operator.** “for an exchange operator, you're neutral party. You're Switzerland.” And patience: “The market really will resist the revolution.” (Abu Dhabi, 2019-11-27).
- **Identity.** “I would probably call it the Intercontinental Massively Scalable Network and Database Company because that's what we are.” “In my mind I really am not a public company CEO, I'm a small business entrepreneur” (both 2020-05-08).
- **Crises as windows.** Defending the mortgage bets into the rate trough: “it's easier to start a new initiative and have market acceptance of your initiative during periods of stress.” Treasury clearing as Geithner's barn redux: “An even bigger barn.” (Sprecher repeating King, 2024-08-12).
- **Culture.** “That's a culture that I guess the tone starts at the top with me to a certain degree, but it's the only tone I know” (2024-08-12). Sprecher credits an organizational-behavior class: good companies have internal conflict and good conflict resolution.
- **Succession and the bench.** The only document is the 2021 letter: “it’s more important than ever to challenge our leaders to take on new tasks and acquire new skills” and “Challenging this group of global leaders with new or expanded roles is fundamental to good governance and stewardship of our firm.” Sprecher gave up the NYSE chair with the self-description “the least qualified person to ever have that title in a 200+ year history” (2024-08-12).
- **Optimistic about** millennial homebuying, Treasury clearing, AI, prediction markets. **Self-deprecating about** the brand: people “don't know whether it's ice cubes or whether it's the immigration service or whatever” (2024-08-12).

### Ben Jackson — President (throughout)
**Talks about:** fixed income data-first, M&A integration, culture imprinting. The 2018 podcast is the corpus's most explicit FI strategy: “many, many different service providers have tried to start with just building an execution platform and have failed.” Jackson names the RFQ incumbents once — “those being MarketAxess Bloomberg and Tradeweb ... tend to be RFQ-driven.” — and concedes “In fixed income, it's early days.” (2018-10-25). On acquisitions: “we've prided ourselves on putting our imprint on each individual company that we acquire” and acquired firms run as “its own little fiefdom” until ICE flattens them. Later voice is operational: “Margins have been compressed to historic lows” (mortgage lenders, 2024-05-15); “The systems we run are the backbone of global financial markets.” (2026-06-03, Anthropic Project Glasswing). *Read:* Jackson owns deals end-to-end (17 items in corpus) yet never speaks about succession or the President's own role. Eight years after "early days," ICE bought MarketAxess ([[ice-marketaxess-deal]]).

### Lynn Martin — FIDS President → NYSE Group President (2022–), FIDS Chair
**Talks about:** data as input and output, humans plus machines, the floor, listings politics. The most-mentioned executive after Sprecher (63 items). “data is the most important output of fully-transparent liquid markets, but then it's a really important input to finding fair value.” Pricing 2.8M bonds: “we also do it with 200 humans.” Candid about growth: “what got us the growth rates we had for the last six and a half years is not what's going to drive our growth” (all 2022-03-08).
- **Shift (the sharpest at ICE).** 2022: “ESG is a primary focus of the leaders I speak with” (2022-03-09). 2025: NYSE Texas opens with Trump Media as first listing — “We are thrilled to open NYSE Texas to corporate issuers and to welcome Trump Media & Technology Group to our NYSE community” (2025-03-31); credit to “the pro-business environment fostered by Governor Abbott in the Lone Star State” (2025-12-01). 2026, in the Trump–Xi delegation: “President Trump has made American AI leadership an explicit national priority.” But the same speech insists “women need seats at the table before the frameworks are finalized” (2026-05-15) — a pro-administration frame with the 2022 inclusion agenda kept inside it.

### Chris Edmonds — CDS clearing builder → Clearing & Risk → CDO → FIDS President (2024–)
**Background:** OTC gas broker at ICAP from 1997, "competitors" with Sprecher; joined 2010 to run ICE Clear Credit; Alabama-born, Chicago-based 13 years. Sprecher calls Edmonds “distinguished as one of ICE’s most skillful operators” (2023-10-26). **Talks about:** clearing discipline, regulatory catalysts, data in the AI era, the client relationship.
- **Clearing philosophy (2022-06-27, vs FTX).** “What we're doing is refereeing not the value, but the performance.” The brief from the founder: “Jeff, I heard don't let the clearing houses blow up and make sure everyone understands our message.” Humility as house style: “We don't know everything. We're not the smartest people in the room”.
- **Speaks the script.** “this company is about transitioning from analog to digital.” (2022); “You’ve heard our CEO talk about the analog to digital transformation” (Apr 2024); “indispensable in connecting people to opportunity” (2023-10-26).
- **Identity claim.** “We’re not an exchange group or a venue that is needing to buy technology—we create technology” (Apr 2024) — written two years before ICE bought MarketAxess's technology.
- **Broker's relationship ethic.** “I need to know their kids’ names.” (Apr 2024).
- **Candour (2026-01-14).** Disputes the interviewer's e-trading share: “I might challenge you on that statistic.” Admits the mandate drove entry: “I don't believe without the regulatory catalyst that we would have made the decision to enter that.” On the mortgage roll-up: “Not sure that everyone understood why or when we were doing it. Jeff had a great vision of what that would look like.” Shift: in 2024 Treasury clearing was “maybe a worst kept secret.” (clip); in 2026 it is mandate-dependent.

## 4. Communication style and narrative craft

**Sprecher — the folksy contrarian.** Short declaratives ("I've never been the low bidder"), self-deprecation (least qualified NYSE chair, rejected by the All-In Summit while Sprecher's spouse was accepted — Sprecher's own anecdote), and parables (Geithner's barn, Enron "may have stolen the idea," the $1 or $1,000 purchase). Sprecher refuses forward guidance on air. *Contrast with Tradeweb's Hult:* Hult narrates an era; Sprecher narrates a *pattern*.

**The house script, with first sightings and diffusion:**
- *analog to digital* — 2019 letter ("moving analog to digital"); Ellie Mae release headline (2020-08-06); 2020–2024 letters; Tyrrell (6× in one 2022 episode), Edmonds (2022, 2024), Blaugrund (2026). Edmonds attributes it: "You've heard our CEO."
- *all-weather* — first in the Dec 2021 staff letter ("an all-weather performer"); 2022 letter 5×; every earnings release from 2Q22; Gardiner (2022), Bowler, Grey, Pawar, Lord Hill. Pawar's line makes the source explicit: town halls and investor calls.

## 5. Culture and talent, as the leaders describe it

- **Founder origin as identity.** Two founders calling on 106–108 firms; "ragtag" Atlanta engineers; day-one prices "inverted" (Wassersug). Sprecher on the people bond: “It was like being in the military together” (2024-08-12).

- **Generalists over specialists.** The rotation table above *is* the talent model: a broker runs data, a benchmark lawyer-regulator type runs mortgage, a GC runs clearing. Bowen's word for the result is "founder led"; *inference:* the CEO's judgment of people substitutes for a formal pipeline.

- **Office and place.** ICE is at four days a week (King, 2024); FIDS leadership must sit in Atlanta (2023–24).

## 6. Leadership lenses

- [[canon-schein-organizational-culture]] — **Artifacts:** the podcast, the ad tagline, the Atlanta HQ, the NYSE floor. **Espoused values:** neutrality, transparency, "all-weather." **Basic assumption:** the founder's pattern-recognition is the strategy; leaders are interchangeable carriers of it. That assumption explains why roles rotate and the script repeats.
- [[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]] — a 26-year founder-CEO-Chair with a board refreshed by that CEO (three founding-era directors out in 2022; new directors from subsidiary boards) concentrates control; the eBay retreat (2020) shows shareholders are the binding check, not the board ([[ice-outside-voices]]).
- [[canon-pfeffer-power]] — power sits with Sprecher and Jackson (deal + integration); segment heads are powerful only while they hold the seat. Location (Atlanta) is a power resource.
- [[canon-kotter-leading-change]] — contrast with Tradeweb's ten-month guided handover; ICE has no guiding coalition for succession, only for each deal (integration team owns the deal).
- [[canon-grove-high-output-management]] — Wassersug's standardization (“scale exponentially without having to scale our staff exponentially”, 2023-01-03) is Grove's leverage argument; the plea to pause deals is the managerial-capacity constraint made public.
- [[canon-horowitz-what-you-do-is-who-you-are]] — the diversity test: the board went to six of ten seats held by women while the CEO disavowed "box ticking." What ICE did is the board; what it says shifted.
- [[canon-tetlock-superforecasting]] — Bowler's "never a good idea to predict" and Sprecher's no-guidance stance versus a mortgage segment "spring-loaded" for falling rates: the forecast is embedded in capital allocation, not in words.
