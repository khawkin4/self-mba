# Note · Goldman Sachs mission & forces — the purpose statement followed the strategy: societal while Goldman diversified, "exceptional" and 1979 heritage once it retreated to banking and asset management. The force that binds hardest is the regulator's capital rule, and the dealer's moat moved from flow to financing

> Strategy / environment-scan note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy: five forces from a dealer's seat: venues, non-bank liquidity, private credit, Big Tech, regulators), **Course 11** (Global Strategy, Macro & Responsible Business: the rise and fade of sustainability and D&I language), **E6** (Crisis Leadership & Resilience: COVID, 2022, SVB, tariffs, the 2026 private-credit stress), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro: tariffs, Iran, US–China, fiscal deficits), **E12** (Culture as Strategy: four core values, the Business Principles, 1MDB) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[goldman-hub]] and [[goldman-strategy-financials]]; counterpart to [[tradeweb-mission-forces]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/goldman/articles.jsonl` (262 items, 2019–Oct 2026). I read all **7 shareholder letters** (FY2019–FY2025; each dated to fiscal year-end but published ~Mar–Apr of the next year), both **Investor Day** sets (2020 consolidated; 2023, eight decks), the **32 earnings releases**, the forward-looking statements in the IR stubs, and the leader podcasts. I pulled the **"About Goldman Sachs" boilerplate from all 38 releases** that carry it and counted theme terms in each letter by script *(counts derived)*. The **Purpose & Values web page scraped empty**, so mission language comes from letters, decks, releases and podcasts. No 10-K, proxy or earnings-call Q&A was used. Everything is Goldman's own channel: a record of what the firm *wants said*, read here for what it reveals and omits. Financial series come from the releases via reader notes A and B and are marked *(derived)* where I computed them.
- **Core claim:** Goldman's stated purpose changed **four times in seven years, and each change tracked the strategy**. A societal mission ("advance sustainable economic growth and financial opportunity", FY2019–20) arrived with the push to diversify into consumer and transaction banking. It vanished in FY2021 behind a returns slogan. In FY2022 it became an aspiration to be "the world’s most exceptional financial institution", just as Solomon narrowed consumer. By FY2025 the values were anchored in **John Whitehead's 1979 Business Principles**. The legal boilerplate followed the same path: "and consumer banking" entered in mid-2021 and was gone by Apr 17 2023, seven weeks after Investor Day 2023. Climate, inclusion and the One Million Black Women program fell from 13, 3 and 12 mentions to **zero** in the FY2023–25 letters *(derived)*, with nothing formally retracted. What Goldman actually optimises for is **ROE against public targets, client wallet-share rank, and "durable" revenue at low capital intensity**. Its tailwinds are volatility, the financing book and the AWM fee build. Its binding constraint is the **Fed's stress capital buffer** (6.7% → 3.4%). From a dealer's seat the forces are mixed: **venues** compress spreads, but Goldman moved its growth into balance-sheet financing that venues can't touch. **Non-bank market makers** and **private-credit funds** are the rivals that matter, and both are also its clients. **Big Tech** set the terms of the consumer bet and supplies its engineers. **Regulators** swung from headwind to tailwind in 2025. **Goldman admits cyclicality, consumer losses, capital rules and AI execution risk. Its channels say almost nothing about rival dealers, venue consolidation, or its conflicts as both originator and investor in private credit.**

| FY2022 letter | Letter, "Our Purpose" | "We aspire to be the world’s most exceptional financial institution, united by our shared values of partnership, client service, integrity and excellence." | **Excellence replaces society.** Same letter: "In 2022, we decided to significantly narrow our ambitions for our consumer strategy." |

**The arc:** 2019–20 *serve society and diversify*; 2021 *produce higher returns*; 2022–23 *be exceptional, no ambiguity*; 2024–25 *values from 1979*. The societal purpose lived exactly as long as the consumer bank did.

**What the boilerplate shows (inference unless noted):**
1. The legal self-description changed **once to add consumer and once to remove it**. The removal came between Feb 21 2023 (last old-style use, the Investor Day notice) and Apr 17 2023 (first new-style use), i.e. right after Investor Day 2023 and alongside the Marcus loan sale, before the GreenSky write-down.
2. **"Financial institution" outlived the consumer bank.** Goldman kept the broader noun after dropping the business that justified it, and stopped listing its businesses at all.
3. The forward-looking statements in IR stubs are a second, finer clock. They list "strategic alternatives for credit card partnerships and the pending sale of GreenSky" (Dec 2023). The phrase "the opportunities presented by AI" first appears in May 2025 (Bernstein conference stub) *(corpus search)*.

### 1c. Values and culture claims

- **Four core values, constant since FY2019:** partnership, client service, integrity, excellence. The letter says Goldman "distilled our Business Principles into 4 core values". The order changes in speech (Solomon, Jan 2026: client service first).
- **The Business Principles, printed every year,** include the line Goldman most needed in 2020: "Our assets are our people, capital and reputation. If any of these is ever diminished, the last is the most difficult to restore."
- **1MDB as the culture test.** Solomon's memo (Oct 22 2020): "Our success as a firm can only be ensured through a culture rooted in integrity, shaped by a long-term mindset". The Board called it "an institutional failure". The fixes were a reputational-risk committee of control-side members "empowered to stop any transaction", and a compliance division that had "nearly doubled in size" since 2012 *(as stated)*. About $174m of compensation was clawed back or cut ([[goldman-one-gs-culture]]).
- **People as the factory.** Waldron (Jun 5 2025): "It's a people firm. It's a human capital driven firm." Solomon (Sep 8 2026): "We are in a period of rapid technological change, but we know our people are still this firm’s greatest asset."
- **One Goldman Sachs** as an operating ethos: a day-one memo (Oct 2018), a repair for a firm that Waldron said had "gotten more Balkanized" (2019), and by 2025 extended to operations. Solomon: "this is a moment for us to expand our One Goldman Sachs ethos to our internal operating model." (FY2025 letter).

- **At the peak** the language was personal: "Advancing diversity and inclusion is a priority of mine" (FY2020). The firm would underwrite US and Western European IPOs only for companies with "at least one diverse board member", raised to two in July 2021. The $750bn ten-year sustainable-finance target (set 2019) was ~55% achieved in three years (FY2022 letter).
- **The last appearance** is a "Sustainable Finance" section in the FY2022 letter. From FY2023 the only D&I sentence left is the Business Principle printed at the back: "our people must reflect the diversity of the communities and cultures in which we operate." FY2024 adds that the firm is "proud of the diversity of the partner class of 2024".
- **The number $750 billion returns in FY2025** with a new meaning: "We expect fee-paying alternative assets under supervision to reach $750 billion by the end of 2030."
- **Reading *(inference)*:** nothing is retracted. The topics simply stop being reported as strategy once the purpose stops being societal. AI fills the vacated space: zero mentions through FY2022, ten in FY2025. ESG and climate *releases* were filtered out of this corpus (DIGEST §1), so this shows a fade in the CEO's own voice, not necessarily in activity.

| Stated | Revealed by action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|

The Management Committee moved to 100% performance-based PSUs (FY2022 letter) | ROE 10.0% (2019) → 23.0% (2021) → 10.2% (2022) → 7.5% (2023) → 15.0% (2025) → 23.5% (Q2 2026) |

**The tension (inference):** the 2020 plan said durability would come from *new* businesses: transaction banking, consumer, wealth for "everyday Americans". ## 4. Five forces from a dealer's seat, 2026

Pressure score: 1 = benign, 5 = severe (judgement). Arrows show change since 2019. GS's channels barely name rivals, so venue and NBLP evidence leans on the Tradeweb study, cited.

| Force | Score | Trend | Evidence | Read *(inference)* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Venues** (Tradeweb, MarketAxess/ICE, CME) | 3 | ↑ | GS's own electronic share of tickets in 2019: ~75% of US credit, ~99.9% of US cash equities (ID20). Portfolio trading "$120bn+ executed since inception" (ID20 slide). GS proposes a TRACE tweak that "could improve execution quality in even the most illiquid bonds" (undated credit-PT article). Griffin: large market makers spend "hundreds of millions of dollars on compute". Jeria on retail flow internalized off-exchange: "non-participable" (Jun 2022). (Jun 8 2026) | **Double-edged.** They take loans dealers once held, *and* they are GBM's financing clients and GSAM's competitors. Capital Solutions is GS's bid to originate for them. | **Big Tech** (Apple, AWS, Google, model labs) | 3 | ↑ | Apple chose GS because "we had no legacy business" (Solomon, Oct 1 2019), then moved the card to Chase (Jan 7 2026). AWS: cloud partner and source of the CIO. Argenti: "those who own the models will own the new operating systems that power AI agents." (Jan 22 2026) | Big Tech holds distribution (Apple) and the platform layer (cloud, models). It took the customer relationship in consumer and could take the interface in institutional workflow. It is also GS's biggest AI-debt issuer client |
| **Regulators** (Fed, SEC, CFTC, EU, PRA) | 3 (was 4) | ↓ since 2025 | SCB 6.7% → 3.4%; Basel III endgame fight; 1MDB. | **Supplier power** (talent, compute, data vendors) | 3 | ↑ | 12,000 engineers; Big Tech hires; MarketView redistributes LSEG, FactSet, MSCI, Axioma and Wolfe data (Nov 2023) | Talent and compute are bid up by the same AI boom GS finances |

**Read:** the forces that compress the *flow* business (venues, NBLPs) are rising. Goldman's response has been to move its growth to where those forces are weak: **balance-sheet financing, alternatives fees and advice**. The forces that matter most for *those* businesses are the **regulator** (capital) and the **private-credit funds** (competitors and clients). That is why the 2024 SCB dispute and the 2026 private-credit stress mattered more to the firm than any venue merger. Contrast Tradeweb, for which the venue mergers are the main threat ([[tradeweb-mission-forces]] §4).

## 5. Crisis tests — what each revealed

| Shock | What Goldman said | Numbers (as stated) | What it revealed *(inference)* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **COVID, Mar–Jun 2020** | Solomon to staff (Mar 1 2020): "We have managed through volatile markets before – it’s core to what we do." Buybacks paused Mar 16; Fed discount window used Mar 17. FY2020 letter on critics who wanted Global Markets shrunk: "wise to stay the course" | Q1 2020 GM return 19.7% vs 11% threshold; Q2 2020 FICC $4.24bn, "its highest quarterly performance in nine years", and Equities $2.94bn (+46%); first SCB 6.7% (Jun 29), cut to 6.6% after the Fed's error; $2.01bn of 1MDB provisions in the same quarter | **Volatility is revenue.** The trading franchise the 2020 plan meant to dilute carried the year. The consumer program offered payment holidays; it was a cost, not a cushion |
| **2022 drawdown** (Ukraine, rates, equity issuance near a two-decade low) | "It was a turbulent quarter dominated by the devastating invasion of Ukraine." (Apr 14 2022). Q4 2022 "an outlier quarter" (Feb 24 2023) | FY2022 ROE 10.2% vs 23.0%; FICC $14.68bn (+38%); ~$10bn negative swing on the AM balance sheet; Russia markdown; realignment announced Oct 18 2022 | **Markets held, the balance sheet and consumer didn't.** The crisis produced the strategic retreat: narrowed consumer, the client-capital AWM model, and the "exceptional" purpose |
| **SVB / regional banks, Mar 2023** | "The events of the first quarter acted as another real-life stress test" (Apr 18 2023). SVB is **never named** in the corpus | Q1 2023 ROE 11.6%; FICC −17% YoY; record Equities financing; Marcus loan sale loss ~$470m booked the same quarter | GS framed itself with "the nation’s largest financial institutions" against the regionals. The consumer unwind accelerated in the same quarter. GS Research later called private credit "a very solid line of defense against the risk of a credit crunch" in that episode (Karoui, Apr 15 2025) |
| **Tariffs, Apr 2025** | "in times of great uncertainty, clients turn to Goldman Sachs for execution and insight" (Apr 14 2025). Waldron: "We have moderated our risk positioning since April 2nd." (Jun 5 2025) | Q1 2025 Equities $4.19bn, a record including record financing; ROE 16.9% (Q1) then 12.8% (Q2); Equities $4.30bn (+36%) in Q2; capital-markets deals paused, then "10 or so IPOs" priced by late May | **Financing plus prime held while the advisory pipeline stalled.** The firm cut risk rather than lean in, consistent with the durability pitch |
| **Private-credit stress, Mar–May 2026** (plus the Iran conflict) | Bantwal: not semiliquid but illiquid; Lynam: limits are "a feature, not a bug"; Brandmeyer: "So, the system is gummed up. The circulatory system is not working." (Jun 8 2026) | Retail redemptions ~10% unannualized in Q1 (Mar 23 2026); alt-manager stocks −30–40% (Blostein); yet Q1 2026 ROE 19.8%, Q2 2026 revenue $20.34bn and ROE 23.5% (records), Equities $7.42bn | **GBM profits from the volatility while AWM carries the stress.** Goldman is long the theme on both sides. The house line (not systemic) held through Q3 2026 (ROE 18.7%, "second highest quarterly net revenues") |

**Pattern:** each shock confirmed that the **trading-and-financing franchise is the shock absorber** and that the businesses meant to diversify away from it (consumer, balance-sheet investing) were the ones that broke. The blind spot: Goldman reports how it *performed* in each crisis, never how a client fared on its platform or its products. That is the outside-voices gap ([[goldman-outside-voices]] §9).

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Purpose as narrative** ([[canon-sinek-start-with-why]]): Goldman's *why* changed four times in seven years. A *why* that follows the *what* is a positioning statement, not a purpose. Test it against the Whitehead principles, which didn't change.
- **Culture** ([[canon-schein-organizational-culture]]): the artifacts (purpose boxes, boilerplate) moved fast; the espoused values (four core values) didn't; the basic assumptions (client first, risk discipline, apprenticeship) show up in crises and in 1MDB's fixes.
- **Industry structure** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): §4. From a dealer's seat, the flow business faces rising rivalry and substitutes. The financing business faces mainly the regulator.
- **Powers** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): GS's durable power is **scale economies in balance sheet and risk** plus a **cornered resource** in senior client relationships. Network effects belong to the venues, not the dealer.
- **Disruption** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): consumer was GS trying to be the disruptor and failing on distribution economics. NBLPs and private credit are disrupting GS from below in intermediation and lending.
- **Antifragile and fragile parts** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): trading gains from disorder; balance-sheet investing and consumer credit were fragile to it. The 2020–25 strategy cut the fragile parts.
- **Luck vs skill** ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]): separate volatility-driven quarters from the financing and fee build before crediting the strategy.
- **Good strategy** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): the Investor Day 2020 plan had a diagnosis (too volatile, too capital-heavy), but its guiding policy (diversify into consumer) was the wrong one. The coherent action that worked was the one it didn't headline: financing.
