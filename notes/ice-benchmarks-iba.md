# Note · ICE Benchmark Administration — from LIBOR's administrator to a builder of replacement rates whose key inputs sit on rivals' platforms

> Company-study / market-structure note · Maps to **Course 07** (Data, Analytics & Decision-Making — waterfall methodologies, fallback logic, representativeness), **Course 03** (Microeconomics & Strategy Foundations — input dependence, focal points), **Course 11** (Global Strategy, Macro & Responsible Business — the LIBOR transition as regulated market design), **E4** (Governance & Board — benchmark administration as a governance product), **E6** (Crisis Leadership — the March 2020 fixing failure), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro — rates regimes, gold) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[ice-hub]]; companions [[ice-fixed-income-data]], [[tradeweb-derivatives-regulation]], [[tradeweb-rates-em]]

- **Source:** ICE corpus at `_ingest/raw/ice/articles.jsonl`. **52 items** read in full: 48 IR press releases from ICE Benchmark Administration (IBA), ICE Data Indices and the FCA-driven LIBOR notices (**Jan 24 2019 – Jul 7 2026**), two Insights pieces ("The LIBOR endgame", dated 2021-12-31 in the scrape but **May 2023 in-text**; the Bassman MOVE interview, **Mar 2023 in-text**), one ICE Vox post (Nigel Topping joining the IBA board, 2023-02-22), and the Wigglesworth podcast (2022-03-14) for the gold-auction segment, whose speaker is not named in the transcript. Facts about the ICE Swap Rate failure of March 2020 come from the Tradeweb study ([[tradeweb-derivatives-regulation]], citing Risk.net) and are labelled as such. ICE's own corpus never describes that failure.
- **Core claim:** IBA spent 2019–2024 running down the benchmark it was famous for. LIBOR went from consultation (Dec 2020) to a "synthetic" zombie compelled by the FCA, and the last setting printed on **30 Sep 2024**. Meanwhile IBA rebuilt itself as a factory of replacement rates: Term SONIA, Term SOFR, the SONIA/SOFR/€STR ICE Swap Rates and their spread-adjusted versions, RFR compounding indexes, and inflation-swap benchmarks. The striking fact is **whose data these rates run on**. Tradeweb's dealer-to-client (D2C) quotes are Level 2 of Term SONIA (2020), the **first** input to ICE Term SOFR (2022), the fallback that saved the ICE Swap Rate in 2020 (per the Tradeweb notes), and the sole named input to the 2026 inflation-swap rates. That is the same Tradeweb whose credit, rates and portfolio-trading businesses ICE competes with, and which in 2024 moved its own U.S. Treasury closing prices from IBA to FTSE Russell ([[tradeweb-rates-em]]). Where IBA owned or controlled the input (the LBMA gold auction it runs, ICE's own SOFR futures as Level 3), it shows robustness and growth. Where it depended on others' willingness (LIBOR panel banks, the Bank Yield Index's bank funding data), the product died or never launched. **A benchmark is only as robust as its least-controlled input.**

## 2. The input map: who owns what feeds each IBA rate

| Benchmark | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 / fallback | Input owner risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ICE Term SONIA (2021) | CLOBs: BGC, TP ICAP, Tradition | **Tradeweb D2C** SONIA swaps | SONIA futures | Three IDBs, then a rival venue |
| ICE Term SOFR (2022) | **Tradeweb D2C** SOFR swaps | — | ICE SOFR futures (ICE-owned) | First line depends on a rival; CLOBs "as liquidity increases" |
| ICE Swap Rate, SONIA/SOFR/€STR | ISR Waterfall (CLOB data; ICE corpus does not detail the levels) | *(Tradeweb notes)* Tradeweb D2C since May 2020 | — | Proved fragile in Mar 2020 |
| ISR Inflation Swaps (2026) | “based on dealer-to-client data provided by Tradeweb” | — | — | Single named source |
| Tradeweb ICE UST Closing Prices (2019) | Tradeweb platform prices | — | — | Tradeweb took the series to FTSE Russell in 2024 ([[tradeweb-rates-em]]) |
| Tradeweb ICE CMT (2020, beta) | Tradeweb VWAP | — | — | Never seen to launch, in either newsroom |
| Synthetic GBP LIBOR (2022–24) | ICE TSRR + ISDA spread | — | — | ICE's own product inside a regulator's formula |
| LBMA Gold/Silver/Pt/Pd | IBA-run electronic auction | — | — | IBA owns the venue; ICE clears it |
| Bank Yield Index (never live) | Bank primary funding transactions | — | — | Needed banks to sign contracts; they didn't, in the corpus |
| AMERIBOR (ICE Data Indices) | AFX overnight unsecured loans | — | — | ICE now owns the exchange that produces the input |
| MOVE (ICE Data Indices) | ICE Data Derivatives implied vols | — | — | In-house |

**The design reversal.** In Term SONIA the inter-dealer CLOBs come first and Tradeweb second. In Term SOFR, Tradeweb comes first and the CLOBs are deferred until liquidity improves. In Feb 2021 the beta Term SOFR was based on "SOFR-linked futures contracts and Overnight Index Swaps", and by Feb 2022 Tradeweb D2C had become the lead input. ICE frames every step as robustness. Tim Bowler: Tradeweb data meant “using the best available data to help us provide reliable and representative rates to users in all market circumstances” (2020-11-24). Tradeweb's Lisa Schirf, in the same release: “Our robust pricing adds to the transparency and auditability of the ICE TSRR calculation”. The word "competitor" never appears in IBA material.

## 3. LIBOR: administrator to undertaker

- **Why it ended, in IBA's words:** “a majority of LIBOR panel banks had communicated to IBA that they would not be willing to continue contributing” (2021-03-05), so IBA “has to cease the publication of the relevant LIBOR settings on such dates” unless the FCA compelled a changed method.
- **The synthetic irony.** For 2022–24 the FCA required sterling LIBOR to be printed as ICE TSRR plus the ISDA fixed spread: **1M +0.0326%, 3M +0.1193%, 6M +0.2766%** (yen used TORF, ×360/365, with its own spreads). ICE's replacement product became the input to the zombie version of its old one. The FCA's own framing, relayed by ICE: such settings “would no longer be representative of the underlying market or economic reality” (2021-09-29). The USD synthetic method is not given in the ICE corpus (context, not from ICE corpus: the FCA used CME Term SOFR plus the ISDA spread, so USD's zombie ran on a rival's term rate).
- **The run-off schedule:** 31 Dec 2021 (most settings) → end-2022 (synthetic JPY) → 31 Mar 2023 (1M/6M synthetic GBP) → 30 Jun 2023 (panel USD) → 28 Mar 2024 (3M synthetic GBP) → **30 Sep 2024** (synthetic USD). The FCA set every date. IBA's releases are notices, not decisions.
- **ICE's own May 2023 analysis of the transition** (in-text date) notes that term SOFR's reliability “depends on the depth and liquidity of the compounded SOFR-based derivatives market”. That is an admission that a term rate is a derivative of someone else's market (inference: for ICE Term SOFR, largely Tradeweb's D2C market). It cites ~US$70tn of USD LIBOR exposure expected to outlive June 2023 at end-2020 (FSB figure), 90% of it derivatives.

## 4. The products that worked, and the one that didn't

- **ICE Swap Rate variants.** Each new rate followed the same beta → benchmark cadence of 2–6 months: SONIA (Oct → Dec 2020), SOFR (Oct → Nov 2021), spread-adjusted SONIA (May 2021 → Jan 2022), SOFR swap spreads/€STR (May → Jun 2024), inflation (2026). Bowler on SONIA: “This is an important step forward in helping the non-linear derivatives market transition from LIBOR” (2020-12-15). De Ruig called the SOFR spread-adjusted rate “a key element in assisting the non-linear derivatives market transition to SOFR” (2023-03-10). These are **legacy-contract tools**: they exist because swaptions and CMS products referenced the old LIBOR ISR.
- **RFR indexes.** They reduce compounding-in-arrears to a ratio of two published numbers. EBRD's treasurer said they “greatly simplify the compounding calculation and reduce reconciliation risks between parties” (2021-09-21). NatWest's Phil Lloyd wanted “a standardized set of compound SONIA indexes that everyone in the whole market can point to” (2021-04-13). This is a focal-point product ([[canon-schelling-strategy-of-conflict]]).
- **The Bank Yield Index failed.** It was “100%-based on executed transaction data” (2021-02-02), but it needed banks to hand over funding data under contract. Four launch targets slipped: Q1 2020, "in 2020", H2 2020, "latter part of 2020". By Feb 2021 it was still beta, and it never reappears. Every BYI release carried the line “There is no guarantee that IBA will continue to test”. Four years later ICE **bought** a credit-sensitive rate instead. AMERIBOR came with AFX, whose own exchange generates the input. Edmonds called AFX “a natural fit to ICE” because AFX's regional, community and minority-owned banks overlap ICE's mortgage-technology clients (2025-01-08).
- **LBMA precious metals: the owned-input success.** IBA took over the gold auction in 2015, which per the unnamed 2022 podcast segment used to run as a phone call among four banks. IBA made it electronic and centrally cleared with ICE as counterparty. The segment says volumes “pretty much more than doubled” and participants nearly tripled, and adds: “It's up to 79% of the volume has gone cleared.” It contrasts the speed of adoption with other markets: “normally that's takes 10 years whereas actually it's taken 10 weeks in the auction” (2022-03-14). By Jul 2026 IBA ran all four LBMA metals. De Ruig: “the auctions providing the foundational infrastructure upon which global precious metals price formation occurs”. LBMA's Ruth Crowell welcomed IBA “as administrator of all four LBMA precious metals benchmarks”.
- **MOVE (ICE Data Indices, not IBA).** It is fed by an ICE affiliate's implied vols and published near real-time from 9am, with a 3pm close. Creator Harley Bassman, in 2019: “rare is the case where a simultaneously low MOVE, flat Yield Curve and tight Corporate Spreads are not soon followed by bothersome market conditions”. In March 2023 (in-text), with MOVE at 160–180: “A MOVE above 150 implies continued risk.”

## 5. Governance as the product

- **People.** Tim Bowler was IBA president through LIBOR's end-game (quoted 2019–2022). Bowler then ran ICE Mortgage Technology (see [[ice-people]], [[ice-mortgage-technology]]). Bowler's 2019 line: “Benchmarks are essential to the integrity and everyday functioning of global markets and the real economies they support”. Clive de Ruig is president from 2023 (Swap Rate expansion, inflation, LBMA, ESMA recognition). Nigel Topping, the UN's COP26 climate champion, joined the IBA board in Feb 2023 as IBA turned to carbon and commodity-traceability services (ICE CRED 2022; cocoa/coffee traceability 2023).
- **The regulatory wrapper is the moat.** ISR is a UK "critical benchmark" and an EU "significant benchmark". ESMA recognition in Feb 2026 keeps ISR and the LBMA Gold Price usable by EU-supervised firms. The 2019 Tradeweb release lists the full IBA badge set: FCA-regulated, EU BMR-authorised, IOSCO compliance statement, EY assurance. De Ruig: “IBA is uniquely positioned to bring the same rigorous governance, data management and technology” (2026-06-05).
- **The weakness of the wrapper:** governance makes a number *trustworthy*; it does not make it *available*. LIBOR's governance was rebuilt (Waterfall, 2019) and it still died for lack of willing contributors.

## 6. What the claims don't prove

1. **Silence on failure.** The ICE corpus never mentions the March 2020 ISR outage. The Tradeweb study ([[tradeweb-derivatives-regulation]]) is the only source here for "22% → 97%". IBA's releases present each input expansion as an upgrade, never as a fix.
2. **No usage numbers.** No release says how many contracts, loans or notional reference TSRR, ICE Term SOFR, the RFR indexes or the ISR variants. A launch is not adoption (the same survivorship point the Tradeweb notes make about "firsts").
3. **The competitor is invisible.** ICE Term SOFR launched into a market where (context, not from ICE corpus) the ARRC had already recommended CME Term SOFR in July 2021. ICE never names CME, the ARRC choice, or its market share.
4. **Partnerships vanish without notice.** Tradeweb ICE CMT is "indicative, Beta" in Mar 2022 and then gone. The UST closing prices moved to FTSE Russell (per [[tradeweb-rates-em]]), and IBA never announced that. The BYI just stops.
5. **Attribution drift in secondary readers.** Two "IBA" quotes in the reader leads (GPU-compute and economic-indicator futures, 2026) are in fact Trabue Bland's, ICE Futures. IBA's remit is narrower than it first looks.

## 7. Strategic read (inference — not stated by ICE)

- **IBA is a governance shell that must rent its inputs.** In rates, the deepest D2C swap data sits on Tradeweb, and the deepest SOFR futures sit at CME. ICE owns neither the lead input nor the main hedge, so its term rates are licensed wrappers around someone else's liquidity. That makes IBA's rates business a classic **complementor dependency** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]). Tradeweb gains data revenue and benchmark status from each deal. It can also walk, as it did with the UST closes.
- **Ownership predicts survival.** Owned or controlled input (LBMA auction, AFX, ICE futures as fallback, ICE Data Derivatives for MOVE) leads to growth. Contracted input from rivals (Tradeweb, panel banks) leads to fragility or exit. The same lesson drives the fixed-income business buying MarketAxess flow ([[ice-fixed-income-data]], [[ice-marketaxess-deal]]).
- **Mandate-made, mandate-ended.** IBA's 2020–24 product line was created by the FCA and ARRC timetable and is now mostly legacy-servicing. Growth must come from new regulated niches: inflation, precious metals, carbon, and AI-era data benchmarks if ICE chooses.
- **The irony of focal points.** LIBOR showed that a focal point can be hollow (few real trades) and survive for years on contracts alone. Term SOFR built on D2C quotes is less hollow, but it is still a *derivative* of a market, which is exactly what ICE's own 2023 piece warns about.

## 8. Canon lenses

- **Perrow, tight coupling** ([[canon-perrow-normal-accidents]]): a single-input methodology (CLOB-only ISR) failed exactly when needed. The waterfall is a redundancy design, and redundancy is only real if the backup input is independent.
- **Schelling, focal points** ([[canon-schelling-strategy-of-conflict]]): benchmarks win by coordination, not quality. RFR indexes and synthetic LIBOR are coordination devices first.
- **Meadows, leverage points** ([[canon-meadows-leverage-points]]): changing *rules* (FCA powers, ARRC recommendations) moved trillions. IBA's methodology tweaks were low-leverage by comparison.
- **Helmer** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): IBA's power is a cornered resource (regulatory authorisation, critical-benchmark status) plus switching costs in legacy contracts, but not the input itself.
- **Taleb** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): the gold auction grew after a stress (the phone-fix era ending). LIBOR was fragile to the exit of contributors it could not compel.
- **Porter** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): supplier power. When one supplier provides the lead input, it captures value at renewal.

## 9. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For IBA:** using the best available executable data, wherever it trades, is exactly what regulators asked for after LIBOR. A D2C-quote input with outlier rules and a futures fallback is more robust than bank submissions. The 0.07bp average gap to CLOB prices in the 2020 test (per [[tradeweb-derivatives-regulation]]) suggests Tradeweb data didn't distort the fix.
- **Against:** a "critical benchmark" whose first-line input is a competitor's proprietary quote feed has a commercial single point of failure that no waterfall fixes. Pricing power at renewal sits with Tradeweb.
- **Both:** for a treasurer choosing between ICE and CME Term SOFR in a loan, the operational question is fallback language and hedge availability (term-rate derivatives are restricted per the ARRC, as ICE's 2023 piece notes), not the administrator's brand.

### Rep
Three computations with numbers from this note.

**(a) Compounding with an ICE SONIA Index.** The index starts at 100 on 23 Apr 2018 and has a value for every calendar day. A £10mm loan accrues from a date when the index is **105.000** to one 91 days later when it is **106.200**. Compute the interest and the annualised compounded rate (ACT/365). *(Answer: 106.2/105 − 1 = **1.1429%** for the period; interest = £10mm × 0.011429 = **£114,286**; annualised = 0.011429 × 365/91 = **4.584%**. Why does a published index beat each party compounding daily SONIA itself? Same inputs, same rounding, no reconciliation breaks: the EBRD line.)*

**(b) Synthetic sterling LIBOR, by hand.** In 2022–24 the FCA required 3M synthetic GBP LIBOR = 3M ICE TSRR + **0.1193%**. Suppose 3M TSRR fixes at **5.2000%**. A legacy £50mm loan pays 3M LIBOR flat for 91 days (ACT/365). Compute the coupon, and the part due only to the spread adjustment. *(Answer: rate 5.3193%; interest = £50mm × 0.053193 × 91/365 = **£663,091**; spread part = £50mm × 0.001193 × 91/365 = **£14,872**. Then: who in this formula owns each input? The FCA sets the formula, ISDA the spread, IBA the term rate, and the CLOB operators and Tradeweb the data under it.)*

**(c) How much of a "critical benchmark" rests on a rival?** Per the Tradeweb study, adding Tradeweb D2C quotes lifted USD ISR publication from **22% to 97%** in the 2020 test, and **1,914 of 5,601** fixings were missing Jan 2–May 7, 2020. What share of published USD fixings would depend on Tradeweb under those conditions? What was the missing rate? *(Answer: (97 − 22)/97 = **77%** of published fixings; 1,914/5,601 = **34.2%** missing. Now apply it to ICE Term SOFR, whose *first* input is Tradeweb. In one paragraph, say what IBA should demand at licence renewal: term, exclusivity, outage SLAs, and a second D2C source. Say what Tradeweb would demand back.)*
