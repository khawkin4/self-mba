# Note · LSEG capital allocation, 2019–2026 — from *why no buyback?* to returning ~10% of market cap a year, then a $3.5bn debt-funded credit-data deal: buybacks first cleared the Refinitiv sellers, then answered the AI de-rating

> Capital-allocation / investor-relations note · Maps to **E2** (Capital Allocation — sequencing organic, M&A and returns; leverage bands), **E8** (Stakeholder & IR — buybacks as a signal; analyst pressure), **E3** (M&A — bolt-on discipline, Octus), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance — EPS accretion, cost of debt, FCF per share) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[lseg-hub]]; companions [[lseg-refinitiv-deal]], [[lseg-ai-everywhere]], [[lseg-post-trade-clearing]]

- **Source:** LSEG corpus: results releases and calls FY18 → Q3 2026, buyback announcements (2022-08-05 onward), AGM statements, deal releases (Quantile, TORA, MayStreet, BETA+ sale, Acadia, Euroclear sale, LCH buy-ins, PTS, Octus, plus the 2026-10-13 press-speculation statement), ARs 2019–2025. **Caveat:** buyback totals are as LSEG states them. Releases mix calendar-year, programme and "since 2022" totals.
- **Core claim:** For three years after Refinitiv closed, LSEG returned nothing beyond dividends, even as analysts pushed (KBW, Mar 2022). Its first buyback (Aug 2022) and the directed buybacks of 2023–24 mostly bought out the Blackstone/Thomson Reuters consortium (>34% → <2%). From 2025 the motive changed to value. Repurchases stepped up from £1bn (2024) to £2.1bn (2025) to a £3bn programme (to Feb 2027), explicitly to exploit the share-price "dislocation" after the AI sell-off, plus about £1–1.5bn more capacity in 2027. Leverage policy loosened in step (1.0–2.0x → 1.5–2.5x in 2023). M&A stayed small (sub-£1bn bolt-ons) for five years until Octus: $3.535bn, debt-funded, EPS-neutral in 2028 (its first full year) and accretive after. The pattern is an FCF compounder (equity FCF £1.7bn → £2.45bn, 2022 → 2025) using its balance sheet to manage its shareholder register as much as its portfolio.

## 2. Three phases

1. **Delever and integrate (2021–mid 2022).** Management sequenced organic investment, then bolt-ons, then returns. Leverage 3.3x → 1.9x in ~12 months. Schwimmer: "We have brought our leverage to within our target range a year ahead of schedule" (2022-03-04).
2. **Clear the register (Aug 2022 – Aug 2024).** Pro-rata participation, a Microsoft placement, then directed buybacks at ~£79–88 a share. The Chair: "The consortium’s holding has reduced from over 34% to around 6% today, following its latest share sale last month." (AGM 2024-04-25). The 2024 H1 release declares the "share overhang eliminated."
3. **Buy the dip, then buy growth (2025–2026).** £2.1bn (2025) and £3bn (to Feb 2027), about 10% of market cap across Jan 2026 – Feb 2027 (Q3 2026 release). Then Octus, the largest deal since Refinitiv, financed with debt. "We have looked at many deals over the last five years and very few made the cuff in terms of strategic fit or financial return." (Schwimmer, 2026-10-22). *("cuff" as transcribed.)*

## 4. The analysts' pressure, era by era

- **2022: why not return cash?** Kyle Voigt (KBW, quoted above). Philip Middleton (BofA) argued LSEG was under-levered relative to peers. A written question in H1 2022 asked about a directed buyback if the "anchor shareholder" sold.
- **2023: you haven't done M&A.** Russell Quelch (Redburn) said LSEG "really haven't executed a material M&A" (paraphrase). Tom Mills (Jefferies): "it feels like you need to be able to do a bit more than that to claw back pricing discount versus competitors." (2023-10-19)
- **2025–26: how far will you lever?** Benjamin Goy (Deutsche, Jul 2025) and Arnaud Giblat (BNP, Feb 2026) on the £3bn pushing leverage to ~2.0–2.1x. Hubert Lam (BofA, Oct 2026) on Octus EPS maths at 5–6% financing cost. Michael Sanderson (Barclays) on retaining Octus staff.

## 5. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Dislocation" is management's valuation call.** Buying £5bn+ of stock at £82–93 averages is right only if the AI bear case is wrong (see [[lseg-ai-everywhere]]).
2. **Directed buybacks at market price gave liquidity to a 37% seller.** That was good for orderly exit, but whether it beat on-market purchases is not shown.
3. **Octus EPS-neutrality in 2028** depends on ~13% assumed growth and ~300bp/yr margin gain at Octus (Q3 2026 call). Both are management assumptions.
4. **Totals overlap.** £4.6bn since 2022 (AR 2025), £5bn by Feb 2026 (Innovation Forum) and £8bn since 2022 (Q3 2026 call) each use different start points and include or exclude announced tranches.

## 6. Strategic read (inference)

- **The buyback was the deal's last integration step.** Paying for Refinitiv in shares created a seller block; buying it back over two years turned an all-share deal into a partly cash one, done slowly.
- **From 2025, returns are an IR instrument.** Each step-up (£500m → £1bn → +£1bn → £3bn) coincides with share weakness on AI fears, and management says so. The risk is that buybacks crowd out strategic M&A just as private-credit and private-markets data become available (Preqin, Burgiss and With Intelligence all went to rivals). Octus suggests management saw that risk.
- **Octus is Refinitiv in miniature with the lessons applied:** cash not stock, light integration first, founder retained and paid partly in LSEG shares, and a strategic fit with Tradeweb's Trumid.

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Capital allocation as the CEO's main job** ([[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]], [[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]]): buy back stock only below intrinsic value. Is the "dislocation" claim a disciplined intrinsic-value call or a signal to the market?
- **Margin of safety** ([[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]]): Octus at ~11–12x revenue / ~29x EBITDA (inference from >$300m revenue, ~40% margin).
- **Agency** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): buybacks and leverage as a commitment device against empire-building. Directed buybacks as a negotiated exit for a principal.
- **Success equation** ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]): separate skill from luck. Margin gains from SwapClear (bought) vs operating leverage (earned).

## 8. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For:** a ~73%-recurring, ~50%-margin FMI with leverage under 2x and £2.5bn+ FCF *should* return most of its cash. Refusing to do mega-deals at peak private-markets valuations kept powder dry for Octus.
- **Against:** management said buybacks would clear the overhang and close the valuation gap. Four years and ~£8bn later, it was still calling the share price dislocated.

## 9. Open questions

1. What is LSEG's internal hurdle (ROIC or IRR) for buybacks vs M&A, and has it disclosed buyback returns?
2. Does Octus close on time (H1 2027) and keep its founder and team?
3. After Octus and Trumid, are buybacks below £1bn in 2027 a sign of strategic M&A priority or of constraint?

### Rep
**Octus accretion, back of envelope.** Use management's numbers. Price $3.535bn, debt-funded at ~5.5%. Octus 2026 revenue ~$300–320m (release: >$300m), ~40% adj. EBITDA margin, low-to-mid double-digit growth and ~300bp/yr margin gain in 2027–28 (CFO, Q3 2026 call).
- (a) 2026 EBITDA ≈ 0.40 × $310m ≈ **$124m**. Gross interest ≈ 0.055 × $3.5bn ≈ **$193m**; the CFO nets this to ~$180m after Octus's own cash. Is the deal accretive before growth? *(No. EBITDA is below interest even before D&A and tax, hence "neutral to EPS in 2028.")*
- (b) At 13% growth and +300bp margin a year, estimate 2028 EBITDA. *(Revenue 310 × 1.13² ≈ $396m; margin ≈ 46%; EBITDA ≈ **$182m**. That is roughly equal to net interest, which is the neutrality point.)*
- (c) Compare: the same $3.5bn spent on buybacks at an assumed ~4.5% FCF yield retires about $160m of FCF a year. Which use do you prefer, and what fact about Octus's growth would change your mind?
