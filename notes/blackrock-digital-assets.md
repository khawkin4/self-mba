# Note · BlackRock and digital assets — from a 2021 dealer-spread critique to the largest tokenized fund: the incumbent tokenizes the rails but keeps the regulated wrapper

> Innovation / market-structure note · Maps to **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation), **Course 10** (Entrepreneurship & Venture: platforms, standards), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy), **Course 11** (Macro: the dollar, stablecoins) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[blackrock-hub]], [[blackrock-anchor-client]], [[tradeweb-tech-ai-data]]

  - the Securitize funding release (from the Tradeweb corpus, 2024-05-01);

  - FY2025 earnings.

  There is **no primary source on IBIT's launch** (Jan 2024) in the corpus, only later mentions. Fink's widely reported 2017 *index of money laundering* remark predates the corpus and is not used here.
- **Core claim:** BlackRock's public stance on digital assets moves through six stages:
  1. **Skeptical (2021):** dealers capture the economics through wide spreads.
  2. **Curious (2022):** "studying digital currencies, stablecoins".
  3. **Cautious (2023):** the FTX collapse, permissioned chains.
  4. **Product (2024):** IBIT, then **BUIDL** on Ethereum with Securitize.
  5. **Evangelical (2025):** *every asset can be tokenized*.
  6. **Institutional (late 2025–26):** a *bridge built from both sides*, guardrails, ~$150B of digital-asset AUM.

  **The economic logic** is that BlackRock wraps crypto and tokenized cash in products it already knows how to run (ETPs, money-market funds, stablecoin reserves), and takes equity in the rails (Securitize, Circle). **What isn't proven:** that tokenization lowers costs at scale. The evidence is asset growth, not cost data.

## 2. BUIDL's structure (from the launch release)

- **Holdings and payout:** 100% in cash, T-bills and repo. The fund "seeks to offer a stable value of $1 per token and pays daily accrued dividends directly to investors' wallets as new tokens each month."
- **Investors:** qualified investors only (Reg D / 3(c)(7)), with a $5M minimum. Transfers run 24/7 between pre-approved holders.
- **Service providers:**
  - **Securitize:** transfer agent, tokenization platform and placement agent (paid an upfront fee plus a NAV-based fee).
  - **BNY Mellon:** custodian.
  - **Ecosystem:** Anchorage, BitGo, Coinbase, Fireblocks.
- **Conflict disclosed in the release itself:** "In addition, BlackRock’s investment in Securitize Markets creates a conflict of interest for the Investment Manager."
- **People:**
  - Robert Mitchnick (Head of Digital Assets): "This is the latest progression of our digital assets strategy,"
  - Carlos Domingo (Securitize CEO): "Tokenization of securities could fundamentally transform capital markets."

## 3. The 2025 op-ed's argument

- **Admission:** "At first it was hard for the financial world—including us—to see the big idea."
- **Benefits:** instantaneous settlement, and replacing paper in private markets. Fractions of large unlisted assets.
- **Analogy:** "If history is any guide, tokenisation today is roughly where the internet was in 1996"
- **Coexistence:** "It won’t replace the existing financial system any time soon."
- **Regulation:** "A bond is still a bond, even if it lives on a blockchain." The authors ask for an updated rulebook rather than a new one, plus guardrails: buyer protections, counterparty-risk standards and digital ID.
- **Bond ETFs as precedent:** "Bond etfs did the same for fixed income, connecting dealer markets with public exchanges so investors could trade more efficiently." The template for tokenization is the ETF ([[blackrock-anchor-client]] §2).

## 4. What the claims don't prove

1. **No cost data.** The corpus has no comparison of tokenized settlement cost against T+1. The benefits are argued, not measured.
2. **Scale is concentrated.** BUIDL is ~$2B (Apr 2025 deck) in a ~$7T money-market fund industry. "Largest tokenized fund" means largest in a small category.
3. **Digital-asset AUM moves with bitcoin.** Earnings tables show $78.4B (Dec 2025) and $79.6B (Sep 2026) (*parsed*). Fink's CNBC figure, "IBIT is now over 100 billion" (Oct 2025), is not reconciled. Market moves are the likely cause (*inference*).
4. **Real-world asset tokens are "up roughly 300% in the past 20 months"** (op-ed). No base or source is given.
5. **Retail demand claims** are self-reported: "More than half of demand for our Bitcoin ETP has been from retail investors" (2025 letter), and "three-quarters of those investors had never owned an iShares product before."

## 5. Strategic read (inference — not BlackRock's words)

- **Fink's own 2021 critique is the business plan.** The complaint was that dealers capture wide spreads. A regulated ETP (IBIT) compresses those spreads and moves the fee to the wrapper's manager. BlackRock didn't change its view of crypto market structure. It found the toll booth.
- **Tokenized cash is a defence of the money-market franchise.** If stablecoins and on-chain collateral become how crypto and tokenized markets settle, the reserve assets have to sit somewhere. BlackRock manages Circle's USDC reserves and BUIDL, so it earns on both the stablecoin and its tokenized-fund rival.
- **Equity in the rails hedges disintermediation.** Stakes in Securitize (with a board seat) and Circle mean BlackRock profits if the new rails win, while BUIDL keeps it relevant if they don't.
- **The venue link.** Tradeweb co-invested in Securitize and is building on-chain Treasury repo (Canton) ([[tradeweb-tech-ai-data]]). Tokenized collateral is where the anchor client and the venue's futures meet. DTCC and LSEG are on the same path (see [[lseg-hub]], [[ice-hub]]).
- **The dollar argument is a hedge in both directions.** Warning that Bitcoin could erode dollar status while selling the largest Bitcoin ETP positions BlackRock to win under either outcome.

## 6. Canon lenses

- **Innovator's dilemma** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): the incumbent avoids disruption by *owning* the disruptive wrapper early and channelling it through existing distribution (iShares, Aladdin, wealth platforms).
- **Standards wars** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): the "bridge" metaphor is a call for *interoperability*, which favours incumbents with distribution over crypto-native closed ecosystems.
- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): Securitize is a supplier, an investee and a potential competitor, all at once.
- **Taleb** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): equity stakes in many rails plus one flagship fund is a barbell. Small, capped bets with open-ended upside.
- **Thiel** ([[canon-thiel-zero-to-one]]): not zero-to-one innovation. It is one-to-n packaging of an innovation others built, which is how incumbents usually monetize.

## 7. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For:** BlackRock brings custody standards, regulated wrappers and distribution that crypto lacked. That is good for investors and for adoption. The op-ed's call for an updated, not new, rulebook is regulator-friendly.
- **Against:** tokenized money-market funds raise run and operational risks when transfers run 24/7. The concentration of reserves, ETPs and rails in one firm is a new form of systemic exposure. The corpus has no stress test or regulator view.
- **Both:** distinguish *tokenized traditional assets* (BUIDL), where incumbents win, from *crypto-native assets* (BTC, ETH), where BlackRock is a packager. The economics and risks differ.

### Rep
**Size the stablecoin-reserve franchise.**

**(a)** BlackRock manages $65B of stablecoin reserves (2026 letter). At a 5bp management fee, what is the annual revenue? Compare with BUIDL at $2B and a 50bp fee.
*Answer:* $65B × 0.0005 = **$32.5M**. $2B × 0.005 = **$10M**. The plain reserve mandate is worth more than the flagship tokenized fund.

**(b)** If tokenized Treasury funds reach 5% of the ~$7T money-market fund industry, and BlackRock keeps a 40% share at a 20bp fee, what is the revenue?
*Answer:* $350B × 40% = $140B × 0.002 = **$280M**.

**(c)** In one paragraph, explain which of the three businesses (ETPs, reserves, tokenized funds) is most exposed to fee competition, and why.
