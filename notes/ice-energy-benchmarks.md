# Note · ICE energy benchmarks — the "technology and data" company still earns its record years from energy volatility, and it wins benchmarks by moving the delivery point

> Practitioner / market-structure note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — benchmark wars, standards, ICE vs CME), **Course 03** (Microeconomics & Strategy Foundations — location basis, liquidity network effects, price caps), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — revenue sensitivity to volatility, operating leverage), **Course 11** (Global Strategy, Macro & Responsible Business — sanctions, EU energy crisis, carbon markets, petrodollar), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro), **E10** (Digital & AI — compute as a commodity) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[ice-hub]]; companions [[ice-clearing-treasury]], [[ice-strategy-financials]], [[ice-mission-forces]], [[ice-people]]

- **Source:** **~60 items** from `_ingest/raw/ice/articles.jsonl`, **Mar 8 2019 – Sep 29 2026**:
  - ~45 IR press releases on energy and environmental products: Murban/IFAD, HOU, Brent, Gasoil, TTF/JKM/LNG, the EU MCM price cap, UK ETS, environmental records, and the Ornn and NATIVX compute futures.
  - Volume-record releases from Oct 2024, Jan 2026 and Mar 2026.
  - Earnings releases for FY2019–FY2025, 1Q26 and 2Q26, for the Energy revenue line.
  - Letters to Shareholders for 2020, 2021, 2023 and 2024.
  - Insights pieces: Barbuto, "To defend USD trade dominance…" (Jul 18 2025, in-text); Bennett, "Scaling environmental markets" (Jul 1 2025, in-text); and the undated "Why financial participants matter to the commodity markets" series (price caps, position limits).

  All figures are ICE's. CME is **never named**; it appears only as "Cushing WTI" / "NYMEX WTI".
- **Core claim:** Energy is still the volatility engine behind the "technology and data" label:
  - **Revenue.** Energy revenue roughly doubled from **$992M (2019) to $2,182M (2025)**, rising from ~19% to ~22% of net revenue. It swung **+46% in 1Q26** (Middle East shock) and **−13% in 2Q26**.
  - **Benchmarks.** ICE wins them less by inventing products than by **moving the delivery point to where the physical market now is**:
    - Murban, priced off an ICE future at Fujairah, with ADNOC (announced Nov 2019, live Mar 2021).
    - Midland WTI delivered in Houston (HOU: a Magellan-only Permian WTI contract relaunched with Enterprise, announced Jun 2021, trading Jan 2022) against CME's landlocked Cushing contract.
    - Midland added to the Brent basket (May 2023).
    - TTF as the European and global gas marker, linked to Platts' JKM by LNG.
  - **Incentives and rules.** The playbook leans on midstream fee waivers (extended five times, now to 2028), on Platts as both partner and rival assessor, and on policy advocacy against price caps and position limits.
  - **Next frontiers.** ICE is extending the same playbook to carbon (~$1tn notional a year for five years; UK ETS auctions to 2028) and to AI compute (GPU futures with Ornn, May 2026, and NATIVX, Jul 2026, both still "planned").

## 2. The playbook: move the delivery point, then clear it next to Brent

**(a) Murban: a producer hands ICE its price.** Al Jaber (ADNOC, 2019-11-10): “For the first time, Murban will be priced on a forward looking, market driven basis based off the ICE Murban future”. ICE supplied a regulated exchange in ADGM and a clearing link. Every launch release repeats that IFAD contracts clear at ICE Clear Europe "alongside" Brent, WTI, Dubai and Gasoil for margin offsets. The sovereign's decision supplies the benchmark status; ICE supplies the plumbing.

**(b) HOU vs Cushing: the location argument.**
- **The trigger.** Hamm (2021-06-21): “when the Cushing, Oklahoma WTI contract traded down to negative $38 it was a wake-up call to the oil industry”. HOU puts delivery at MEH and ECHO in Houston, with 14+ docks and export access.
- **Linking to Brent.** ICE then tied HOU to Brent. Midland became deliverable into Dated Brent in May 2023, and the HOU spec was aligned with Platts' Midland spec in 2024.
- **Barbuto's 2025 framing:** “the traditional WTI Cushing benchmark has lost relevance, with pricing power shifting to Houston where U.S. crude meets global markets”. NYMEX WTI share fell “from 62% in 2017 to 40% today, in favor of ICE Brent.”
- **Delivery evidence.**
  - Jan 2025: “Physical deliveries of ICE Midland WTI were twice that of the Cushing WTI contract in 2024.”
  - Jul 2025: “ICE Midland WTI sees three times as much volume going to delivery as NYMEX WTI (Cushing) each month”.
- **The basis claim:** pricing Midland off HOU rather than as a Cushing differential “has been $0.21 per barrel cheaper for refiners over the last 18 months.”
- **The petrodollar wrapper.** Barbuto asks the U.S. Energy Dominance Council to push domestic pricing onto HOU to counter yuan oil futures. A commercial benchmark contest is being reframed as dollar policy (inference).

**(c) Brent as the hub that keeps absorbing grades.** Barbuto (2021-07-21): “Key to Brent’s multi-decade success as the global crude oil benchmark, has been its ability to evolve”. Midland now sets Dated Brent “~50% of the time” (2025). Adding a U.S. grade to a North Sea benchmark makes Brent more representative and also drains relevance from WTI Cushing. Both serve ICE (inference).

**(d) TTF and JKM: gas goes global through LNG.**
- **Origin.** Bennett (2019-03-08): “Back in February 2010, ICE identified TTF as an emerging natural gas hub.” By 2020 the letter called it the “Brent of Natural Gas”.
- **The Russia shock.** It made TTF the price of scarcity. Bennett (2022-10-27): “The price of natural gas in Europe remains high because of an imbalance between supply and demand”. ICE responded with LNG futures.
- **The JKM link.** In Sep 2026 Bennett described the LNG link: “ocean tankers act as a virtual pipeline connecting JKM and TTF pricing”. JKM itself is a **Platts** assessment that ICE lists and clears.

**(e) Sanctions-proofing the contract.** When the EU banned Russian oil imports, ICE rewrote Gasoil to exclude Russian-origin diesel. Barbuto (2023-02-07): “the contract is EU sanctions compliant and fully representative of physical distillates flows”. A benchmark survives only if its deliverable barrels remain legal to deliver.

## 3. Platts: partner, input and rival

- **Partner and input:**
  - JKM, Dubai and WIM settle on Platts assessments.
  - Platts analytics run inside ICE Connect (2020).
  - Platts and ICE jointly ran the Brent consultation (2021).
  - Platts' Midland quality spec defines what HOU delivers (2024).
  - Platts now assesses Midland *as a differential to HOU* (Jan 2025). That is the strongest external validation in the file.
- **Rival:** Barbuto (2023-05-24): “alternative assessments of WTI in Houston only consider trades based on a smaller proportion of Midland origin crude shipped to Houston.” That jab is aimed at assessment-based Houston prices from PRAs, probably including Platts and Argus (inference).
- **The pattern:** ICE prefers exchange-delivered prices where it can (HOU, Brent futures, TTF), and lists PRA-settled contracts where it can't (JKM, Dubai). See [[spglobal-hub]].

## 4. Policy advocacy: defend the market's right to set the price

- **The undated series** "Why financial participants matter to the commodity markets" makes the case.
  - On price caps: “As an exchange, ICE’s role is not to judge whether a price is too high or too low but whether it is representative.”
  - On position limits: “applying inflexible position limits within legislation can prove counterproductive to well-functioning markets.”
  - It prefers exchange "accountability levels" and cites the CFTC's 2008 finding of no evidence that speculators drove oil prices.
- **In practice (Jan 2023).** ICE complied with the EU gas cap on ICE Endex but built an exit. Bland: “we are preparing an alternative venue in London to act as an insurance option for customers if the MCM prevents them from trading”. Post-Brexit, London is outside EU rules. ICE used its multi-jurisdiction footprint as a hedge against regulation, the mirror image of how mandates pull it into clearing ([[ice-clearing-treasury]]) (inference).
- **The host's view.** Josh King, interviewing Duffy (2023-03-09): “A price cap goes against the very purpose of exchanges”.

## 5. Carbon and compute: the playbook extended

- **Carbon.**
  - ICE has hosted UK carbon auctions since 2012 and UK ETS auctions since 2021, re-won through 2028.
  - Bennett (2026-01-30): “2025 marks the fifth consecutive year where ICE’s environmental markets have traded the equivalent of $1 trillion in notional value”.
  - **But the voluntary market hasn't scaled.** Bennett's own essay (2025-07-01) admits: “Almost four years on from this article’s origin we barely started buying.” Also: “When it comes to carbon markets, however, nobody outside a government has chosen to act at scale.”
  - Compliance cap-and-trade is the business (EUA, UKA, CCA, RGGI, Washington, EUA2). Voluntary credits are a sideline.
- **AI compute.**
  - Bland (2026-07-01): “Driven by the transformative force of AI, compute has quickly become an important asset class”.
  - NATIVX's Crawford: “Compute is now an asset class, and like every asset class, it needs a public price and a market.”
  - **The design tells.** The NATIVX index is "energy-normalized" and will trade "alongside ICE's natural gas and power futures". The pitch is cross-margin and hedging against the power leg, the same co-location logic as Murban next to Brent.
  - **The demand link.** Bland had already tied data centres to gas demand (2025-11-10): “Rapid data center growth is anticipated to increase U.S. gas-fired power demand”.

## 6. Volatility is the revenue model

- **Records track shocks:** March 2020, Oct 2024 (“Against a backdrop of risk and uncertainty across multiple fronts geographically”, Bland), Jan 2026 (Venezuela, Iran, winter) and March 2026 (Middle East).
- **Jackson (2026-03-27):** “In periods like this, more than ever, the resilience of exchange and clearing infrastructure is critical.”
- **The earnings swing.** 1Q26 energy revenue was +46% ($814M). The quarter's adjusted operating income was a record $1.9bn, with a 65% consolidated adjusted margin. In 2Q26 energy fell to $518M (−13%). Exchanges revenue fell from $1,781M to $1,464M QoQ, and the adjusted margin from 80% to 75%.
- **Sprecher's 1Q26 line:** “our customers increasingly relied on our mission-critical markets, data, and technology to navigate complexity and manage risk.”
- **The counter-case is 2022.** Peak gas prices came with *lower* energy revenue (−6%). Extreme volatility can shrink volumes if margin requirements spike and hedgers step back (inference; ICE gives no explanation). The "volatility is good for ICE" view holds for moderate, tradeable volatility, not for a crisis that drives participants out.

## 7. What the claims don't prove

1. **The share numbers are ICE's and the bases shift.**
   - "62% in 2017 to 40% today" gives no denominator: it could be volume, OI, or futures only. The same sentence says "over the past 15 years" but starts the series in 2017.
   - HOU deliveries are "twice" Cushing's (Jan 2025) and then "three times" (Jul 2025). One is annual, the other "each month".
2. **Brent's reach drifts:** "approximately 80% of global crude" (Feb 2023), "three quarters of internationally traded crude oil" (2023–24), "~75% of physically traded global oil" (2025).
3. **Adoption needed subsidies.** The pump-over waiver began as "the first year" (2022). It was then extended to Mar 2023, Dec 2023, Dec 2024, Dec 2025 and Dec 2028. A benchmark that needs fee waivers six years out has not yet proved it stands on liquidity alone.
4. **The HOU delivery figures don't line up.** "~5M bbl a month" (Dec 2023) vs "over 4.4M a month" in H2 2023 (Feb 2024) vs 82M in 2024 (~6.8M a month).
5. **"$0.21/bbl cheaper" is unaudited.** It covers 18 unspecified months, uses ICE's own method, and has no CME comparison.
6. **The compute futures are announcements, not markets.** Neither Ornn nor NATIVX had a launch release by Oct 22 2026. Bakkt is the corpus's warning about crypto futures where "there wasn't" a market ([[ice-hub]]).
7. **"$1tn notional" is a round floor** repeated for five years. Contract growth fell from +40% (2024) to +4% (2025).
8. **Survivorship, and "new" that isn't.** HOU was not a clean-sheet launch. It is the **ICE Permian WTI** contract (code HOU, Magellan-only at MEH, with 2019 dock- and storage-auction releases), restructured and renamed after a second pipeline partner joined (2021-12-02 release). The single-terminal version's volumes are never reported. The success story starts the clock at the 2022 relaunch.

## 8. Strategic read (inference — not stated by ICE)

- **The descriptor hides the engine.** ICE dropped "exchange" from its self-description in 2021. Energy still went from ~16% (2022) to ~22% (2025) of net revenue, and set every volume record. In a shock quarter it is >25%. The label sells the recurring-revenue story to investors; the earnings still swing with oil and gas.
- **The location wedge is the winning tactic.** CME owns the U.S. crude contract at Cushing. ICE doesn't attack it head-on. It builds where the physical flow has moved (Houston, exports), links that point to Brent, and gets the PRAs to re-base on it. This is benchmark competition by redefining the relevant market, not by price.
- **Clearing is the glue.** Each new benchmark is sold on margin offsets against Brent: "over 90%" → 95% → 98%, now under IRM 2. Even a trader who prefers Cushing pays more margin by clearing it away from their Brent book. See [[ice-clearing-treasury]].
- **Regulation is used both ways.** ICE welcomes rules that create markets (UK ETS, EUA2, sanctions-compliant Gasoil). It routes around rules that cap them (the parallel TTF venue). It also lobbies to keep speculators in.
- **Compute is a real option, cheap to write.** Listing costs are low and the narrative upside (AI) is high. The test is whether GPU-hour pricing is standardised and fungible enough to deliver against. Energy-normalisation is ICE's bet that power is the common unit (see [[wss-ai-economics]]).

## 9. Canon lenses

- **Standards and lock-in** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): a benchmark is a standard. Once PRAs, producers (Continental) and contracts reference HOU, switching back is costly.
- **Network effects and power** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): liquidity attracts liquidity, and cross-margin offsets turn that into switching costs. Cornered resources come from partners: ADNOC's pricing decision, midstream terminals.
- **Five forces** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): suppliers of the benchmark input (Platts, midstream) hold bargaining power, and ICE pays them in waivers and co-branding.
- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): Platts complements ICE in JKM and Brent, and competes with it in Houston assessments.
- **Fragility** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): ICE's earnings gain from moderate disorder (2024–26) but not from extreme disorder (2022).
- **Disruption from below** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): HOU is a sustaining move for ICE and an attack on the incumbent's location assumption. Compute futures test whether an exchange can create a market rather than serve one.
- **Strategy kernel** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): diagnosis, Cushing is landlocked. Guiding policy, price at the water and link to Brent. Coherent actions: terminals, waivers, spec alignment, PRA buy-in.

## 10. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For ICE:** the outside evidence is real. Platts and General Index re-based on HOU, Bloomberg built a Murban index, ADNOC moved its OSP, and the UK government re-appointed ICE twice. Brent survived 2022 and absorbed Midland. These are third-party decisions, not ICE claims.
- **Against:** NYMEX WTI remains a deep, liquid contract (context, not from ICE corpus). HOU's January 2026 record ADV of ~96k is still small next to WTI's. The waivers show HOU needed paid help. Energy revenue fell in the biggest gas crisis in decades. And the compute and voluntary-carbon stories have yet to show a working market.
- **Both:** a hedger should measure *basis risk*, HOU vs Cushing vs the physical sale price, on their own barrels. They should weigh margin offsets at the clearinghouse that holds their Brent book, and treat ICE's $0.21/bbl as a hypothesis.

### Rep
Three short computations with numbers from this note.

**(a) Delivery share.** HOU delivered **82M bbl** in 2024, and ICE says that was "twice" Cushing. In Jul 2025 Barbuto says HOU delivers "three times" as much. Compute HOU's share of combined HOU + Cushing deliveries under each claim, and HOU's 2024 monthly average against the ~5M bbl a month of 2023. *(Answer: twice → Cushing ≈ 41M, HOU share = 82/123 ≈ **67%**; three times → **75%**. 82/12 ≈ **6.8M bbl a month**, +37% vs 5M. Then argue why delivery share can overstate benchmark share. Most hedgers never take delivery, and Cushing's low deliveries may reflect cash-settled use rather than irrelevance.)*

**(b) Revenue sensitivity.** Energy revenue was $814M in 1Q26 and $518M in 2Q26. Exchanges revenue was $1,781M and $1,464M, and consolidated adjusted operating income was ~$1.9bn and ~$1.6bn. Compute the QoQ energy drop, its share of the consolidated adjusted-operating-income decline, and energy's share of consolidated net revenue each quarter ($2,977M; $2,666M). *(Answer: −$296M energy against ~−$0.3bn adjusted operating income, so nearly **100% pass-through**, consistent with high incremental margins. Share: 814/2,977 ≈ **27%** → 518/2,666 ≈ **19%**. Then use 2022 (−6% in a crisis year) to explain why the relationship is not monotonic in volatility.)*

**(c) Basis value.** Barbuto claims pricing Midland off HOU was **$0.21/bbl cheaper** than Cushing-plus-differential. A Gulf Coast refiner runs **150,000 b/d** of Midland. Compute the annual saving. Then compare it with the inter-terminal fee ICE's partners waive (10¢/bbl on non-HOU transfers) if 20% of barrels would otherwise need a transfer. *(Answer: 150,000 × $0.21 × 365 ≈ **$11.5M** a year. Waiver value: 150,000 × 0.20 × $0.10 × 365 ≈ **$1.1M** a year. The basis claim is ~10× the subsidy. Say what independent data, such as PRA Midland-MEH vs Cushing+diff prints, you would need to believe the $0.21.)*
