# Note · JPMorganChase's mission and the forces on it — "fortress" and "port in the storm" never changed; the identity added "technology company" and "national-security financier," and the named threat moved from Big Tech to market makers and blockchain

> Strategy / environment-scan note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — five forces, substitutes), **Course 11** (Global Strategy, Macro & Responsible Business), **E6** (Crisis Leadership — COVID, 2022 rates, SVB, tariffs, Iran), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro), **E12** (Culture as Strategy) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[jpmc-hub]] and [[tradeweb-mission-forces]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/jpmc/articles.jsonl`: the mission, principles, risk and competition sections of Dimon's letters AR2019–AR2025, the LOB and COO letters, the 2025 Investor Day, ten earnings calls, and the SRI and policy op-ed releases. This is JPMC describing itself. Admitted forces are marked **A**; inferred ones **I**.
- **Core claim:** The **operating identity never changed** from 2019 to 2026: a fortress balance sheet, "not a conglomerate", "a port in the storm", and "the walls... not particularly high". Three layers were **added**: a codified Purpose ("Make dreams possible for everyone, everywhere, every day", introduced in the AR2022 letter), a "technology-driven company as much as we are a bank" (COO letters 2025–26), and a **national-security role** (the $1.5T SRI, defence forums, the "From the desk of" policy forum). The **forces** changed more than the mission. Rates went from a headwind (2019–21) to the biggest tailwind (2022–26). Regulation went from the main constraint to a partial tailwind (2025–26). The named substitute threat moved from **Big Tech and Walmart** (2020–22) to **Apple, Stripe and shadow banks** (2023–24) to **Block, Citadel Securities, Revolut, Stripe, stablecoins and "market infrastructure providers"** (2025–26). JPMC admits competition, regulation, macro and cyber risk. It does not discuss venue power over dealers, AI payoff uncertainty (until 3Q26) or concentration in Dimon.

**The arc:** 2019–21 *crisis strength and stakeholder purpose*; 2022–24 *codified Purpose, source of strength, guardian of the system*; 2025–26 *technology company, national resilience, "on offense"*. Inclusion language (racial-equity commitments, "bank of choice for all") thins after 2024. The AR2025 LOB letters rename DEI as "DOI".

### 1b. The constant principles
The eight "steadfast principles" (seven in AR2022, eight from AR2023) are nearly identical each year:
- the fortress balance sheet;
- "we are not a conglomerate";
- the walls "not particularly high";
- the U.S. government as a "silent partner" (Buffett's flag);
- people with "guts, brains, integrity";
- "a source of strength" (added AR2023).

The "moats... not particularly deep" sentence (AR2021) became "walls... not particularly high" from AR2022 on. The metaphor changed; the admission did not.

## 4. Five forces on the CIB (inference — framework applied to JPMC's evidence)

| Force | Strength | Trend | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rivalry** | High | ↑ | Banks "investing heavily to reclaim share" (AR2025 CIB); Equities co-#1, FICC share below pre-COVID |
| **Substitutes** (nonbank market makers, private credit, stablecoins, venues' all-to-all) | High | ↑↑ | Citadel Securities named (AR2025); $1.8T leveraged private credit; "market infrastructure providers" named as competitors |
| **Buyer power** (asset managers, hedge funds, corporates) | Medium–high | ↑ | Wallet consolidating among the largest institutions; 40% of traders use single- and multi-dealer platforms; clients "reducing the number of dealers" (Grant) |
| **Supplier power** (regulators as quasi-suppliers of permission; tech vendors; venues) | High | ↓ (2025–26) | Deregulation; "Software providers must prioritize security over rushing features." (Opet) |
| **Entry barriers** | Very high for full-service; low for niches | ↔ | ~$19.8B tech budget; capital; but "over a hundred" fintech rivals tracked (AR2025) |

## 6. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Port in the storm" is self-graded.** Market share in crises is shown, but against bank peers only. Nonbank liquidity in the same episodes isn't measured.
2. **The Purpose has no metric.** "Make dreams possible" sits beside metrics that are all financial (ROTCE, overhead ratio, share).
3. **Named competitors are a selection.** The AR2025 list says JPM tracks "over a hundred" rivals but names four. The choice signals where management wants attention, such as policy on market makers and payments.
4. **Tailwinds are partly cyclical.** Rates and volatility explain much of 2022–26. Management concedes Markets 2Q26 was "hard to imagine" repeating.

## 7. 2030 scenarios (inference)

| Scenario | Drivers | What JPMC would say |
|---|---|---|
| **Fortress compounding** | SLR fix, lighter G-SIB, steady volatility; deposit tokens hold; AI unit costs fall | "endgame winner"; buybacks resume |
| **Disintermediated dealer** | Treasury clearing and all-to-all move flow to venues and nonbank streamers; stablecoins win the tokenized-asset cash leg | Markets share slips; Payments carries the franchise |
| **Credit-cycle reckoning** | Private-credit losses; rate spike; financing book stress | "port in the storm"; buys distressed assets |
| **Political capture risk** | SRI and policy forum tie the bank to an administration; a swing reverses rules ("ping-pong") | Regulatory volatility as a top risk |

## 8. Canon lenses

- **Paranoia as strategy** ([[canon-grove-only-the-paranoid-survive]]): Gordon Smith's AR2020 CCB letter cites "Only the paranoid survive". The rising competitor list is that habit in practice.
- **Good strategy** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): "fortress", "not a conglomerate" and "global, complete and at scale" are guiding policies with real trade-offs (no large M&A, capital held back). The Purpose is not.
- **Five forces** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): §4. Substitutes are the force that moved most.
- **Black swans and fragility** ([[canon-taleb-black-swan]]): Dimon's risk sections list tail risks every year. The firm's model is to be the survivor that buys others in the tail.
