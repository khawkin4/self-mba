# Note · BlackRock's private-markets pivot — GIP + HPS + Preqin is the BGI playbook rerun: buy scale in the fast-growing category, buy its data layer, pay in locked-up stock, sell it through retirement channels

> M&A / capital-allocation note · Maps to **E3** (M&A, Deals & Restructuring), **E2** (Capital Allocation), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation: multiples, IRR, dilution), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy), **Course 11** (Macro: bank retrenchment, infrastructure gap) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[blackrock-hub]], [[blackrock-strategy-financials]], [[blackrock-aladdin-data]]

- **Source:**
  - deal releases and investor decks for GIP (Jan 2024), Preqin (Jun/Jul 2024) and HPS (Dec 2024), plus their closing releases;
  - Kreos (2023), ElmTree (2025), Viridium (2025) and Partners Group (2024, 2026);
  - the AI Infrastructure Partnership releases (2024–25) and Great Gray (2025);
  - the 2023 and 2025 Investor Day decks;
  - the 2024–2026 Fink letters.

  All are in `_ingest/raw/blackrock/articles.jsonl`. Deal multiples and IRRs are **BlackRock's own deck figures**.
- **Core claim:** in 2024–25 BlackRock spent roughly **$27B** (Fink, CNBC 2025-01) to turn itself from an index and ETF giant into a public-plus-private platform.
  - **The template is the 2009 BGI deal.** Fink's own framing: "Now, we see an opportunity to do for the public-private market divide what we did for index vs. active."
  - **Scale:** GIP brings the #2 infrastructure manager; HPS a top-five private credit manager.
  - **Data and benchmarks:** Preqin, i.e. "index private markets".
  - **Founders locked in:** payment is mostly in deferred stock. GIP was ~30% deferred; HPS 100% in exchangeable units.
  - **Distribution:** wealth models, 401(k) target-date funds, SMAs and the 50/30/20 portfolio.
  - **Price:** the deals are justified by the higher multiple private-markets fees should earn.
  - **What isn't yet visible:** organic fundraising against the $400B 2030 target, and the deals' returns against the 15–18% IRRs BlackRock projected.

## 2. The argument, in BlackRock's words

- **Category shift.** "The future standard portfolio may look more like 50/30/20—stocks, bonds, and private assets like real estate, infrastructure, and private credit." (Fink, 2025-03-31) The same split appears as house view in the Great Gray release: "BlackRock believes the portfolio of the future will comprise 50% public equities, 30% public fixed income, and 20% private markets." (2025-06-26)
- **Bank retreat.** "Market forces, technology, and regulation are consistently moving financial activity to where it can be done most efficiently," (HPS release, 2024-12-03). The HPS deck cites the "Basel III endgame increasing minimum bank capital requirements".
- **Convergence.** "The future of fixed income is building public and private portfolios to optimize liquidity, yield, and diversification." (HPS release)
- **Infrastructure.** "The future of infrastructure is public-private partnership." (Fink, 2024-03-26). Also "Infrastructure represents a generational investment opportunity." (Fink, GIP close)
- **Identity.** "We have a clear goal to become the premier long-term capital partner across public and private markets." (Fink, 2024-11-19)
- **Data as moat.** "Our Aladdin technology, including eFront, and soon Preqin, will make access to private markets simpler and more transparent." (Fink, HPS release)
- **Retirement access.** "We need to make it clear: Private assets are legal in retirement accounts." (Fink, 2025-03-31)
- **One-stop shop.** "The creation of PFS means that we can answer far more client needs with far fewer calls," (Fink, HPS close)

## 3. Deal economics compared

| | GIP | Preqin | HPS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline price | $3B cash + ~12M shares (nominal ~$12.55B) | £2.55B (~$3.2B) cash | ~$12B, all equity |
| Multiple (BlackRock deck) | ~25–29x 2024E P/FRE | 13x 2024E P/Revenue | ~30x 2025E P/FRE |
| Projected IRR (deck) | 15% | ~18% | 16% |
| Funding | Cash funded by new debt | Up to $3B incremental debt; Debt/EBITDA to ~1.4x | SubCo units (~6% of shares outstanding) |
| People | Founders lead infra; Ogunlesi to the board | O'Hare becomes Vice Chair | Kapnick, French, Patterson to the GEC; Kapnick board observer |
| Admitted weakness | "Not a primary rationale for transaction" (cost synergies) | Dilutive in 2025 | "fast growing but increasingly competitive market" (Kapnick) |

*Read:* each deal is priced off peer private-markets multiples. BlackRock's stated logic is that "durable private markets and technology revenue streams" deserve a higher multiple than its own blended one. The bet is a **re-rating**, not only earnings accretion (*inference from the decks*).

## 4. What the claims don't prove

1. **IRRs are BlackRock's projections** at signing, not outcomes. No post-close return is reported.
2. **Fundraising targets:** the 2021–23 organic target ($100B+) stood at $82B by 1Q23 and was labelled "on track". The $400B 2025–30 target has only one year of data in the corpus. Private markets AUM is $322.6B (2025-12-31) and $336.0B (2026-09-30), parsed from earnings tables.
3. **The +50bp/yr claim** for private assets in target-date funds (Great Gray) is a modelled figure under BlackRock's own capital-market assumptions.
4. **Infrastructure demand numbers** ($68T to 2040 in the 2025 letter, $94T in the 2025 deck) come from different sources and are not reconciled.
5. **The liquidity risk is acknowledged but not quantified.** Fink: "You are introducing instruments that have less liquidity. Let's be clear." (CNBC, 2025-10-17). In 2026 Fink defends a 5% redemption gate in private credit: "We’re protecting the 95%—that’s our fiduciary duty."
6. **GAAP vs adjusted results.** The deals widen the gap: Q3 2026 operating margin was 30.0% GAAP vs 47.8% adjusted, driven by acquisition-related noncash items (earnings release).

## 5. Strategic read (inference — not BlackRock's words)

- **Buying talent, paying in time.** Deferred stock plus retention pools turns founders into long-term shareholders. Two of them (Ogunlesi, Kapnick) are now at board level. The deals double as a **leadership pipeline** ([[blackrock-people]]).
- **The data layer makes the rest work.** Without Preqin, BlackRock is one more large GP. With Preqin in Aladdin, it can benchmark, index and risk-model privates for everyone, *including competitors*. That is the BlackRock analogue of owning the S&P 500 methodology for private assets ([[blackrock-aladdin-data]]). The conflict risk is real: the deck promises "information barriers".
- **Distribution is the unique asset.** No pure private-markets firm has iShares' wealth-platform reach, Aladdin's 200+ insurers or BlackRock's ~$1.7T DC book. GIP and HPS bring product, BlackRock brings the shelf.
- **There is an irony on liquidity.** The 2023 letter warned of a "third domino" in illiquid-heavy asset owners. Two years later BlackRock pitches 20% private allocations for individuals.
- **AI infrastructure is GIP's growth story.** Hyperscaler capex "goes beyond what any single company or government can finance" (Microsoft's Brad Smith, 2024-09-17). The Q3 2026 release adds "hyperscaler financings" to BlackRock's business mix.

## 6. Canon lenses

- **Capital allocation** ([[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]], [[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]]): paying 25–30x fee earnings needs a margin of safety in the re-rating thesis. Ask what happens if private-markets multiples converge down.
- **Agency** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): the deferred-stock structures are textbook alignment devices. Check the counter-risk: founders whose wealth is in BLK stock may resist decisions that hurt BLK's multiple but help their own platform.
- **7 Powers** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): Preqin plus Aladdin aims at *cornered resource* (proprietary data) and *switching costs*. GIP and HPS add *scale economies* in origination.
- **Innovator's dilemma** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): BlackRock is disrupting active management's fee pool from below (ETFs) and from above (privates) at once.
- **Mauboussin** ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]): separate skill from luck in the projected IRRs. Infrastructure and credit returns since 2020 have benefited from a rates and spread regime.

## 7. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For:** scale in origination matters in private credit and infrastructure. Clients want fewer managers. The BGI precedent worked. GIP V closed above its $25B target.
- **Against:** private credit had a growing stress narrative in 2025–26 (First Brands and Tricolor, raised by David Faber on CNBC). Integration risk was high enough that the board kept its lead independent director twice past retirement. And retail private-markets products need evergreen structures with gates, which work until they don't.
- **Both:** judge the strategy by three numbers each year: private-markets fundraising against the $400B path, the private-markets plus technology revenue share against 30%, and adjusted vs GAAP margin.

*Answer:* 10 turns × $400M = **$4B gap**. 1 turn × $15B = **$15B created**. That is the re-rating bet in one line.
