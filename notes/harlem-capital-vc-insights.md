## 1 · Fund economics — the bedrock mechanics
- **2-and-20:** ~2% annual management fee (a $500M fund → $10M/yr for salaries/offices/diligence) +
  **20% carried interest** = the GP's cut of *profits* (elite funds 30%; challengers waive/taper the fee).
- **The 4-step distribution waterfall:** (1) return of capital to LPs, (2) **8% preferred return / hurdle**,
  (3) GP **catch-up**, (4) **80/20** split above. Example: $100M fund returns $300M → return $100M → $200M
  profit → GP carry = $40M.
- **European vs American waterfall:** VC uses **European** (LPs get *all* capital back first, then 80/20) —
  because VC loses half+ the portfolio, LPs won't let the GP take carry on the first winner. PE uses
  **American** (deal-by-deal carry, with **clawbacks** if later losses shortchange LPs).
- **Carry is split into units, set up front,** and is **$0 if the fund never reaches full-carry mode** — and
  then you likely can't raise the next fund. The J-curve + power law (~75% of startups fail) mean winners
  must cover all losses *and* return profit; **markups are only leading indicators** (can evaporate 70%).
- **The carry tax fight:** capital-gains vs ordinary-income treatment ("carry loophole") — perennial.

## 2 · Fund size is the master constraint (everything cascades from it)
- **Camp → ownership → reserves → check size all flow from fund size.** Three portfolio-construction camps:
  concentrated (<20 deals), diversified (20–45, the seed average ~28 per Sapphire), spray-and-pray (45+).
- **Ownership is math indexed to fund size:** sub-$50M funds can run low ownership (5–8%) because a
  $500M–$1B outcome returns the fund; at **$100M+ you must own 10–20% or the math fails.** HC: *"$1–2.5M for
  10–15%"* — can't go below that threshold. First US funds average $40–50M (the 5–8%-ownership sweet spot).
- **Price only matters relative to fund size** (echoes Keith Rabois "seed price doesn't matter" — true only
  when small). "Markets always correct" — outsized-outcome arguments collapse when valuations drop 70%.
- **Reserves/SPVs are a capacity question,** not a right/wrong — how many boards a human can sit on,
  back-office load. Back into max check: camp → ownership target → reserve model (~1:1) → solve for check.

## 3 · The two jobs of VC (different skill sets)
- **(1) Pick/win deals vs. (2) run the fund/firm** (from TPG's Jim Coulter). Great sourcing makes a great
  *angel*; great fund-management is a separate money-management skill (a16z's $4.5B fund ≠ deal-picking).
  *"Minimize headcount, maximize AUM."* Emerging managers wrongly think picking = managing.
- **LPs buy institutionalized *process*, not a lucky home run** — they'd take a steady 2x ×10 funds over a
  5x-1x-3x-2x scatter. They invest in repeatable sourcing + memos + back-office. "Can you operate at scale
  for long periods?"
- **Deal memos = 30–60 pages, built in PowerPoint** (deals close in 48–72h): consolidate diligence for fast
  partner review, prove process to LPs, and **gift the memo to the founder post-close** (+ share downstream
  to short-circuit the next investor's diligence). Founders are stunned — it shows their flaws *and* that
  "we invested" ≠ "we think you're flawless; the reward simply exceeded the risk."
- **Junior-speaks-first IC** (associate → GP) + comments in Word not Google Docs, so seniors don't anchor.

## 4 · How VCs decide — the investment bar
- **HC's box (need 2 of 4 *great*):** Founder, Market, Business, Ownership. Two great → invest; you never
  get all four. *"You don't know a 1-in-1,000 company until you've seen 1,000"* — reps compound judgment.
- **The lens shifts with reps + the cycle:** deal #1 was an unknown accelerator founder; #50 was someone
  known 12 years → **network-driven**, and **market now matters far more** ("great founder + great company"
  isn't enough without market tailwind + large TAM). Downturn → away from consumer, toward B2B/vertical
  software + AI.
- **"Is it venture?" three tests** (John Henry): market size; founder vision/storytelling (the swing factor —
  Casper/SoulCycle/WeWork "weren't obviously venture" but executed that way); *will investors actually fund
  it* (Amazon unprofitable for ~decades but fundable). Venture = trade equity for 50%+–3x annual growth and
  a mandatory IPO/acquisition exit; a 10–15%/yr business is a great company but **not venture.** *(↔
  entrepreneurship-playbook §5 "don't put jet fuel in a Camry.")*
- **Founder signals others use:** Founders Fund — must be the founder's *life's work* (not a "whiteboard
  founder"). a16z — the **"idea maze"** (do they know which turns lead to death vs treasure, who the past
  casualties were, which tech moves the maze walls?); if stumped in 20 min, bad sign. GV — back **velocity**
  (direction + speed), build-for-yourself intuition; joy/delight drives adoption more than productivity.
- **HC's early risk-reduction model:** diverse founders often build $100M–$500M companies, so early on HC
  **didn't invest pre-revenue** (preferred ~2 yrs of revenue) — the model doesn't *require* every bet to be
  a unicorn. Macro: 60% of *exits* are enterprise but 60% of exit *value* is consumer (the giant outcomes
  skew consumer). West Coast = spray-and-pray for billion-dollar swings; East Coast = "intelligent investor"
  risk-reduction temperament.

## 5 · Building a fund from nothing (the founder-of-a-fund playbook)
- **The 4 things LPs buy, ranked** (Brandon Bryant): **(1) Team** (who, tenure together, track record — HC
  met 16 yrs ago, interned/roomed/banked, group-text → LLC; don't raise solo if avoidable). **(2) Strategy**
  (stage/asset class + why *you* are uniquely qualified; research what the LP needs to add to their book).
  **(3) Deals & Sourcing** (could be #1 — you need an angel portfolio; LPs reference-call every founder;
  *sourcing is the future bet*). **(4) Returns** (take 10–12 yrs → least important for Fund I, critical by
  II–III; markups as leading indicators).
- **Start as an angel syndicate:** HC began 2015 as an LLC, 4 partners × $5k/deal → one **$25k "Harlem
  Capital Angel" check** ("buying businesses instead of bottles" on IB salaries) — one entity converts
  better than four small checks. Built a track record → institutional Fund I.
- **Fund I was brutal:** 18 months, ~400–500 meetings, 12 of 18 months *while at HBS* (Amtrak to NYC
  Wed→Sun, fundraised through spring break); 55 LPs, **6 institutions** (rare for a first-timer). **Fund II:
  $134M in 6 months** (post-George-Floyd tailwind). Took **zero salary** while raising to avoid "funding
  their lifestyle with fees." Check size scaled $25k angel → $250k Fund-I-first (a terrifying 10x) → $2M
  Fund-II-average in ~3 years — *it gets easier.*
- **GP commit mechanics:** standard 1% (HC set it on the *target* not the cap); Fund II used an **SVB GP
  credit line** (bank funds 60% of the stake, GP puts 40% cash) because stacked commitments with no realized
  carry strain a young GP. "Use other people's money as much as possible" (Henry Kravis was an LP).
- **Most common LP "no":** don't back first-timers; don't believe diversity is an asset class; "you can't be
  a specialist for 30–40% of the market." HC reframe: *"we know the underserved 2% better than anyone."*
- **Fundraising discipline:** **ask for the money, not advice** ("advice is cheap" — a billionaire's note);
  **do the LP-intro homework for them** (scrape the boards your LPs sit on, hand them 5–10 names, ask for
  2–3 intros — "it's not my job to find people in *your* network"); rising minimums ($100k→$250k→$500k)
  force first-movers; an **anchor who advocates** (TPG rode along to vouch; sourced 3 of 6 institutions) is
  worth multiples of its check. "Don't take the no personally — the people you expect to back you won't."
- **The intern engine as moat + mission multiplier:** 6 interns × 3 classes/yr; applications 125 → 1,250;
  **24 alumni now work at other VC funds** — seeding diverse investors industry-wide to hit the 1,000-founder
  goal HC alone (8–15 deals/yr) can't.

## 6 · Brand / media / trust as the scalable lever
- **Naval's leverage, applied to a small seed fund:** capital and headcount don't scale — **code and media
  do** — so HC goes all-in on media as its sourcing *and* capital engine. *"You don't have to create, you
  just document"* the journey (wins and losses). "Purpose opens doors profit can't."
- **Brand → inbound, at every scale:** "people banged down our doors at $25k checks"; **~$50M of LP capital
  traced to LinkedIn DMs + press.** One Stanford-MBA DM → KKR's Roberts family → first $1M check → TPG anchor
  → ~$21M direct + halo. A Sunday WSJ print feature → Jamie Dimon's chief of staff emailed within days; two
  billionaires invested after a CNBC hit. Founders now DM "I've been watching your YouTube" — *that is
  sourcing.*
- **Platform discipline:** curate LinkedIn for the premium audience (bosses/LPs/founders follow there);
  diversify platforms (you can lose a channel — post-Elon, X reach collapsed vs LinkedIn). Newsletter/YouTube
  as owned platforms. Time portfolio press to when product/waitlist can convert, or right before a raise to
  harvest investor inbound.
- **Trust is the real moat (pre-fundraise):** "the future of investing is trust" — when everyone has money
  and AI to reach founders, the winner is whoever the founder *trusts* enough to let on the board. So
  **track founders for years before they have a company** (ex-OpenAI/Anthropic founding engineers — many
  2024–25 **$100M–$1B seed rounds** went to them); be the first call at company formation. The SoHo HQ
  (incubate a billion-dollar company in-house, "turn overhead into an asset"), dinners, paddle, basketball
  are all relationship/sourcing surface. "Maximize your surface area for luck."

## 7 · The diverse-founder thesis (pitched as inefficiency, not charity)
- **~3% of VC goes to women/underrepresented founders who are ~70%+ of the population → a market
  inefficiency → invest in an overlooked group for outsized returns + impact.** Funding ebbs/flows with news
  cycles (spikes post-George-Floyd, #MeToo) → **invest counter-cyclically.**
- **Two-sided market:** capital scales in weeks (OpenAI $40B→$100B) but you must *create* diverse founders
  (give them confidence to leave Facebook) — the slow side. "You can't be what you can't see" forces
  ecosystem/media/events/reports a white-male-Stanford fund never needs ("they can put a sign on the corner
  and find them"). A diverse GP *investing in diverse founders* has deeper network effects than a diverse GP
  with a white portfolio. "Talent is evenly distributed; opportunity is not."
- **Downstream-capital risk is the killer in a downturn** (Henri, post-SVB): early-stage isn't immediately
  hurt, but harder GP fundraising → harder *downstream* rounds for portfolio cos. HC did **zero deals in
  one Q4** (a first). Post-crash vintages historically outperform; 2021–22 vintages won't.

## Founder-side takeaways (the mirror — what to do differently knowing how VCs think)
1. **Know if you're even a venture business** — a path to $100M–$1B+ via IPO/acquisition + 50%+ annual
   growth. If you're a great cash-flow business, a "no" isn't an insult. *(↔ playbook §5.)*
2. **Match your raise to the investor's fund size** — a tiny check from a big fund (or asking a small fund to
   lead a hot priced round) breaks *their* ownership math. Understand their portfolio construction first.
3. **Engineer warm intros and do the work for them** — pre-build the target list, ask for 2–3 specific
   intros; the DM that became $21M proves warm + inbound beats spray (~2% cold-reply). *(↔ playbook §4.)*
4. **Ask for the check, not advice;** project calculated conviction.
5. **Storytelling + materials are the gate past ~$3M revenue** — investors see 4–5k cos/yr, pick 2–3; have
   a tight framework, painkiller-not-vitamin clarity, idea-maze depth. *(↔ playbook §1 "interesting = no.")*
6. **Treat the raise as a distinct, time-boxed job** (3–6 months + ~8 weeks of data-room prep); switch from
   "sell customers" mode to "sell the vision" mode.
7. **Build cash-efficiency / a clear milestone in** — don't assume a quick markup round (the 2021 fallacy);
   downstream-capital risk can strand you. *(↔ playbook §5 "between-rounds death.")*
8. **Win on more than money** — at 10 term sheets, pick the investor with unique value-add/network/trust;
   and remember your investor still prices real risk in you (the memo proves it).
9. **Use brand strategically but authentically** ("document, don't create"); time announcements to convert
   the funnel. **"You're always launching"** — give each pivot a real 90-day–6-month test, then kill fast.
