# Note · Goldman Sachs One GS & culture — a firm that admitted it was 'Balkanized' turned culture into a scorecard: day-one memo (2018), GBM merger (2022), wallet share +370→390bp, OneGS 3.0 (2025), with 1MDB as the controls ledger

Reader notes A, B, C, G and E1 were used as a map. **Caveats:** (1) all of it is Goldman-published — letters, decks and in-house podcasts with in-house hosts (Siewert, Nathan); (2) letters are dated to fiscal year-end in the corpus but were published around Mar–Apr of the next year; (3) the Jan-2024 Solomon episode page says "2023" but the transcript says 2024; (4) there are no earnings-call transcripts, proxy statements or 10-Ks, so pay mechanics, partner counts by year and committee charters are what management chose to say about them.
- **Core claim:** One Goldman Sachs started as an **admitted organisational failure**. On day one (Oct 1 2018) Solomon, Waldron and Scherr issued a memo and a cross-divisional pilot for about 30 clients. A year later Waldron said the firm had "gotten more Balkanized" and that its structure "was getting in the way of the ethos". Over the next six years the idea was **wired into structure**: the Oct 2022 merger of banking and markets into GBM so it "could be benchmarked against" peers, One GS in "compensation and performance review" (Feb 2023), and 100% performance-share pay for the Management Committee. It was also turned into **public KPIs**: top-3 rank with 51 → 77 of the top 100 clients, then 117 of the top 150, and GBM wallet share +370bp (2022) → +390bp (2025). By 2025 Waldron could say "We kind of obsess over those rankings. We're not obsessing over revenue." In 2025 the ethos was **extended inward** as OneGS 3.0, the AI-propelled operating model, which got its own CAO in May 2026. The deeper change is from an *adaptive partnership* that ran "in a real shroud of privacy" to a public company that **keeps public score** on 1-year → 3-year plans, ~35 KPIs and dated targets. **1MDB is the other side of the same ledger.** $3.42bn of 2020 provisions, an "institutional failure" verdict from the Board, ~$174m of clawbacks and pay cuts, and a reputational-risk committee "empowered to stop any transaction" are the controls that a more collaborative, cross-selling firm needs.

## 2. The diagnosis (2018–19): the firm said it, not the press

- **The memo.** Waldron: "on the first day in our job, David, Stephen and I put out a memo that talked about One Goldman Sachs". Solomon describes the same day as a pilot to see the biggest clients "through their eyes, instead of through the structure we've set up" (both 2019). The 2019 letter put it in the first person: "We launched One Goldman Sachs on my first day as CEO."
- **The admission.** Waldron (Sep 2019) dated the problem to the post-crisis years. "One of the things that we've suffered from I think in the last ten, 12, 15 years" was that "we have gotten more Balkanized. We do operate in more individual units." The fix was not to change values but to remove the structure: "the organizational structure of the firm was getting in the way of the ethos of the firm", and "there's a lot of organizational calcification if you will that we've got to cut through". He was confident because he compared it with his old firm: Bear Stearns "would never have been able to achieve this."
- **The operating rule at the top.** "You can kick and scream and fight behind closed doors, but when you come out, you set a direction" (Waldron, 2019). The deliberate public unanimity of Solomon and Waldron is part of the design (see [[goldman-people]]).

## 3. From adaptive partnership to plan-plus-KPIs

| Year | What changed | Verbatim marker |
|---|---|---|

It used to be on a one-year basis"; "We're very focused on creating KPIs and metrics and making sure that we've got real mathematical grounding where we can hold people accountable" — while trying to "give people the space to make adjustments, be creative, make some mistakes, fail, readjust" |
| 2019 | A plan is new for this organisation | Solomon: "This organization has always been very adaptive, it's always been very flexible." Now "you have to have a plan". L19: "we have embarked on an effort to evolve our culture more in favor of incentivizing investments for the future through a multi-year planning process" |
| 2020–22 | First public strategy, targets and segment KPIs | Solomon (The Hague, Jun 2022): the partnership "operated, you know, in a real shroud of privacy"; "I'm the first CEO, and this leadership team is the first leadership team to really lay out publicly a clearly articulated strategy" |
| Jan 2022 | KPI count | "we set up about 35 KPIs to track progress". L21: "Today, we're tracking more than 30 key performance indicators and we believe we will meet or exceed 95 percent of them." |
| Feb 2023 | Accountability as an investor message | *Why Invest* deck: *Transparency / Accountability / Alignment*; "Robust disclosure of KPIs"; One GS *incorporated into compensation and performance review process* (slide text, split across columns) |
| Jan 2025 | Scorecard verdict | Solomon: "I'm encouraged that we have met or exceeded almost all of the targets we set in our strategy to grow the firm five years ago" |

**Read.** The partnership's old strength was being *nimble*. Solomon kept the word but moved it down the list ("This quality remains valuable", L19). The cost of public scorekeeping is that misses are public too. The consumer pre-tax target and the 2020 ROE >13% target, missed in 2022–23 (see [[goldman-consumer-retreat]], [[goldman-strategy-financials]]), were scored in the open, and the reply was to restate the frame as returns "through the cycle" (inference from the 2023 letter and decks).

## 5. Hard-wiring: structure, pay and ownership

- **Structure (Oct 2022).** Solomon (Feb 2023): banking and markets clients "overlapped", and peers report one business, "so it could be benchmarked against people that we compete against". The 2022 letter: "One Goldman Sachs is now the organizational philosophy that underpins how we cover our clients in an increasingly complex world."
- **Ownership.** "Today, we have partners who are responsible for owning the entire firmwide relationship with One Goldman Sachs clients" (L22). The 2023 GBM deck lists the process: "Assign ownership", "Expand to top 150", then move clients from top-3 to top-1 or top-2.
- **Pay.** The ID20 slide shows "Increased use of performance-based equity" for the Management Committee and "Incentivizing a client-centric and collaborative culture" firmwide. By the 2022 letter: "All our Management Committee members receive 100 percent of their annual stock-based compensation in performance-based shares that are earned based on future results." Its stated purpose: "This is intended specifically to enhance collaboration and align our entire leadership team with long-term shareholder value creation." L21 adds the firmwide frame: a framework that "relies heavily on equity awards to incentivize long-term value creation".
- **Guardrail.** The One GS deck's own endnote: "One Goldman Sachs is subject to all existing firm policies and procedures", with Compliance training on confidential information, "need-to-know" and conflicts. Cross-selling across a bank, an asset manager and a wealth arm is exactly where information barriers matter (see §8).

## 7. The 2025 triumvirate reshuffle and the One GS co-heads

- **What changed (Jan 21 2025):** IB co-heads McClure, Gutman, Posnett; FICC co-heads Brauth, Shah, Sehgal; Equities co-heads Potishko, Goddeeris, Hoxha; client-coverage co-heads Kelly and Morgan; Gutman and Shah also **co-CEOs of GSI**; Gnodde to Vice Chairman. Storey and Pasquariello "assume additional responsibilities as co-heads of One Goldman Sachs with Meena Flynn". The release frames this as a culture statement: "This group of leaders represents the very best of our culture of excellence, client service and teamwork."
- **Why, in Solomon's words (L24):** three needs — "to create more operating leverage for our senior leadership", "to give our people more responsibility and opportunities for growth", and "to ensure we have a strong group of leaders who have been tested". Also: "Now we are focused on setting up the firm for the next five years."
- **What the One GS seat signals.** In 2023 One GS was co-run by Kim Posnett (Global Head of Investment Banking Services). In 2025 it went to two senior *relationship* figures from both sides of GBM. Pasquariello is the Global Head of Hedge Fund Coverage and the in-house *Great Investors* host. One GS has moved from programme to standing coverage office *(inference)*.
- **The pattern continues:** Jan 2026, seven AWM leaders join the Management Committee (private credit, External Investing, wealth, alternatives for wealth, public investing). May 2026, Feldgoise (M&A) and Schiffrin (GBM risk) join, and Leslie becomes CAO for OneGS 3.0.

## 8. OneGS 3.0 — the ethos turned inward

- **Definition (L25):** "In 2025, we announced the launch of One Goldman Sachs 3.0, our new operating model propelled by AI." "In short, this is a moment for us to expand our One Goldman Sachs ethos to our internal operating model." Six workstreams: client onboarding/KYC, vendor management, regulatory reporting, lending, enterprise risk management, sales enablement. Goals: resilience and scale, productivity and efficiency, profitability, client experience, employee experience, risk management.
- **Expectation reset (Jan 2026):** "I love the way that sounds. But the reality of it is that it's narrower than that grand vision of reimagining the firm." The hard part is changing "the human capital we use around that process"; "Changing processes in a big enterprise is hard work." Headcount: "if we get this right, I don't think it significantly lowers the number of people we have." Details in [[goldman-ai]].
- **Ownership:** a named executive (Leslie, CAO, May 2026) is tasked to "transform its operating system and create more capacity to invest in its growth". *Read:* 1.0 was client coverage (2018), 2.0 was the 2022 structure and pay, 3.0 is the back office *(the 1.0/2.0 labels are inference; GS only names 3.0)*.

| 2020 litigation provisions | **$3.42bn**: EPS −$9.51, ROE −3.9pp | L20 |
| Clawbacks from Leissner, Ng, Vella | ~$76m sought, ~$24m held | PR Oct 22 2020 |
| Five former senior executives (former CEO, COO, a CFO, two vice chairmen) | ~$67m forfeited (2011 LTPIP awards) | same |
| Current CEO, COO, CFO, GSI CEO | $31m cut to 2020 pay | same |
| **Total** | "These clawbacks, forfeitures and compensation reductions will total approximately $174 million in the aggregate." | same |
| Controls | Firmwide Reputational Risk Committee "made up of predominantly control-side members who are empowered to stop any transaction"; compliance "has nearly doubled in size" since the deals; sovereign financings need certifications "on the use of proceeds within six months"; Compliance Forensics and Insider Threat programmes; a special Board committee oversees remediation | same |

- **Solomon's framing:** "we did not adequately address red flags and scrutinize the representations of certain members of the deal team, most notably Tim Leissner". He uses the collective-responsibility logic that One GS also relies on: "we are responsible for each other's actions", and "When a colleague knowingly violates a firm policy, or much worse, the law, we – as a firm – have to accept responsibility". The memo closes on culture: "Our success as a firm can only be ensured through a culture rooted in integrity, shaped by a long-term mindset".
- **In the letters:** L19 files Malaysia under *Integrity* ("Particularly in the wake of our experience in Malaysia, I am keenly aware of how the actions of a few can harm our firm"). L20: "the behavior of a few can do harm to our franchise". After FY2020 it disappears from the letters.
- *Derived:* the ~$174m of pay actions is **~5.1%** of the $3.42bn of 2020 provisions. The $31m cut to current leaders is ~0.9%. The verdict was institutional, but the financial cost to individuals was a small share of the bill.

## 11. What the claims don't prove

1. **Wallet share is not attributable to One GS.** +390bp coincides with financing growth, competitor exits (CS is dropped from the peer set after FY22) and two volatility booms. No disclosure separates the effect of coverage from that of balance sheet.
2. **Client rankings are GS-compiled** (the footnotes cite "Client Ranking/Scorecard/Feedback and/or Coalition Greenwich"). The survey figures (97%, 94%) come from clients GS chose to survey.
3. **"Less collaborative friction" is unmeasured.** No engagement scores, internal referral revenue after 2020 (>4,000 referrals), or attrition by level appear after 2020.
4. **PSU-only pay shows design, not outcome.** The vesting metrics, payout history and whether One GS metrics enter them are in the proxy, which is not in the corpus.
5. **1MDB controls are self-reported.** "Nearly doubled" compliance has no headcount or cost figure. There is no later disclosure of how many deals the reputational-risk committee stopped.
6. **RTO's benefit to apprenticeship is asserted, not shown.** No retention, promotion-speed or analyst-survey data appear.

## 12. Strategic read *(inference)*

- **Culture was rebuilt as an accountability system, not a slogan.** The sequence was diagnosis, then structure, then pay, then metric, then operating model. That is the opposite of a values campaign. Each step made the next one measurable, and investors were the audience for the measurement.
- **One GS is the client-side version of "durable revenues."** It aims at more wallet from the same top-150 and more financing per client. That fits the 2025 claim that financing and wallet share lowered markets volatility from 24% to 6% (see [[goldman-strategy-financials]], [[goldman-markets-platforms]]).
- **Scorekeeping changed the partnership's politics.** In 2019 Solomon framed partner exits as people who didn't want to "sign up for another five years". Once there was a plan, the partnership became a body that signs up to it. Smaller classes, 100% performance shares at the top and partners owning client relationships push partner identity from franchise-builder toward plan-executor.
- **1MDB is why a cross-selling culture needs a veto.** One GS increases internal referrals and information flow. 1MDB showed what happens when a deal team's word is not checked. The reputational-risk committee, with control-side power to stop deals, is the counterweight. Watch whether it appears in private-credit allocation conflicts between GBM origination and AWM funds ([[goldman-awm-private-credit]]).
- **OneGS 3.0 is One GS's hardest version.** Coverage collaboration changed incentives. Process redesign changes jobs. Solomon's "narrower" and "hard work" are the hedge.

## 13. Canon lenses

- [[canon-schein-organizational-culture]] — **Artifacts:** the day-one memo, earnings calls opened to all staff, the Miami partners meeting, the 60-year Management Committee dinner. **Espoused values:** four core values, then Whitehead's 14 principles (1979, L25). **Underlying assumption:** "partnership" means collective responsibility. That is the logic of both One GS (share the client) and the 1MDB clawbacks (share the blame).
- [[canon-kotter-leading-change]] — Waldron's 2019 admission is Kotter's *urgency* step, from inside. The co-signed memo is the guiding coalition. The 2022 reorganisation and PSU pay *anchor the change in structures*. The 2023 Cultural Stewardship sessions are anchoring in culture.
- [[canon-doerr-measure-what-matters]] and [[canon-4dx-four-disciplines-execution]] — top-3 rank with the top 150 and wallet share are *lead* measures for revenue, which is the *lag*. Waldron's "We're not obsessing over revenue" is a 4DX statement. The risk is Goodhart: once rankings are pay-relevant, they get managed.
- [[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]] — 100% performance shares and the clawback of 2011 awards are textbook agency fixes. A public company with ~450 partners recreates partnership skin-in-the-game through equity.
- [[canon-perrow-normal-accidents]] — 1MDB as a *tightly coupled* failure: sovereign client, bond underwriting, a deal team's representations and weak challenge. The fix is to *decouple* by giving the control side a veto.
- [[canon-horowitz-what-you-do-is-who-you-are]] — culture as what is paid for. Putting One GS into performance reviews counts. The values page does not.
- [[canon-bossidy-charan-execution]] — Waldron's "COO first" stance, the biweekly execution meeting and three-year plans are Bossidy-and-Charan's *people, strategy, operations* processes run by the No. 2.

## 14. Practitioner counterpoint

- **Bull (management):** the firm named its silo problem, fixed the structure, aligned pay and published results that moved: 51 → 77 top-100 clients, +390bp of wallet, σ 24% → 6%, and ROE 15.0% in 2025 against a 2020 target of >13%. Culture was made legible to investors without giving up the partnership.
- **Skeptic:** the KPIs are self-defined and re-based (top 100 → top 150; peer sets change). Wallet gains coincide with financing growth bought with balance sheet and with competitors shrinking. The pay framework rewards *hitting the plan*, which may crowd out the adaptive opportunism that made the partnership valuable. 1MDB happened inside a culture that already called itself collaborative and client-first. The fix was more control functions, not more culture.
- **Synthesis:** One GS is best read as an *incentive and reporting* redesign that made cross-selling the default and visible. Its success measure (share of wallet) is real but cannot be attributed to it alone. Its ethical counterweight (the reputational-risk veto) is what makes the cross-selling safe. Both have to be watched together.
