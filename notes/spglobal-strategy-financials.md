# Note · S&P Global strategy & financials — a cyclical ratings engine bolted to subscription data, run for margin and buybacks; the merger doubled the size, then the portfolio was cut back to the core

- **Source:** S&P Global earnings releases **FY2018 – Q1 2023** (full text, tables trimmed at 15k characters), the annual-report letters for 2019–2025, the **Investor Day decks of Dec 1 2022 and Nov 13 2025**, the 2026 Mobility Global deck, and deal releases (`_ingest/raw/spglobal/articles.jsonl`). **Gap:** the IR feed's earnings items from Q2 2023 onward are stubs that link out to PDFs. FY25 MI and Indices revenue are taken from [[tradeweb-ai-price]] §5.
- **Core claim:** S&P Global runs a **high-margin, cyclical Ratings business** (adjusted margins around 57–69%) next to **slower, lower-margin subscription businesses** (MI at 31–36%). It also owns an **index franchise** with ~70% margins, partly owned by CME. From 2018 to 2021 the cycle was kind. Revenue rose from **$6.26B to $8.30B**, adjusted EPS from **$8.50 to $13.70**, and the adjusted margin from **48.8% to 55.2%**. The IHS Markit merger then roughly **doubled the revenue base** (2022 pro forma $11.8B) just as debt issuance collapsed. 2022 pro forma revenue fell **4%**, and EPS guidance fell about **17%** within the year. From 2023 to 2025 the cycle turned back up: revenue **$12.5B → $14.2B → $15.3B**, adjusted margin **50.4%** in 2025, adjusted EPS **$17.83**. Throughout, management **returned more cash than the business generated**: S&P says 145% of adjusted FCF since 2022, funded partly by disposals. The Cheung-era targets (2025) are *lower in ambition* on margin than the 2020–22 promises, because margins are already near 50%.

## 2. Ratings cyclicality — the swing factor

| Period | Ratings move (S&P says) | Driver |
|---|---|---|
| Q4 2018 | Revenue −16%, transaction −29% | Issuance stall |
| Q1 2019 | −7% | Bank-loan ratings weak |
| **Q2 2020** | **+26%** to $1,006M; transaction +48%; adj. margin **69.1%** | COVID liquidity issuance |
| Q1–Q3 2021 | +23%, then +14% | Leveraged loans, M&A |
| **2022** | Transaction revenue **−31% / −44% / −56%** (Q1–Q3); Q4 −51% to $249M | Rate shock. "Debt issuance volumes have been extraordinarily weak year-to-date." (2022-06-01) |
| Q1 2023 | −5% to $824M | Still soft |
| 2023 | $3.3B, +9%, 56.5% margin | Recovery |
| 2025 → | Investor Day 2025 cites an **$8.2T** three-year maturity wall vs a **$6.5T** average | Refinancing tailwind |

In 2019 Peterson said "We don't let market disruptions interfere with our business plans." He also said "we remain confident that, in the long run, global debt market growth will track GDP growth." Both are true over a cycle and false inside one. In 2022 S&P put the possible Ratings shortfall at up to **$600M** in a single year.

## 4. Innovation and recurring-revenue metrics

- **Vitality Index:** "In 2022, we introduced the Vitality Index as a measure of our innovation." Vitality revenue was 8% of the total (2021), more than 10% (2022) and 11% (2023, about $1.42B, +18%).
- **Recurring revenue:** ~70% (2020) → 76% projected post-merger → 76% TTM Sep 2025 ($13.3B ex-Mobility).
- **Technology spend:** ~$2B (2022), split 73% infrastructure and 27% R&D, with a target of 60/40 by 2026. AI: "over $1 billion" invested since Kensho in 2018 (2025 letter).
- **Kensho as a cost line:** in 2019–2020 Kensho shows up in margin explanations ("primarily due our investment in artificial intelligence and analytics through the acquisition of Kensho"). "Kensho retention-related expenses" were still adjusted out at the end of 2020. See [[spglobal-ai-data-layer]].

## 6. How the story was told

1. **2019–2021: compounding and confidence.** The 2021 letter: "As great as last year was for our company, in many ways 2022 is shaping up to be even better."
2. **2022: resilience framing.** The Q2 headline was *S&P GLOBAL DEMONSTRATES RESILIENCE IN SECOND QUARTER RESULTS* with adjusted revenue −5%. The FY22 letter is candid: "our financial performance in 2022 did not match the previous year".
3. **2023: secular framing.** Peterson: "Secular trends continue to serve as strong tailwinds for our company, while cyclical trends can impact different parts of the business in different ways."
4. **2024–2026: focus and margin.** Disposals are described as "portfolio optimization". The July 2026 MI reorganisation is pitched by Cheung as "growing our revenue and improving margins".

## 7. What the claims don't prove

1. **Pro forma, adjusted and recast keep changing the base.** 2022 is pro forma, 2023 is "ex-divestitures", 2024 is "ex-Engineering Solutions", and 2026 is recast without Mobility, with new expense allocations (2026-07-06). Trend lines need the reconciliation tables, which this corpus does not contain after Q1 2023.
2. **The adjusted margin excludes merger amortization.** Reported MI margins fell to 11.8% (Q4 '22) and 21.4% (Q1 '23) on IHS Markit intangibles, while adjusted MI margins were 31–32%.
3. **The "upper end of our 2022 targets" starts from a trough.** A 9% CAGR from depressed 2022 Ratings revenue includes cyclical recovery. It is not all strategy.
4. **The Vitality Index is self-defined.** "New or enhanced products" is S&P's own classification, with no external audit in the corpus.
5. **The AI investment figure has no denominator.** "Over $1 billion" since 2018 includes the Kensho purchase. AI revenue is not disclosed.

## 8. Strategic read (inference — not stated by S&P Global)

- **The merger was a hedge against Ratings.** Subscription revenue from MI, CI and Mobility softened 2022, but it also lowered the blended margin. S&P spent 2023–26 selling the lowest-margin pieces (Engineering Solutions, software, Mobility) to rebuild toward 50%.
- **Indices are the quiet compounder.** About 70% margins, asset-linked fees, ETD volumes that rise with volatility, and +14% in FY25. The CME minority interest is the one structural leak.
- **MI is the problem child.** It is the largest subscription division and has the lowest margin. It has had three leaders in two years and a 2026 reorganisation framed around margin. AI distribution through LLMs ([[spglobal-ai-data-layer]]) is MI's growth bet.
- **Capital return is the product for shareholders.** Buybacks absorbed the merger's share dilution. The $12B ASR in a down year signalled that per-share math comes first.

## 9. Canon lenses

- **Capital allocation as CEO's main job** ([[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]], [[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]]): buy back stock when it is depressed, and do not overpay in M&A. The 2022 ASR was timed into a drawdown, while the stock-funded merger came near a cyclical high.
- **Cycles and luck** ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]): separate skill (subscription growth, index pricing) from luck (issuance cycles) before crediting management for the 2023–25 recovery.
- **Power** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): Ratings (oligopoly plus regulatory status) and Indices (network effects in ETFs and futures) carry pricing power. Desktop data competes on cost.
- **Measurement** ([[canon-kahneman-sibony-sunstein-noise]]): self-set metrics (Vitality, synergy run-rates, adjusted margins) should be scored against GAAP and against peers.

## 10. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For S&P:** ten years of near-double-digit EPS compounding, margins at 50%, and a business whose biggest risk (issuance) mean-reverts. The 2022 collapse lasted about four quarters, and the merger cushioned it.
- **Against:** much of the 2022→2025 "growth" is cyclical recovery plus buybacks. Returning 145% of FCF is not repeatable without more disposals. And AI may compress desktop pricing in MI, the division the merger made largest.
