# Note · S&P Global's people — a textbook CEO handover at the top, two years of churn underneath it, and a company whose acquirer handed its data division to the target

> People & perspectives study · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & Org Behavior — succession, span of control, internal vs external hires), **E1** (Leading at Scale — leading other leaders through a merger and a reorg), **E4** (Corporate Governance & The Board — the board as succession engine, chair rotation, ex-target directors), **E7** (Exec Presence & Comms — Peterson vs Cheung voice, house phrases), **E12** (Culture as Strategy — "People Forward", purpose officer abolished) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[spglobal-hub]]; companions [[spglobal-outside-voices]], [[spglobal-mission-forces]], [[spglobal-ihs-markit-merger]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/spglobal/articles.jsonl`. That is **492 items, Jan 2019 – Oct 23 2026**: S&P Global press and IR releases, the chairman/CEO letters from the 2019–2025 annual reports, and the Investor Day 2022 and 2025 decks. Every appointment, departure and board release was read in full. Quotes are verbatim and dated by release date. Annual-report letters are dated by the year they describe. There are **no earnings-call transcripts and no third-party press** in the corpus. Every voice below is one S&P chose to print. Pronouns follow the releases (Ms. Cheung, Ms. Clay, Ms. Moore; Mr. Peterson, Mr. Saha and others). Where a release gives none, this note uses names or *they*.
- **Core claim:** The top handover was textbook. The board **"unanimously elected"** Martina Cheung in June 2024. She took over in November. Doug Peterson stayed as adviser to the end of 2025 and the chair rotated six months later. Cheung was a 14-year insider. She had run Market Intelligence (MI) and Ratings, led ESG, and served as Chief Strategy Officer. Underneath, the leadership bench turned over almost completely in two years:
  - a CFO gap filled by an interim for a year;
  - **three MI leaders in two years**, then MI split between two co-heads;
  - new heads at S&P DJI and Mobility in 2025;
  - a new Chief Technology & Transformation Officer and Chief Legal Officer in 2026;
  - the Chief Purpose Officer role abolished.
  The pattern started earlier. In the 2020 merger structure, the acquirer gave its data division (MI) to the target's executive, Adam Kansler. S&P's own MI head, Cheung, went to Ratings. The detour put her on the path to CEO.

**What the chart says.**
- **The CEO handover was the one seat run like a relay.** The board named a successor (Jun 27 2024). The heir joined the board on Jul 1 and took over on Nov 1. The predecessor kept a board seat until May 2025 and an adviser role to Dec 31 2025. The chair who ran the process (Thornburgh) then handed over too.
- **The CFO seat had the opposite pattern.** On Dec 7 2023 Steenbergen's exit was announced with about three months' notice "to pursue another professional opportunity". No successor was named. Craig, the Chief Accounting Officer, held it for about a year while "internal and external CFO candidates" were evaluated. The gap overlapped the CEO change.
- **MI is the hot spot.** Cheung (to 2021), then Kansler (2022–Oct 2024), then Saha (Nov 2024–Jul 2026), then two co-heads. That is three leaders and one split in under five years, and two leaders plus the split in two years.
- **Insiders run the core; outsiders fill new capabilities.** Insiders: Cheung, Le Pallec (joined Ratings 1999), Ernsberger (joined Platts 1996), Saha, Craig, Eager (20+ years at CARFAX). Outsiders: Aboaf (State Street), Bhathena (FIS), Kim (Gartner and Thomson Reuters), Clay (Cboe). Every one of those outsiders arrived in 2025–26.
- **The target supplied leaders.** IHS Markit people got MI (Kansler), Mobility (Tavernier), Vice Chair (Yergin), four board seats, and later Chief Client Officer (Moore, from Markit). Its CEO, Lance Uggla, stayed one year as special adviser.

## 2. Profiles

### Douglas L. Peterson — CEO 2013 – Oct 2024
**Background:** joined in 2011 as President of Ratings ("rebuilt trust in the ratings agency", per his 2024 bio). **Talks about:** demand rising in crises, scale, "powering markets", AI inside existing franchises.
- **Crises raise demand.** "The demand for our ratings, benchmarks, research, data, and analytics is greater than ever during uncertain and volatile markets," (Oct 27 2020). And in 2019: "We don't let market disruptions interfere with our business plans." (May 2 2019, in a quarter when Ratings revenue fell 7%).
- **Scale is the strategy.** "This merger increases scale while rounding out our combined capabilities" (Nov 30 2020).
- **Optimism that misfired once.** The 2021 letter says "As great as last year was for our company, in many ways 2022 is shaping up to be even better." The 2022 letter admits "our financial performance in 2022 did not match the previous year."
- **AI as a natural extension.** "Generative AI was one of the biggest global news stories last year. It’s really a game changer." (2023 letter).
- **On his own exit:** "The Board and I have had a robust succession process in place for many years, and now is the ideal time for this transition." (Jun 27 2024).
- **Record, as S&P states it** (Jun 27 2024): revenue $4.45B → $12.50B; adjusted EPS $2.75 → $12.60; more than $30B returned; market cap $16B → over $135B.

### Martina L. Cheung — CEO from Nov 1 2024
**Path:** joined 2010 as VP Operations, Ratings → Chief Strategy Officer → Head of Risk Services, MI → President of MI (to 2021; "the division exceeded $2 billion in annual revenue") → Head of the ESG team (Dec 2020) and Sustainable1 sponsor → President of Ratings (Feb 2022) → CEO. The succession release also credits her as "instrumental" in the SNL (2015) and IHS Markit (2022) deals. **Talks about:** data and technology serving the customer, then AI raising the value of proprietary data, then "trusted, connected" data.
- 2020 (running MI): "Technology and data science are playing a crucial role in how we bring value to our customers," (May 14 2020).
- 2021 (ESG peak): "The launch of S&P Global Sustainable1 signifies the critical intersection between sustainability and business performance" (Apr 22 2021).
- 2024 (first letter as CEO): "I didn’t know you do that, too." She says CEOs often told her this, and she uses it as the reason for the Chief Client Office. Also: "GenAI will have profound and accelerating effects across all sectors of the economy and in society."
- 2025: "Separating Mobility will allow us to continue to focus on our core businesses and pursue our growth strategy," (Apr 29 2025). And "we are seeing more tailwinds than headwinds." (2025 letter).
- 2026: "In a world where data is abundant, but not all created equal, customers need trusted, connected, essential intelligence that brings context and conviction to decision-making" (Jul 6 2026). In the same release: "These changes within Market Intelligence align our capabilities, enabling us to elevate the customer experience while growing our revenue and improving margins."
- **Culture line:** "One of the first things I did as CEO was to affirm my commitment to fostering a culture where our people feel valued." (2024 letter). She calls it the *People Forward* philosophy.

### Richard E. (Dick) Thornburgh — director 2011–25, Chair Oct 2020 – May 2025
Ex-Credit Suisse CFO and CRO. Chaired the board through the merger and the CEO selection.
- On his appointment (Sep 30 2020), the release says the board was overseeing a move "from a conglomerate to an operating company model."
- On Peterson: "Doug led the evolution of our Company from a diversified publishing holding company to a focused, markets-oriented information services company." (Jun 27 2024). And: "He was bold while remaining humble." (2024 letter).
- On Cheung: "Martina is the right leader to take the baton from Doug and lead S&P Global." (2024 letter).

### Ian P. Livingston — Chair from May 7 2025
Director since 2020. Ex-CEO and CFO of BT Group, ex-UK trade minister.
- "In a fast-changing world, S&P Global's mission to provide Essential Intelligence has never been more important." (Mar 25 2025).
- On AI: "the growth of artificial intelligence, which provides significant opportunities for the business and some potential challenges." (2025 letter). He names blockchain and quantum computing as other areas the board is watching.

### Ewout Steenbergen — CFO Nov 2016 – Mar 2024
Credited with the Kensho deal, the IHS Markit merger, climate-adjusted EPS and the Vitality Index. Also ran Engineering Solutions before it was sold in 2023.
- "I have full confidence in the future of the company." (Dec 7 2023, the exit release).
- ESG voice in 2021: "Linking our line of credit to SBTi-verified targets makes tangible S&P Global's commitment to becoming net-zero by 2040," (Apr 26 2021).

### Christopher Craig (interim CFO 2024–25) and Eric Aboaf (CFO from Feb 2025)
- **Craig:** Chief Accounting Officer, 13+ years at the company. Peterson: "Chris has been instrumental in developing our financial management strategy" (Feb 2 2024). No public quote from Craig.
- **Aboaf:** CFO of State Street for nearly eight years; earlier Citizens and Citi Treasurer. He was also given Strategy, M&A and Ventures, a scope taken from Moore. Cheung praised his "deep understanding of the markets and regulatory landscape in which we operate" (Oct 15 2024). There is no Aboaf quote in the corpus. He presented the financial targets at Investor Day 2025.

### Adam Kansler — President of MI, Feb 2022 – Oct 2024
Was IHS Markit's President of Financial Services, and named to run the combined MI on Dec 16 2020.
- "We are highly disciplined in reviewing our businesses and focusing our investment in our core areas of growth." (Feb 20 2024: buying Visible Alpha while exploring a sale of Fincentric).
- 2023 on private markets: "the scale of private market financing has reached a point where knowledge of these markets is essential." (Apr 18 2023).
- Replaced eight months after the Visible Alpha quote. Special adviser to March 2025.

### Saugata Saha — Platts/Commodity Insights 2021–24, MI and Chief Enterprise Data Officer 2024–26
Ex-McKinsey. Was CFO of MI and of Platts, and ran FP&A and corporate strategy. The release credits his Commodity Insights years with "growing annual revenue by 10% since the merger".
- "The expansion of private credit and the emergence of secondary trading necessitates common identifiers," (Jun 24 2025).
- "Data consumption is not one-size-fits-all; our customers require sophisticated, AI-enabled solutions" (Jul 17 2025).
- Left Jul 30 2026 "to pursue another opportunity". Instead of naming a new MI president, the company split MI and moved the Enterprise Data Organization under the CTTO.

### Sally Moore — Strategy/M&A → Chief Client Officer (Nov 2024) → MI co-head (Jul 2026)
Joined Markit in 2006 and ran its European credit and global loan businesses. Before that, Thomson Reuters LPC.
- "We've heard from our clients that fragmented data and inconsistent reporting standards are creating operational inefficiencies" (Sep 15 2025).
- "The use of AI in financial services is rapidly accelerating and evolving, from tightly controlled workflows to fully autonomous, multi-agent systems" (Jul 21 2026).
- "We are meeting clients where they work, embedding S&P Global's trusted, high-quality data directly into their workflows" (Aug 12 2026).

### Darren Thomas — Head of Lending Solutions → Enterprise Solutions → MI co-head and ELT (Jul 2026)

### Bhavesh Dayalji (Kensho), Swamy Kocherlakota (digital) and Firdaus Bhathena (CTTO)
- **Dayalji:** CEO of Kensho, and Chief AI Officer from late 2023. On Jul 6 2026 the title became "Head of Kensho Data & Intelligence". Main lines: "we want to make our data more easily and immediately accessible to the entire LLM ecosystem," (Nov 13 2024). "S&P Global is committed to meeting our customers where their workflows take place," (Jul 15 2025). "For S&P Global, the data retrieval layer is only the beginning." (Jul 21 2026).
- **Kocherlakota:** CIO, then Chief Digital Solutions Officer and sponsor of AI and Kensho (late 2023). Previously Visa and BNY Mellon. "AI is undeniably accelerating progress and driving transformative change across various sectors," (Dec 4 2024). Kocherlakota is not named in any 2026 release in the corpus, and the 2026 technology structure puts Bhathena on top.
- **Bhathena:** a new role, effective Apr 27 2026. Previously FIS Global CTO (24,000 staff) and CVS Health. "build a world-class, unified technology engine that harnesses the full power of data and AI" (Mar 31 2026). The data organization moved under Bhathena eight weeks later.

### Dan Draper → Catherine Clay (S&P Dow Jones Indices) and Cameron Drinkwater (product)

## 4. Communication style and narrative craft

**Peterson: the macro statesman.** He opens with the world ("two wars, the polarization of politics"), ties it to demand, then lists metrics. His slogans are strategic frames, not products: *Powering the Markets of the Future*, then *Powering Global Markets*. He admits weakness once ("did not match") and reframes it in the same sentence as discipline.

**Cheung: the operator-integrator.** Her slogans are about structure: Chief Client Office, Enterprise Data Organization, CTTO, *People Forward*. She likes anecdotes from customers ("I didn’t know you do that, too."). Her strategy is stated as three objectives and every release maps to one. She names margin openly. "improving margins" is in the July 2026 reorg quote, where Peterson-era releases would have said growth.

**House phrases and first sightings:**
- *essential intelligence*: boilerplate from 2019. A capitalised brand by 2025 (Livingston).
- *make decisions with conviction*: corporate wording. The divisions still say *confidence* in 2026.
- *power(ing) the markets of the future* (2019–21) → *powering global markets* (2022–24) → *Advancing Essential Intelligence* (2025–).
- *meeting customers where they are / where their workflows take place*: Dayalji, Mounts, Eramo, Moore, 2025–26.
- *trusted*: the most frequent adjective in 2026 AI and digital-asset releases.

**What they never talk about:**
- Why each of these left: Steenbergen, Kansler, Saha, Draper, Tavernier.
- The pricing business and the MarketAxess deal after October 2024.
- Fee levels, client churn and headcount after the merger (the 2025 chairman letter gives "more than 40,000 people").
- Analysts' questions (the corpus has no call transcripts).

## 5. Culture and talent, as the leaders describe it

- **Values change with the merger.** In 2019 the values were *excellence, relevance, integrity* (2019 letter). The 2021 merger letter adds new values, *discovery, partnership, integrity*. The purpose is "Accelerate Progress" (2023, O'Hanlon: "Our company's purpose – to accelerate progress –").
- **Purpose function downgraded.** The Chief Purpose Officer (Manis) left in Oct 2024 and the role was not refilled. Corporate Responsibility disclosures moved under the CFO.
- **People function upgraded.** Girish Ganesan became Chief People Officer (2024). Mandatory "AI for Everyone" training followed (2025), and StepForward ($10M, Dec 2025): "StepForward extends our People Forward philosophy into global communities".
- **Integration as culture.** IHS Markit brought over 15,000 people (2024 letter). Ex-target executives held MI, Mobility and the Vice Chair seat. Board town halls ("1,500 employees" at the Javits Center, 2023 chairman letter) are the board's culture check.
- **Absent:** attrition, return-to-office, integration friction, and why the President role in MI stayed empty after Saha.

## 6. Leadership lenses

- [[canon-kotter-leading-change]]: the CEO change shows Kotter's guiding coalition. There was a public plan, a long overlap and a named successor. The MI reorg shows the opposite: a sudden exit, "We expect to move quickly", and a structure in place of a person.
- [[canon-grove-high-output-management]]: Cheung's structural moves (CCO, EDO, CTTO) are Grove's *managerial leverage*. Each one is a lever affecting many divisions. The cost is a thinner bench of division presidents.
- [[canon-pfeffer-power]]: power moved from divisions to enterprise functions (client, data, technology). Watch who reports to whom. The EDO moved from the MI president to the CTTO in eight weeks.
- [[canon-schein-organizational-culture]]: the artifacts are *People Forward*, Spark Assist and the town halls. The espoused value is "Essential Intelligence". The basic assumption is that *the data is the moat*. The 2026 Farsight quote ([[spglobal-outside-voices]]) challenges exactly that assumption.
- [[canon-drucker-effective-executive]]: Drucker's *first things first*. Cheung's first year was subtraction (Mobility, OSTTRA, software sales) before addition (With Intelligence).
- [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]]: the S&P letters re-theme every year ("Resilience", "Uncertain World, Discover Opportunity", "Let’s Unlock Potential Together"). Unlike Bezos, they never re-attach or score earlier promises. The ESG targets of 2021–22 are never revisited by name.
- [[canon-heath-switch]]: the CCO "I didn’t know you do that, too." anecdote is Heath's *find the bright spots / shape the path*. Customers did not know the catalogue, so the change is in go-to-market, not product.
