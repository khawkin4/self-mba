# Note · ICE through outside eyes — rivals, regulators and customers invited onto ICE's own stage say more than its partners do; the people with real power over ICE (antitrust, mandates, insourcing customers, shareholders) mostly speak through actions, not quotes

> Stakeholder / outside-view note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy: coopetition with CME, Tradeweb, Platts; buyer power of insourcing customers), **Course 08** (Negotiation: deal-counterparty testimonials, the FTC remedy), **Course 11** (Global Strategy & Macro: ADNOC, Texas, the Trump–Xi delegation), **E4** (Governance: the shareholder veto of eBay), **E8** (Stakeholder & IR: whose voice ICE prints, and where), **E9** (Geopolitics: sovereign producers and regulators as gatekeepers) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[ice-hub]]; companions [[ice-people]], [[ice-mission-forces]], [[ice-marketaxess-deal]], [[tradeweb-outside-voices]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/ice/articles.jsonl` — 783 items (614 IR releases, 112 Insights, 13 ICE Vox, 8 features, 29 podcast transcripts, 7 shareholder letters), 2018-03 → 2026-10-22. Read in full for this note: the outside-guest podcasts (Lukken 2018-03-15; Lutnick 2021-06-07; Wigglesworth 2022-03-14; Adeyemo 2022-11-17; Duffy 2023-03-09; Broeksmit 2023-03-20; Behnam 2023-08-14; Hasker 2024-07-08; McPartland as host 2026-01-14; LaValle 2026-02-04; Portney 2026-04-27), the ICE-executive episodes that carry outside clips (Edmonds 2022-06-27; Sprecher 2024-08-12), the deal releases (Ellie Mae 2020-08-06, Black Knight 2022-05-04, the FTC/Constellation/PennyMac releases of 2023, Circle 2025-03-27, Polymarket 2025-10-07 and 2026-02-11, OKX 2026-03-05, Securitize 2026-03-24, MarketAxess 2024-08-05 and 2026-07-30), the eBay statements (2020-02-04/06), and ~25 customer-quote releases. **No press coverage, sell-side research or critic is in the corpus** — every voice was published by ICE.
- **Selection-bias warning (applies to every line):** ICE chose all of these voices, and the podcast host was ICE's head of communications until Aug 2024. Deal quotes are co-drafted; customer quotes are launch testimonials; guests were booked by ICE. Yet the podcast format lets guests talk for 40+ minutes, so a few uncomfortable sentences survive — CME's chair on ICE's tech bet, the MBA chief calling the industry "painfully slow" at ICE's own conference, an analyst's statistic disputed on air. There is **no** FTC quote, no MarketAxess comment before 2024, no PennyMac side of the story, no investor quote on eBay, and no FICC or Bloomberg voice. Read everything as "what this speaker was willing to say in ICE's house."
- **Core claim:** Outside voices fall into four groups. (1) **Peers and regulators on the podcast** (Duffy, Behnam, Lukken, Lutnick, Hasker, Portney, Adeyemo) agree with ICE's market-structure creed — intermediated clearing, regulated wrappers, data moats — and disagree only on technology (Duffy: cloud) and crypto timing. (2) **Deal counterparties** (Concannon, Coplan, Star Xu, Jabbour, Corr, Spaht, Allaire, Domingo) praise ICE's *trust brand*; their incentive is the price or the legitimacy. (3) **Customers** (BlackRock, T. Rowe, Apollo, JPM, Goldman, Citi, NatWest; UWM, Fifth Third, Lennar, Carrington) praise standardization and integration — and two of the mortgage names are evidence of buyer power (Carrington *returned*; UWM took servicing in-house). (4) **Skeptics** are rare and mostly about the *industry* ICE serves (Broeksmit, Wigglesworth, LaValle's former view). The real power over ICE sits with actors who are never quoted: the FTC, the SEC's Treasury-clearing mandate, the FCA/ESMA as benchmark licensors, customers who can insource (PennyMac), and shareholders who killed the eBay approach in 48 hours.

## 2. What customers value — and where their power shows

**What they value (in their words):**
1. **Standardization of plumbing.** Cohen: “As the growth of the ETF industry accelerates, it is essential for the infrastructure powering the market to scale rapidly.” (2019-10-28). Goldman's Amy Hong: “ICE ETF Hub enables us to improve operational efficiencies in the primary markets for ETFs through standardization and modernization of processes.” (2020-03-02). JPM's Christopher Berthe: “With ETFs being an important aspect of our franchise strategy, we are always looking for ways to introduce efficiency into our operating model” (2019-10-28).
2. **Benchmarks everyone can point to.** NatWest's Phil Lloyd on compound SONIA indexes: “To have a standardized set of compound SONIA indexes that everyone in the whole market can point to” (2021-04-13). Citi's Mariam Rafi on CDX options clearing: “We’re excited to work with ICE Clear Credit as they introduce and build an innovative solution for clearing of CDS Index Options” (2021-02-17).
3. **Data for new asset classes.** Pybus (iShares): fixed-income ETFs “are expected to grow to $6 trillion in AUM by 2030” (2025-06-03). Middleton (T. Rowe, ICE Compass anchor): “supports our continued evolution toward a more transparent, data-driven, and scalable trading model.” (2026-06-09). Needleman (Apollo, private credit): “to enable an investor experience for private credit that more closely resembles that of public markets.” (2026-07-27).
4. **Integration across the loan life.** Plum (Fifth Third): “ICE’s approach to modernizing housing finance with integrated technology and seamless data sharing aligned perfectly with what we’re seeking to accomplish” (2024-03-19). Escobar (Lennar): “We also see the clear benefit in leveraging the deep integrations between Encompass and MSP.” (2024-03-18).

**Where customer power shows (actions, not quotes):**
- **Leaving and returning.** Carrington *returns* to Encompass (2024-01-16) — Rose: “We’ve watched as ICE has made significant investments in Encompass over the years,” — which means it had left. ICE's own Black Knight Q&A concedes rivals “who can and do take accounts away from both ICE and Black Knight” (2022-07-07).
- **Insourcing.** PennyMac built its own servicing system; an arbitrator awarded ICE $155M because the build used MSP confidential information. ICE's line: “PennyMac is a former client of Black Knight” (2023-12-05). UWM's win is phrased as taking servicing in-house: “We are excited to partner with ICE Mortgage Technology to bring our servicing portfolio in-house.” (2025-04-30) — ICE sells the tools to the customer who would otherwise outsource to a servicer.
- **Anchor clients shape products.** BlackRock (ETF Hub 2019), T. Rowe (Compass 2026), Apollo (private-credit IDs 2026) all appear at launch. *Inference:* as in Tradeweb's case, the anchor gets design influence and publicity; ICE gets a reference customer.
- **Mortgage industry pushback on its own stage.** Broeksmit, opening at ICE Experience 2023: “I hesitate to say it given that I'm talking at the ICE Mortgage Technology Experience” — then “the mortgage business has not been a poster child for adoption of digitization. I think we've been painfully slow.” and “our cost to produce alone has remained stubbornly high” (2023-03-20). Broeksmit also: “Jay Powell can lower rates all he wants, but that doesn't lower your mortgage payment.” This is the customer base explaining why ICE's mortgage thesis takes longer than the deal models ([[ice-mortgage-technology]]).

## 4. Skeptics and independent framing

**Every critical or cautionary outside line I could find (complete set):**
1. **Broeksmit (MBA, 2023-03-20)** — "painfully slow," costs "stubbornly high," on ICE's stage (§2).
2. **Wigglesworth (FT, 2022-03-14)** — passive concentration: “I talk about a big three, but it's really a big two.” and the unease line. Relevance: ICE's index business rides passive flows ($794B benchmarked to ICE indices by 2025, per digest); the critique is aimed at BlackRock and Vanguard, ICE's anchor customers. On Barclays selling BGI: “Unambiguously one of the dumbest sales, I think, in the history of the financial industry”.
3. **Duffy (CME, 2023-03-09)** — the technology dissent: “I think that ICE has a different viewpoint on this when they acquired IDC for their data business and technology needs” and the cloud line. On crypto: “everything needs to have a use case.”
4. **McPartland (Coalition Greenwich, 2026-01-14)** — the consensus statistic: “So investment-grade corporate bonds, about half or so trade electronically today. It's about a third of high yield.” Edmonds disputes it on air (3.2M bonds priced, 25–40K trade). *Read:* the outsider's number is the market's; ICE's rebuttal is that the denominator should be all bonds — the mirror of Tradeweb's TRACE-denominator argument ([[tradeweb-credit]]).
5. **LaValle (CoinDesk Indices, 2026-02-04)** — LaValle's earlier view of tokenization: “I would say it's a solution looking for a problem”; still wary: “The gamification of trading and the desire for risk somewhat concerned me”.
6. **Lutnick (Cantor/BGC, 2021-06-07)** — on crypto: “What does Bitcoin have? Nothing.” — four years before ICE's Polymarket and OKX stakes. On resilience: “most people's disaster recovery plans are a disaster.”
7. **Crypto natives, relayed by Blaugrund (2026-02-18)** — the NYSE permissioned chain is “just kind of window dressing.”
8. **A Texas legislator to Edmonds (Uri hearing clip, 2022-06-27)** — “You are testifying that it doesn't matter what we do.”
9. **IPC's Kurt Adams relaying a client's complaint about IPC's own trading-desk stack (2025-02-03, ICE Voice partner episode)** — “Why do you force fragmentation into the market?”

**Quoted authorities ICE uses to frame its case:**
- **Group of 30**, in Hamill's Treasury-clearing piece: “The Treasury market became essentially dysfunctional.” (Sept. 3, 2025, in-text) — used to argue for a second CCP.
- **Tim Geithner**, as retold by Sprecher: “if you build a big barn and you open those barn doors, I will make sure the horses run right in front of you.” (2024-08-12) — the founding parable for regulation-led entry.
- **Gary Gensler** (clip on T+1): “we're just back to where we were in the 1920s.” — Sprecher's gloss: “We don't always agree” (2024-08-12).
- **Matt Levine**, read by host King: “the traditional finance is about the extension of credit and crypto is not.” (2022-06-27).
- **Warren Buffett**, via Edmonds's mother on CDS: “Warren Buffet called those weapons of mass destruction this morning.” (2022-06-27, spelling as transcribed).
- **Sharon Bowen** (ICE director, ex-CFTC) on regulation: “there's no Democratic answer or Republican answer, there's a right answer” (2022-02-14) — four years before NYSE Texas and the Trump–Xi trip ([[ice-people]]).

**Framing over time (podcast era):** 2018–21 *founder's exchange* (Lukken, Lutnick: clearing works, electronification is a barbell) → 2022–23 *crypto contest* (FTX hearings, Behnam, Duffy as ally) → 2023–24 *mortgage reality check* (Broeksmit, Walden) → 2026 *data and tokens* (McPartland, LaValle, Portney). The guests track ICE's capital allocation, two years behind it.

## 6. Competitors as seen in the corpus

- **Tradeweb.** Partner in data (closing prices 2019, CMT 2020, Term SONIA 2020, Term SOFR 2022, inflation swap rates 2026). Named as a rival exactly once: Jackson's “those being MarketAxess Bloomberg and Tradeweb ... tend to be RFQ-driven.” (2018-10-25). Two Tradeweb executives are quoted in ICE releases (Olesky 2019, Schirf 2020). Tradeweb's own newsroom calls ICE a muni rival and benchmark partner ([[tradeweb-outside-voices]]).
- **MarketAxess.** Silent until the 2024 network link, then the acquisition. No pre-2024 comparison of share or protocols in any ICE item.
- **Bloomberg.** Implied only: the RFQ trio (2018), a news source in podcasts, "costly terminals"-style framing in data pieces. Never quoted. See [[bloomberg-hub]].
- **CME.** Implied in product releases (HOU against "the traditional WTI Cushing benchmark," Barbuto 2025-07-18; regional gas vs Henry Hub, Jackson 2018); explicit only when Duffy visits and King keeps score.
- **Nasdaq.** Sprecher on the pre-ICE NYSE: “the NYSE was nowhere with tech entrepreneurs. They went over to our competitor.” (2018-04-03).
- **FICC / DTCC.** The incumbent in Treasury clearing is never named; Edmonds's framing is customers freed from “the terms that are being dictated to them” (2026-01-14) and Hamill's: existing models “are regarded as somewhat basic compared to the advanced portfolio risk management” (2025-09-03). DTCC appears as a tokenization partner instead.
- **Constellation Software.** Created as a rival by the FTC remedy (Empower LOS, Optimal Blue), partly financed by ICE's $500M note. Eight months later ICE's President announces a rebuilt pricing engine: “Margins have been compressed to historic lows” for lenders (Jackson, 2024-05-15) — ICE re-entering the product category it had to sell (inference).
- **Other index providers.** “$28 billion in AUM transitioned to our indices from other index providers” (2023 letter) — S&P, MSCI, Bloomberg implied, never named ([[spglobal-hub]]).
- **BGC / Cantor.** Lutnick is a guest, not a rival, on air.
- **Conspicuously absent:** any competitor criticizing ICE; any client saying it moved flow away (except via Carrington's return); any regulator criticizing ICE on the record; antitrust commentary on MarketAxess.

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): CME is ally (FTX, gas caps) and rival (WTI, rates, market cap); Tradeweb is data supplier and RFQ rival; Platts is licensor and price-reporting peer; Constellation is a rival ICE financed. Almost every node in ICE's value net sits in two boxes.
- **Buyer power** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): the mortgage base is fragmented (6,500 lenders per Tyrrell) but its largest players can insource (PennyMac, UWM). Concentrated buy-side anchors (BlackRock) shape ETF and index plumbing.
- **Power-dependence** ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]): the FTC and SEC score highest (no alternative to their approval); the crypto investees depend on ICE more than the reverse.
- **Testimonials** ([[canon-cialdini-influence]]): authority (Geithner, G30, CFTC chair) and social proof (BlackRock, Goldman, JPM at launches) do the persuading; discount sellers' and investees' praise.
- **Information goods** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): Hasker's tech-debt test and LSEG-as-customer line, Duffy's cloud bet and ICE's proprietary stack are all arguments about who owns the data layer.
- **Selection** ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]): WYSIATI — the absence of FTC, FICC, Bloomberg or PennyMac voices is the corpus design, not the world.

### Rep
**Part A (Course 08, the remedy that funded a rival).** ICE agreed to buy Black Knight at $85/share (~$13.1B, 2022-05-04), repriced to $75 after the FTC suit, and closed at ~$11.9B (2023-09-05). Optimal Blue went to Constellation for $700M, of which $500M was an ICE note. Compute (1) the headline price cut in percent, (2) the share of the Optimal Blue price ICE financed, (3) what an FTC economist would say ICE retained.
*Answer: (1) (85−75)/85 = 11.8%. (2) 500/700 = 71%. (3) ICE kept a creditor's claim on a now-independent rival and, by 2024-05-15, a rebuilt in-house pricing engine — the FTC got structural separation, but ICE kept economic exposure and the option to re-compete.*

**Part B (E8, whose voice gets printed).** From §4, list the nine critical outside lines. For each, mark whether the target is (a) ICE, (b) ICE's customers' industry, or (c) a third party. Then write two sentences on what share of criticism ICE allows to be about itself.
*Expected tally: Duffy (tech choice), McPartland (indirectly, by being disputed), the crypto natives (window dressing) and the Texas legislator target ICE — 4 of 9; the rest target mortgage lenders, passive managers, crypto, or a partner's product (IPC). None concerns ICE's fees, data prices or antitrust.*

Using only the corpus, draft three questions for ICE's next investor day on the cloud/IDC choice, FICC competition and MarketAxess.
