# Note · LSEG's exchange and digital-markets bets — a shrinking UK listings franchise answered with private markets, 24/5 trading, tokenised cash and a digital depository; lots of firsts, no revenue yet

> Market-structure / innovation note · Maps to **Course 10** (Entrepreneurship & Venture — option bets inside an incumbent), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — venue competition, 24-hour trading), **Course 11** (Global Strategy — London as a financial centre, UK listing reform), **E10** (Digital Transformation — DLT, tokenisation, agentic trading), **E9** (policy) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[lseg-hub]]; compare [[market-structure-24-5-trading]], [[market-structure-24-5-institutional-response]], [[tradeweb-tech-ai-data]]

- **Source:** LSEG corpus. Capital Markets/Equities releases (IPO reviews, UK Capital Markets Industry Taskforce 2022-07-19, Turquoise, the PISCES approval 2025-08-26, Wayve/Moneybox first trades 2026-07), the digital releases (AAX 2019, FTSE DAR 2020, LCH DigitalAssetClear 2023–25, DMI 2025-09-15, DiSH 2026-01-15, DSD 2026-02-12, Canton Super Validator 2026-09-28, Partior 2026-09-17), LSE 24 (2026-07-21), UK tokenised equities with Payward (2026-09-01), results calls. **Caveat:** none of these businesses reports revenue separately. All volume and participant numbers are LSEG's.
- **Core claim:** The original London Stock Exchange is now LSEG's smallest major business. In the old Capital Markets segment, equities were ~3% of 2022 revenue (CMD), against Tradeweb's ~13%. It has lived through a weak UK IPO market: 74 new issues in 2022 vs 174 in 2021, money raised £10.7bn vs £34.8bn; "2023 was a poor year globally for IPOs". LSEG's response is a portfolio of first-mover options:
  - a regulated **private-company market** (PISCES, first approval 2025; first trades 2026);
  - **DLT fund infrastructure** on Azure (DMI, 2025);
  - **tokenised commercial-bank cash** (DiSH, 2026);
  - an on-chain **Digital Securities Depository** (2026);
  - **LSE 24**, a 24/5 venue for "digital, algorithmic and agentic trading" (ETPs H1 2027);
  - **UK tokenised equity structures**, including listing Kraken-parent Payward's xStocks in 2027.

  The common logic is a "funding continuum" from private to public and from traditional to digital. All of it is pre-revenue or pilot-stage, and the core UK listing problem is a policy question LSEG can only lobby on.

## 2. The thesis in LSEG's words

- **Continuum.** "In private markets, we are developing an innovative trading venue for private companies to create a funding continuum between private and public markets." (Schwimmer, AGM 2024-04-25). "Our ambition is to be the first global exchange group that is genuinely indifferent as to whether a company is public or private" (Roos, 2022-03-16).
- **Tokenise everything, slowly and under regulation.** "LSEG is building to a future where most bonds issued and available on exchanges – and eventually most securities – are tokenised" (DSD release, 2026-02-12). "Tokenisation has the potential to change how investors access, and how issuers use, financial markets" (Hoggett, 2026-09-01).
- **Regulated venue for agents.** LSE 24 is for "digital, algorithmic and agentic trading" and will "enable secure native connectivity for the next generation of agent-based trading capabilities" (2026-07-21).
- **The neutral cash leg.** "LSEG DiSH provides the neutral, trusted third-party option required to connect independent payment ecosystems securely with the potential to do so at scale." (Andrew Williams, PTS, 2026-09-17)

## 3. Why the old franchise needed options

- **Equities are small and pressured.** Equities -11.6% (Q1 2023), -8.6% (Q3 2023), then +8.5% (Q3 2024) and +9.4% (Q3 2026) as markets recovered. SETS yield fell from 0.73bp to 0.66bp (AR 2022), and LSEG lost UK cash-equity trading share (FY22 call).
- **Listings depend on UK policy.** The Taskforce, FCA reform and PISCES are all lobbying outcomes. The 2019 risk register named competition for primary listings. It disappeared as a standalone risk afterwards ([[lseg-mission-forces]]).
- **24-hour pressure is coming from US venues and crypto.** See [[market-structure-24-5-trading]]. LSE 24 is a defensive answer that starts with ETPs, where London is a hub.

## 4. What the claims don't prove

1. **"First" is not "scale".** PISCES has its first approval and first trades. DMI has a first transaction. DiSH and DSD are PoCs or plans "subject to regulatory approval." The corpus gives no volume figures.
2. **Partner endorsements are launch quotes.** Barclays, Lloyds, NatWest and others commit to "exploring," not to volume.
3. **Agentic trading demand is asserted, not measured.**
4. **Tokenised equities with Payward/xStocks** sit in a regulatory grey zone (rights-preserving structure "being assessed").

## 5. Strategic read (inference)

- **These are cheap options on regulatory change**, built on assets LSEG already has: LCH and PTS for settlement, Azure via Microsoft for DMI, the exchange licence for PISCES and LSE 24. Each pays off only if UK or EU rules move. LSEG is positioned to be the regulated default if they do.
- **The real economic bet is DiSH/DSD as collateral plumbing, not retail tokens.** Tokenised cash for intraday repo and margin connects to LCH's €100bn+ of cash collateral. That is where volume and NTI live.
- **The identity cost.** In AR 2025 the founding business shrinks to "Digital & Securities Markets" under one executive. LSEG's narrative centre is data and AI, and the London Stock Exchange brand is now a policy platform for London as a financial centre.

## 6. Canon lenses

- **Options and experiments** ([[canon-ries-lean-startup]], [[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): many small, capped-downside bets with asymmetric upside if regulation shifts.
- **Disruption from below** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): crypto venues (24/7, tokenised) are the low-end entrant. LSE 24 and xStocks co-opt rather than resist.
- **Platform launch / chicken-and-egg** ([[canon-hagiu-wright-multi-sided-platforms]], [[canon-parker-platform-revolution]]): PISCES and DMI need issuers and investors at the same time. Apex Group and anchor banks are the seeding strategy.

## 7. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For:** an exchange group that owns a CCP, a depository-to-be and a cloud partner can build regulated tokenised rails faster than a crypto firm can earn a licence. Partior and Canton plug LSEG into where banks already experiment.
- **Against:** nothing here offsets UK listings decline at scale. Tokenisation pilots have run industry-wide since 2016 with little volume. LSE 24's first product (ETPs) is a niche.
