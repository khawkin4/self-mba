## 1 · Market Mondays — trading & market psychology (Ian Dunlap)
- **Risk-to-reward is the #1 indicator** (then # of trades/yr, then direction). Under a volatile regime, "4× your target" (risk 1 to make 15–20); under calm, compress targets. Biggest era-mistake: too-short targets.
- **Scaling-out / "house money":** start ~10 contracts; once up 100%, sell enough to recover principal, let the rest run; reinvest *realized* profit into longer-dated calls. Hard ~50% stop-loss.
- **LEAPS + shares + intermediate calls:** own shares (belief), a long-dated LEAP (past 1yr+1day for long-term cap-gains), plus shorter calls to capture intermediate moves. **10-year minimum hold** — "you're having a one-night stand with stocks you should be marrying." Weekly chart, MAs 72/210/420; the 210 is the buy-the-dip line. Don't over-indicator.
- **ETFs auto-rebalance into winners** (SMH/QQQ shift weights as leaders change) — the allocation map shows where institutions move money. **DCA automatically:** "the first thing you pay every month is what you buy into the market."
- **Relationships > information or talent** — a source at Nvidia HQ flagged ARM; acting on it is the edge ("information you don't act on is just entertainment"). **ARM thesis:** collects royalties on every chip (~92–97% margin, near-zero capex).
- **Trump as a *signal*, not a stock-picker:** be in quality AI names before the tweet; his tweets are "late validation." Crashes are "30–40 things stacked," not one (expects a correction ~2027). Buffett Indicator at all-time highs — but argues earnings justify it this cycle.
- **Character is a balance-sheet item** — leadership/relationships (Anthropic's Amazon/Google/Nvidia ties vs. OpenAI's exits) are a real investment variable. *(↔ [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]], [[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]].)*
- **Operational gotcha:** dormant accounts (~18mo–2yr) can be escheated to the state; keep money moving.

## 2 · The "9-to-5 Millionaire" — buy-and-hold rentals (Storm Leroy)
- **"Your job is your first business partner"** — the W-2 enables the mortgage, down payment, credit. Replacement-income math: $40K Section-8 house, ~$8K down, ~$600/mo net (~80% cash-on-cash); replace a $60K salary = ~10 properties; accelerate with tax refunds → out in 5 yrs.
- **Buy tenant-occupied Section-8** (govt rent never late, didn't miss in COVID; you inherit the certification). **Demand the rent roll** (3 lates/yr = red flag); trust it only with a property manager attached.
- **The "money team" (referral, none salaried):** realtor (off-market only), inspector, **+ send your PM with the inspector** (building vs. tenant condition; inspectors leak intel — an Amazon warehouse tip → 15 properties bought ahead of the news). PM = 10% fee, "they do 100% of the work, I keep 100% of my time."
- **Finance with hard money/commercial, not banks** ("banks make you a homeowner, not an investor"; the 10-mortgage limit is impractical). **Three-LLC structure:** Acquisition LLC (build entity credit) → Holding LLC in an anonymity state (NM/NV/WY/DE, asset protection) → Property-Mgmt LLC in home state (collect rent so a property freeze can't freeze cash; pay business-privilege tax not income tax). *(↔ [[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]] structure.)*
- **Rockefeller trust engine** (read the 1934 trust): on death, sell short-term holds → trust-owned brokerage for *grandchildren*; $1M @6% ×21yr ≈ $3.8M, take half each generation, rest compounds. Each generation opens its own trust + life-insurance on every member = "your own bank" heirs borrow from. Term policy sized to the mortgage so the main payout isn't eaten. Start **revocable**; "pour-over will"; title each holding LLC in the trust's name to auto-fund on death.
- **Mindset:** "surround yourself with people who can see your vision"; "guard your escape route" (stayed an extra year purely for medical benefits — medical bankruptcy is the real risk); group economics: "100 doors" ≈ 10 partners — spread capital across 5 properties for 5× equity/write-offs.

## 3 · $12K student loan → $40M real estate (Jude Bernard)
- **Refi/HELOC compounding loop:** buy below market → values rise ~50% → refinance to pull cash + equity → buy more. **HELOC vs. cash-out refi:** HELOC = revolving, interest only on the draw, lower cost; **draw your HELOCs proactively** (banks cancel them when values drop — happened 2007, pre-empted March 2020).
- **"Poor man's market analysis":** don't run demographics — watch what businesses move in (a Starbucks/coffee shop = they already did the analysis; "copy their homework").
- **Leverage real estate INTO stocks:** March 2020 pulled ~$1M+ from HELOCs into the COVID bottom (Amazon, Tesla, Apple, index funds). "Real estate and stocks are a one-two punch."
- **2007 lesson:** over-leverage took him from +$3M to ~-$1M; survived by grinding side hustles and keeping every property "by hook or by crook."
- **The 4 C's:** Credit, Collateral, **Connects** ("net worth is your network" — most important), Character ("if your name is mud, you're dead — the streets talk"). **"Wait for my pitch"** — in ~$100M of deals only twice bought something on the MLS ("if everybody can see it, it ain't my deal").
- **Assemblage chess move:** bought a $700K brownstone for $400K with no intent to use it — a neighbor wanting the block bought it for $900K ($100K in → $500K profit). **Sell Grandma's house only *with a purpose*** (redeploy the proceeds). Give-back: Brooklyn Bank financial-literacy nonprofit.

## 4 · NFL money reality & flipping (Brandon Copeland — NFL + Wharton)
- **NFL contracts are mostly fiction:** a "3yr/$1.2M" deal netted **~$30K** after being cut (non-guaranteed). Teams cut at **3:56 PM** to dodge the Tuesday-4PM-ET weekly guarantee. **Jock tax:** taxed at each *game state's* rate (CA ~13%; FL/TX/TN none; Detroit double-taxes).
- **Real estate for *control*** ("the only person I can blame is me"). **Rentals = marriage, flips = dating** — flip first to learn. Detroit case: $36K auction buy + ~$12K rehab → $127K sale (~115%).
- **The $30K-minimum margin rule:** only do deals you can clear ≥$30K on — the cushion absorbs surprises (people targeting $15K go red on the first change-order). "People generally can't do what they tell you they can."
- **Mentor pitch that works:** "I'm doing this with or without you — you can join, and I'll never call you about it again." **Add value beyond money** ("I'll organize your books for access"). **Bring family in as co-investors** so they care and learn the lens. Keep RE as *secondary* income so delays don't force bad decisions. **Teach to verify learning** (his Penn "Life 101" has students teach high-schoolers).

## 5 · Credit, completely (Shaunda Martin, FICO-certified)
- **FICO (lenders) ≠ Vantage (Credit Karma).** 5 factors: payment history **35%**, utilization **30%**, age **15%**, inquiries **10%**, mix **10%**.
- **Utilization reports once a month — on the *statement* date, not the due date.** Keep statement-date balance **<10%** (carry 1–3%, not $0 — a $0 reads as "doesn't use it"). Pay in full by the due date to avoid interest.
- **Base profile (~4 accounts → ~680–700):** 2 revolving cards + 1 short installment (<36mo) + 1 long installment; then **don't apply for 6–9 months** (age). **#1 mistake: applying too fast.** Never drop below ~3–4 open accounts (thin-file penalty).
- **One late payment ≈ up to −100 pts**, needs 11 on-time to offset; call for a **goodwill adjustment** before it posts at 30 days. **48% utilization = "maxed" to lenders** (each card over it ≈ −15 mortgage pts).
- **Credit-limit-increase recipe:** request every ~91–120 days; for 60 days prior run autopay + ≥2 recurring charges + ≥10 transactions/mo (low usage is the #1 denial reason).
- **Dispute the right way (FCRA §611):** never claim "not mine" unless true fraud; request *investigation* then *re-investigation demanding proof* (collectors often can't prove they own the debt).
- **Kids:** add as authorized user ~17 (6mo before 18) so they have a score at 18; on the 18th birthday open their *own* card; apply for a card *before* a student loan (a loan's term-end shrinks credit age). **Don't close old cards.** Avoid bought trade-lines (post-2009 law omits them from real FICO) and consolidation (kills age). Real reports free at **annualcreditreport.com**; real FICO at myFICO/Experian.

## 6 · The founders' core philosophy (Morning Hustle / BlackRock / CNBC)
- **"You can't save (or work) your way to wealth — you must invest and own."** Cash at ~2% vs. 8–10% inflation loses buying power. **Financial discipline > financial education.**
- **Owner vs. consumer:** find income *inside what you already do* (teacher → curriculum; agent → developer/broker). You already enrich LVMH/Apple by spending — own a share.
- **Three levers at month-end:** cut expenses / make more / **both**. **House hacking** (live in one unit of a multi-family, tenants cover the mortgage); start stocks with a few hundred in the **S&P 500 / index funds**; if you can't do RE solo, **partner**. Treat your brokerage like a recurring bill.
- **Trading ≠ investing:** people "lost money in crypto" by treating a long-term volatile asset like a trade. "Get-rich-quick = go-broke-fast." Hold strong opinions **loosely** (Munger: "no capacity for people who can't change their mind").
- **The mission stat:** 37% of Americans (72% of Black families) can't cover a $400 emergency; 68% of Black under-40 now invest (since EYL launched 2019). Goal = financial *fluency*, a language the masses can speak ("edutainment," teach-to-learn). **Follow the institutions** (the U.S. buying into Intel; ETF allocation maps) as validation.

## 7 · Contrarian: stocks > a home (Market Mondays clip)
- **Opportunity-cost thesis:** "Don't put more into a mortgage on a property you don't fully own than you put into the market." "Don't be house rich and pocket poor." Claim (attributed to Buffett): with $1M, stocks beat real estate ~95% of the time. **"You can't work your way to wealth."** Define *your* number; stop comparing to society's standard. RE caveat: buy only what you can afford — "a mortgage benefits the bank more than anybody." *(↔ [[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]], `learn/unit-05`, `unit-06`.)*

## Cross-episode themes (the 8 patterns)
1. **You can't save or work your way to wealth — invest and *own*.** (Every episode.) Own a share of what you already consume.
2. **Buy-and-hold long-term; the enemy is selling early.** Stocks (10-yr min, "marry don't date") and RE (hold decades; "don't sell Grandma's house without a purpose").
3. **Leverage + structure is the engine, not just the asset** — HELOC/refi recycling, hard money over banks, the 3-LLC + Rockefeller-trust + life-insurance architecture (appears in 3 episodes).
4. **Relationships/access beat talent or information** — "net worth is your network," off-market "wait for my pitch," source-driven trades. Information you don't *act* on is entertainment.
5. **Tax & fee literacy is where the wealthy quietly win** — jock tax, statement-date utilization, escheatment, depreciation/write-offs, business-privilege tax, holding losses to offset gains.
6. **Calculated risk with predefined stop-losses** — risk-to-reward first, the $30K margin cushion, "if it's too good to be true, it is." **Discipline > information.**
7. **Financial *fluency* (decoded, relatable, lifelong)** — explicitly aimed at the racial wealth gap ($400/72% stat).
8. **Follow the institutions/government as validation** — Trump tweets as "late validation," the U.S. into Intel, ETF rotation maps.
