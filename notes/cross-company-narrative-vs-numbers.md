# Note · Narrative vs numbers — every firm's story is a claim about its future growth, margins and risk; tested against the filings, three stories hold, four are unproven, and one broke

> Valuation / strategy note · Maps to **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — Damodaran's narrative-and-numbers method), **Course 05** (Marketing & Brand — positioning to investors and partners), **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations), **E7** (Executive Presence & Comms), **E3** (M&A — the story as deal currency) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of the cross-company set: [[cross-company-financials]], [[cross-company-say-vs-do]], [[cross-company-attention-map]], [[cross-company-capability-matrix]]

- **Source:**
  - **Narratives:** each company's mission-forces, people and hub notes (`notes/<company>-mission-forces.md` etc.) and the newsroom attention data.
  - **Numbers:** [[cross-company-financials]] (SEC XBRL, FY2019–Q2 2026) and the audited FY2025 revenue mix in [[cross-company-say-vs-do]].
  - **LSEG** from its annual reports; **Bloomberg** is private, so its narrative can't be tested against financials.
- **Core claim:** Narrative isn't decoration. Every firm uses it to do three jobs: to **set the multiple** investors apply ("technology and data company", not "exchange"), to **recruit partners** ("open", "neutral", "select smart"), and to **manage regulators** ("fortress", "fairness to markets"). Following Damodaran, each story is a claim about four value drivers: **growth, margins, reinvestment and risk**. Each can be checked.
  - **Supported:** Tradeweb's core, JPMorgan, and Thomson Reuters on margins.
  - **Partly supported:** ICE, S&P Global, Goldman, BlackRock.
  - **Not yet supported:** LSEG's AI story, and every firm's "frontier" claims (tokens, AI revenue).
  - **Broken:** MarketAxess, whose story ended in a sale.
  
  The most useful signal is how a narrative fails: **KPIs get redefined, the gap between adjusted and GAAP figures widens, payouts exceed free cash flow, and the core goes quiet in the newsroom.**

---

## 1. Method: turn each story into checkable drivers

For each firm:
1. State the **dominant narrative** in its own words, from the mission-forces notes.
2. Name its **audience**: investors (multiple), partners (trust and dependence), regulators (licence to operate).
3. Translate it into what it must imply for **growth, margin, reinvestment and risk**.
4. Test it against the filings.
5. Give a verdict: **Supported**, **Partly**, **Not yet** (plausible but no number shows it), or **Contradicted**.

This is Damodaran's discipline, applied across a set ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]] for separating luck from skill, [[canon-tetlock-superforecasting]] for scoring claims).

## 3. What narrative does that numbers can't

**1. It sets the multiple.** The same cash flow is valued differently as an "exchange", a "data company" or "AI infrastructure". ICE's noun changed three times (exchange → data → technology). LSEG's twice. S&P's slogan three times. Each change is a request for a different peer group.

**2. It recruits partners.** For counterparties, narrative works as a promise.
- **LSEG's "open"** tells Microsoft and the LLM labs it won't lock them out, while it builds a credit stack (Octus + Tradeweb + Workspace + FTSE prices) that looks a lot like ICE's ([[lseg-mission-forces]]).
- **Bloomberg's "fairness to markets"** made it a credible partner for the EU consolidated-tape bid, and a non-threatening partner for Clearwater.
- **Tradeweb's "select smart"** tells dealers, its liquidity providers and former owners, that it will protect them from information leakage. The A2A leakage whitepaper is the evidence offered for that promise ([[tradeweb-a2a-information-leakage]]).
- **ICE's "all-weather"** is aimed at lenders as much as shareholders. Debt-funded deals (Ellie Mae, Black Knight, MarketAxess) need lenders to believe cash flows are stable.

**3. It manages the licence to operate.** JPMorgan's "fortress" and "national security" framing, Goldman's return to "1979 principles", and BlackRock's silence on ESG after 2022 are all addressed to regulators and politicians as much as investors ([[blackrock-stewardship-backlash]], [[jpmc-capital-regulation]]).

**4. It recruits talent and sets the internal agenda.** "Frontier markets" at Tradeweb and "AI everywhere" at LSEG tell employees where careers are made. The attention map shows the internal agenda moving before the revenue does ([[cross-company-attention-map]]).

**So narrative creates real value when it changes behaviour:** partners integrate, regulators grant mandates, clients route flow. It destroys value when it **substitutes** for the numbers, so that the story keeps going while the drivers stall.

## 4. How a narrative breaks: the MarketAxess pattern, and who shows signs of it

MarketAxess shows the full sequence ([[marketaxess-strategy-financials]], [[marketaxess-mission-forces]]):
1. **The identity sentence stays fixed** ("preferred all-to-all") while the core driver (US credit share) declines.
2. **The scoreboard is redefined**: high-grade share → HG+HY → composite → "three channels". Each new metric flattered the result.
3. **"Records" continue** (17 straight record-revenue years) while margins and EPS fall.
4. **Capital return outruns cash**: a debt-funded buyback at 165% of FCF.
5. **The core goes quiet**: fee talk rises, credit-share talk falls.
6. **Resolution**: sale to ICE.

**Early-warning signals in the other firms (a watchlist, not a forecast):**

| Signal | Who shows it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| KPI demoted or retired | **LSEG** (ASV retired from 2027 after slowing); **Tradeweb** (2026 argument that the TRACE denominator is inflated) | [[lseg-ai-everywhere]], [[tradeweb-credit]] |
| Adjusted-vs-GAAP gap widening | **ICE** (mortgage ~1% GAAP vs 41% adjusted); **BlackRock** (GAAP margin −9pp) | [[ice-strategy-financials]], [[cross-company-financials]] |
| Payout above FCF | **S&P** 121%, **Thomson Reuters** 123%, **BlackRock** 112% (2019–25) | [[cross-company-financials]] §4. *Partly explained by asset sales; watch if it continues without them* |
| Core quiet, frontier loud | **S&P** (ratings 31% of revenue, ~7% of attention); **Tradeweb** (rates 63% vs 22%); **Thomson Reuters** (AI 59% of attention) | [[cross-company-say-vs-do]] |
| Narrative reversal | **BlackRock** ESG (58% → 0% of attention); **Goldman** purpose changed four times | [[blackrock-stewardship-backlash]], [[goldman-mission-forces]] |

None of these firms is MarketAxess. Their core drivers are still growing. But these are the places to look first if one of them starts to stall.

## 5. Narrative as a variable in investment and partnership decisions

**For an investor:**
1. Write the company's story as four numbers (growth, margin, reinvestment, risk).
2. Compare them with what the current price implies. That needs market data, the next step in [[cross-company-financials]] §8.
3. Re-test every quarter against the filings. A story that needs **new metrics every year** is in trouble; one that needs **new markets every year** may just be ambitious.

**For a partner:** test whether the story is backed by commitments that are costly to reverse:
- **Ownership:** LSEG holds 50.9% of Tradeweb; Goldman co-owns LSEG's post-trade business.
- **Exclusivity terms:** Reuters News is exclusive to LSEG's desktop to 2048; Tradeweb data moved from exclusive to non-exclusive in 2025.
- **Integration depth:** Tradeweb inside Aladdin; Tradeweb FTSE prices inside WGBI.
- **Behaviour under stress:** liquidity "dwindled" in 2022 (Bruni); Risk.net found a "dearth of bond liquidity" in March 2020 ([[tradeweb-outside-voices]]).

A narrative backed by those commitments is a real asset. Without them it's marketing.

**For a competitor:** the gap between narrative and numbers shows where a rival is distracted. ICE is telling a data story while energy pays its bills. S&P is telling an AI story while ratings carry it. MarketAxess told an "intelligent marketplace" story while US credit leaked away.

## 6. What this does not prove

1. **Verdicts are judgements** on public numbers over seven years. "Not yet" isn't failure: AI and tokenization revenue may simply be early.
2. **No market prices.** Whether each story is already in the share price, so that it can't move the price further, is untested. That's the biggest gap.
3. **Bloomberg is untestable** on financials.
4. **Narratives come from company-curated corpora.** The outside-voices notes are the only check, and they're thin on criticism.

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Narrative and numbers** (Damodaran, the curriculum's Course 02 source): every story implies a value. Value a story you can't put into numbers at zero.
- **Positioning** ([[canon-lafley-martin-playing-to-win]], [[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): a narrative is "where to play / how to win" made public. Rumelt's "bad strategy" is a narrative with no coherent actions behind it.
- **Influence and commitment** ([[canon-cialdini-influence]], [[canon-schelling-strategy-of-conflict]]): partners believe commitments, not claims. Schelling: credibility comes from options you have given up.
- **Forecasting** ([[canon-tetlock-superforecasting]]): turn each narrative into dated, checkable claims and score them.
- **Agency** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): management's story and shareholders' interests diverge most where pay is tied to adjusted metrics.

## 8. Open questions and next steps

1. **Price the stories:** add market data to estimate what growth and margins each share price implies, then compare those with the narrative's implied drivers.
2. **Proxy pay:** which metrics are executives paid on? That tells you which narrative they'll defend hardest.
3. **Log the claims:** put each "Not yet" verdict into `acumen/JUDGMENT-LEDGER.md` as a dated, checkable prediction. For example, "LSEG discloses AI or data-licensing revenue by FY2027" or "Tradeweb reports a digital-assets revenue line by FY2027".
