# Note · LSEG and Refinitiv — the $27bn all-share bet beat every synergy target and still did not change the growth rate of what it bought

> M&A / deal-anatomy note · Maps to **E3** (M&A, Deals & Restructuring — structure, remedies, integration, the overhang), **E2** (Capital Allocation — paying in stock, deleveraging, directed buybacks), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — synergy NPV, EV/EBITDA, accretion), **E5** (Transformation at Scale — 5.5k → 25k staff), **E4** (Governance — a seller holding ~37%) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[lseg-hub]]; companions [[lseg-capital-allocation]], [[lseg-workspace-data]], [[lseg-tradeweb-control]]

- **Source:** the LSEG corpus at `_ingest/raw/lseg/articles.jsonl`. It includes the full press archive from 2019 (the deal statements of 27 Jul and 1 Aug 2019, the HKEX letters, the EC and CFIUS updates, completion, the Borsa Italiana sale), results releases, the 2021–2026 call transcripts (investor education days Jul and Oct 2021, CMD Nov 2023, Innovation Forum Nov 2025) and the filtered 2019–2025 annual reports. **Caveat:** LSEG does not host transcripts from before Jul 2021, so the 2019 deal call is missing. The retrospective numbers are management's own.
- **Core claim:** LSEG paid in its own shares (EV ≈US$27bn, sellers got ~37% economic / <30% voting) for a data business its own executives later called under-managed. It fought off a rival bid (HKEX), sold Borsa Italiana to win EU approval, and beat every synergy target: £562m cost vs >£350m, £292m revenue vs £225m, per the Nov 2025 retrospective. Then it spent about £5bn absorbing the sellers' stock through buybacks. What did *not* change is the core growth rate. Data & Analytics grew about 5% in 2021 and still grows about 5% in 2026. The deal achieved diversification and scale, then margin and cash. It did not deliver the growth acceleration the "trebling" language implies.

## 2. Why LSEG bought it, in its own words

- **Rationale on the day.** "With the acquisition of Refinitiv, we will transform our position as a leading global Financial Markets Infrastructure group." (Schwimmer, 2019-08-01). The Chair: "This transaction is a defining moment for LSEG in terms of its strategic importance." (Don Robert, 2019-08-01)
- **What the seller's management thought the asset was.** "Our aim is to capture the opportunity of data which we believe is driving unprecedented change in the global financial community." (David Craig, Refinitiv CEO, 2019-08-01)
- **What LSEG later said it had actually bought.**
  - "Growth had been anaemic for many years with steady market share losses in a healthily growing industry." (Schwimmer, 2025-11-10)
  - "Much of what we've achieved over the last three years has been about fixing the basics and running an undermanaged business better." (Manz, CFO, 2023-11-16)
  - "Thomson Reuters viewed itself largely as a media business. We view ourselves as systemic market infrastructure." (Schwimmer, 2023-11-17)
- **Insiders on the legacy.** "This business has had a growth challenge. Revenues have declined in recent years" (Dean Berry, 2021-07-02). "Thomson Reuters, and then Refinitiv, lacked the capabilities to keep up" (Neill Penney, FX, 2021-10-01). "After a lack of focused attention prior to Blackstone, the business is transforming" (Andrea Remyn Stone, 2021-07-02).

## 3. The rival bid: HKEX, September 2019

HKEX made an unsolicited offer, about three-quarters in HKEX shares, conditional on LSEG dropping Refinitiv. LSEG's board rejected it within two days and published a letter whose arguments are worth studying as a template:
- **Strategy and value.** "The Board has fundamental concerns about the key aspects of the Conditional Proposal: strategy, deliverability, form of consideration and value." (Robert, 2019-09-13)
- **Process.** "We were very surprised and disappointed that you decided to publish your unsolicited proposal within two days of our receiving it." (Robert, letter to HKEX, 2019-09-13)
- **Deliverability.** LSEG named UK Enterprise Act review, CFIUS, Italy's "golden powers" and HKEX's government links as hurdles. It called HKEX's claim of a swift and certain deal "simply not credible."

*Read (inference):* HKEX offered a geographic exchange-to-exchange merger. LSEG's defence was the counter-strategy: become a multi-asset data company. The rejection is the moment LSEG commits to *data, not just exchanges*.

## 4. The price of approval: Borsa Italiana, and what came back

- The EC remedy was to sell Borsa Italiana (with MTS and CC&G) to Euronext for €4.325bn. Proceeds cut Refinitiv debt. LSEG: "We believe the sale of the Borsa Italiana group will contribute significantly to addressing the EU’s competition concerns." (Schwimmer, 2020-10-09)
- **Second-order cost:** Euronext became a clearing defector. It moved derivatives clearing away from LCH SA (announced 2021-11-08, "less than c. 1%" of revenue). That cost about £35m in 2024 and £30m in 2025 (calls), and LCH bought out Euronext's 11.1% of LCH SA for €111m (2023-06-26). See [[lseg-post-trade-clearing]].
- **Another second-order cost:** in April 2019 Borsa had been called "a core part of the Group." Eighteen months later it was a remedy.

| Leverage | back to 1–2x in 24–30 months | — | 3.3x → 1.9x in ~12 months |
| Integration cost | — | — | ~£1.4bn |
| D&A growth | 4–6% medium term (Jul 2021) | — | ~5% in 2021, ~5% in 2025–26 (Workflows ~3%) |
| Refinitiv D&A growth rate "trebled" | — | — | "trebling the growth rate of the Refinitiv Data & Analytics businesses we acquired" (CMD, 2023-11-16) |

The CFO's verdict in 2023: "the thesis that we had at acquisition has been proved out" (Manz, 2023-03-02). Proch's in 2025: "expectations were low off the back of the Refinitiv deal" (2025-11-10).

## 6. The overhang: a seller that owned a third of you

- The structure gave the sellers ~37% economic and <30% voting interest. That avoided a Takeover Code bid obligation (inference from the 30% threshold). They had a 2-year lock-up, then one-third per year, plus up to three board seats while holding ≥25%.
- Martin Brand (Blackstone) sat on LSEG's board until April 2026 while also having chaired Tradeweb until 2022 (see [[lseg-tradeweb-control]]).
- **The unwinding.** A pro-rata buyback from Aug 2022 was followed by Microsoft buying ~4% from the consortium (Dec 2022). Then came directed buybacks: £750m (Sep 2023), £500m (Mar 2024), £500m (May 2024). The Chair reported "The consortium’s holding has reduced from over 34% to around 6% today, following its latest share sale last month." (Robert, AGM 2024-04-25)
- *Read (inference):* the buybacks first served as orderly exit liquidity for the sellers. Only after Aug 2024 did they become a response to valuation (see [[lseg-capital-allocation]]).

## 7. What the claims don't prove

1. **Synergies are self-scored.** Revenue synergies are counted from "synergy-related products" LSEG itself classifies. Tracking stopped in 2025, just as the target window closed.
2. **"Trebled the growth rate" depends on the baseline.** Refinitiv's pre-deal D&A growth was close to zero (Trading & Banking was shrinking). Trebling ~1.5–2% gives about 5%, which is roughly market growth. Lefferts described strategic-account growth under Refinitiv as "the growth rate between 2018 and 2020 was 0%." (2023-11-17)
3. **Cost synergies overlap with normal cost-out.** Refinitiv was already running a $650m cost programme under Blackstone ($613m achieved by Sep 2020). Some of what LSEG booked was continuation.
4. **The ~11x EV/EBITDA figure is management's** (Nov 2025). It uses the 2019 EBITDA base, not the post-integration one.
5. **Integration cost is incomplete.** The £235m impairment in 2024 (FX and index re-platforming software "no longer in use") and the Euronext attrition are not in the £1.4bn.

## 8. Strategic read (inference)

- **The deal bought position, not momentum.** LSEG got real-time data distribution (#1 or #2 globally), FXall, Matching, Tradeweb control and World-Check. Its most dynamic assets after 2021 were the transactional ones (Tradeweb, SwapClear) and Risk Intelligence, not the desktop.
- **Paying in stock made the sellers the swing shareholder for three years.** That set the capital-return agenda (directed buybacks) and the board's composition. It also gave Microsoft a convenient way in.
- **The deal changed LSEG's identity before it changed its economics.** By 2026 LSEG describes itself as a data and AI company. 44% of income is D&A and about 73% is recurring, versus ~40% recurring in 2018.

## 9. Canon lenses

- **Winner's curse and integration** ([[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]]): a premium paid in shares by a buyer whose own shares rose ~29% on the news is a margin-of-safety question. Was the buyer's currency overvalued?
- **Strategy as a guiding policy** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): the HKEX letter is a clear diagnosis plus a guiding policy (multi-asset data, not regional exchange consolidation).
- **Agency** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): sellers with ~37% economics and board seats, and a lock-up timetable, created a principal–principal tension that the buybacks resolved.
- **Change at scale** ([[canon-kotter-leading-change]]): a 5x headcount jump, brand retirement (Refinitiv dropped 2023) and five D&A heads in five years.
- **Thinking in time** ([[canon-neustadt-may-thinking-in-time]]): Thomson's 2004 Tradeweb purchase, Thomson Reuters' 2018 sale to Blackstone and LSEG's 2019 purchase are the same *media/data parent* pattern repeating ([[tradeweb-hub]] §2).

## 10. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For the deal:** LSEG went from a European exchange group to a top-three global data company in two years. It delevered a year early and returned about £5bn without wrecking the balance sheet. Few FMI deals this size closed with a forced divestiture and still beat their cost targets.
- **Against:** the market judged the asset by growth. D&A stuck at ~5% plus the AI narrative pushed the shares into "dislocation" in 2025–26 (see [[lseg-ai-everywhere]]). The best growth asset bought, Tradeweb, was explicitly excluded from synergies.

## 11. Open questions

1. What did revenue synergies contribute after 2024, once tracking stopped? Is the ~5% D&A rate a ceiling?
2. Would LSEG have done better keeping Borsa Italiana and conceding something else? What did the EC actually require?
3. Octus ($3.535bn, 2026) is the next large data deal. Does it repeat Refinitiv's integration pattern ("light integration" for two years)?
