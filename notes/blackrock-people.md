# Note · BlackRock's people — a founder-CEO with no named heir; power is spreading to the CFO, the COO and acquired founders while the founder stays

> Leadership / org note · Maps to **E1** (Leading at Scale), **E4** (Corporate Governance & the Board), **Course 09** (Leadership & Organizational Behavior: power, succession), **E7** (Executive Presence) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[blackrock-hub]], [[blackrock-fink-letters]], [[blackrock-outside-voices]]

- **Source:** all in `_ingest/raw/blackrock/articles.jsonl`:
  - the 13 Fink letters;
  - the 2023 and 2025 Investor Day decks, specifically who presented;
  - AGM transcripts 2021–2023;
  - board and executive releases (Shedlin→Small 2022; Nasser 2023; Gerber 2023, 2024, 2025; Ogunlesi 2024; Fleming/Murphy 2025; Lemkau 2026);
  - deal releases naming acquired leaders.

  Pronouns are not stated in the sources, so this note uses names or *they*. Outside facts (for example Ramji becoming Vanguard CEO) are marked *context*.
- **Core claim:**
  - **Fink's position.** Larry Fink has been Chairman and CEO throughout and says every year that a departure is not imminent: "While I have no intentions of leaving BlackRock anytime soon, I also will not be here forever." (2020)
  - **Founders and candidates have thinned out.** Co-founders stepped back (Barbara Novick 2020, Ben Golub 2022). Long-assumed candidates left (Salim Ramji, *context*; Mark Wiedman, noted in the 2025 letter). President and co-founder Rob Kapito is almost invisible in the public record.
  - **Who the bench is now, as the documents show it:**
    - CFO **Martin Small** opens and closes both Investor Days.
    - COO **Rob Goldstein** is the voice of Aladdin and digital assets, and the only executive who co-signs with Fink (Economist, Dec 2025).
    - **Acquired founders** sit at board and GEC level: Bayo Ogunlesi (GIP), Scott Kapnick (HPS), Mark O'Hare (Preqin).
  - **The board** tilts toward wealth and dealmaking, and kept its lead independent director twice past retirement to oversee the deals.
  - **Succession is a process without a name.** The documents show distributed power, not a designated heir.

## 2. Profiles

### Larry Fink — co-founder, Chairman & CEO
- **Background, in Fink's own words:** started at First Boston in 1976. "I lost $100 million on a series of bad trades at First Boston and…well, nobody needs to hear that story again." (2024) The letters credit that loss for BlackRock's focus on risk.
- **Signature views:**
  - Purpose as profit's engine (2019).
  - Climate risk is investment risk (2020–21).
  - Capital markets over banks (2023 onward).
  - Retirement as the core business (2024).
  - Private markets as the next index-vs-active (2025).
  - Tokenization (2025–26).
  - Investing as optimism ("Growing with your country", 2026).
- **Succession formula, every year:**
  - 2021: "While I have no intention of leaving BlackRock anytime soon", followed by years of work "on building a strong bench of talent that represents the breadth of our business."
  - 2022: the stated goal is that "when all of BlackRock’s founding partners, including Rob Kapito and me, have moved on, the firm is in even better hands than it is today."
  - 2023: "my most important responsibility now is growing and mentoring leaders across the firm."
  - 2026: "In 2025, we expanded our Global Executive Committee to include more emerging enterprise leaders who will help advance our strategy in the years ahead."
- **Admitted reversals:** on crypto, "I yeah, but I grow and learn." (CNBC 2025-10). On the retirement backlash, see [[blackrock-fink-letters]] §3.
- **Communication style:** the 2019–22 letters are institutional and directive. From 2024 they become personal memoir (parents, grandchildren). Fink is absent from the slide text of both Investor Day decks. *Inference:* the CFO and COO are deliberately put in front of investors.

### Rob Kapito — co-founder, President
- Almost absent from the corpus. Kapito signs the 2020 client letter as a GEC member and appears in earnings-call participant lists. In 2021 and 2022 Fink names Kapito as the other founder that succession planning is built around.
- No quotes, no deal role and no Investor Day section in the corpus. *Inference:* Kapito's role is internal, and the President title is not the succession track.

### Rob Goldstein — COO
- **Voice of the technology stack:**
  - Azure (2020): "As both a user and a provider of Aladdin, this decision reflects BlackRock’s ongoing commitment to continuous innovation and scalable operating solutions,"
  - Snowflake (2021): "Aladdin has been on a journey for three decades to put data at the heart of the investment process."
  - Preqin (2025): "BlackRock is a perpetual reinvention machine, evolving continuously to stay ahead of our clients’ rapidly changing needs."
- Presents "Platform as a Service" (2023) and "Powering Scale" (2025) at Investor Day.
- **Co-author** with Fink of the Dec 2025 Economist op-ed, the only shared byline in eight years of documents. *Inference:* the strongest succession signal in the corpus.

### Martin Small — CFO (2023–)
- Joined in 2006 in Legal, from Davis Polk. Later headed US Wealth Advisory and US/Canada iShares (per the CFO release). Fink describes Small's career as a "One BlackRock" career.
- Fink credits Small with the phrase *units of trust*: client dollars are "what our CFO, Martin Small, refers to as “units of trust.”" (2023)
- Owns the 2030 framework: "Leading asset owners want to work with platforms, not monoline product providers." (2025 Investor Day)
- *Inference:* a CFO from inside the business who fronts both investor days is a contender, or at least the firm's financial voice for any transition.

### Gary Shedlin — CFO ~2013–2022, then Vice Chairman
- A banker by background who advised on BlackRock's "most transformational transactions". Moved to strategic clients.

### Salim Ramji — iShares & Index (to 2024)
- Main voice on bond ETFs: they "have become the catalyst of a more modern, more digital and more transparent bond market." (2022). On ETF reach: "ETF assets are still less than 3% of the markets they seek to access globally" (2021).
- Presented the 2030 iShares targets at Investor Day 2023, then is absent in 2025. *Context, not in corpus:* left to become Vanguard CEO in 2024.

### Mark Wiedman — Head of Global Client Business (to 2025)
- Owned the *5% ambition* (2023 deck) and the retail push into private markets: "We are simplifying how individual investors and advisors access private markets," (2024-09-12).
- 2025 letter: "After 20 years building a number of businesses at BlackRock", Wiedman "has decided to pursue the next chapter in his career outside of the firm."

### Rick Rieder — CIO, Global Fixed Income
- Presents fixed-income scale at both Investor Days: 6,600 trades a day, and execution-cost savings on BlackRock's own benchmark.
- A consistent view on the income regime: "We continue to believe that the Income pillar is the better place to look in Fixed" (2025 deck; the slide line breaks there).

### Aladdin leaders: Sudhir Nair, Tarek Chouman, Kamya Somasundaram
- Nair (Preqin, 2024): "This presents a substantial opportunity for Aladdin to bridge the transparency gap between public and private markets through data and analytics,"
- Chouman came from eFront and first presented in 2025.
- Somasundaram is BlackRock's voice on the Tradeweb integration ([[blackrock-anchor-client]]).

### Stewardship: Barbara Novick → Sandra Boss → Joud Abdel Majeid
- Novick, a co-founder, built stewardship and moved to senior advisor in 2020.
- Boss announced the 2022 *tactical* pullback on climate proposals.
- Abdel Majeid (2023) frames enforcement as governments' job: "We also recognize that governments and policymakers are ultimately responsible for enforcing relevant laws and" (2023 AGM; the transcript breaks there).

### Acquired founders: the new power center
- **Bayo Ogunlesi (GIP):** non-independent director, GEC member and Senior MD. Fink: "Bayo is also a founder, with a proven track record of value creation and stewardship of an outstanding firm." (2024-11-19). GIP pitch: "This platform is set to be the preeminent, one-stop infrastructure solutions provider for global corporates and the public sector," (2024-01-12)
- **Scott Kapnick, Scot French, Michael Patterson (HPS):** GEC members. Kapnick is a board observer and chairs the PFS Executive Office. Kapnick: "Our partnership with BlackRock will further strengthen our position in this fast growing but increasingly competitive market." (2024-12-03)
- **Mark O'Hare (Preqin):** Vice Chair.
- All were paid mostly in deferred BlackRock stock ([[blackrock-private-markets]] §3).

### The board
- **Murry Gerber**, Lead Independent Director since 2017, on the board since 2000, ex-EQT CEO: announced retirement (Nov 2023), was asked to stay for GIP (Mar 2024), and asked again for GIP, Preqin and HPS (Mar 2025): "In light of these significant transactions and integrations, the independent directors have requested that Mr. Gerber stand for re-election"
- **Amin Nasser** (Aramco CEO, 2023): "The continuity of regional expertise on BlackRock’s Board reflects the importance of the Middle East to the firm’s long-term strategy."
- **Gregory Fleming** (Rockefeller Capital), **Kathleen Murphy** (ex-Fidelity) in 2025, and **Gregg Lemkau** (BDT & MSD) in 2026: wealth and dealmaking expertise.
- **Bill Ford** chairs compensation and defends the 198:1 CEO pay ratio (2022 AGM). **Gordon Nixon** chairs nominating and defends the combined Chair/CEO role.

## 4. Leadership lenses

- **Power** ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]): board seats and deferred stock are formal power for the acquired founders. The CFO's Investor Day role is visible power. The founder keeps both chairs.
- **High output management** ([[canon-grove-high-output-management]]): "my most important responsibility now is growing and mentoring leaders" is Grove's leverage argument applied by a CEO who stays in office.
- **Succession and culture** ([[canon-schein-organizational-culture]]): *One BlackRock* is the cultural glue, used to describe Small's career, the 2024 re-architecture and PFS. Ask whether the acquired cultures (GIP, HPS) assimilate or run semi-autonomously.
- **Leading at scale** ([[canon-marquet-turn-the-ship-around]]): distributed authority versus a founder-centred brand. The letters are first person.
