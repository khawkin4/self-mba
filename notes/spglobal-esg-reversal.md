# Note · S&P Global's ESG reversal — the same executive built an ESG franchise and then folded it; a $800M forecast met $359M of revenue, and "net zero" became "energy expansion, not replacement"

> Case note (narrative risk and forecasting) · Maps to **Course 11** (Global Strategy, Macro & Responsible Business: ESG done seriously, or not), **Course 07** (Data, Analytics & Decision-Making: base rates, forecast scoring, moving definitions), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy: data-product adjacency), **E5** (Transformation & Change Management: building and then dismantling an organisation), **E8** (Stakeholder & IR: how a company retires a story) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[spglobal-hub]]; companions [[spglobal-mission-forces]], [[spglobal-people]], [[spglobal-strategy-financials]]

- **Source:** S&P Global corpus `_ingest/raw/spglobal/articles.jsonl` (492 texts, 2019–Oct 2026). The ESG thread comes from about 35 items: the RobecoSAM/SAM deal (2019), the ESG Scores launch (2020), the Sustainable1 launch and appointments (2021–23), the net-zero items (2021), the CEO open-letter notice (2022), Shades of Green (2022), and the ESG revenue lines in the 2021–2024 annual-report letters and the May 2024 AGM slides. Also used: the leadership release of Oct 2024, the boilerplate change of Apr→Jul 2024, the 2025 CEO letter, and the 2026 sustainable-bond and energy releases. The full text of Peterson's Mar 2022 open letter is **not** in the corpus; only the release announcing it is. Ratings' ESG Evaluation score releases (about 30) were skipped as ratings commentary.
- **Core claim:** From 2019 to 2022 S&P Global built a full ESG stack:
  - SAM's Corporate Sustainability Assessment (bought from RobecoSAM).
  - ESG Scores, Trucost climate data and The Climate Service.
  - Second-party opinions (Shades of Green).
  - ESG indices.
  - A centralised organisation, **Sustainable1**, launched in Apr 2021 and led by **Martina Cheung**.

  In its own annual reports S&P forecast the business at **~$600M by 2025** (AR 2021) and then **$800M by 2026** at a 34% CAGR (AR 2022). Reported revenue was **$244M (2022), $301M (2023), $359M (2024)**, and by 2022 the category had been *widened* to include energy-transition products. Through 2024 the story was retired step by step:
  - "ESG" left the boilerplate (Apr→Jul).
  - Sustainable1 was folded into Commodity Insights (Oct), under Cheung, by then CEO.
  - The Chief Purpose Officer role ended (Oct).
  - The CEO letter added the "critical role that hydrocarbons will continue to play" (AR 2024).

  By the 2025 letter the frame was **"energy expansion, not replacement"**. The lesson is less "ESG failed" than *the forecast was a narrative*. The revenue grew, but at roughly a third of the promised rate.

## 2. Forecast vs outcome

| Forecast (source) | Scope as defined then | Target | Actual nearest year | Hit rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AR 2021 | "ESG and climate-related product revenue" | ~$600M in 2025 | $359M in 2024 (wider scope) | ~60% of target, on a broader definition |
| AR 2022 | Sustainable1, incl. EV and "some energy transition products" | $800M in 2026 (34% CAGR) | $359M in 2024; 2025 not disclosed | On the required path ($244M × 1.34² ≈ $438M for 2024) the 2024 result is **~18% short**, and $800M would need ~49% a year in 2025–26 |

**Two quiet moves (inference from the wording):**
1. **The scope widened.** The 2021 base ($98M) counted ESG and climate products. From 2022 the line is *sustainability and energy transition*, which adds Platts energy-transition prices and Mobility EV data. Like-for-like ESG growth was therefore lower than the headline.
2. **The target disappeared.** No letter after AR 2022 repeats the $800M target. The 2025 letter reports no ESG figure at all.

## 3. How the narrative moved

1. **2019–20, *ESG is data.*** Buy the assessment (SAM), score everyone (ESG Scores), plug the scores into indices. ESG is sold as an *input* across divisions.
2. **2021, *ESG is identity.*** Sustainable1 launches, S&P joins GFANZ, endorses Say on Climate, takes a net-zero-linked revolver, and the boilerplate puts ESG in its second sentence. The forecast reaches $600M.
3. **2022, *ESG needs defending.*** The CEO writes an open letter on ESG *scores*, and the forecast rises to $800M. *(Inference: an open letter defending scores suggests the scores were under attack. The letter's text is not in the corpus.)*
4. **2023–24, *sustainability and energy transition.*** The language softens, Yergin talks of a "multidimensional" transition, and Sustainable1 goes into Commodity Insights.
5. **2025–26, *energy expansion.*** The CEO frames the next two decades around energy *demand* driven by AI data centres. Sustainable-bond issuance is described as plateauing.

## 4. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Demand … has continued to accelerate and grow"** (Cheung, Jun 2023) was true of revenue growth (+24% in 2023) but not of the forecast path. Growth was real; the forecast was not.
2. **"Proprietary" ESG data was not exclusive.** RobecoSAM kept access to the CSA data it sold. Competitors (MSCI, LSEG, Morningstar/Sustainalytics) sold rival scores; *context, not in the corpus*.
3. **The fold was described as integration.** The 2024 letter says the move "allows us to merge our best thinking". It never says ESG under-delivered.
4. **The corpus has no customer voice saying why** demand fell short (regulation, the ESG backlash, budget cuts). Every explanation in §5 is inference.

## 5. Strategic read (inference — not stated by S&P Global)

- **Forecasting was done from the story, not from base rates.** In 2021 the business made $98M. A 6× forecast in four years implies ~57% a year, above even the fastest data-product adoption S&P reports elsewhere (private markets +25% in 2024). The outside view would have asked how many data categories have ever compounded at 50%+ for four years.
- **The same executive built and folded it, and that is the right reading of leadership, not a contradiction.** Cheung launched Sustainable1 in 2021 and moved it into Commodity Insights in her first leadership reshuffle as CEO. It shows the identity was a portfolio bet, not a belief. In energy, customers' demand pulled toward *expansion* (CERAWeek, Platts' oil-and-gas base).
- **Commodity Insights was always the natural home.** Platts' customers are oil, gas and power firms. CERAWeek in 2023 argued for 28% more upstream investment. Selling *energy transition* inside an energy division turns ESG from a values product into a price-and-data product.
- **Ratings kept the defensible piece.** Second-party opinions (Shades of Green) sit next to credit ratings and serve a sustainable-bond market of about $800–900B a year, even after growth stalled. Scores are a commodity; opinions on debt deals are not.
- **What survived:** climate-risk data (physical risk), EU disclosure data (SFDR), energy-transition prices (hydrogen, carbon credits). These are the regulation-driven and price-driven pieces. **What faded:** the brand, the org chart, the forecast.

## 6. Canon lenses

- **Base rates and forecasting** ([[canon-tetlock-superforecasting]], [[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]): the $600M → $800M forecasts are an inside view. Score them as forecasts.
- **Narrative and noise** ([[canon-kahneman-sibony-sunstein-noise]]): changing the definition of the metric between forecasts makes the error hard to see.
- **Leading change** ([[canon-kotter-leading-change]]): Sustainable1 had a coalition, a vision and early wins. What it lacked was a durable reason for customers to pay. Change efforts can be "successful" internally and fail commercially.
- **Strategic inflection** ([[canon-grove-only-the-paranoid-survive]]): the 2022 rate shock and the political backlash were inflection points. S&P responded with a quiet fold, not a public pivot.
- **Justice and responsibility** ([[canon-sandel-justice]]): when a company sells "responsible" scores, does it owe customers an explanation when it stops featuring them?

## 7. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For S&P:** revenue grew every year ($244M → $301M → $359M). Folding a sub-scale unit into the division that serves its buyers is good management, and the climate data still sells. Not every forecast miss is a failure.
- **Against:** the forecasts were printed in shareholder letters, and the miss was never acknowledged. A shareholder could have priced a $800M business into the multiple. The silent retreat damages the credibility of the next forecast, for example private markets at $600M or the AI data portal.

### Rep
**(a) Score the forecasts.** Base: $98M (2021). AR 2021 forecast ~$600M for 2025. What CAGR does that imply? *(Answer: (600/98)^(1/4) − 1 ≈ **57%** a year.)* AR 2022 forecast $800M for 2026 at 34% from the 2022 base. Check: 244 × 1.34⁴ ≈ **$804M**, consistent. Now use the actuals: $244M → $359M in two years is (359/244)^(1/2) − 1 ≈ **21%** a year. At 21%, what is 2026 revenue? *(Answer: 359 × 1.21² ≈ **$526M**, about 34% below target.)*

**(b) Adjust for scope.** Suppose the 2022 redefinition added about $100M of energy-transition and EV revenue that the 2021 definition excluded. Restate 2024 on the old scope (≈ $359M − $100M × 1.2 growth ≈ $239M) and compute the like-for-like CAGR from $98M in 2021. *(Answer: (239/98)^(1/3) − 1 ≈ **35%** a year. Strong growth, still far below 57%.)* Then write two sentences on what an analyst should have asked at the 2022 Investor Day.

**(c) Write the retraction.** In ≤100 words, write the paragraph a CFO would put in the 2025 letter to retire the $800M target honestly. State the number, the reason and what replaces it. Compare it with the line S&P actually used: "energy expansion, not replacement".
