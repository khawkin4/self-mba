# Note · Thomson Reuters' people — a family-controlled board hired an outside operator, who hired an outside change team, then handed the microphone to product and AI leaders; the CEO's language moved from "rigor" to "fiduciary-grade AI"

> People & perspectives study · Maps to **E1** (Leading at Scale — outside CEO, outside change team, succession), **E4** (Corporate Governance — controlling family, Founders Share Company, board composition), **E7** (Executive Presence & Communication — Hasker's voice, product leaders as spokespeople), **E8** (Stakeholder & IR — who speaks to investors), **Course 09** (Leadership & Org Behavior) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[thomsonreuters-hub]]; companions [[thomsonreuters-change-program]] and [[thomsonreuters-outside-voices]]

- **Source:** every attributed quote in the 293 TR items, 2019–2026 (`_ingest/raw/thomsonreuters/articles.jsonl`). This covers results releases (where only the CEO is quoted), leadership and board releases, AI and product releases, the 2019 AGM transcript (Smith, David Thomson, Bello) and the 2021 Investor Day transcript (13 executives plus analyst Q&A). Pronouns follow the source where it states them. Elsewhere this note uses names or they/them.
- **Core claim:**
  - **Smith (to Mar 2020)** sold the trading business and framed it as a relaunch.
  - **Hasker (from Mar 2020)** is an outsider: PwC, McKinsey partner, Nielsen president/COO, TPG/CAA. He came in when the family-controlled board decided to replace Smith, a process the board denied in Oct 2019 and confirmed in Feb 2020.
  - Hasker brought in **outside operators** (Roth from HSBC, Wong from Facebook, later Hron as CTO) to run a pre-registered change program. From 2023 he let **product and AI leaders** carry the public story.
  - His own voice moved through four stages:
    1. Operator: "Job one…", "rigor".
    2. Capital allocator: return of capital "funded with LSEG sale proceeds".
    3. AI evangelist: "outpace those who resist".
    4. Trust-and-liability seller: "fiduciary-grade AI".
  - The **Thomson family (Woodbridge, ~66–70%)** and the **Founders Share Company** (guardian of the 1941 Trust Principles) are the fixed points. They are seldom quoted, and their wording barely changes.

## 2. Profiles

### Jim Smith — CEO to Mar 2020, then Thomson Reuters Foundation chair
- **Role in the story:** sold 55% of F&R to Blackstone (2018), agreed the Refinitiv–LSEG deal (2019) and handed over a company that was "on its firmest footing in years". The Oct 2019 board statement ("The Board is fully supportive of Jim Smith and his management team.") came four months before his replacement was named.
- **Signature views:**
  - "There is a trend toward consolidation of the global financial markets infrastructure and data segments" (Aug 1 2019).
  - "the most important part of transaction was that it gave us the opportunity to reposition the new Thomson Reuters" (Jun 5 2019).
  - At the AGM: "We live in a time when objectivity, accuracy, fairness and transparency are under attack." That is the news-company CEO's register, which Hasker doesn't use.
- **Exit framing (Feb 25 2020):** "This is the right time to pass the baton to the next generation of leadership", citing "the spin-off of our market data and trading operations". He was the only TR leader to call the F&R sale a *spin-off of trading*.

### Steve Hasker — President & CEO, Mar 2020 →
- **Background, as the release gives it:** PwC (chartered accountant); MBA and international affairs master's from Columbia; McKinsey partner in media, information and technology; Nielsen global president and COO; CEO of CAA Global and senior adviser at TPG. An operator and private-equity profile, not a content or news one.
- **Stage 1 — operator (2020–22):**
  - "Job one for me is to get on the ground and spend plenty of time with our customers and employees," (Feb 25 2020)
  - "we begin the transition from a holding company to an operating company." (Nov 3 2020)
  - "Prevailing tailwinds are favorable and play to our strengths." (Feb 23 2021)
  - "the word I'd like to leave you with is rigor, as we think about our M&A activities from here on out." (Mar 16 2021)
  - "we continue to look to supplement organic growth with targeted acquisitions that can bolster our positions and where we are an advantaged owner." (Feb 8 2022)
- **Stage 2 — capital allocator (2023):**
  - "we plan to execute a return of capital of at least $2 billion later this year, funded with LSEG sale proceeds." (Feb 9 2023)
  - "Our capital capacity and liquidity will also remain a key asset as we look to continue innovating in our markets" (Aug 2 2023)
- **Stage 3 — AI evangelist (2023–25):**
  - "Generative AI empowers professionals to redefine their work and discover innovative approaches" (May 23 2023)
  - "there is a trust gap with professionals" (Jun 21 2023)
  - "another step in our 'build, partner and buy' strategy to bring generative AI solutions to our customers" (Jun 26 2023)
  - "AI-empowered professionals and their companies will outpace those who resist this transformative era" (Jul 9 2024)
- **Stage 4 — trust, liability and ownership (2026):**
  - "CoCounsel is built for moments when being almost right is not good enough." (Feb 24 2026)
  - "We call this 'fiduciary-grade AI.'" (May 5 2026)
  - "If AI is to start taking on a more meaningful share of professional work, we need to assess it to a meaningful standard." (May 27 2026)
  - "That’s an advantage only Thomson Reuters has, and it shows in the results" (Aug 24 2026)
- **Style:** short, declarative, investor-facing. He is the only executive quoted in the results releases from May 2020 on (Smith in 2019). He rarely names competitors. His one public refutation was "polar opposite view" (2021). He moves the frame to the customer ("moments that matter most") instead of to TR.

### Mike Eastwood — CFO 2020–26
- The most candid voice in the corpus:
  - "We have not sold any LSEG shares to date."
  - "we are significantly below the B2B industry average today. Our goal is not to get to the average but to far exceed that." (on NPS)
  - "We're going to spend our time executing like hell, in regards to the Change Program" (all Mar 16 2021).
- On the float cap: Woodbridge owns 66%, and ~70% is the cap.
- On history, describing the IR head's tenure (Jan 2022): "our acquisition of Reuters and our partnership with Blackstone for Refinitiv and the subsequent sale to London Stock Exchange Group."
- Retired as CFO in 2026 to chair the Thomson Reuters Foundation, the same path Smith took.

### Kirsty Roth and David Wong — the 2020 change hires
- **Roth** (COO/CTO): "we will build once and deploy many times." "although late to the game, we will benefit from that work". At launch she conceded the culture needed "a test and learn culture where mistakes can and will be made". She is not quoted in the 2023–26 AI releases.
- **Wong** (CPO): the most consistent product voice, 2021–2026:
  - "AI models are only as good as the data that trains them." (2021)
  - "Research that previously took hours can now be delivered in minutes, removing uncertainty, and delivering accurate outcomes" (Nov 1 2023)
  - "Partnering with leading LLM providers is a key part of our strategy" (Aug 12 2024)
  - "Agentic AI isn’t a marketing buzzword. It’s a new blueprint for how complex work gets done," (Jun 2 2025)
  - "This is where AI starts to feel less like a tool and more like a teammate," (Aug 5 2025)
  - "Thomson Reuters is building CoCounsel Legal to be the fiduciary-grade system at the center of how legal work gets done" (May 12 2026)
  - "turns AI that answers questions into AI that does the work." (Oct 22 2026)
  - Wong's arc runs from data to partners to agents to a "fiduciary-grade system".

## 4. Communication style

- **One-voice IR:** only the CEO speaks in results releases. CFOs speak only at investor days (not captured for 2024) and on calls (not in the corpus).
- **Product leaders as the AI chorus:** 2023–26 AI releases quote Wong, Hron, Ramanathan, Clayton McDonnell and Beastrom, usually with the same three claims in each: trusted content, expert validation and a time saved.
- **No named rivals:** in 293 items, only LexisNexis and Bloomberg Law are named as competitors, both by Beastrom in 2021. Harvey and the frontier labs as competitors are never named. The labs appear only as partners.
- **Coined terms carry the story:** "Big 3", "build, partner, buy", "professional-grade", "Fiduciary-Grade AI™", "verification layer". Each marks a phase.

## 5. Leadership lenses

- **The outsider CEO** ([[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]], [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]]): Hasker's record reads as an operator-allocator's. Cost program, focus, cash returns, small deals. That suits a controlled company whose owner values the dividend streak.
- **Leading change through hires** ([[canon-kotter-leading-change]]): a guiding coalition built mostly from outsiders in 2020, then rotated once the program closed (Roth does not appear in the 2023–26 AI releases). The coalition was built for the program, not kept on after it.
- **Executive voice** ([[canon-duarte-resonate]], [[canon-minto-pyramid-principle]]): Hasker's quotes follow answer-first structure (result, then reason, then the next step). The 2026 "almost right is not good enough" line is the most resonant single phrase TR produced in the period.
- **Power in a controlled company** ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]): the real power sits with Woodbridge (votes, float cap) and the Founders Share Company (Trust Principles veto). Neither is in the results releases. The CEO's room to manoeuvre is wide on operations and narrow on ownership structure.
