# Note · S&P Global through outside eyes — partners praise the data, regulators speak through remedies, and the only real skeptic was invited in by S&P itself

> Stakeholder / outside-view note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — coopetition, buyer and supplier power, regulators as a force), **Course 08** (Negotiation & Influence — reading the other side's incentives and testimonials), **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations — whose voice gets printed, and why), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation — AI and crypto partners' framing) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[spglobal-hub]]; companions [[spglobal-people]], [[spglobal-mission-forces]], [[spglobal-ihs-markit-merger]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/spglobal/articles.jsonl`. That is **492 items, Jan 2019 – Oct 23 2026**: S&P Global press and IR releases, the annual-report letters and the investor-day decks. Every quote or attribution by a non-S&P person was read in context. Nothing outside S&P's own channels was consulted.
- **Selection-bias warning:** S&P chose every voice here. Partner quotes are co-written press releases. Divestiture buyers are quoted on the day they buy. There are **no** earnings-call transcripts, analyst notes, press articles, short-seller reports or customer complaints in the corpus. The corpus is also silent on three things: no regulator speaks in its own words, no competitor criticizes S&P, and nothing is said about the ICE–MarketAxess deal (Jul 2026) or what it means for S&P's pricing feed. Read each line as *what this speaker wanted S&P to print*.
- **Core claim:** Outside voices fall into four groups.
  1. **Partners** are the AI platforms (Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, AWS, IBM, Cohere), crypto infrastructure (Chainlink, Kaiko, Centrifuge, Derivnex), venues (Cboe, B3, MarketAxess, CME) and dealers (Barclays). They praise S&P's *trusted data*, because each needs that trust to sell its own product.
  2. **Buyers of S&P's divested assets** (KKR, News Corp, FactSet, Morningstar, SLB, STG) are quoted only on the day they buy.
  3. **Regulators speak through remedies, not words.** The EC, DOJ and CMA forced asset sales. The SEC fined S&P three times: $9M, $2.5M and $20M.
  4. **Real skepticism is almost absent.** The sharpest line is from a partner S&P had just invested in (Farsight's CEO): the data-access moat is ending. The second sharpest comes from S&P's *own* research: MI on large stock mergers, and Ratings on hyperscaler cash flow.

## 2. What partners value, in their own words

1. **Trust, not features.** *Trusted* is the word partners repeat. Kurian: "turn its essential intelligence into faster, more secure, AI-ready insights" (2025-12-10). Soubiran calls it "a globally trusted benchmark brand". Nazarov: "Chainlink enables S&P to extend its reach directly into the digital asset economy." (2025-10-14). Each partner is borrowing S&P's reputation for its own channel.
2. **Closing the gap between trading and valuation.** MarketAxess's Sweeney (above) states the deal's logic exactly. A venue price sitting inside an end-of-day evaluation means TCA and fund NAVs use the same levels. This is the clearest outside statement of why S&P took a venue feed. See [[spglobal-pricing-fixed-income]] and [[tradeweb-ai-price]].
3. **Benchmarks as liquidity infrastructure.** IHS Markit's Sophia Dancygier, in 2020, before the merger: "Our collective range of indices and associated financial products are the liquidity cornerstone of the global investment community" (2020-04-08). Cboe's Hocking: "Cboe and S&P Dow Jones Indices have a long-standing history of collaboration" (2023-09-25).
4. **Opacity in private markets as the opportunity.** Ansari (Mercer): "for too long, our clients have struggled with inconsistent reporting formats and fragmented data across private asset classes." (2025-09-15). Talmor-Gera: "Private markets are too important to remain opaque." (2025-09-30).
5. **Fixing old plumbing.** Derivnex's Harrington: "much of the infrastructure supporting them remains fragmented" (2026-10-13). DTCC's Stewart on T+1: "Connecting client reference data with SSI instruction statuses helps market participants to address discrepancies that could impede or delay settlement," (2024-03-13).

**What partners fear (scarce, so weight it up):**
- **Bank dependence for contributed data.** Visible Alpha's CEO thanked the banks "that have been with us since the beginning" (2024-02-20). The product needs sell-side contributors to keep supplying.
- **Allocation is still hard in crypto.** Pantera's Morehead: "the biggest friction point in crypto hasn't changed; it's knowing how to allocate" (2026-07-21).
- **Climate data quality.** Citi's Webley: "Quality climate data is one of our core requirements" (2022-02-09).

## 4. Regulators and markets — voices heard only through what they forced

**The merger remedies (2021–22).** No regulator is quoted. Their positions are visible only in what S&P had to give up. See [[spglobal-ihs-markit-merger]].
- **UK CMA** (Oct 19 2021): S&P reports that the merger raised no concerns "except in relation to certain commodity and petrochemical price assessments in the UK."
- **European Commission** (Oct 22 2021): "In response to concerns raised by the EC, S&P Global has committed to divest CUSIP Global Services and its Leveraged Commentary and Data (LCD) business". The EC thereby reached into S&P's own assets, not only IHS Markit's.
- **US DOJ** (Nov 12 2021): requires the OPIS, CMM and PCW sale. The settlement "remains subject to court approval, does not require the companies to make any additional divestitures."
- **Result:** CUSIP Global Services went to **FactSet** ($1.925B), LCD to **Morningstar** ($650M), and OPIS, CMM and PCW plus Base Chemicals to **News Corp** (~$1.15B + $295M). Regulators seeded three competitors.

**SEC enforcement (three settlements).**
| Date | Unit | What | Penalty | Language |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-05-17 | S&P DJI | An undisclosed QA mechanism on the S&P 500 VIX Short-Term Futures Index ER, Feb 5 2018 | $9M | S&P DJI "neither admits nor denies the SEC's allegations". Found to have "acted negligently" |
| 2022-11-14 | Ratings | A 2017 RMBS rating | $2.5M | "the SEC acknowledged SPGR's remedial acts and its cooperation with the SEC staff" |
| 2024-09-03 | Ratings | Off-channel communications (industry sweep) | $20M | "the SEC recognized SPGR's remedial acts and its cooperation with the SEC staff" |

**Licensing and access regulators.**
- PBoC and NAFMII (2019) and CSRC (2020) allowed a wholly foreign-owned rating agency into China's interbank and exchange bond markets. Berisford: "This approval is the latest step in an ongoing, thoughtful dialog with China's regulators about the future they envisage for their financial markets," (2019-01-28).
- ESMA endorsed the S&P 500 and DJIA under the EU Benchmark Regulation (BMR) in 2019.
- The 2025 investor deck notes that any holder of 5% or 10% of SPGI can affect Ratings' EU and UK registrations.

**Markets pushing back (users as regulators).**
- **Brent, 2021-03-10:** Platts reversed a CIF Rotterdam change three weeks after announcing it. It cited "extensive feedback from market participants" and said "there is not agreement on how it would be fully reflected into the wider Brent complex."
- **GICS, 2022-03-31:** S&P DJI and MSCI dropped reclassification of renewables and consolidation of banks "due to lack of market consensus."
- **MegaCap, 2026-06-04:** after consultation, S&P DJI kept the S&P 500 rules unchanged for megacap IPOs.

## 5. Skeptics and independent framing

**The complete set of critical or cautionary views I could find:**

1. **Farsight's CEO Samir Dutta (2026-07-23), inside an S&P release:** "The industry has spent years competing on access to data. The next frontier is helping firms turn that data into client-ready work". That says S&P's core asset (access) is becoming table stakes. S&P had just taken a minority stake in Farsight. ([[spglobal-ai-data-layer]])
2. **S&P MI's own Quantamental study (2020-10-19):** "Large mergers and acquisitions often negatively impact growth, profitability, and returns, sometimes for years after the change." The same release says smaller cash deals "tend to be more successful". Six weeks later S&P signed a $44B all-stock merger.
3. **S&P Ratings on the AI boom (2026-08-27):** "S&P Global Ratings expects all six hyperscalers to generate negative free operating cash flow in 2026 and 2027 with recovery not projected until 2029." Its own credit analysts are more cautious about AI than its marketing.
4. **S&P's own disclaimers, an internal skepticism about its own products:**
   - "A VRA is not a credit rating and does not comment on yield levels." (2026-10-04).
   - Stablecoin assessments are labelled "not credit ratings" (2025).
   - Credit Memo Builder "is not a substitute for independent credit analysis or credit ratings issued by S&P Global Ratings".
   - UNEP adds that its work "does not imply endorsement of S&P Global nor any associated companies."
5. **Ratings on stablecoins (2022-06-17):** "certain algorithmic types are less likely to maintain their promised stability".
6. **Vanguard's Davis (2026-02-05):** "stretched valuations in equities". A partner's market caution, not about S&P.
7. **Missing:** sell-side analysts on the 2022 guidance suspension; anyone on the CP+ deal after ICE's MarketAxess bid; Moody's, MSCI or FactSet as critics; customers on data prices.

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): MSCI is co-owner and rival. CME is JV partner and futures home. ICE is Brent co-governor, pricing rival and soon owner of the MarketAxess feed. Bloomberg is EMS partner and desktop rival. Almost every node in S&P's value net sits in two boxes.
- **Supplier power** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): S&P's evaluated prices depend on dealer contributions (Barclays) and a venue feed (CP+). Its estimates product depends on bank contributors (Visible Alpha). Its AI distribution depends on platform owners. The suppliers of S&P's raw inputs are concentrated.
- **Power and dependence** ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]): crypto partners depend on S&P's brand. S&P depends on MarketAxess/ICE for CP+ and on hyperscalers for AI channels. Map each partner by importance × lack of alternatives.
- **Testimonial framing** ([[canon-cialdini-influence]]): divestiture buyers praising the asset they just bought, and investees praising S&P (Kaiko, Farsight), are *authority and social proof* manufactured by the transaction. Discount them accordingly.
- **Information goods** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): every partner wants S&P's *data* inside its product. The fight is over distribution and who sees the user. Dutta's line is the information-goods warning: when access is cheap, value moves to whoever turns data into work.
- **Selection** ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]): this is a curated sample. The absence of critics is not evidence that there are none.

**Practitioner counterpoint.**
- **For S&P:** the breadth of partners is itself evidence. Four hyperscalers, two frontier-model companies, CME, Cboe, MarketAxess and Bloomberg all chose to put S&P data or benchmarks into their products. Nobody does that with a brand they distrust.
- **Against:** every one of those partners could switch to another data supplier, or build its own. MarketAxess's owner will soon be a pricing competitor. And S&P's own analysts (hyperscaler FOCF) and researchers (large stock M&A) are its most credible critics.

**Open questions to track:**
1. Does the CP+ contract survive ICE's purchase of MarketAxess (close expected H1 2027), and will S&P ever say so?
2. Will any AI platform launch its own financial data set that competes with Kensho's?
3. Will an SEC or EU action touch the "not a rating" products (VRA, stablecoin assessments)?
4. Do bank contributors (Barclays, Visible Alpha brokers) keep contributing as S&P sells their data back to them?
