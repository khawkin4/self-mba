# Note · LSEG's mission and the forces on it — the purpose sentence never changed; the identity moved from *exchange group* to *data + infrastructure* to *AI data layer*, and the main threat moved from regulators and integration to the AI narrative

> Strategy / environment-scan note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — five forces for an FMI-plus-data group), **Course 11** (Global Strategy & Macro — Brexit, EU location policy, China, tariffs), **E9** (Geopolitics), **E6** (Crisis Leadership — COVID, Ukraine, the VTB default, SVB/CS), **E12** (Culture as Strategy — open access as an identity), **E10** (AI as a force) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[lseg-hub]]; companions [[lseg-people]], [[lseg-outside-voices]]; compare [[tradeweb-mission-forces]]

- **Source:** LSEG corpus: "About LSEG" boilerplate across 887 press releases 2019–2026, AR purpose/strategy pages and principal-risk sections 2019–2025 (filtered extracts), the CMD 2023 and Innovation Forum 2025, and results releases. **Caveat:** some old pages carry retroactively refreshed boilerplate, so boilerplate dates come from first appearance in dated text. The principal-risk trend arrows are graphics and did not survive extraction.
- **Core claim:** LSEG's *purpose* line has been fixed since 2020: "Driving financial stability, empowering economies and enabling customers to create sustainable growth." Its *identity* has moved three times: (1) "an international markets infrastructure business" (2019) → (2) "a leading global financial markets infrastructure and data provider" (2020–25, the Refinitiv word) → (3) "LSEG creates opportunity across the global economy." and "LSEG. Make more possible." (2026), with AI as the organising theme ("LSEG is even more valuable in an AI world."). Under all three, one operating principle is constant: **open access / interoperability**. It started as a clearing principle and is now a cloud and AI distribution principle. The forces moved too. In 2019–22 the threats were regulators and integration (EC remedies, Brexit equivalence, Refinitiv tech debt). In 2023–24 they were pricing and competition (Bloomberg, S&P, Credit Suisse/UBS consolidation). From 2025 the defining force is the AI-disruption narrative, which management fights with data, partnerships and buybacks. The tailwinds (electronification through Tradeweb, volatility through LCH, passive through FTSE Russell) did most of the growth work.

### 1b. Boilerplate drift

| Period | Key phrase | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| Early 2019 | "an international markets infrastructure business"; ~4,500 staff | Exchange + index + clearing |
| Mid-2019 → 2025 | "LSEG is a leading global financial markets infrastructure and data provider, playing a vital social and economic role in the world’s financial system." | "Data" added with Refinitiv. "With our open approach, trusted expertise and global scale" |
| 2021–23 variant | "more than a diversified global financial markets infrastructure and data business" … "for more than 300 years" | Heritage plus breadth. Venues list "FXall and Tradeweb (through a majority ownership interest)" |
| 2026 | "creates opportunity"; "make more possible"; "we partner with our customers at every stage of trading" | Brand language replaces category language |

### 1c. Values and culture claims
- Values from 2023: Integrity, Partnership, Excellence, Change (AR 2023).
- The culture story is "fixing the basics" (Manz) and "high-performance culture". "We are systemically important in the major geographies of the world, with a trusted reputation and strong high-performance culture." (Schwimmer, AGM 2024)
- In AR 2025 the employee survey flagged poor internal communication of strategy (ADMITTED).

### 1d. What LSEG actually optimises for (inference, judged by actions)
1. **Recurring revenue share** (~40% → ~73%), first via Refinitiv and then via LDAs.
2. **Margin and FCF per share** from 2024 (Proch's agenda; +210bp in 2025).
3. **Control of key utilities** (LCH buy-ins to ~95%, LCH SA 100%, Tradeweb 89.9% of votes) while keeping customers inside the governance.
4. **Being the neutral supplier** to platforms it does not own (Microsoft, LLMs, Snowflake, Databricks) rather than owning the front-end.

### Principal-risk register, 2019 → 2025 (what LSEG chose to list)
- **2019:** the Refinitiv transaction (new), Brexit/EMIR 2.2, competition as a standalone risk ("Competitive markets are, by their very nature, dynamic, and the effects of competitor activity can never be fully mitigated.").
- **2020–21:** Borsa divestment, COVID remote working, third-party/cloud concentration (new standalone), Eikon disruption.
- **2022–23:** Russia/Ukraine, brand unification, the Microsoft partnership as a transformation risk ("The LSEG-Microsoft partnership will also result in changes to LSEG’s products, commercial models"), LLMs in model risk.
- **2024:** Azure migration, DORA, CrowdStrike, data licensing ("failure to meet data licencing and regulatory obligations could also adversely affect our reputation and financial performance").
- **2025:** tariffs, agentic AI ("The growing use of artificial intelligence particularly ‘generative’ and ‘agentic’ models introduces new dimensions of risk"), disruptive tech that "could reshape market dynamics, introduce new competitors".
- **Never listed:** Bloomberg by name, UK listings decline, Tradeweb conflicts. Cloud-concentration language *softened* after 2021 as the Microsoft dependency grew (inference from wording).

## 4. Forces map, 2026

| Force | Strength | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Rivalry** | High in data and indices; low in IRS clearing | Bloomberg, S&P, ICE, FactSet, MSCI in data/indices; Eurex and CME at the clearing edges |
| **Buyer power** | High | A dozen global banks negotiate data price, clearing economics and governance together |
| **Supplier power** | Medium | Exchanges' data (recoveries), Reuters news (30-year contract), Microsoft/AWS cloud, Tradeweb (an affiliate) |
| **Threat of substitutes** | Rising | LLM front-ends; self-indexing; agents replacing seats |
| **Threat of entry** | Low (CCP, real-time network); medium (AI-native data layers, crypto venues) | DLT and cloud "could lower entry barriers" (principal risks) |
| **Regulation** | Two-sided | Creates moats (CCP, BMR benchmark admin) and threats (EU location, remedies, FCA data study) |
| **Technology** | Two-sided | AI as demand driver and substitute; tokenisation as option ([[lseg-digital-markets]]) |

## 5. Crisis tests — what each revealed

| Crisis | What happened | What it showed |
|---|---|---|
| HKEX bid (Sep 2019) | Rejected in 2 days, withdrawn in 4 weeks | Board conviction in the data strategy |
| COVID (Mar 2020) | Record LSE trades (44.8m in March); SwapClear records; AGM held without shareholders | Volatility as a revenue driver; operational resilience |
| Refinitiv EC review (2020–21) | Borsa Italiana sold | Regulators can set the price of strategy |
| Russia/Ukraine (2022) | Exited Russia (~£60–80m revenue); deleted Russia from FTSE indices; VTB default at LCH EquityClear contained within initial margin | Sanctions compliance plus CCP resilience |
| SVB/Credit Suisse (2023) | Credit Suisse loss feeds into ASV over 2024–25 | Customer consolidation as a slow-burn headwind |
| AI sell-off (late 2025 – 2026) | Buybacks scaled to ~10% of market cap; Innovation Forum; MCP partnerships; ASV retired | Narrative risk, answered with capital and partners |

## 6. Scenarios to 2030 (inference, not LSEG's)

1. **AI layer wins (bull).** MCP/usage pricing adds 2–3pp to D&A growth from 2027. Licensed data becomes a toll on agents. Octus + Trumid make LSEG–Tradeweb the open alternative to ICE–MarketAxess in credit.
2. **Utility with a data tail (base).** D&A stays ~5–6%, Markets grows with volatility, margins rise via insourcing and SwapClear economics. Buybacks dominate shareholder returns.
3. **Disintermediated (bear).** LLM vendors or Bloomberg capture the front-end, data becomes wholesale, seat erosion outpaces usage revenue, and EU policy pulls euro swaps toward Eurex.

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Purpose vs identity** ([[canon-sinek-start-with-why]], [[canon-schein-organizational-culture]]): a fixed "why" (stability, growth) with a changing "what". Open access is the cultural artefact that survives.
- **Five forces** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]) and **7 Powers** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): network effects (SwapClear), switching costs (benchmarks), cornered resource (WGBI, real-time network).
- **Inflection points** ([[canon-grove-only-the-paranoid-survive]]): the 2025 AI de-rating is the *10x force*.
- **Systems and leverage points** ([[canon-meadows-thinking-in-systems]]): volatility as a reinforcing loop for Markets and a balancing loop for D&A (budgets).
- **Black swans and antifragility** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): an "all-weather" model that gains from volatility in Markets.
