## Core claim

At pre-seed the bet is placed on the **founder's non-obvious vision and their ability to tell it**, not on traction.
Cheap AI prototypes have *raised* the bar: everyone can now show a demo, so a demo has stopped being a signal.

## Framework: what they actually screen for

| Signal | What it looks like | Who stressed it |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder–market fit + unique insight** | A domain résumé is table stakes. The edge is the *intersection* of domain experience with an unusual second experience, which yields a view "five smart people in a room" wouldn't reach | All three |
| **"Born to win," not "right to win"** | Obsession that shows even in conversation. Not an incubator founder hunting for an idea | Sandhya |
| **Asymmetric thinking** | Founders who look for deviations, never averages. They make you see a market that doesn't exist yet (Fitbit's brick-with-AA-batteries prototype) | Rohit |
| **Customer contact before product** | "Who is the customer, how well do you know them, how will you reach them?" comes before the demo | Rohit |
| **Talent magnetism** | People quitting a strong company to follow you is the strongest pre-product green flag | Austin |
| **Two-register communication** | Can go deep technically *and* explain it to a layperson. The same skill drives hiring, fundraising and press | Austin, Sandhya |
| **"Why you win" over "moat"** | Moat is overrated at pre-seed. Answer why *you* beat five equally smart teams who reach the same conclusion | Sandhya |
| **Venture scale** | "$10M revenue potential" isn't a venture case | Sandhya |

## Red flags

- **AI-written cold outreach.** Spotted within a couple of words. "You invested in X, so look at us" is not an argument.
- **No path to a warm intro.** Not a judgment outsourced to the referrer. The point is that failing to find one is an
  "invisible" negative signal. Network angle: the firm's portfolio founders and employees count as paths in.
- **Not knowing your competitive set,** including anything that competes for the customer's *money or time*. If the
  VC names a competitor you haven't heard of, that's a flag.
- **Fundraising aversion.** "I want to get this over with so I can get back to the business" tells the VC you don't
  see capital-raising as part of the CEO job. Sandhya's own arc as a first-time GP shows this mindset is fixable.
- **"Show more traction" is a coded message.** It means some part of your story isn't believed. It's not a fixed
  metrics threshold.

## Relationship tactics

- Open with **"we're not raising right now."** It makes the conversation non-transactional and unlocks candid feedback.
  VCs give feedback only when asked.
- Treat the lead as a **7–10-year board relationship**. The scarce resource is founder time, not money.
- **The 9pm test:** can you text your first investor at night and get an answer in about 15 minutes?
- Geography: not a constraint for these three (Duo/Michigan; board seats in Zurich and Helsinki).

## Applies when

- Deep-tech, AI or new-category bets where no traction is possible yet, and a lead underwrites the team at $1–5M.
- The founder has a real, explainable experience asymmetry.

## Breaks when

- **The warm-intro filter hits founders without networks.** The panel conceded this (women and founders of color get
  "advice instead of money") and estimated 10–100 missed deals a year. Sandhya pushed back with a "no excuses" story:
  three months of grinding every contact from her career. Both are true. The filter is a *bug* the firms profit from
  tolerating.
- "Born to win" and "I can feel the passion over a few dinners" are unfalsifiable, so pattern-matching bias gets in.
  Compare with [[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]-style intuition-validity conditions: the feedback loop runs
  7–10 years and is survivorship-filtered.
- **Competitor overfunding** (an audience question: a rival raised $30M at $100M pre-launch). The panel's answer: it
  doesn't set your valuation, it raises your burden of proof. That's dodgy advice for hardware, where capital *is*
  the moat.

## Practitioner counterpoint

- [[harlem-capital-vc-insights]]: says to engineer warm intros (pre-built target lists, 2–3 specific asks). That's
  the tactical answer to this panel's structural complaint.
- [[primary-venture-partners-insights]]: scaled-seed firms *industrialize* sourcing (BDRs, talent teams). That works
  against the "only via network" claim and suggests cold inbound is becoming more viable at large seed platforms.
- [[entrepreneurship-operator-playbook]]: "between-rounds death." The panel's "you'll need $200M before product"
  remark is the extreme case. Size the pre-seed to reach a fundable milestone, not a demo.
- [[tiger-sisters-private-capital-101]]: the VC-as-clubby framing, confirmed here from the inside.
