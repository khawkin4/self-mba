# Note · JPMorganChase through outside eyes — the analysts are the only standing skeptics, partners praise the rails, and the sharpest critiques of JPM's theses appear on JPM's own pages

> Stakeholder / investor-relations note · Maps to **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations — the analyst Q&A as accountability), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — who holds power over JPM), **Course 05** (Marketing — partner quotes as proof), **E4** (Governance — proxy advisors, regulators) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[jpmc-people]] and [[tradeweb-outside-voices]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/jpmc/articles.jsonl`: analyst questions on ten earnings calls and at the 2025 Investor Day; partner, client and expert quotes in 140 web items (Kinexys releases, Markets articles, research pages); and outside parties Dimon argues with in the letters. JPM mentions in the Tradeweb corpus are marked [TW]. **No independent press was collected.** Every outside voice here was chosen by JPMC, except the analysts, who chose their own questions.
- **Core claim:** Four groups speak about JPMC in its own corpus. **(1) Sell-side analysts** are the only consistent skeptics. Mike Mayo (Wells Fargo) asked questions on every one of the ten calls read, mostly on expense and technology returns, and on succession. Others pressed on capital return, stablecoins, private credit, agentic AI and the co-president choice. **(2) Partners and clients** (Coinbase, Mastercard, Siemens, BMW, B2C2, Ethereum Institutional, BlackRock) praise the *rails*, not JPM's economics. **(3) Experts quoted on JPM pages** supply the strongest counterarguments to JPM's own theses: depegs (Better Markets), deposit-token reach (Fireblocks), agentic-AI risk (SEDA), the "invisible bubble" in volatility (Prescient), and Risk magazine on "run-the-bank" vs "change-the-bank". **(4) Regulators, proxy advisors and aggregators** appear only as Dimon's opponents. Power over JPM sits with regulators first, the largest institutional clients second and nonbank competitors third. Venues hold little visible power in this corpus, which itself is a JPM position ([[jpmc-markets-venues]]).

**Pattern:** analysts ask for **targets and returns**. Management gives **doctrine** ("output, not an input", "table stakes", "trust me"). Over six years the only quantitative concession was Barnum cutting the AI-spend figure *down* in 3Q26.

## 5. Who holds power over JPM (inference)

1. **Regulators.** They set capital, the binding constraint on Markets and on returns. Every letter spends its longest section on them.
2. **The largest institutional clients.** Wallet consolidation (client share up 230bp since 2019) makes the top asset managers and hedge funds both JPM's best customers and its biggest negotiators. BlackRock shows up as client, counterparty and voice.
3. **Nonbank competitors.** Citadel Securities in markets, Stripe and Block in payments, and stablecoin issuers in deposits set the price ceilings. Dimon names them.
4. **Analysts and investors.** They can't force targets, but six years of Mayo's questions forced the 3Q26 AI-spend correction.
5. **Venues.** They have little visible power in JPM's own telling, but real power where the dealer needs distribution (Treasury algos, credit). The ICE–MarketAxess and Tradeweb–Trumid consolidation could raise it ([[ice-hub]], [[marketaxess-hub]]).

## 6. Canon lenses

- **Influence and social proof** ([[canon-cialdini-influence]]): partner quotes in Kinexys releases (Coinbase, Mastercard, Ethereum) are social proof for a contested product.
- **Co-opetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): Coinbase is client, partner (consumer link) and rival (Base stablecoin flows) at once.
- **Agency and accountability** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): the analyst Q&A is the only live accountability channel in the corpus. Its weakness is that management can decline to answer.
