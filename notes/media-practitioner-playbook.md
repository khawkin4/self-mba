## 1 · Athletes & investing — high income ≠ wealth (12 episodes)
The throughline: a big paycheck is *income*, and income stops; **wealth comes from saving discipline +
ownership of assets.** The earning window is brutally short (avg NBA career ~4.7 yrs, retirement ~32–37)
and the broke stats are consistent across sources: **~60% of NBA / ~78% of NFL players hit hardship within
2–5 yrs of retiring** (Barkley says "80%"). *(Reinforces `learn/unit-01` revenue≠profit, `unit-05` durable
vs. fleeting.)*
- **Spending is the variable, not income.** "It's all about the *out*-take" — a player who made $35M over
  10 yrs can still go broke. Reframes: Barkley/Dr. J — *"you can afford that car at 20; can you at 60?"*
  ($200K saved on a cheaper car compounds 40 yrs); Herm Edwards — *"you only need one"* (one car, one
  house); Foyle — *"one Ferrari is enough."*
- **Shaq's rip-the-$100 rule (~75/25):** rip $100 → save $50; of that $50 the wealthy *"take half [$25] and
  put it all away — invest it, don't touch it"*; only the last $25 is fun money. Save/invest ~75%, spend
  ~25%. Plus his father's line: *"education is something that can never be taken away"* (he earned a PhD).
- **The named broke-mechanisms (remarkably consistent):** lifestyle inflation (*"I didn't go broke buying
  one mansion — I went broke maintaining five"*), the **entourage** (Grant Hill's mom to Barkley: *"they're
  never going to stop, and the first time you tell them no, they hate you"* — *"if your circle only stays
  because you pay, it's a cage"*), ego/predatory investments, taxes + fees (sub-40% take-home), short
  window, lost identity in retirement, and — uniquely stressed — **misaligned advisors** + a scarcity/"we
  made it" family mindset with no inherited money values.
- **The athlete-to-OWNER playbook (the role models).** Magic's seed line to an 18-yr-old Shaq: *"it's okay
  to be beloved by the fans, but at some point you want to start owning things."*
  - **Magic Johnson** — the most *replicable* model (only ~$40M career salary → ~$1.5B built via serial
    JVs: Coke bottling, theaters in underserved LA, the **first-ever Starbucks franchisee** (~125 stores,
    sold for ~$75–100M), then 60% of EquiTrust life insurance (>$1B) + team stakes. Edge: *"expertise
    reaching the African-American customer"* (sweet-potato pie, not scones).
  - **Junior Bridgeman** — the purest *skill-built* example: a modestly-paid player → ~500–1,000 Wendy's/
    Chili's franchises → billionaire, no marquee name. Barkley: the NBA should teach rookies *him.*
  - **Shaq** (24 Hour Fitness/Krispy Kreme/Five Guys/Big Chicken), **Curry** (Thirty Ink: Curry Brand, a
    VC fund, production co.; the Under Armour "marketing steal of the century" → his own standalone brand),
    **LeBron** (Rich Paul = income + Maverick Carter = business, a de facto family office).
- **Influence/relationship capital is an under-priced, *ownable* asset (Kai Cunningham, Limited Ventures).**
  Modern portfolio theory doesn't price an athlete's influence — pool 50–100 of them for **access at scale**
  to private/PE/VC deals; trade likeness for *equity*, not cash (Beyoncé: *"pay me in equity"*). But cap any
  high-risk class at <~30% of net liquid worth; *"most of the upside in venture is in the boring businesses."*
- **The mentor (no financial stake) is the missing safeguard** (Cedric Nash mentors Chiefs players free,
  *"play defense — you already won the game"*; live off asset income, never touch principal; Kobe
  cold-calls CEOs for their reading lists). Kobe's transition test isn't *"where's the most revenue"* but
  *"what's my next passion?"* Patience rule: *"until you find people who genuinely care, do nothing — park
  it in T-bills/CDs."* Foyle: *"think of yourself as a small business" — get involved, don't outsource.*

## 2 · Earn Your Leisure / Market Mondays — ownership for everyone
EYL's core message: **financial literacy + ownership is accessible to the blue-collar / middle class**,
not just the already-rich. **→ Full per-episode extraction (all 11 videos pulled) in
[[eyl-insights]].** Headlines:
- **The "9-to-5 millionaire":** build wealth *while keeping the job* — "your job is your first business
  partner" (it qualifies you for the mortgage/credit). Real estate is the recurring engine; the product
  is **hope + a concrete path**, which lands where a textbook doesn't.
- **You can't save or work your way to wealth — invest and *own*** (the single most-repeated line); own a
  share of what you already consume. *(Ties to `learn/unit-06` value = future cash; ownership = a claim.)*
- **Structure is the engine, not just the asset** — HELOC/refi recycling, hard money over banks, the
  3-LLC + Rockefeller-trust + life-insurance architecture for protection, tax efficiency, dynasty.
- **Trading ≠ investing; relationships > talent; tax literacy is where the wealthy win; follow the
  institutions.** See [[eyl-insights]] for the full eight cross-episode themes.
- Source: Earn Your Leisure / Market Mondays — the highest-volume vein (~530K chars across 11 distinct
  episodes: wealth-building, real estate, credit, NFL money, market breakdowns).

## 3 · Forbes — investing & entrepreneurship (9 episodes; Ackman the centerpiece)
**Bill Ackman's investing philosophy** (the ~200K-char Steve Forbes interview) — a practitioner restatement
of the value canon:
- **Own "royalty / annuity" businesses that can grow forever.** Value = present value of cash over the
  business's *life*, so the business must **endure** — and the ideal earns a perpetuity *without* having to
  reinvest capital (a royalty): asset managers, franchise restaurants (franchisees fund the capex; the
  brand takes a royalty per coffee/donut), Universal Music, Hilton, even Uber (~20% of every ride). The
  hard part isn't spotting the royalty — it's predicting *which endure.* *(↔ [[canon-helmer-7-powers]],
  [[canon-greenwald-competition-demystified]].)*
- **Valuation: a stock can be expensive at 10× and cheap at 50×.** P/E is a crude shortcut; cheapness is
  about *future* cash, not the optical multiple. **Chipotle** during the 2015–16 food-safety crisis didn't
  look statistically cheap, but was — if you believed the trajectory was fixable (→ ~9×). *(↔
  [[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]] price≠value.)*
- **Concentration follows the standard.** *"You get 1–2 good ideas a year — that's enough."* Demanding
  super-durable royalties leaves few names; he holds few and long.
- **Capital structure is a weapon — match it to your horizon.** An open-ended hedge fund whose capital
  *"can leave overnight"* is a bank run waiting to happen. **Permanent capital** literally saved him in the
  Valeant squeeze (rivals shorted his longs betting redemptions would force a fire sale; he borrowed and
  *bought control of his own vehicle*). The Buffett/Berkshire model: an insurer *inside* a diversified,
  profitable holdco gets a better rating → runs lower leverage → invests the float more aggressively →
  makes money on *both* sides. *(↔ [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]] capital allocation.)*
- **"Lending on character" cuts both ways** — JPM lent him unsecured mid-crisis (he'd never defaulted); he
  later *volunteered* to pay 25bps more when an old line turned unprofitable for the bank. Reputation
  compounds like capital.
- **Personal compounding / temperament** (taught to students): *"compounding is the solution to most
  progress"* — ~0.1%/day, invisible for weeks then it curves up; **never look back at where you were.** Get
  healthy (he went zero-sugar), keep perspective, *"love saves us all."*
- **The activist tool is for those nobody knows yet** — his first trade (Wendy's/Tim Hortons split, forced
  via a Blackstone fairness opinion filed in a 13-D → stock doubled). He's "retired" from it because *"people
  take our call now."* Fannie/Freddie thesis: a recap/exit from a 16-yr "temporary" conservatorship.

**Entrepreneur profiles (the recurring tactics):**
- **Inexperience/youth as a moat** — Kate Barton (Forbes 30u30 fashion): no training = *"no preconceived
  idea of how it had to be done"*; patented an inside-out draping technique; launched **extreme-first** to
  imprint brand DNA so small later products read as hers; a viral "goldfish bag" = a free conversation
  starter. Lucy Guo (youngest self-made woman billionaire): *"your network is your net worth"*; optimize for
  compounding **knowledge** over money (left millions when she stopped learning); *"just ask — the first yes
  changes your life."*
- **Customer discovery beats idea-hoarding** — Decagon ($1.5B AI unicorn in ~1 yr): the product came from
  weeks of enterprise meetings, not a preset idea; win the use case with obvious ROI (*"I spend $50M now;
  I can spend $10M"*); fundraising was a *byproduct*, never the goal. *(↔ entrepreneurship-playbook §1.)*
- **Capital discipline + reputation** — Roy Carroll ($2.9B real estate): reinvested *a third of profits*
  annually from year one; during the 2008–09 crash *paid off* his condo tower and never cut a price → a
  durable reputational moat. Anastasia Soare built the **eyebrow category** from the golden ratio; Avenue
  Capital shifted to long-term PE capital *before* 2008 → had dry powder when redeeming hedge funds died.
- **Convertibles = positive asymmetry** (Tracy Maitland, Advent): *"heads you win, tails you don't lose"* —
  most of a stock's upside, a fraction of the downside; accessible via a $500–1,000 convertible fund. An
  overlooked asset class *because* it's overlooked. *(↔ [[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]] asymmetry.)*
- **"50 entrepreneurs" greatest hits:** passion as survival (not slogan); the **idea muscle** (write 10
  ideas/day); *"sound crazy but be right"*; story over stats (Godin's lemonade stand); *"you don't have to
  be the best, just dangerous"*; partner non-negotiables = intelligence + energy + **integrity** (the worst
  case is *"a smart, hardworking crook"*); pre-define the worst case before you start.

## 4 · HBR IdeaCast — operating, leadership & decisions (9 episodes)
**Andy Jassy — running a giant *"like the world's largest startup"* (6 ingredients):** (1) **solve a real
customer problem** (*"companies fall in love with the technology… and haven't solved anything
remarkable"*); (2) hire **builders** (dissect a good experience and rebuild it); (3) hire **owners**
(*"what would I do if this were my money?"*); (4) **speed is a leadership decision**, not a constraint of
size; (5) **be scrappy** (S3 started ~13 people, EC2 with 11); (6) **take risks / tolerate failure**
(type-A achievers *"play not to lose"*). Mechanics: push **two-way-door (reversible) decisions** down to
the people doing the work; raise the IC-to-manager ratio ≥15%; a *"no bureaucracy"* email alias →
**375 processes changed**; distinguish *process* (needed at scale) from *bureaucracy* (no creative value).
*(↔ [[canon-cagan-inspired]], [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]], [[canon-marquet-turn-the-ship-around]],
[[canon-alchian-demsetz-firm]].)*
- **Commander's Intent (Col. Gibson) = centralized command, *decentralized execution*:** state the **why**,
  the **key task (3–5 things, not 20)**, and the **end state** — then *don't over-specify the how* (*"if you
  keep telling people how, they'll wait to be told"*). High standards + latitude = invented new ways to
  clear the bar. *(↔ [[canon-marquet-turn-the-ship-around]], OKRs as the "end state.")*
- **Decision-making is a *process*, not an event (Garvin & Roberto):** run **inquiry** (weigh many options,
  welcome dissent), not **advocacy** (champion-and-defend). The **3 C's**: promote *cognitive* **Conflict**
  / suppress *affective* friction (devil's-advocate framing, break up coalitions, have people argue a role
  that isn't theirs); **Consideration** (win acceptance even when you reject the recommendation); **Closure**
  (don't satisfice early or dither late). *(↔ structured decision-making / red-teaming.)*
- **Make every meeting matter:** ≤7 people, ≤1 hr, ban devices (multitasking *"takes 50% longer, 50% more
  mistakes"*), stand-ups are ~34% shorter for the same output, **never meet just to update** (that's email
  — and update meetings *deplete executive resources*). Pre-commit **3–5 desired outcomes**; *"if you can't
  say what the meeting will accomplish, don't have it."* *(↔ [[canon-grove-high-output-management]].)*
- **Leadership style ≠ personality — it's learnable behavior (Suzanne Peterson):** decode attributions into
  behaviors ("abrasive" = disagreeing *disagreeably*); balance **power** vs. **attractive** behaviors
  (people stall by leaning *attractive*); over-rotation is the classic error (do 2–3 tweaks, not 5–6);
  behavior first, feelings follow. Names the women/POC **double bind** (likability drops as success rises).
- **What AI *can't* do, and the operating pivot (McKinsey/Sternfels):** half the value of AI is
  **organizational change, not tech** (break the walls between the 4–5 departments in a process); the
  durable human edge is **aspiration, judgment, and discontinuous (non-linear) thinking** — so they hire
  for **resilience > perfect marks**, real teamwork, and learning aptitude, and shifted from fee-for-service
  to **underwriting client outcomes** (skin in the game; ~⅓ of revenue). *(↔ [[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]], incentive alignment.)*
- **Great leadership = co-creation, not followership (Linda Hill's ABCs):** **Architect** the culture for
  *"collective genius,"* **Bridge** outside for talent/tools, **Catalyze** the whole ecosystem — and *"let
  go of formal authority"* (it buys compliance, not the commitment innovation needs). Plus **Strack's
  "Strategize Your Life"** (BCG portfolio logic on your 168-hr week; fix the high-importance/low-
  satisfaction quadrant) and the **interview guide** (3–4 story-driven messages; opinions form in 30s).

## Cross-cutting
- **Income vs. wealth → ownership** is the unifying lesson: a paycheck is rented; **assets are owned.**
  Athletes, EYL, Magic/Bridgeman, and Ackman's royalty businesses all converge on *own the durable,
  cash-producing thing.* *(↔ [[eyl-insights]], [[harlem-capital-vc-insights]].)*
- **Durability / non-replicability is the prize, and capital *structure* is a weapon** — Ackman's
  permanent capital, Avenue Capital's pre-2008 PE shift, Carroll's reinvest-a-third discipline: the patient
  structure beats the optically faster one. The same lesson the VC note tells from the allocator's seat.
- **Reputation/character compounds like capital** — Ackman's "lending on character," Carroll paying off his
  tower, the entrepreneurs' non-negotiable "integrity." *(↔ [[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]].)*
- **The canon and the street agree** — Jassy's "customer problem + owners + speed" *is* Bezos + Cagan +
  Marquet; commander's intent *is* decentralized command; Garvin/Roberto inquiry *is* structured
  decision-making; Ackman *is* value investing. The practitioner layer makes the canon **concrete and
  motivating** for a beginner.
