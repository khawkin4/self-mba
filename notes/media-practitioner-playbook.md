## 1 · Athletes & investing — high income ≠ wealth
The throughline of the athlete interviews: a big paycheck is *income*, and income stops; **wealth comes
from saving discipline + ownership of assets.** (Directly reinforces `learn/unit-01` revenue≠profit and
`learn/unit-05` durable vs. fleeting.)
- **Shaquille O'Neal's "rip the $100" rule:** save **half** immediately; of that half, the truly wealthy
  *"take half of that 50 [and] put all that away"*; only the rest is *"house money… to play with."* Invest
  the savings and don't touch it. A vivid, beginner-grade savings/compounding discipline (CNBC Make It).
- **Stephen Curry — the athlete-to-owner playbook:** at his peak he converted earnings *and* cultural
  influence into **ownership** — a *"sprawling business portfolio that spans brand partnerships, media,
  investments, and philanthropy"* (Bloomberg). The lesson: build durable equity *beyond the core skill*
  while you're at the top, because the playing career ends. *(Echoes [[canon-helmer-7-powers]] branding;
  ownership over salary.)*
- **The cautionary frame ("why athletes go broke"):** without assets and discipline, peak income
  evaporates — consumption scales with the paycheck, then the paycheck stops. Sources: Bloomberg
  (Curry), CNBC Make It / WSJ (Shaq), Yahoo Finance ("sustainable wealth for athletes' families").

## 2 · Earn Your Leisure / Market Mondays — ownership for everyone
EYL's core message: **financial literacy + ownership is accessible to the blue-collar / middle class**,
not just the already-rich.
- **The "9-to-5 millionaire":** you can build wealth *while keeping a regular job* — start a business or
  invest (real estate is their recurring engine) on the side. Repeated profiles of working people
  (a police officer; a Brooklyn entrepreneur) who reached millionaire status without quitting first.
  The product is **hope + a concrete path**, which is why it lands where a textbook doesn't.
- **Assets over consumption** — own the income-producing thing (a business, real estate, equity) rather
  than spending the income. *(Ties to `learn/unit-06` value = future cash; ownership = a claim on it.)*
- Source: Earn Your Leisure (the highest-volume vein pulled — 650K+ chars across wealth-building,
  ownership, real estate, and market-breakdown episodes).

## 3 · Forbes — investing & the long game (Bill Ackman)
- **Entrepreneurial wiring early:** Ackman ran a Harvard travel-guide hustle that *"for a college kid was
  real money"* — the instinct to *own and build* preceded any title (Forbes).
- **Long-term investing:** the interview's theme is patience + conviction in quality businesses over
  cycles — the antidote to the Peloton-style fad chasing in `learn/unit-05`. *(Reinforces
  [[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]] price≠value + [[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]] process over
  outcome.)* Also in the vein: Forbes "secret to building wealth with credit," entrepreneur profiles.

## 4 · HBR IdeaCast — operating at scale (Andy Jassy / Amazon)
The sharpest operating lesson of the pull. Jassy on running a giant company *"like the world's largest
startup"* — four things, which map almost 1:1 to the canon:
1. **Solve a real customer problem** — *"a lot of… technology companies fall in love with the technology…
   and they haven't really solved anything remarkable."* *(= [[canon-cagan-inspired]] value risk;
   [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]] customer obsession.)*
2. **Hire disproportionately many *builders*** — people who *"dissect a customer experience and figure out
   what's wrong with it, even if it's pretty good, and then rebuild it."*
3. **Owners** — *"what would I do if this were my money? …people who really feel accountable"* — which is
   why they *flatten the org* so the people doing the work think like owners. *(= [[canon-marquet-turn-the-ship-around]] authority to where the work is; [[canon-alchian-demsetz-firm]] ownership & incentives.)*
4. **Speed** — *"speed disproportionately matters in every business at every time."* *(= [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]] high-velocity, two-way doors.)*
- Also in the vein: McKinsey on surviving/​reinventing around AI; HBR Guide to making meetings matter
  ([[canon-grove-high-output-management]] — meetings as the medium of management).

## Cross-cutting
- **Income vs. wealth** is the unifying beginner lesson the practitioner sources teach better than the
  textbooks: a paycheck is rented; **ownership of assets is owned.** (Athletes & EYL both hammer it.)
- **The canon and the street agree** — Jassy's "real customer problem + owners + speed" *is* Bezos +
  Cagan + Marquet in plain operator language; Ackman *is* value investing. The practitioner layer makes
  the canon **concrete and motivating** for a beginner.
