## Power mapping (who actually decides)

The governing idea, straight from Jeffrey Pfeffer's *Paths to Power* / *Power* (the Stanford GSB class the sisters cite repeatedly): **"power isn't about being right — it's about having enough support and alliances to survive being right."** Merit alone loses to people who understand influence. [[canon-pfeffer-power]] [[canon-greene-48-laws-of-power]]

- **Power ≠ title.** Power = who controls resources, who influences decisions, who's "pulling the strings behind the scenes." Track *that*, not the org chart.
- **The two case studies.** *Sheryl Sandberg (right way):* accumulated **influence, not titles** — chief of staff at US Treasury → BizOps under Eric Schmidt at Google (deliberately cross-functional, became the "go-to" partner) → COO at Facebook. Hired for her **power map spanning sectors** (tech + government), not just strategy chops. *Carol Bartz (cautionary):* brilliant, brought in to turn around Yahoo, executed objectively-correct tactical moves but **never built her internal power map**, so she had no allies to "survive being right" — fired by phone call.
- **The drill — map your power web:** pick the **5 people who will influence your next opportunity** (raise, role, deal). Tag each as **advocate / neutral / unaware**. Connect with one this week to move them up a tier. "Being excellent without visibility is a silent career killer."
- **Reframe the "ick."** Pfeffer's own disclaimer: this is *a* description of how power works, not the only way to live. The sisters' resolution: the ick fades when (a) you genuinely believe your mission is right for the team/business, and (b) you operate inside *your own* values — there's a Machiavellian extreme they refuse, and authentic operators they respect *more*, not less.

## Meeting tactics (decisions happen before the meeting)

Goldman rule #6, "seek consensus and input from colleagues," decoded: **the decision is not made in the decision-making meeting.**

- **Pre-wire / back-channel.** Walk in already knowing, with ~85%+ confidence, what every person thinks and how they'd "vote" and why. Ideally you've had **1:1s with each attendee**, surfaced their concerns/rebuttals, and addressed them in the **low-stakes setting first** — never put it all on the line in the high-stakes room. "The meeting is more of just a way for everyone to state it in front of everyone else." Top ~1% of performers do this. [[canon-cialdini-pre-suasion]]
- **Buy-in via authorship.** "The best way to get people on board… is to have them feel like part of it was their idea that you are now executing on their behalf." Brainstorms often serve buy-in as much as ideation.
- **Speak last + summarize-then-add.** In a meeting, summarize others succinctly, then build: *"What I heard Britney say was XYZ, and Tim said ABC — I really like that because…"* then make your point. Naming people makes them **perk up, feel heard, and auto-support you** because you built on them. This is how "great presenters" get cast as the connector who pulls it all together. [[canon-carnegie-win-friends]]
- **Seat selection is a nonverbal vote.** Where you sit signals how you regard your own role. Sit at the table (Sandberg: "take a seat at the table") to be a player; sit on the side wall if you deliberately want low presence. Studies + lived experience: women gravitate to the back, men to the table — be conscious that others are being strategic even if you aren't.

## Negotiation (find the orange, don't split it)

Framing: "I think every conversation is a negotiation," but calling it that sounds adversarial when most of the time it isn't. Goal = **expand the pie, not divide it.** [[canon-fisher-ury-getting-to-yes]] [[canon-voss-never-split-the-difference]]

- **The orange (integrative negotiation).** Don't reflexively cut 50/50 — *information-gather*. One party wants the **peel** (baking), the other the **juice**. Symbiosis = each gets **100%** of what they actually need. "1+1 = 5" when your top priority doesn't conflict with theirs (Stanford negotiation class, Prof. Michelle Gelfand). Zero-sum fights get "ugly"; most situations only *look* zero-sum because you assumed it.
- **Comp example.** You want salary; there may be more wiggle room in **bonus/equity**. If you don't learn what *they* can flex, you can't build the best deal. Anchor on shared interest: "both of us want me to stay and grow here — now fill in the pieces."
- **The 3-step influence framework:**
  1. **Research the counterpart** — their goals, KPIs, motives.
  2. **Frame your ask so it also advances their goals.**
  3. **Enter ready to trade** — bring creative, non-obvious options so the puzzle pieces fit.
- **Know the resource map.** Influence starts with knowing what *you* uniquely provide and what *everyone else* controls (Snap example: panic over building a last-minute deck dissolved once they realized the partner had a deck-building team — collaborate by mapping resources).

## Feedback & SBI (impact, never intent)

From Stanford's "Touchy-Feely" (Interpersonal Dynamics) — also in the Netflix culture deck, Microsoft new-manager training, and Adobe's move off annual reviews. [[canon-stone-difficult-conversations]] [[canon-scott-radical-candor]] [[canon-patterson-crucial-conversations]]

- **SBI = Situation → Behavior → Impact.**
  - **S:** when/where it happened (the lament, contextualized).
  - **B:** what they did, **objectively, no judgment** — "observed behavior no one can contest."
  - **I:** the effect / how it made you feel.
- **Why most feedback fails:** people jump straight to Impact. Do S and B *first* to disarm defensiveness.
- **"Crossing the net."** Never assume the other person's **intent** — you can't know their head. Speak only to **impact**. Pair with Brené Brown's **"The story I'm telling myself is…"** to separate your interpretation from reality.
- **Syntax that disarms:** say **"I felt X when this happened"** not **"you made me feel X."** Tiny change, large effect ("a bit disarming… the power of language"). Works in personal life too ("Thanks for telling me that").
- **Worked script (work):** *"In yesterday's client pitch [S], you interrupted me while I was presenting our strategy [B], and it made me feel undermined and less confident in front of the client [I]."*
- **Invert SBI to *request* feedback:** *"When I shared the product plan [S], what stood out about how I presented it [B], and what was the impact on the team's decision [I]?"* — "Don't just wait for feedback, design for it."
- **Ship early (the feedback fallacy, HBR / Radical Candor).** People grow from **insights**, not praise or critique — and only when feedback is **directional + timely**, not perfect. "Top women ship early and often." Train the muscle of speaking before your thought is fully baked (HBS made 50% of every class grade pure participation — *that you spoke at all*, not how brilliant it was).

## Executive comms & SCQA (clear > smart)

From Matt Abrahams' strategic-comms class; SCQA framework by Barbara Minto (Pyramid Principle). "Being clear is more powerful than being smart." If you can't make your point in ~30 seconds, people tune out. [[canon-minto-pyramid-principle]] [[canon-duarte-resonate]]

- **SCQA = Situation → Complication → Question → Answer.** Worked example (marketing-spend pitch): *"Right now CAC is rising faster than LTV [S]. If we don't adjust, we'll overspend [C]. So what do we do? [Q] Shift 40% of spend to UGC creators [A]."*
- **Test it:** say your pitch aloud to a friend; if they can repeat the idea in **one sentence**, it works.
- **Simplify ruthlessly** (Glenn Kremen's "Winning Writing," Stanford): drill of taking ~10 terms and simplifying, simplifying, simplifying. Over-smart phrasing buries the point; plain language lands (the "big beautiful bill" effect — extreme, but the mechanism is real).
- **The 15 / 5 / 2 prep.** Before any senior conversation, prepare the **15-minute, 5-minute, and 2-minute** versions of your message. Lead with the shortest; if they want depth they'll ask, or follow up by email. "Twitter, not Tolstoy" for written notes to execs — lead with the new info/value, minimal real estate.
- **Email haiku (Kim Scott, via Lorraine K. Lee).** Make the email short enough to **fit a phone screen without scrolling.** Spend the extra 20–30 min cutting; it prevents clarifying round-trips. Subject line is prime real estate: front-load "Action needed," "FYI no response needed," "Due April 7." Finance extreme: whole message in the subject line + **(EOM)**.
- **Amazon's 6-pager / "future press release"** uses SCQA to state the problem, the user pain, and what the product clearly solves. Repeatable template for any launch memo.

## Body language & visibility (perception is the product)

With Lorraine K. Lee (*Unforgettable Presence*). First impressions form in **~1/10 second** (and "last a lifetime" — Goldman rule #10: formed in ~30 sec across **visual / verbal / vocal**, sisters add "vibes").

- **Executive presence** = confidence, calm, gravitas — felt through **eye contact, posture, tenor, steadiness of voice**, often more than content. People weight *how you look and sound* heavily.
- **Kill RBF ("resting business face").** On video especially, neutral reads as angry and gets magnified. Antidotes: **smile, slow nods, eyebrow raises** on what resonates. Disproportionately penalizes women (expected to signal warmth) — but it's within your control.
- **Use your hands on camera.** Hand gestures lift **anecdote recall ~33%** and read as warmer/more trustworthy. Fix your framing: ~3–5 fingers of space above your head, show some torso, so gestures land in-frame instead of by your ears. Movement holds attention (the "Cocomelon" principle). Match gesture to message ("one, two, three" enumerated physically) so visual + vocal align.
- **First-impression hygiene:** no phone when greeting; never open a call with "one sec, let me send this" — it signals you're not present.
- **Brag without bragging.** Hard work doesn't speak for itself — "if no one knows about it, what's it all for?" Tactics: use **"we/us/our"** (reads more senior; "I"-heavy reads junior, per HBR), **frame wins as team learnings** ("this might help everyone…"), and **ground everything in data tied to the bottom line** — "no one can argue with that." On LinkedIn, mix three content pillars — **personal / educational / professional** — to buffer self-promotion (educational is easiest to start, personal resonates most).
- **Repeat 7× in 7 ways.** People retain on repetition across channels/time zones — Slack *and* email *and* the team meeting. Say it where the decision-makers actually are (the right Slack channel, the company intranet), not just your small team.
- **Minimizing language to cut** (Dr. Deb Gruenfeld, Stanford — *"language doesn't reflect power, it creates power"*): "I just wanted to…", "I think maybe…", "Does this make sense?", "Sorry I'm late." Swaps: → "Following up on…" / "I recommend…" / "the data suggests…" / **"Let me know if you have any questions"** (sure, still collaborative) / **"Thank you for your patience"** (starts the meeting positive instead of apologetic). Record yourself, **count the hedges**, rework. Use the **power of the pause** instead of "um/like" (how Michelle Obama, *Becoming*, sounds so deliberate — cadence and pauses rehearsed in).

## Networking (serendipity favors the prepared & open)

- **Mindset first.** Outcomes track your internal state more than the room — bring real energy or give yourself grace and skip it; a wrong-headspace conference is a wash (the London Shop Talk story).
- **Don't go alone / make a "conference buddy."** A buddy lets you regroup, strategize ("split for 20 min, debrief"), and **extract you from dead conversations.**
- **Safe entry points:** the food/drink area; large open circles (read the room — never crash an intense 1:1); opener *"Is this your first time at [event]?"*
- **Skip the ticket, take the meetings.** Camp the **conference-hotel lobby**, pre-arrange meetings with attendees; serendipity does the rest (lobby chats → LinkedIn intros → new doors).
- **Do the prep:** get the attendee/speaker list, pick targets, **reach out beforehand** ("I saw you'll be at [event] — would love to connect while we're both in Miami"). Look people up at signup events; 3 seconds of research enriches every chat.
- **Mentorship — earn it, don't ask for it cold.** "Will you be my mentor?" is jarring. Let it form over **~3 coffee chats**; ask friends *who their mentors are* and get introduced; seek mentors **in other orgs** (different perspective, can sponsor you in rooms you're not in). As mentee, **come with a tight agenda** ("here are the 3 things I'd love to cover — cool?") — it signals you respect their time and makes them open their network. Keep an actual **tracker/list** of people to follow up with.
- **Manage mentors** (Goldman rule #7). *Pre:* prepare ~10 questions, research their LinkedIn, **bring a notebook and write down answers** (Albert: "no one has ever written down anything I've said — I knew I wanted her on my team"). *Post:* follow up within ~15 min of effort — **specific** thank-you referencing what you took away ("when we talked about XYZ, it left an impact in ABC way"). The follow-up *is* the relationship.
- **Exit gracefully:** (a) pre-arrange a buddy to "come get me in 20 min"; (b) own it — *"I have to head out soon and want to say bye to Amy — should we connect on LinkedIn to continue this?"* You only owe "the thinnest gossamer of an excuse."
- **Allies & cross-company web.** Seek mentors *and* peer allies on arrival; cultivate relationships **across the whole company**, not just your group — a broad web is far more powerful. Build **trust that buys benefit-of-the-doubt** with senior partners (off day forgiven; ~0.5× the defensive mental gymnastics). With execs, be **prepared but not robotic** — "they want to work with a person, not a robot." Mandy: invest in relationships *because the people make you happy and you learn from them*, not transactionally — they bear fruit (board seats, deals) over years; "women are the worst at it" because of competing care duties, so deliberately create the dinner the men get from golf.

## Asking for money & promotions ("Just ask")

Mandy Ginsburg (ex-CEO, Match Group): *"Open your mouth and ask if you want something… the worst that happens is no — what's the best case?"* Asking is a **trainable muscle**, hardest for those not raised doing it. [[canon-cialdini-influence]]

- **Ask even if you'll be told no** — it plants in your manager's head that it matters to you; it pays off in this job or the next. "Squeaky wheels do get paid more" (stated as a fact she dislikes, alongside: you must *also* create real value). Trigger that finally moved one host: discovering people she'd hired, levels below her, were paid more.
- **The "because" effect (Ellen Langer copier study, ~93% compliance).** Adding a reason — *even a weak/circular one* — sharply raises a yes. "Can I cut the line **because** I need to make copies?" still worked. Everyday use: *"Can you send this by Friday because I'm excited to share what you did with the company"* / *"because I'm OOO Friday."* Use the power consciously (and yes — people ask for discounts at Louis Vuitton "because I want one," and get them). [[canon-cialdini-influence]]
- **Promotions are pre-wired months ahead** (Lorraine). Most people wrongly raise it at review time. Instead: **tell your manager you want it ~6 months early** (don't assume they know — many people *don't* want promotion); learn **who's actually in the room** (it's not just your manager); identify the missing skill/objection and address it; then **repeat the ask** periodically (not every 1:1). Bring your manager on board so they do the off-stage work — line up yes-votes, defuse objectors in advance — "the two of you against the world." Net: your boss doesn't have to *like* you, but must **respect your work and find you make their life easier** (10× performer or reliably pleasant — ideally both).
- **Comp psychology (board-level, Mandy).** Pay must be **aligned to value creation**. Risk tolerance is individual — offering **option vs. RSU choice** lets people self-manage risk; most humans choose the lower-risk path. Run **pay-equity audits** (Match found women in accounting underpaid because long-tenured women fell behind market-rate external male hires) — tenure quietly skews comp.
- **Teach it forward:** girls pull back at puberty exactly when they should speak up. Make the ask *real* by writing/saying it (journaling, mirror, "manifest it out loud").

## Decision tools (rewire your operating system)

- **Jobs-To-Be-Done** (Clay Christensen, HBS). "People don't buy products, they **hire** them to do a job — usually **emotional**, not just functional." LinkedIn profile update = visibility vs. validation vs. getting hired; Sisters Matcha = also hiring the *narrative/belonging*. **Drill:** what are people actually hiring *you* for — competence, calm, confidence, entertainment ("personality hire")? [[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]
- **Decision trees & expected value.** Beat paralysis: (1) list options, (2) sketch outcomes, (3) assign **probability × payoff**, (4) compute EV. Map **best/worst/most-likely** with values, multiply by likelihoods. A "great-on-paper" job scored *meh*; entrepreneurship (low near-term value, high option value/upside) scored higher. "Gut is cute; billion-dollar companies use math" (Netflix greenlights, VC bets, Amazon pricing). The framework lets you "be wrong **safely**" — worse than a wrong call is staying stuck. Mantra (Tomer Cohen, LinkedIn CPO): *"I may be wrong, but I'm not confused."*
- **OKRs-for-life** (Andy Grove/Intel → Google → *Measure What Matters*). **Objective** = the goal; **Key Results** = the measurable milestones that ladder to it. Works personally (Objective: improve mental health → KR1 3 therapy sessions this quarter, KR2 journal 2×/week, KR3 one full rest day). Benefits: the laddering *forces* clarity on what actually achieves the goal; **shared OKRs create social accountability + team support**; they're a **guardrail** to decline work that doesn't ladder ("I'd love to, but I need to focus on my stated OKR"). Watch the failure mode: personal OKRs not laddering to the org's. [[canon-doerr-measure-what-matters]] [[canon-grove-high-output-management]]
- **Separate fact from feeling (CBT / "emotional agility," Susan David).** "Separate stimulus from response / signal from noise." Tool: **"The story I'm telling myself is…"** Indra Nooyi wrote inflammatory remarks **verbatim** in tense meetings to reflect later instead of reacting. **Drill:** two columns — *what was said/done* (observed behavior) vs. *how I feel* — then ask "did the event cause my emotion, or my interpretation of it?"
- **Ruthless calendar alignment (Essentialism, Greg McKeown).** Success = doing **less but better**; your calendar reflects your true priorities — if it's not blocked, you don't really value it (Sandberg's non-negotiable family-dinner block = leadership by *action*). **Drill:** color-code last week green (aligned) / red (not) → cut or delegate the red, or others control your time by default.
- **Identity-based habits (Atomic Habits, James Clear).** Set identity, not just goals: say **"I am a writer,"** not "I want to write." Beyoncé→Sasha Fierce (mental priming); Simone Biles affirmations ("I am unshakable" — identity locking). **Drill:** pick the identity you're stepping into and say the **"I am…"** statement daily, then take one action that proves it.

## Asking smart, attention to detail, intellectual honesty (Goldman ethos)

- **Ask the dumb questions** (rule #9: "Ask questions; assuming is too dangerous"). "It's smart to be dumb and ask" — pretending to know makes you a **midwit** who acts on false confidence. *Distinction:* a *clarifying* question ("could you explain that another way?") is fine; a *stupid* question is re-asking something already answered (to you) — so **write answers down** and don't ask twice.
- **Attention to detail is critical — someone will notice** (rule #8). Junior people own the details (a missing comma can lose a client) so seniors can zoom out to strategy; you're rewarded for precision and reliability. (Self-aware caveat: Goldman's perfectionism could tip into self-importance — calibrate.)
