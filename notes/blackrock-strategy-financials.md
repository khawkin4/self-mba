# Note · BlackRock strategy and financials, 2018–Q3 2026 — a flow machine whose margin depends on markets; the 2024–25 deals are a bet that private markets and technology earn the company a higher multiple

- **Core claim:**
  - **The engine is flows.** BlackRock gathered $124B–$698B a year in net new assets, a record in most years since 2019, led by iShares.
  - **Revenue and margin follow markets.** Revenue fell 8% in 2022, then grew 14% (2024) and 19% (2025). Adjusted margin moved from 46.8% (2021) to 41.7% (2023), which the 2025 deck labels the market-cycle low, then to 47.8% (Q3 2026). The 2025 deck restates earlier years on a recast basis, so the 2021–22 margins come from the 2023 deck.
  - **Strategy moved through three phases:**
    - 2019–21: ETFs, Aladdin, sustainability, China.
    - 2022–23: defence and restructuring.
    - 2024–26: transformation through acquisitions (GIP, Preqin, HPS, about $27B).
  - **The 2030 ambition (Investor Day 2025):** revenue above $35B, adjusted operating income roughly doubled to ~$15B, market cap doubled to ~$280B. Private markets plus technology should reach at least 30% of revenue.
  - **The bet** is that those revenue streams command "higher multiples". GAAP results lag adjusted ones as acquisition costs flow through.

## 3. The 2030 ambition, and what it requires

From the 2025 Investor Day deck and the 2026 letter:
- **Growth:** ≥5% organic base fee growth through the cycle, and "low-to-mid-teens technology ACV growth".
- **Margin:** ≥45% adjusted operating margin through the cycle.
- **Revenue:** "Looking ahead to 2030, we aspire to deliver more than $35 billion in revenue, with 30% or more coming from private markets and technology." (2026 letter)
- **Fundraising:** $400B cumulative private-markets gross fundraising, 2025–2030.
- **Scale:** roughly double operating income (to ~$15B) and market cap (to ~$280B).

**Implied arithmetic (derived, not BlackRock's):**
- 2024 revenue was $20.41B (2025 deck). Reaching >$35B by 2030 needs ~9.8% CAGR.
- The ≥5% organic base fee growth target leaves the rest to market beta, performance fees and pricing.
- At 30% of $35B, private markets plus technology must reach ~$10.5B of revenue.

## 4. What the claims don't prove

1. **Adjusted vs GAAP.** FY2025 diluted EPS was $35.31 GAAP vs $48.09 adjusted. In Q3 2026 the operating margin was 30.0% GAAP vs 47.8% adjusted. The gap is acquisition-related noncash charges and a noncash charitable contribution. The targets are all on the adjusted basis.
2. **Records are partly beta.** AUM rose from $5.98T to $15.49T (2.6x) over 2018–Q3 2026. That combines flows with a strong equity market. Organic base fee growth (5–11%) is the cleaner measure.
3. **The fee mix is diluting.** Core equity ETFs earn ~8bp and cash 2–3bp (2025 deck). Institutional index assets have run net outflows every year (about −$119B in FY2025, *parsed*). Fee rate is "an output of client preferences and markets" (2025 deck), which avoids the question.
4. **The private-markets fundraising record is mixed.** $82B was raised against a $100B+ target for 2021–23.
5. **No segment margins.** Technology and private markets are not reported with their own operating income.

## 5. Strategic read (inference — not BlackRock's words)

- **The re-rating thesis.** BlackRock trades like an asset manager on a blended multiple. Private-markets peers trade at 25–35x fee earnings (per the deal decks) and software at higher revenue multiples. If 30%+ of revenue sits in those buckets, the blended multiple should rise. The deals are partly multiple arbitrage, and the 2030 market-cap target makes the bet explicit.
- **Flows have scale advantages.** Consolidation onto "fewer organizations" (Fink, CNBC 2025-01) favours the largest platform. Records in 2024–25 support the claim, although markets helped.
- **Exposure to beta is structural.** Most revenue is AUM-linked, so a bear market of the 2022 kind cuts revenue and margin together. Private-markets fees (on committed capital) and technology (ACV) are the hedge. That is a second, quieter rationale for the pivot.
- **Capital allocation moved from buybacks to M&A.** The 2018–2020 pattern was ~$3.6–3.8B a year returned to shareholders. From 2024 debt and stock fund acquisitions, though buybacks continue.

## 6. Canon lenses

- **Capital allocation** ([[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]], [[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]]): judge management by per-share value. The deals issued ~12M shares (GIP), units worth ~6% of the share count (HPS) and more (ElmTree). Ask whether the adjusted EPS growth survives on a fully diluted, GAAP basis.
- **Margin of safety** ([[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]]): how much of the 2030 target relies on market appreciation? Run a 0% beta case.
- **Success equation** ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]): separate skill (organic growth) from luck (market beta) in record years.
- **Good strategy** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): the diagnosis (fee compression and beta exposure), the guiding policy (public plus private plus technology) and the coherent actions (deals, Preqin, distribution) are all present. Score the coherence.

## 7. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For:** records of 11% organic base fee growth (2021), 9% (2025) and 10% LTM (Q3 2026) are exceptional for a firm of $10T+, and they come with margin expansion.
- **Against:** the adjusted metrics flatter. The private-markets build is expensive, and the history of asset-manager M&A is mixed. The Q3 2026 47.8% margin comes near a market high.
- **Both:** watch the three-number dashboard: organic base fee growth, adjusted margin vs GAAP margin, and private markets plus technology as a share of revenue.

## 8. Open questions to track

1. FY2026 results (Jan 2027): does organic base fee growth stay ≥5% if markets fall?
2. The GAAP–adjusted gap: when do acquisition amortizations taper?
3. Private-markets fundraising for 2025–26 against the ~$67B-a-year path to $400B.
4. Any segment disclosure for technology or private markets?
