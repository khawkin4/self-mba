# Note · ICE clearing & Treasury — the franchise is the clearinghouse, and regulation is how ICE gets in the door

> Practitioner / market-structure note · Maps to **Course 03** (Microeconomics & Strategy Foundations — regulation as market-maker, netting as a network effect), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — challenger vs incumbent utility, switching costs), **Course 06** (Operations & Supply Chain — risk waterfalls, margin models, migration logistics), **Course 11** (Global Strategy, Macro & Responsible Business — Brexit, post-crisis mandates, Basel), **E6** (Crisis Leadership — COVID, FTX), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[ice-hub]]; companions [[ice-energy-benchmarks]], [[ice-strategy-financials]], [[ice-people]], [[ice-outside-voices]], [[tradeweb-derivatives-regulation]]

- **Source:** **~40 items** from `_ingest/raw/ice/articles.jsonl`, **Feb 18 2019 – Oct 22 2026**. They comprise:
  - ~24 IR press releases: ICE Clear Credit records and new members, CDS index options, the ICE Clear Europe CDS exit, ESMA recognition, the IRM 2 phases, and the Treasury clearing steps of Jun 2024, Aug 2025 and Feb 2026.
  - Earnings releases for FY2020–FY2025, 1Q26 and 2Q26, for the CDS Clearing revenue line.
  - Two Insights pieces: Hamill's Risk.net reprint "U.S. Treasury Clearing Success Depends on Competition and Choice" (Sep 3 2025, in-text), and Edmonds' "Fixed income in 2025" (undated).
  - Seven "Inside the ICE House" podcasts: Lukken/FIA 2018-03-15, Edmonds 2022-06-27, Duffy 2023-03-09, Behnam 2023-08-14, Sprecher 2024-08-12, Edmonds/McPartland 2026-01-14 (the 2026-03-27 Middle East release supplies the latest CDS record).

  Everything here is ICE's own voice or a guest on ICE's own podcast. FICC/DTCC, the incumbent Treasury CCP, is **never named** in the corpus.
- **Core claim:** ICE's clearing franchise matters more than its "technology and data" label suggests, and every big step into a new clearing market came through regulation:
  - **CDS clearing (2009).** It was born from the crisis, on a promise Sprecher attributes to Tim Geithner: build the barn and the government will run the horses past it. It now claims **>80% share**, with records of **$1.1tn in a day (Sep 2024)** and **$2.678tn (Mar 20 2026)**.
  - **Brexit.** It forced the ESMA recognition and the **$400bn CDS open-interest move** out of ICE Clear Europe (Oct 2023, "~$5bn" of industry capital saved).
  - **Treasury clearing.** The SEC mandate pulled ICE into U.S. Treasuries. Edmonds says plainly that ICE **would not have entered without it**. ICE Clear Credit went live on **2026-02-03** as the "first ever alternative venue". Repo is due in Q4 2026, ahead of the mandate dates of Dec 31 2026 (cash) and Jun 30 2027 (repo).
  - **Defence.** The same regulatory skill works in reverse. In 2022 ICE and CME together fought FTX's auto-liquidating, non-intermediated clearing model. The CFTC chair later said approval was "not close".
  - **The moat is netting.** One pool, one margin model (IRM 2, rolled out Jan 2022 → Oct 2026), and offsets "as high as 98%".
  - **The tension.** In Treasuries ICE must sell "choice" against exactly that netting logic, because the incumbent holds the netting set.

## 2. Three pillars of the franchise

**(a) CDS: built on the crisis, defended by netting.** Sprecher's origin story is explicit. Geithner told Sprecher “if you build a big barn and you open those barn doors, I will make sure the horses run right in front of you.” (2024-08-12). The mandate delivered the flow, and ICE's job was to build the barn. Every later CDS product went into the same portfolio: single names, then index options (2020), with Ivanov (2022-04-26) touting “Margining of index, single name and option instruments in one capital efficient portfolio”. Hamill (2025-09-03) cites the result: “as evidenced by our greater than 80% market share.” Stress produces records rather than outflows. Ivanov (2020-06-08) put Q1 2020 above the GFC: “record CDS market activity in comparison to that experienced during the 2008/2009 financial crisis”.

**(b) Margin as product: IRM 2 and the offsets.** Edmonds (2021-11-22) framed IRM 2: “By modelling the portfolio as a whole, IRM 2.0 allows for offsets to be reflected in the final initial margin value”. The rollout went equity index (Jan 2022), then energy (Nov 2025), ERCOT (May 2026) and rates (Oct 2026). That is four and a half years to move one clearinghouse family off IRM 1, and CDS was explicitly excluded. Serafini (2026-10-22): “Margin should reflect the risk of positions a member holds”. The energy notes advertise offsets of "over 90%" (2023), then "as high as 95%" (2024), then **"as high as 98%" across 800+ oil contracts** (2025–26). See [[ice-energy-benchmarks]]. This is the lock-in. A position cleared away from ICE loses its offset against everything else held at ICE (inference).

**(c) Geography as regulation: Brexit and the CDS move.** ICE Clear Europe needed ESMA recognition twice, once for no-deal (2019) and once post-transition (2020). It then gave up CDS clearing altogether. The **$400bn** move to ICE Clear Credit was run through ICE Link's matching algorithm. Ian Springle: “Total capital savings to the industry resulting from the migration is estimated to be in the vicinity of $5 billion.” (2023-10-30). The capital saving is the netting argument in reverse: one pool instead of two (inference).

## 3. The FTX fight (2022–23): defending intermediation

In 2022 FTX US Derivatives asked the CFTC to amend its DCO licence so it could clear directly for end users, with algorithmic auto-liquidation instead of FCMs and margin calls. ICE's case against it, made by Edmonds on the podcast (2022-06-27):

- **Collateral timing.** Futures are “our system, the traditional future system, is a pay as you go.” Crypto is "go as you pay": “And if your collateral's insufficient, your party's over.” Edmonds uses a farmer who is auto-liquidated over a weekend and cannot rebuild the hedge.
- **Resources.** Cover-2 means the simultaneous failure of the two largest members, in practice two GSIBs. FTX proposed a **$250M** guarantee fund: “how do we know that $250 million is sufficient to cover the simultaneous failure of the largest two positions they hold?”
- **Contagion and equivalence.** Two standards will be arbitraged against each other in a crisis. A different CFTC protocol could also jeopardise the foreign "comparable compliance" that lets non-U.S. investors use ICE.
- **Sprecher's version** (Piper Sandler clip, played on the episode): global stress tests assume intermediation, so “would we just create a global crash with an algorithm?”

**Allies:**
- **CME's Duffy** (2023-03-09) on Edmonds: “He did a masterful job at that hearing”. Duffy also argued “All the rules written under the Commodity Exchange Act had a futures commission merchant in mind when they were written.”
- **CFTC chair Behnam** (2023-08-14): “we were not close despite what you might've heard from other people”. On the broker: “it's a positive point of friction”. Behnam also warned that the proposal “wasn't the first of its kind”, so the fight is likely to recur.

**What the fight reveals:** ICE's clearing economics rest on the FCM layer, and FCMs are under capital pressure. Lukken (2018-03-15) complained that Basel rules “they actually count clearing and client margin for clearing as leverage.” Lukken added: “there are fewer and fewer FCMs in the world”. ICE defends intermediation while its distribution channel thins. RBC joining as an FCM (2025) is the kind of win that matters, which may be why it got a release (inference).

## 4. Treasury clearing: a challenger CCP, pulled in by the mandate

**Timeline.** SEC rule (2023, per Hamill) → ICE announcement **2024-06-24** → rulebook published **2025-08-19** → target "by the end of 2025" (Hamill) → actual go-live **2026-02-03** → repo planned for **Q4 2026** → mandate **Dec 31 2026 / Jun 30 2027**. The launch slipped about five weeks past the 2025 target. The voluntary run-in promised as "up to 12 months" shrank to about 11 months before the cash mandate.

**Why ICE entered:**
- **Edmonds** (2026-01-14): “I don't believe without the regulatory catalyst that we would have made the decision to enter that.” It is the clearest admission in the corpus that the mandate, not organic demand, creates the market. Edmonds's economic thesis: “So you may make less per transaction, but you do incredibly more transactions along the way”.
- **Sprecher** (2024-08-12) was candid about the difficulty: “It's a complicated project. It's a work in progress for us.”

**The pitch: "choice" against a single incumbent.**
- **Hamill** (2025-09-03): “Existing Treasury clearing models are regarded as somewhat basic compared to the advanced portfolio risk management” of futures and OTC CCPs.
- **ICE's design**, as Hamill describes it:
  - Done-with, done-away and self-clear access.
  - Venue-neutral connectivity across CLOB, RFQ and voice.
  - The CDS risk waterfall, segregated client collateral, Cover-2, and Monte Carlo IM with a two-day margin period of risk.
  - Clients can settle through their member or directly with the CCP on a net basis.
  - A promise that done-with trades keep today's balance-sheet treatment.
- **The goal**, in Hamill's words: “Our goal is to make 2026 a year of competition and choice for market participants.”
- **Edmonds' framing:** clients will access the market on their terms “versus the terms that are being dictated to them because they're part of a particular client base somewhere.” That is the only shot at the unnamed incumbent.
- **The honest caveat sits in Hamill's own text:** “While concentrating trades at a single clearing house can optimize capital utilization, netting and portfolio margining efficiencies”. Netting is the incumbent's moat in Treasuries, exactly as it is ICE's in CDS.

**The venue view.** [[tradeweb-derivatives-regulation]] (Jun 2026 item) reports that done-with Sponsored Repo has become the default route to compliance, that done-away is limited, and that CME Securities Clearing also competes with FICC. That Tradeweb item still describes ICE as a firm that "may enter", months after ICE's go-live, which shows how little the venue side was crediting ICE's service (inference). Both firms treat the mandate as the catalyst. Tradeweb rides it as an execution venue; ICE rides it as a CCP.

## 5. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Leading" is self-measured.** The ">80% market share" has no denominator, period or source. The single-day records ($1.1tn, then $2.678tn) are ICE's own, and the "any CDS clearinghouse" comparison is unverified in the corpus.
2. **The cumulative boilerplate doesn't reconcile.**
   - Jun 2020: ">$100tn" and ">$135tn gross" appear in the same release.
   - Nov 2020: "$283tn" (ICE Clear Credit plus ICE Clear Europe). May 2023 still says "$283tn".
   - Oct 2023 and Jun 2024: "~$200tn".
   - Sep 2024: ">$350tn". Aug 2025: ">$385tn". Feb 2026: ">$400tn".
   - The metric drifts between two-sided, gross and "combined notional of parties", and between one clearinghouse and two.
3. **Volume records ≠ revenue.** USD index CDS notional rose **+27%** in 2025 ($22tn → $28tn) and options more than doubled, yet CDS Clearing revenue *fell* from **$343M to $338M**. The 2022–23 jump (192 → 360) coincides with the ICE Clear Europe migration and rate volatility, and ICE never decomposes it.
4. **"Choice" has no adoption data.** There is no release on ICE Treasury clearing volumes, members or open interest between Feb 3 and Oct 22 2026. Repo is still "planned".
5. **Dates moved without comment.** The 2025 outlook gives Dec 31 [2025] / June 2026. Hamill gives Dec 31 2026 / Jun 30 2027 (context, not from ICE corpus: the SEC extended both by a year in Feb 2025). The launch "by the end of 2025" became Feb 2026.
6. **The FTX record is survivorship.** ICE's argument "won" because FTX collapsed in Nov 2022 (context, not from ICE corpus), not because the CFTC ruled on the merits. Behnam's "not close" came afterwards, on ICE's stage.
7. **Margin offsets are marketing maxima.** "As high as 98%" is a best case for a specific spread, not an average client outcome.

## 6. Strategic read (inference — not stated by ICE)

- **Clearing is where the margin, data and lock-in meet.** Exchanges trade, but the CCP holds the collateral. That is $114.6bn at mid-2026, earning "net interest income and fees on certain clearing margin deposits" in OTC & Other. The CCP also sets the offsets that make leaving expensive. The "technology and data" descriptor (see [[ice-mission-forces]]) undersells this.
- **ICE is a regulatory-arbitrage specialist in the good sense.** CDS (Dodd-Frank), Brexit (EMIR recognition), Treasuries (SEC) and the TTF parallel venue (EU price cap, see [[ice-energy-benchmarks]]) are all cases of mandates or rules redrawing a market where ICE is ready with infrastructure. Edmonds says so: “Historically, ICE has done pretty well each and every time we see these regulatory catalysts change”.
- **Treasuries invert ICE's position.** In CDS, ICE is the netting incumbent. In Treasuries, it is the challenger asking clients to split a netting set. That only pays where done-away access, cross-margining with ICE's CDS or rates, or settlement flexibility outweighs lost netting at the incumbent. The repo launch and any cross-margin approval are the real tests. Cash Treasuries alone are a thin wedge.
- **The FTX fight and Treasury entry are one strategy.** Both defend the intermediated, member-mutualised CCP model, first against disintermediation and then as the template for the next mandated market.
- **People signal priority.** A clearing operator (Edmonds) was put in charge of data, and a regulatory chief (King) took over the six clearinghouses. The "data company" is run by clearing people; see [[ice-people]].

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Switching costs and scale** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): netting economies grow with the pool, and IRM 2 offsets make each additional product stickier. Counter-positioning works against ICE in Treasuries.
- **Coupling and tight systems** ([[canon-perrow-normal-accidents]]): Sprecher's "global crash" worry is a tight-coupling argument. Algorithmic liquidation across interlinked markets removes the human slack (margin calls, discretion) that Edmonds defends.
- **Fragility and stressors** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): CDS clearing posts records in March 2020, September 2024 and March 2026. The franchise feeds on stress, provided the CCP itself doesn't fail.
- **Credible commitments** ([[canon-schelling-strategy-of-conflict]]): the Geithner "barn" is a regulator's commitment to supply demand without ordering it. The SEC mandate is the hard version.
- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): ICE and CME, rivals in energy, act as complements against FTX. Duffy praises Edmonds on ICE's podcast.
- **Leverage points** ([[canon-meadows-leverage-points]]): rules about who may clear (FCM-only) and how margin counts (Basel leverage) are higher-leverage than any product feature.
- **Platform tipping** ([[canon-hagiu-wright-multi-sided-platforms]]): CCP markets tip to one netting set. "Choice" is the entrant's attempt to stop tipping.

## 8. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For ICE:** the execution record is real. ICE ran a $400bn cross-border CCP migration without incident, cleared index options with a common exercise platform, and handled COVID and the 2026 Middle East shock with records. Big banks (Citi, J.P. Morgan, RBC, Mizuho, Intesa) keep joining. Cover-2 and Monte Carlo IM are industry standard, not marketing.
- **Against:** a second Treasury CCP fragments netting, so dealers may post more total margin, not less. Done-away is costly for FCMs, whose numbers Lukken says are shrinking. CDS clearing revenue is flat despite record notional, which hints at fee pressure or mix. ICE has published no Treasury adoption data at all.
- **Both:** a buy-side desk should model total margin and balance-sheet cost under three set-ups: (i) all at the incumbent, (ii) split, and (iii) ICE with CDS/rates cross-margin if approved. It should choose on that model, not on "choice".

## 9. Open questions to track

1. **Repo go-live (planned Q4 2026).** Does it launch on time, with sponsored *and* done-away access?
2. **Adoption.** Will ICE disclose Treasury clearing members, ADV or OI before the Dec 31 2026 cash mandate?
3. **Cross-margining.** Will regulators allow Treasury positions to offset CDS or ICE rates futures? Without it, why split netting sets?
4. **CDS revenue vs notional.** Why did 2025 revenue fall while notional set records? Watch 3Q26 (due Oct 29 2026) after the 1Q26 spike to $112M.
5. **IRM 2 completion.** When do ags, equity derivatives and the remaining energy move? Will CDS ever move?
6. **Mandate drift.** Do the Dec 2026 / Jun 2027 dates hold under the current administration? Edmonds expects "timeframes to be adjusted".
7. **Direct clearing returns.** Behnam said FTX's proposal won't be the last. Does a crypto-native or prediction-market DCO revive non-intermediated clearing, given ICE's own Polymarket stake?

### Rep
Three short computations with numbers from this note.

**(a) Cover-2, by hand.** FTX proposed a **$250M** guarantee fund. Suppose a stress test gives the two largest members uncovered losses (stress loss minus their initial margin) of **$190M** and **$140M**, and no single member exceeds $190M. Is the fund Cover-2 compliant? What if it only had to meet Cover-1? *(Answer: Cover-2 needs $190M + $140M = **$330M**, a **$80M shortfall**, so the fund covers only ~76%. Cover-1 needs $190M, so $250M passes with $60M to spare. FTX's implicit argument was that auto-liquidation caps losses before they reach the fund. Explain why Edmonds' "too much unknown" is really an objection to an untested assumption about liquidation prices in a crash.)*

**(b) Netting vs "choice".** A dealer holds +$10bn of 10Y Treasuries against −$9.5bn of a near-identical issue. Assume IM is 2% of the *net* exposure when both legs sit in one CCP and the portfolio model nets them at 95%, and 2% of each *gross* leg when split across two CCPs. Compute IM both ways. Then apply ICE's "as high as 98%" oil offset to a Brent/HOU pair with $5M standalone IM per leg. *(Answer: one CCP: residual = $10bn − 0.95×$9.5bn = $0.975bn, × 2% ≈ **$19.5M**. Split: ($10bn + $9.5bn) × 2% = **$390M**, ~20× more. Oil: 2 × $5M × (1 − 0.98) = **$0.2M** vs $10M unnetted; at 2023's "over 90%", ~$1M. This is why Hamill concedes concentration "can optimize capital utilization", and why ICE needs done-away access or cross-margining to win in Treasuries.)*

**(c) Fee capture.** CDS Clearing revenue was $343M (2024) and $338M (2025), while USD index CDS cleared rose from $22tn to $28tn. Using USD index notional as a crude proxy, compute revenue per $1tn each year and the change. *(Answer: 343/22 ≈ **$15.6M** per $tn; 338/28 ≈ **$12.1M** per $tn, about **−23%**. List three reasons the proxy misleads: EUR volumes, single names and options mix; fee caps and tiering; and compression reducing billable notional. Then say what Edmonds' "less per transaction, … incredibly more transactions" predicts for Treasury clearing fees.)*
