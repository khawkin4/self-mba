# Note · Meadows — *Thinking in Systems*

> Canon · Cluster: Mental Models & Thinking · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **Course 06** (Operations)

- **Source:** Donella Meadows, *Thinking in Systems: A Primer* (2008)
- **Core claim:** A system is interconnected elements organized to achieve something. Most people
  intervene at the **wrong leverage point** — pushing on flows when the *structure* of the feedback
  loop is the real driver. Most org problems are **structural, not personal.**
- **Framework:**
  - **Stocks** (accumulations), **flows** (rates of change), **feedback loops** (reinforcing or
    balancing), **delays** (why systems oscillate and overshoot).
  - **Leverage points (least → most powerful):** parameters/numbers (weak) → buffer & stock sizes →
    feedback loop strength → **rules & incentive structures** → information flows → **goals** →
    **paradigm/purpose of the system** (strongest).
  - Key heuristic: **replace the person but keep the system → same behavior.**
- **Applies when:** a problem keeps recurring despite fixes — a sign you're tuning parameters when
  the loop structure or the system's *goal* is what needs changing.
- **Breaks when:** the high-leverage interventions (changing goals/paradigm) are exactly the
  hardest to execute and most resisted — the framework tells you *where* to push, not *how* to win
  the political fight to do it. Systems maps can also become elaborate procrastination ("paralysis
  by diagram"). And boundaries are chosen, not given — draw them wrong and the model misleads.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/operations, r/managers) — ops leaders
  echo "you don't have a people problem, you have a process problem" — but warn that *some* problems
  really are the person, and systems-thinking can become an excuse to never hold anyone accountable.

fixing a *number* when the *loop* (or the system's goal) is the cause?
