# Note · Thaler & Sunstein — *Nudge*

> Canon · Cluster: Behavioral Design & Decision Science · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **Course 05** (Marketing & Brand)

- **Source:** Richard Thaler & Cass Sunstein, *Nudge* (2008)
- **Core claim:** People make **predictably irrational** decisions, so **choice architecture** — how
  options are presented — profoundly shapes what they pick. Design the environment so the better
  choice is *easier* without removing any choices ("libertarian paternalism").
- **Framework:**
  - **Defaults are the most powerful nudge** — most people stick with whatever is pre-selected
    (opt-out organ donation vastly beats opt-in, same people, different default).
  - Other levers: salience, framing, feedback, error-expectation, structuring complex choices.
  - The operating rule: **want a behavior → make it the default; want to prevent one → add friction.**
- **Applies when:** designing products, policies, onboarding, or your own environment — anywhere a
  default or a bit of friction can move behavior at scale and near-zero cost.
- **Breaks when:** "who chooses the nudge?" — choice architecture is **never neutral**, and the
  *chooser's* judgment can be biased or self-interested (dark patterns are nudges turned predatory).
  Nudges produce **small effects** and often fade; they're no substitute for fixing the underlying
  incentive or product (a nudge on a bad default just papers over it). And libertarian paternalism
  draws fire from both sides — manipulative to some, too weak to others.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/ProductManagement, r/marketing) — PMs live
  this: the default checkbox / pre-filled option drives most outcomes. Same mechanism as Atomic
  Habits' "make it easy" and Switch's "shape the Path" ([[canon-clear-atomic-habits]],
  [[canon-heath-switch]]).
