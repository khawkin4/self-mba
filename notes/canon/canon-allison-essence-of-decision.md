# Note · Allison — *Essence of Decision*

> Canon · Cluster: History & Judgment · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro)

- **Source:** Graham Allison, *Essence of Decision: Explaining the Cuban Missile Crisis* (1971; rev. 1999)
- **Core claim:** The *same* organizational decision looks completely different through **three
  lenses** — and if you only use the first, you'll be baffled by why orgs do "irrational" things.
- **Framework — the three models (applied to the Cuban Missile Crisis):**
  1. **Model I — Rational Actor** — the org as a single rational decider optimizing its interest.
     (Clean, but explains the *least*.)
  2. **Model II — Organizational Process** — decisions as **outputs of standard routines and SOPs**;
     orgs do what their playbooks allow, not what's optimal.
  3. **Model III — Governmental (Bureaucratic) Politics** — decisions as the **result of bargaining,
     coalitions, and turf** among individuals with different interests ("where you stand depends on
     where you sit").
  - The "irrational" decision usually wasn't — it was **organizational inertia (II)** or **political
    compromise (III).** Every major decision has all three.
- **Applies when:** explaining or predicting any large-organization decision — government, enterprise,
  your own company's baffling choices.
- **Breaks when:** with **three lenses you can explain *anything* after the fact** — the framework is
  more powerful as *understanding* than *prediction* (it rarely tells you which model will dominate
  *next* time). It can also become a sophisticated excuse ("it's just politics") that **lets bad
  decisions off the hook.** Best fused with March's garbage-can model (the randomness layer,
  [[canon-march-primer-decision-making]]) and Pfeffer's power (the how-to layer,
  [[canon-pfeffer-power]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers, org-behavior threads) — the
  durable move: when a decision baffles you, **stop assuming Model I** — ask whether it's a *routine*
  (II) or a *turf bargain* (III). Usually III.
