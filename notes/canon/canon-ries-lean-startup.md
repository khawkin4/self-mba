# Note · Ries — *The Lean Startup*

> Canon · Cluster: Product Thinking & Innovation · Maps to **Course 10** (Entrepreneurship & Venture)

- **Source:** Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup* (2011)
- **Core claim:** A startup (or a new initiative inside a big company) is a **search vehicle under
  extreme uncertainty**, not a small version of a known business — so it needs different management:
  experimentation, not execution-of-a-plan.
- **Framework:**
  - **Build–Measure–Learn** — the core feedback loop; minimize *total time through the loop*.
  - **MVP** — the *smallest thing that tests your riskiest assumption*. Not a crappy product; a
    deliberate experiment.
  - **Validated learning** is the unit of progress (not features shipped, not vanity metrics).
  - **Pivot or persevere** — decide on *evidence*, not hope, at regular intervals.
  - **Innovation accounting** — actionable metrics over vanity metrics.
- **Applies when:** the problem/solution is genuinely *unknown* and cheap to test — early products,
  new markets, internal 0→1 bets.
- **Breaks when:** the method is **iterative/local** — it optimizes toward the nearest validated
  improvement and can miss the **bold, non-incremental** bet that needs conviction *before*
  evidence exists (Thiel's whole critique — some things you must build at scale on a thesis,
  [[canon-thiel-zero-to-one]]). "MVP" is widely abused as an excuse to ship junk. And in deep-tech,
  hardware, or regulated domains the cheap-fast-loop assumption fails. It's an antifragile small-bet
  engine ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]) — powerful, but not every problem is a search problem.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/startups, r/Entrepreneur) — founders say
  the discipline is **"riskiest assumption first"**; the trap is MVP-theater — shipping a thin
  feature and calling random usage "validation."
