# Note · Rumelt — *The Crux*

> Canon · Cluster: Design Thinking & Problem-Solving · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy), **Course 12** (Capstone)

- **Source:** Richard Rumelt, *The Crux* (2022) — the sequel to *Good Strategy Bad Strategy*
- **Core claim:** Strategy begins by finding **the crux** — *the* hard part of the problem that, if
  solved, **unlocks everything else** — then concentrating resources against it. Most people avoid
  naming the crux because it's uncomfortable.
- **Framework:**
  - **The crux** = the most important challenge that is *also addressable* — the intersection of
    **importance** and **solvability** (not just the biggest problem, the most *pivotal tractable*
    one).
  - **Diagnose → find the crux → take coherent action against it** (sharpening the kernel of
    [[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]).
  - The crux is **the thing you don't know how to do, the constraint you don't want to face, the
    tradeoff you've been avoiding** — and naming it honestly *is* the strategic act. Everything else
    is secondary.
- **Applies when:** a strategy has sprawled into a long list of priorities — the crux discipline
  forces you to find the *one* pivotal challenge and mass force there.
- **Breaks when:** **"there is one crux" can oversimplify** genuinely multi-front problems (some
  situations have two coupled cruxes; force one and you lose the other). **Identifying** the crux is
  also the hard part the book somewhat assumes — in a fog, the "obvious" crux is often *wrong*, and
  betting everything on a mis-identified crux is catastrophic. It's a close cousin of Goldratt's
  single constraint ([[canon-goldratt-the-goal]]) and Meadows' highest leverage point
  ([[canon-meadows-leverage-points]]) — and shares their risk of misplacing the lever.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/strategy, r/consulting) — the usable
  discipline: when a strategy is a laundry list, ask **"what's the *one* thing that, if we cracked
  it, makes the rest easier?"** — and notice the urge to avoid the uncomfortable answer.
