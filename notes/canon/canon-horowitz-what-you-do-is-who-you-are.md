# Note · Horowitz — *What You Do Is Who You Are*

> Canon · Cluster: Culture & Change · Maps to **E12** (Culture as Strategy), **Course 09** (Leadership & OB)

- **Source:** Ben Horowitz, *What You Do Is Who You Are* (2019)
- **Core claim:** **Culture isn't what you say — it's what you do, especially in the hard cases.**
  It degrades naturally and needs constant active maintenance.
- **Framework — culture as designed behavior:**
  - Studies leaders who built culture under extreme conditions — Toussaint Louverture, the samurai
    (*bushido*), Genghis Khan, Shaka Senghor — to extract transferable mechanisms.
  - **Shocking rules** — a rule strange enough that people ask "*why?*", and in answering internalize
    the principle behind it (the surprise is the teaching mechanism).
  - **Who you promote and who you fire are the loudest cultural signals** — louder than any values
    doc. Culture is *what behavior gets rewarded*, observed.
  - Decide the few **virtues** that actually matter and engineer them into visible practice; lead by
    living them, because people watch what leaders *do* under pressure.
- **Applies when:** you're deliberately *building or resetting* a culture and want concrete
  mechanisms rather than aspirational values.
- **Breaks when:** "shocking rules" can **backfire or look gimmicky** if the principle behind them
  isn't genuinely lived — a stunt without follow-through teaches cynicism. The historical case
  studies are vivid but **selectively narrated** (survivorship + storytelling bias,
  [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]] has the same risk). And it under-addresses Schein's hard
  truth that *unconscious assumptions* resist even well-designed rules
  ([[canon-schein-organizational-culture]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/startups, r/managers) — founders find the
  "who you promote/fire" lens the most actionable: your real culture is visible in your last three
  promotions. Operationalizes Walsh's standards ([[canon-walsh-score-takes-care-of-itself]]).
