# Note · Parker, Van Alstyne & Choudary — *Platform Revolution*

> Canon · Cluster: Network Effects & Platform Strategy · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy), **Course 10** (Entrepreneurship)

- **Source:** Geoffrey Parker, Marshall Van Alstyne & Sangeet Paul Choudary, *Platform Revolution* (2016)
- **Core claim:** Platforms create value by **facilitating exchange** between producers and consumers
  rather than owning production. The shift from **pipelines** (linear value chains) to **platforms**
  (networked ecosystems) is the defining economic transformation.
- **Framework:**
  - **The core interaction** — the single most important exchange the platform enables; get this
    *one* thing right before anything else.
  - **Network effects** — more users → more value → more users (the engine; Helmer's "network
    economies," [[canon-helmer-7-powers]]).
  - **Governance** — the rules of who participates and how (quality, trust, take-rate).
  - **The chicken-and-egg / cold-start problem** — you need both sides, but neither joins without the
    other; **solving cold-start is the hardest strategic problem in platform building** (seeding one
    side, single-player utility, marquee users).
- **Applies when:** designing or evaluating any two-sided marketplace or ecosystem business.
- **Breaks when:** it can make "**just build a platform**" sound like a strategy when most products
  should stay **pipelines** — platform dynamics are brutal and winner-take-most, and the graveyard of
  failed marketplaces is enormous (cold-start kills most). Network effects also **aren't automatic or
  permanent** — multi-homing and disintermediation erode them
  ([[canon-cusumano-business-of-platforms]]). And the book under-weights how *regulation* and
  *trust/safety* costs scale non-linearly.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/startups, *Acquired*) — founders confirm
  cold-start is everything; the cliché "come for the tool, stay for the network" (single-player value
  first) is the most-cited solution.
