# Note · Larson — *An Elegant Puzzle*

> Canon · Cluster: Org Design & Mechanisms · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E1** (Leading at Scale)

- **Source:** Will Larson, *An Elegant Puzzle: Systems of Engineering Management* (2019)
- **Core claim:** Engineering organizations are **systems with throughput constraints** — manage
  them by state, not by motivation. Organizational debt compounds like financial debt.
- **Framework:**
  - **Four team states:** *falling behind* → *treading water* → *repaying debt* → *innovating*. You
    manage each differently.
  - **Key counterintuitive rule:** you **can't fix a falling-behind team by adding goals** — you fix
    it by **adding people or cutting scope**. (Adding goals to an overloaded team makes it worse.)
  - Practical systems: team sizing, managing migrations, organizational debt, when to centralize vs.
    federate.
- **Applies when:** a team is underwater and leadership's instinct is to "set clearer priorities" —
  Larson says that's the wrong lever; change the load, not the exhortation.
- **Breaks when:** it's written from a **high-growth, well-funded tech** context — "just add
  headcount" assumes you *can* hire, which fails in constrained or non-tech orgs where cutting scope
  is the only lever. The four-state model is clean but real teams sit in several states at once. And
  the systems lens, like Meadows', can under-weight when the bottleneck is genuinely one person.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/ExperiencedDevs, r/engineeringmanagement)
  — EMs cite the "can't fix falling-behind with goals" insight as career-changing; the debt framing
  gives language to push back on perpetual feature pressure.
