# Note · Doerr — *Measure What Matters*

> Canon · Cluster: Org Design & Mechanisms · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **Course 09** (Leadership & OB)

- **Source:** John Doerr, *Measure What Matters* (2018) — the OKR system, inherited from Grove at Intel
- **Core claim:** **OKRs** create alignment, focus, and accountability — but only if treated as a
  *direction-setting* tool, not a task list.
- **Framework:**
  - **Objective** — *what* you want to achieve: qualitative, inspiring, slightly uncomfortable.
  - **Key Results** — *how you'll know* you got there: quantitative, specific, time-bound (3–5 per O).
  - Properties: **alignment** (everyone sees how their work connects up), **focus** (few objectives
    per cycle), **accountability** (results tracked publicly).
  - Calibration: **~70% achievement is the sweet spot** — hitting 100% means you sandbagged. (Stretch
    OKRs vs. committed OKRs are scored differently.)
- **Applies when:** an org is busy but unaligned — OKRs force the conversation about the *few* things
  that matter and make progress visible.
- **Breaks when:** the **#1 failure mode is treating OKRs as a to-do list** — bottom-up activity
  dressed as outcomes. They also corrupt when tied to compensation (people sandbag to hit bonuses,
  killing the stretch). KR-gaming: people optimize the metric, not the goal (Goodhart's law). And
  cascading OKRs rigidly top-down recreates the leader-follower trap that
  [[canon-marquet-turn-the-ship-around]] warns against — Doerr advocates a *mix* of top-down and
  bottom-up.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/ExperiencedDevs corpus + Lenny's Podcast / Will Larson transcript)*
  Goodhart's law isn't a footnote in the wild — it's the dominant complaint. In r/ExperiencedDevs
  "**I'm giving up; Becoming a yes man**" (1,668↑) a senior engineer lists what actually gets
  rewarded: *"Ability to attach metrics to your work and then make those metrics look good… It
  doesn't matter if you have high quality & actionable metrics, you just need numbers that look
  good. '200% increase in usage' sounds better than 'we have 2 new users.'"* That is the
  KR-gaming failure mode the book warns about, observed live. **But the deeper corpus critique is
  Larson's:** teams confuse *measuring* progress with *making* it — *"we weren't actually
  prioritizing improvements, we were just prioritizing measurement"* (Lenny's Podcast). The Grove
  original ([[canon-grove-high-output-management]]) was leaner precisely because it kept the metric
  subordinate to the outcome.
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** The point of an objective-and-metric system is *focus
    through constraint*, not coverage. Larson frames it the way Doerr frames a good Objective:
    *"the goal of good strategy is not to appease everyone… the goal of good strategy is to dictate
    how we invest the limited capacities we have into the problems we care about"* (Lenny's
    Podcast — Will Larson). OKRs are that constraint made legible: a few objectives, public, that
    *"focus people's energy on the problems that we value as a company."*
  - **Real application (mined):** The corpus surfaces the right *unit* of measurement — outcomes,
    not activity. A r/ExperiencedDevs thread, "**A 5 min weekly habit completely changed my
    performance review**" (1,611↑), describes a weekly "brag document" logging top accomplishments;
    it works because it tracks *results* shipped, not hours worked — the same O-vs-task-list
    distinction Doerr insists on, applied to one engineer's quarter.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** Practitioners report that once a number is the
    target, people optimize the number, not the goal — *"you just need numbers that look good"*
    (r/ExperiencedDevs, 1,668↑) — and that measurement itself can crowd out the work: *"we weren't
    actually prioritizing improvements, we were just prioritizing measurement… you do have to cut
    at some point to actually make impact"* (Larson). The KR is a proxy; the corpus reality is that
    proxies get gamed and rituals calcify unless leadership keeps re-anchoring on the real Objective.
  - **Sources in corpus:** Lenny's Podcast — "The engineering mindset | Will Larson (Carta, Stripe,
    Uber, Calm, Digg)" (youtube.com/watch?v=Z9ftpRhRiJE) · r/ExperiencedDevs "I'm giving up;
    Becoming a yes man" (1,668↑), "A 5 min weekly habit changed my performance review" (1,611↑).
