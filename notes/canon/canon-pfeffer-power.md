# Note · Pfeffer — *Power*

> Canon · Cluster: Power & Organizational Politics · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E1** (Leading at Scale)

- **Source:** Jeffrey Pfeffer, *Power: Why Some People Have It — and Others Don't* (2010)
- **Core claim:** Organizational power **doesn't come from being right.** It comes from controlling
  resources, building alliances, managing information, and understanding who actually decides. People
  who ignore power dynamics don't transcend them — they just **lose to people who don't.**
- **Framework:**
  - **Sources of power:** control of resources, position in the information/communication network,
    alliances, reputation, and personal qualities (will, energy, focus, ambition, political skill).
  - **Build power deliberately:** get into a department/role *near the power*, build a personal brand,
    network *before* you need it, ask for things, and don't wait to be recognized on merit.
  - Performance ≠ advancement; **visibility + relationships with decision-makers** often matter more.
- **Applies when:** you need to *get things done* in any real organization — Pfeffer's uncomfortable
  honesty is that political skill is a prerequisite, not a dirty word.
- **Breaks when:** the playbook can curdle into **pure careerism** that erodes trust and, at scale,
  the org itself — power *acquired* is not the same as power *deployed well* (the values question
  Pfeffer brackets out). It also reads as cynical-by-design; in genuinely high-trust, mission-driven
  teams, naked power-seeking backfires and Carnegie's "make others feel valued"
  ([[canon-carnegie-win-friends]]) outperforms. And Munger's lens applies: understand the
  *incentives* before you assume someone's power play is what it looks like
  ([[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/cscareerquestions, r/managers) — the hard
  truth people relearn: "doing great work and waiting to be noticed" loses to those who manage
  visibility and relationships. The cynical extreme is Greene ([[canon-greene-48-laws-of-power]]).
