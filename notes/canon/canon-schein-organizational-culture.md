# Note · Schein — *Organizational Culture and Leadership*

> Canon · Cluster: Culture & Change · Maps to **E12** (Culture as Strategy), **Course 09** (Leadership & OB)

- **Source:** Edgar Schein, *Organizational Culture and Leadership* (1985)
- **Core claim:** Culture operates at **three levels**, and the dysfunction lives in the *gap*
  between what people say they believe and what they actually, unconsciously assume.
- **Framework — the three levels:**
  1. **Artifacts** — the visible: office, dress, rituals, language. (Easy to see, hard to interpret.)
  2. **Espoused values** — what people *say* they believe (the values on the wall).
  3. **Basic underlying assumptions** — what people *actually* believe, often unconsciously. This is
     the real culture.
  - **Leaders create/embed culture** through what they *pay attention to*, how they *react in
    crises*, how they *allocate resources*, and *who they hire, promote, and fire.*
  - Culture change is hard because you're shifting **unconscious assumptions**, not posters.
- **Applies when:** diagnosing why "our values" don't match behavior — the gap between level 2 and
  level 3 is the actual operating culture.
- **Breaks when:** the model is a brilliant **diagnostic but a weak lever** — it tells you culture
  lives in assumptions, not how to *change* them at speed (Kotter and Heath are the change engines,
  [[canon-kotter-leading-change]], [[canon-heath-switch]]). "Read the assumptions" is also
  interpretively slippery — two observers infer different assumptions from the same artifacts. And
  in practice leaders over-index on level-1 artifacts (perks, slogans) precisely because level 3 is
  invisible and slow.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/managers corpus + Schein-model transcripts)*
  The corpus shows the level-2 / level-3 gap as a live operational wound, not a diagram. In **"Our
  untracked (unlimited) PTO policy…"** (9.8k↑) the *espoused value* ("unlimited PTO," manager's
  discretion) collides with the *basic assumption* the instant HR "flipped a shit" that a report took
  20 days and demanded a write-up "for abusing the PTO policy" — the real culture was *don't actually
  use it.* **"Got them a raise. They used it to quit."** (2.9k↑) exposes another assumption: the org
  treats a pay correction as a retention favor owed loyalty, not a market-rate baseline. The operator
  translation stays "culture is the worst behavior the leader *tolerates*" — Walsh's standards
  ([[canon-walsh-score-takes-care-of-itself]]) and Horowitz's "what you do"
  ([[canon-horowitz-what-you-do-is-who-you-are]]) are the actionable versions.

- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** the iceberg is the whole point — *"shine's three levels…
    are rather like an iceberg in that the most important bits are the parts you can't see"* and
    *"if you really want to shift a culture you have to shift the assumptions that underpin
    everything… if you can't shift assumptions you cannot change culture"* (Management Courses —
    Mike Clayton). Schein's own definition, quoted in the corpus, is *"a pattern of shared basic
    assumptions… that have worked well enough to be considered valid and therefore is to be taught
    to new members of the group as the correct way to perceive, think and feel"* (EPM) — culture is
    *learned solutions*, which is why it resists posters.
  - **Real application (mined):** EPM walks the model end-to-end on a sales org whose reps "work
    against each other": *artifact* = salespeople undercut each other for a bonus; *value* =
    "competition is encouraged… their entire reward structure is set up to support this"; *assumption*
    = the founder believed "individuals perform best when they stand to win from their actions." The
    fix isn't a slogan — it's rewiring the artifact (commission to first rep on an inquiry) and the
    reward structure underneath the assumption. The r/managers PTO thread is the same diagnosis in the
    wild: the policy (artifact) and "take time off" (espoused value) sat on an unspoken assumption that
    real users are abusers.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** EPM names the model's own limits — *"there's no one
    definition of organizational culture, so the model that works best for you will depend on what
    culture means to you,"* and *"individuals drive culture, so what you think is a cultural problem may
    just be an issue with one or a few individuals."* Practitioner threads sharpen this: the manager
    in **"you stop getting 'real' feedback"** (3.3k↑) shows assumptions are nearly impossible to
    *read* from inside the iceberg — "you can go months thinking you're doing fine while blind spots
    keep growing" — which is exactly why Schein diagnoses well but doesn't *change* at speed.
