# Note · Schein — *Organizational Culture and Leadership*

> Canon · Cluster: Culture & Change · Maps to **E12** (Culture as Strategy), **Course 09** (Leadership & OB)

- **Source:** Edgar Schein, *Organizational Culture and Leadership* (1985)
- **Core claim:** Culture operates at **three levels**, and the dysfunction lives in the *gap*
  between what people say they believe and what they actually, unconsciously assume.
- **Framework — the three levels:**
  1. **Artifacts** — the visible: office, dress, rituals, language. (Easy to see, hard to interpret.)
  2. **Espoused values** — what people *say* they believe (the values on the wall).
  3. **Basic underlying assumptions** — what people *actually* believe, often unconsciously. This is
     the real culture.
  - **Leaders create/embed culture** through what they *pay attention to*, how they *react in
    crises*, how they *allocate resources*, and *who they hire, promote, and fire.*
  - Culture change is hard because you're shifting **unconscious assumptions**, not posters.
- **Applies when:** diagnosing why "our values" don't match behavior — the gap between level 2 and
  level 3 is the actual operating culture.
- **Breaks when:** the model is a brilliant **diagnostic but a weak lever** — it tells you culture
  lives in assumptions, not how to *change* them at speed (Kotter and Heath are the change engines,
  [[canon-kotter-leading-change]], [[canon-heath-switch]]). "Read the assumptions" is also
  interpretively slippery — two observers infer different assumptions from the same artifacts. And
  in practice leaders over-index on level-1 artifacts (perks, slogans) precisely because level 3 is
  invisible and slow.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers) — the operator translation:
  "culture is the worst behavior the leader *tolerates*" — Walsh's standards
  ([[canon-walsh-score-takes-care-of-itself]]) and Horowitz's "what you do"
  ([[canon-horowitz-what-you-do-is-who-you-are]]) are the actionable versions.
