# Note · Cialdini — *Pre-Suasion*

> Canon · Cluster: Behavioral Design & Decision Science · Maps to **Course 08** (Negotiation), **Course 05** (Marketing & Brand)

- **Source:** Robert Cialdini, *Pre-Suasion* (2016) — the sequel to *Influence*
- **Core claim:** **What you do *before* you make your argument matters more than the argument
  itself.** Persuasion is won in the moment of *attention*, before any content is delivered.
- **Framework:**
  - **Channeled attention** — whatever is focal at the moment of decision carries disproportionate
    weight; directing attention *is* directing the conclusion.
  - **Privileged moments** — the instant right before a choice is the most influenceable; engineer
    it (a question, an image, a frame that primes the desired identity or association).
  - **Priming identity** — ask "are you adventurous?" before offering something new and "yes" rates
    jump — the product didn't change, the primed self-concept did.
  - The setup (environment, opener, first question) shapes the decision as much as the facts.
- **Applies when:** designing the *opening* of a pitch, negotiation, landing page, or meeting — the
  pre-frame, not just the substance.
- **Breaks when:** pre-suasive effects are **fragile and short-lived** — the priming evaporates fast
  and **collapses if the substance can't deliver** (a great pre-frame on a weak offer just sharpens
  the disappointment). It shares the behavioral-science **replication exposure**
  ([[canon-ariely-predictably-irrational]]) — some priming results are contested. And used cynically
  it's manipulation that erodes the trust real influence depends on
  ([[canon-carnegie-win-friends]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/marketing, r/sales) — the usable, robust
  version: **open by directing attention to the one thing you want weighted** (a question, a
  reference point) before presenting — Voss's accusation-audit is a cousin
  ([[canon-voss-never-split-the-difference]]).
