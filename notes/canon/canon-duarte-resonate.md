# Note · Duarte — *Resonate*

> Canon · Cluster: Communication & Storytelling · Maps to **Course 05** (Marketing & Brand), **E7** (Exec Presence & Comms)

- **Source:** Nancy Duarte, *Resonate* (2010)
- **Core claim:** Presentations should follow **story structure, not report structure.** The
  presenter is **not the hero — the audience is**; the presenter is the *mentor.*
- **Framework:**
  - **The "what is" / "what could be" oscillation** — alternate between the current reality and the
    proposed future; the **gap creates tension** that holds attention and pulls the audience forward.
  - **End on "the new bliss"** — the future state realized; leave them in the world you're proposing.
  - **The audience is the hero** (hero's-journey mapping) — they undergo the change; you guide.
  - Open with a strong "what is," build through repeated contrast, cross the threshold (the call to
    adventure / your ask), end at transformation.
- **Applies when:** any persuasive presentation meant to **move people to act or change** — pitches,
  visions, launches, keynotes, change communications.
- **Breaks when:** **not every communication should be a story** — a status update, a technical
  decision doc, or a data review is *better* served by Minto's answer-first pyramid
  ([[canon-minto-pyramid-principle]]); story structure on routine info wastes everyone's time and
  reads as theatrical. The "what is/what could be" pattern is also **formulaic when overused** —
  audiences pattern-match the manipulation. And great structure can't rescue a **weak underlying
  idea or false "bliss."**
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/PublicSpeaking corpus + Stanford GSB / TEDxEast transcripts)*
  The durable technique people keep is Duarte's: **contrast/tension** (what is vs. what could be) is
  what holds a room — *"you need to make this difference as big as possible… it works like a
  cliffhanger in a movie"* (TEDxEast). **But the corpus reveals where the book is silent:** the
  loudest practitioner pain isn't structure, it's *delivery under pressure.* r/PublicSpeaking's top
  threads are dominated by it — "**I want to be more articulate**" (247↑), a vocal coach's "**what
  most people get wrong about their speaking voice**" (185↑), and a raw "**Made a COMPLETE fool of
  myself at Toastmasters tonight**" (117↑) describing "speaking as fast as an auctioneer, trembling
  hands… literally running away from the podium." A perfect "what is / what could be" spine dies if
  you can't hold the room while you deliver it. The emotional engine pairs with Sinek's "why"
  ([[canon-sinek-start-with-why]]) and Heath's "motivate the Elephant" ([[canon-heath-switch]]).
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** Duarte's whole method came from one drawn shape. After "two
    years of research" she sketched a presentation "figure," overlaid it on **MLK's "I Have a Dream"**
    and **Steve Jobs's 2007 iPhone launch**, and *"it worked."* The mechanism: *"At the beginning of
    any presentation you need to show what you have… the status quo… and then compare that to what
    could be. You need to make this difference as big as possible… In the middle, one can move back
    and forth and oscillate between what is and what could be"* — ending *"with new hope… a new
    utopia"* (TEDxEast). Crucially, the speaker is *not* the star: *"the speaker is not the hero; the
    hero of our idea is the audience… You are not Luke Skywalker, you are Yoda."*
  - **Real application (mined):** the Jobs teardown. Duarte maps the 90-minute iPhone keynote and
    shows him oscillating — *"here's this new phone, and here are the competitors that suck"* — then
    *"he admires his own product… he models for the audience the emotions he wants them to feel,"*
    landing on the new bliss (the Gretzky "skate to where the puck is going" close). The companion
    Stanford GSB clip supplies the slide-level rule: every slide needs **one big idea amplified by
    contrast**, because the opposite of contrast is *"camouflage… right now in slide decks,
    everything looks the same."*
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** *Resonate* perfects structure but the
    r/PublicSpeaking corpus shows real audiences are won or lost on **delivery and nerves**, which
    the book barely touches — threads beg for help being "more articulate" (247↑) and confess
    freezing up, voice shaking, going "completely blank" in front of a group (195↑). A vocal coach's
    advice ("you probably speak too high when you're nervous," 185↑) does more for an average
    presenter on Monday than another oscillation will. Story structure is necessary, not sufficient.

### Rep
Take your next presentation. Rewrite the spine as alternating **"what is" → "what could be"** beats,
ending on the **new bliss.** Make the *audience* the hero of the change, not you or your product.
