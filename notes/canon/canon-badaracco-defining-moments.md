# Note · Badaracco — *Defining Moments*

> Canon · Cluster: Ethics & Judgment · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E11** (Personal Operating System)

- **Source:** Joseph Badaracco, *Defining Moments* (1997)
- **Core claim:** The **hardest ethical decisions aren't right vs. wrong** — they're **right vs.
  right**: competing values, competing loyalties, competing visions of who you want to be. These
  decisions **reveal and shape character simultaneously.**
- **Framework — the questions (note: identity, not analysis):**
  - **What do my deep feelings tell me?** (intuition as data, not noise)
  - **Which option passes the "newspaper test"** — could I defend this publicly, on the front page?
  - **Which option best expresses who I am and who I want to become?**
  - **Which will I be most proud of over time?**
  - Right-vs-right conflicts come in recognizable forms: truth vs. loyalty, individual vs. community,
    short-term vs. long-term, justice vs. mercy.
- **Applies when:** a genuine **values dilemma** — not "do the right thing vs. the profitable thing,"
  but two *defensible* goods that can't both be honored.
- **Breaks when:** the **identity/feelings framing can rationalize self-interest** — "which option
  expresses who I am" is dangerously close to "which option I already wanted," and the newspaper test
  rewards what's *defensible* over what's *right* (a skilled rationalizer passes it easily). It offers
  **little for power-asymmetric** situations where the "right vs. right" framing obscures a real
  victim. Pair with a principled lens (Sandel's three theories, [[canon-sandel-justice]]) so the
  decision isn't purely introspective. Horowitz's "what you do is who you are" is the cultural-scale
  echo ([[canon-horowitz-what-you-do-is-who-you-are]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/Ethics corpus + ethical-leadership transcripts)*
  The corpus confirms Badaracco's core wager — that the *real* dilemmas are right-vs-right, not
  right-vs-wrong — but mostly from outside business. A r/Ethics post on the abortion debate (1,144↑)
  is exactly his structure: *"even I consider abortions to be one of the few actual ethical dilemmas…
  Almost no one seems to be able to admit that [it] is a very complex and difficult ethical dilemma.
  And that there are actual, good reasons for both sides."* That's right-vs-right paralysis in the
  wild. **Where practitioners push back on the introspective framing:** a Global Ethics Solutions
  training video warns that "situational ethics" — *"what's right for you at the time of the crisis"* —
  is precisely how people rationalize, which is the same trap the note flags ("which option I already
  wanted"). The portable gut-check leaders actually keep is the *newspaper/character* test, but the
  corpus leans toward **process over feeling**: collect, study, analyze, *then* choose, with feelings
  as one input rather than the verdict. "Begin with the end in mind" is Covey's version of the
  proud-over-time question ([[canon-covey-7-habits]]).
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)* *(corpus thin — flagged for re-pull: the pull returned r/Ethics
  philosophy threads and adjacent ethical-leadership videos rather than Badaracco-specific "Defining
  Moments" / right-vs-right practitioner material)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** the book's mechanism is that hard calls are *judgments of
    character made without clean rules*. The closest transcript echo: *"there are more areas in your
    workplace that are not governed by specific rules than are — this is where your character and good
    ethical judgment kick in… it's all about knowing where your boundaries are"* (Global Ethics
    Solutions, "Ethical Decision Making: Kinds of Ethical Dilemmas"). Simon Sinek frames the same
    capacity as **courage** — *"the courage to speak truth to power, the courage to do the right thing
    and have integrity"* (Simon Sinek, "What Makes a Leader Great?") — which is Badaracco's
    *who-I-want-to-become* question in another register.
  - **Real application (mined):** the corpus's clearest right-vs-right case is the abortion thread on
    r/Ethics (1,144↑): *"How do we wage individual freedom against the rights of another existence?"* —
    two defensible goods that can't both be honored, the poster's complaint being that everyone
    collapses it into right-vs-wrong. The "no ethical consumption under capitalism" thread (399↑)
    mines the *short-term-vs-long-term / individual-vs-community* axis: *"ethics is… a spectrum of harm
    reduction… a measurable drift toward fewer bad outcomes,"* not an on/off switch — the practitioner
    rejection of all-or-nothing moral framing Badaracco also resists.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** the danger named in the note shows up verbatim in the
    training material — **"situational ethics," deciding by "what's right for you at the time of the
    crisis,"** is how self-interest gets laundered as judgment. The corpus prescription is *process and
    preparation* ("clear thinking, courage, strong character… preparation and planning"), not
    introspection alone — a useful guardrail on Badaracco's feelings-first questions. And Sandel's
    *Justice* lectures in the same pull (Rawls' difference principle, Kant on treating persons as ends)
    supply the **principled lens the note says to pair with** — evidence the right-vs-right method
    needs a theory of justice underneath it, not just identity.

### Rep
Take a real right-vs-right call you're facing. Name **both goods** in tension (e.g. loyalty vs.
truth). Run the **newspaper test** *and* ask which choice you'll be proud of in five years. Note where
they disagree.
