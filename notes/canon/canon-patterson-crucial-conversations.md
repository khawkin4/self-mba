# Note · Patterson, Grenny, McMillan & Switzler — *Crucial Conversations*

> Canon · Cluster: Information & Communication · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E7** (Exec Presence & Comms)

- **Source:** Kerry Patterson et al., *Crucial Conversations* (2002)
- **Core claim:** A conversation turns **crucial** when **stakes are high, opinions differ, and
  emotions run strong** — and under those conditions people default to **silence** (withdraw, avoid,
  sugarcoat) or **violence** (control, label, attack). The goal is **dialogue**: shared meaning
  flowing freely.
- **Framework:**
  - **Start with Heart** — clarify what you *actually* want (and don't want); refuse the "win vs.
    keep-the-peace" false choice.
  - **Learn to Look** — notice the moment **safety** is at risk (the shift to silence/violence is the
    tell).
  - **Make it Safe** — restore safety *before* content via **mutual purpose** ("we're on the same
    side") and **mutual respect**; contrasting ("I don't mean X, I do mean Y").
  - **Master my Stories** — separate **facts** from the **story** you've built on them ("she ignored
    me" → "she didn't reply"); you feel what your story, not the fact, dictates.
  - Then **STATE** your path and **explore theirs.**
- **Applies when:** real-time high-emotion exchanges — conflict, escalations, pushing back up the
  hierarchy — where you need to *hold* the conversation, not just plan it.
- **Breaks when:** the toolkit is **skills-heavy and hard under live pressure** — "make it safe"
  while your own amygdala is firing is exactly when it's hardest, and the techniques can feel
  **formulaic/manipulative** if the mutual purpose isn't genuine. It also assumes **roughly symmetric
  power**; in steep hierarchies or abusive dynamics, "create safety" can't be done unilaterally. Deep
  overlap with Difficult Conversations ([[canon-stone-difficult-conversations]]) — same family, more
  in-the-moment.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers, r/leadership) — the stickiest
  tool is **"separate fact from story"** — most conflict escalates on the story we invented, not the
  fact. Radical Candor is the feedback-specific cousin ([[canon-scott-radical-candor]]).
