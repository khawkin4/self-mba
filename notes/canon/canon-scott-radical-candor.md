# Note · Scott — *Radical Candor*

> Canon · Cluster: Leadership & Presence · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E7** (Exec Presence & Comms)

- **Source:** Kim Scott, *Radical Candor* (2017)
- **Core claim:** Good feedback sits at the intersection of two independent axes; most people
  default to being *nice* instead of *honest*, and problems fester.
- **Framework — the 2×2:**
  - **Care Personally** (do you give a damn about the person) × **Challenge Directly** (will you tell
    the hard truth).
  - **Both high → Radical Candor.**
  - Care, don't challenge → **Ruinous Empathy** (the most common failure — kind but dishonest).
  - Challenge, don't care → **Obnoxious Aggression.**
  - Neither → **Manipulative Insincerity.**
- **Applies when:** you owe someone a hard truth and feel the pull to soften it into uselessness —
  performance feedback, a struggling peer, an underperformer you like.
- **Breaks when:** "challenge directly" is **culturally and power-relative** — what reads as candor
  from a peer reads as aggression from a boss, and norms vary by culture and seniority; applied
  naively it licenses bluntness rebranded as honesty. "Care personally" can't be performed; without
  real relationship the framework collapses into the aggression quadrant. And candor up the power
  gradient (challenging your boss) carries risk the 2×2 doesn't price in.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers) — the recurring warning: people
  weaponize "radical candor" to excuse being a jerk while skipping the "care" axis entirely. Why
  we avoid it ties to loss-aversion / conflict-avoidance ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]).
