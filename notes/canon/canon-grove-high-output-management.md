# Note · Grove — *High Output Management*

> Canon · Cluster: Leadership & Presence · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E1** (Leading at Scale)

- **Source:** Andrew Grove, *High Output Management* (1983)
- **Core claim:** **A manager's output = the output of their team + the output of adjacent teams
  they influence.** This reframes management from *supervision* to *leverage*.
- **Framework:**
  - **Managerial leverage:** some activities produce far more output per unit time. *High-leverage:*
    1-on-1s, decision-making, training, setting direction. *Low-leverage:* monitoring (necessary
    but cheap). Maximize time on high-leverage activity.
  - **Management as a production system:** inputs → process → output, with quality checks and
    limiting steps. Meetings are the **medium** of managerial work, not an interruption.
  - **Task-relevant maturity (TRM):** how much direction to give = the person's experience *with
    that specific task*, not their general seniority. Low TRM → structured/directive; high TRM →
    delegate + monitor.
  - **Origin of OKRs** — Grove's "objectives + key results" is the seed Doerr later spread
    ([[canon-doerr-measure-what-matters]]).
- **Applies when:** you manage people (or a process) and want to find where one hour of yours moves
  the most output — the foundational operating manual for a manager.
- **Breaks when:** TRM is per-task — managers routinely misjudge it from a halo of seniority, then
  under- or over-direct. The "production system" lens can over-mechanize creative/knowledge work
  where output isn't linear. And leverage thinking can rationalize neglect: "monitoring is
  low-leverage" becomes an excuse to stop paying attention.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/managers + r/Leadership corpus)*
  The IC→manager threads echo Grove almost verbatim. A r/managers post, "**The strangest part of
  becoming a manager: you stop getting 'real' feedback**" (3.3k↑), nails the output shift —
  *"the more senior you get, the less honest input you receive, right when you probably need it the
  most… unless you actively fight for it, you can go months thinking you're doing fine while blind
  spots just keep growing."* And the single most-upvoted thread in the pull, "**The 'silent
  resignation' nobody notices**" (9.4k↑), is Grove's 1-on-1 thesis in the wild: *"they still
  deliver, still smile in meetings but the spark's gone… spotting it early and fixing it is one of
  the most important things a manager can do."* **The recurring pain point isn't believing 1-on-1s
  matter — it's that managers skip them until the volcano has already erupted.**

- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** the leverage equation is the load-bearing idea —
    *"a manager's output is equal to the output of their organization plus the output of the
    neighboring organizations under their influence… You are not paid for what you do. You are paid
    for what your team does. Think of it like a sound system. You are the amplifier."* (Insight
    Atom). The job is to find the few knob-turns that make the speakers loud, not to hum louder
    yourself.
  - **The toolkit, verified across transcripts:** **the limiting step** ("build the entire
    production process backwards from the time it takes to boil that egg" — Insight Atom);
    **training as the highest-leverage act** — *"Say you spend 12 hours writing a paper… 10 people
    read it. Next year those 10 people will work a total of about 20,000 hours. If that paper
    results in a 1% improvement… your company gains 200 hours"* (Abi Tyas Tunggal, quoting Grove);
    and **the 1-on-1 as the employee's meeting** — *"It is not for you. It is for the employee…
    you should be shutting up and listening… It's where you hear the low-level rumbling before the
    volcano erupts"* (Insight Atom).
  - **Real application (mined):** task-relevant maturity shows up as a live experiment in r/Leadership's
    "**I handed off a project to a new hire**" (1.8k↑): the lead resisted micromanaging a quiet
    junior, gave *"one clear goal, shared context"* and *"own this,"* and got back a campaign idea
    *"I wouldn't have thought of myself."* That's high-TRM delegate-and-monitor paying off — the
    counterpart to the burnout thread (737↑) where a manager *"assumed silence meant everything was
    fine"* and only caught it in a 1-on-1.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** Grove's clean production-system frame meets a
    messier reality — practitioners report that the *feedback* a manager needs to find the limiting
    step dries up exactly when they're promoted (r/managers, 3.3k↑), so leverage math is only as
    good as the honest signal feeding it. And "monitoring is low-leverage" curdles into neglect:
    the silent-resignation (9.4k↑) and quiet-burnout (737↑) threads are stories of managers who
    stopped paying attention because the dashboards still looked green.
