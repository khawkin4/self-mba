# Note · Cusumano, Gawer & Yoffie — *The Business of Platforms*

> Canon · Cluster: Network Effects & Platform Strategy · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation)

- **Source:** Michael Cusumano, Annabelle Gawer & David Yoffie, *The Business of Platforms* (2019)
- **Core claim:** Not all platforms are alike — and the durable ones manage a few brutal strategic
  tensions deliberately.
- **Framework:**
  - **Two types:** **Innovation platforms** (others build *on top* of you — iOS, AWS, Salesforce) vs.
    **transaction platforms** (you facilitate *exchange* — Uber, Airbnb, marketplaces). The most
    durable are **hybrids**.
  - **The hard strategic choices:**
    - **How open?** — too open loses control/quality; too closed limits growth.
    - **How to price each side?** — usually *subsidize the harder-to-attract side*
      ([[canon-hagiu-wright-multi-sided-platforms]]).
    - **Multi-homing** — users on multiple platforms weaken your network effects; raise switching
      costs or exclusivity to fight it.
    - **Platform envelopment** — when (and whether) to compete with your own ecosystem partners; it
      *always* creates tension and can poison the ecosystem.
- **Applies when:** running or investing in a platform that must decide openness, pricing, and how to
  treat its own complementors.
- **Breaks when:** the strategic-choice menu is clean but **executing it is political and dynamic** —
  the "compete with your ecosystem?" call (Sherlocking) buys short-term revenue at long-term trust
  cost, and the book can't tell you the threshold. Also written near the **techlash/antitrust**
  inflection — the "envelop everything" playbook now collides with regulation in ways the framework
  underweights. Complementor relationships are the unstable co-opetition of
  [[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]] at scale.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/startups, r/venturecapital) — the live
  debate: how much to let partners thrive vs. absorb their best features — every successful platform
  eventually faces the envelopment temptation.
