# Note · Helmer — *7 Powers*

> Canon · Cluster: Landscape & Competitive Awareness · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy), **Course 12** (Capstone)

- **Source:** Hamilton Helmer, *7 Powers: The Foundations of Business Strategy* (2016)
- **Core claim:** There are **exactly seven** sources of *durable* competitive advantage. Most
  claimed "advantages" don't survive the test — if you can't name which power you have or are
  building, you probably don't have one.
- **Framework — the 7 Powers:**
  1. **Scale economies** — unit cost falls with volume.
  2. **Network economies** — value rises with number of users.
  3. **Counter-positioning** — you adopt a model the incumbent *can't* copy without damaging their
     existing business (the deepest and most overlooked power).
  4. **Switching costs** — customers face loss/friction from leaving.
  5. **Branding** — a *justified* perception of quality that supports pricing power.
  6. **Cornered resource** — exclusive access to a valuable input (a patent, a key person, a deposit).
  7. **Process power** — embedded organizational capability that can't be copied quickly (e.g. Toyota).
  - A real power = a **Benefit** (it improves your economics) **that persists** (a Barrier the
    competitor can't or won't cross). Helmer's rigor is the *Barrier* test.
- **Applies when:** stress-testing any strategy or moat claim — "how will we win" (from
  [[canon-lafley-martin-playing-to-win]]) must resolve to *one of these seven* or it's a wish.
- **Breaks when:** the taxonomy is a **static classification, not a playbook** — it tells you what a
  power *is*, not how to *originate* one (Helmer's "invention" stage is thin). Powers can also
  erode (yesterday's network effect undone by multi-homing) — durability is relative, not absolute.
  And counter-positioning, the prized power, is the hardest to recognize before it's obvious.
  Sharper successor to Porter's generic strategies ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/SecurityAnalysis, investing threads) —
  investors use 7 Powers as a *moat checklist*; the discipline is forcing yourself to name the
  specific barrier, which kills most lazy "great brand / great team" moat claims.

### Rep
Take one company you admire. Name its single strongest power from the seven — and the *specific
barrier* that stops competitors from copying it. If you can't name the barrier, it's not a power.
