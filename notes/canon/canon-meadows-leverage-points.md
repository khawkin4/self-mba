# Note · Meadows — *Leverage Points*

> Canon · Cluster: Design Thinking & Problem-Solving · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **Course 06** (Operations)

- **Source:** Donella Meadows, *Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System* (essay, 1999;
  expanded from her systems work)
- **Core claim:** There are **twelve places to intervene in a system, ranked from least to most
  effective** — and **most interventions happen at the bottom** of the list (tweaking numbers), where
  they barely matter.
- **Framework — the 12 leverage points (least → most powerful):**
  12. Constants, parameters, numbers (taxes, standards) — *weakest.*
  11. Buffer sizes · 10. Stock-and-flow structure · 9. Lengths of delays · 8. Strength of balancing
  loops · 7. Gain of reinforcing loops · 6. Information flows (who has access) · 5. **Rules**
  (incentives, constraints) · 4. **Self-organization** (power to add/change structure) · 3.
  **Goals** of the system · 2. **Paradigm** — the shared assumptions the system arises from · 1. The
  **power to transcend paradigms.**
  - Big takeaway: **the highest-leverage interventions challenge the *goal* and the *paradigm***,
    which is why they're rare and usually come from **outsiders** — and why number-tweaking feels
    productive but rarely moves the system.
- **Applies when:** choosing *where* to intervene in any persistent systemic problem — policy, org
  design, a stubborn metric — and checking you're not just adjusting parameters.
- **Breaks when:** **leverage and *feasibility* run opposite** — the highest points (goals,
  paradigms) are the **hardest to move and most resisted**, so "aim high on the list" can mean **aim
  at the impossible** while the achievable parameter tweak goes undone. Meadows herself cautioned the
  ranking isn't mechanical — push the wrong high-leverage point in the wrong direction and you do
  *more* damage, faster. Pair with Rumelt's crux (importance × *solvability*,
  [[canon-rumelt-the-crux]]) to balance leverage against tractability.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/systemsthinking, policy/ops threads) — the
  durable habit: when you reach for a number/parameter fix, **ask whether the *rule*, *goal*, or
  *paradigm* is the real lever** — usually it is. The foundation is her *Thinking in Systems*
  ([[canon-meadows-thinking-in-systems]]).

### Rep
Take an intervention you're planning. Where does it sit on the 12 — a *parameter* (low) or a *rule /
goal / paradigm* (high)? If low, ask what higher-leverage point you're avoiding, and why.
