# Note · Minto — *The Pyramid Principle*

> Canon · Cluster: Communication & Storytelling · Maps to **E7** (Exec Presence & Comms), **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making)

- **Source:** Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle* (1987) — the McKinsey communication standard
- **Core claim:** **Start with the answer.** Audiences lose attention fast, so if you *build up* to a
  conclusion they've checked out before you arrive. Structure every communication as a pyramid:
  **answer first, then support.**
- **Framework:**
  - **Top:** the single main point / answer (the "governing thought").
  - **Middle:** 3–4 supporting arguments that *group and summarize* the level below.
  - **Bottom:** the data/evidence under each argument.
  - **MECE** — **M**utually **E**xclusive, **C**ollectively **E**xhaustive — every grouping has **no
    overlaps and no gaps.**
  - **SCQA** opener — **S**ituation → **C**omplication → **Q**uestion → **A**nswer — to set up the
    pyramid's top.
- **Applies when:** any business writing or presentation — memos, board decks, recommendations,
  emails that must drive a decision.
- **Breaks when:** **answer-first assumes the audience trusts you (or the answer) enough to hear it
  cold** — for a hostile, skeptical, or emotionally-invested audience, leading with the bald
  conclusion triggers resistance; you sometimes need narrative tension first (Duarte's "what is / what
  could be," [[canon-duarte-resonate]]) or a pre-frame ([[canon-cialdini-pre-suasion]]). MECE can
  also become **over-engineered, bloodless** structure that's *correct* but unpersuasive. It's
  built for **logical recommendation**, less for **inspiration**.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — Minto explainer transcripts corpus)*
  Practitioners who teach this for a living flag two failure modes the book underplays. First,
  **structure is downstream of analysis:** the EPM "*Minto Pyramid Principle Explained*" walkthrough
  warns that "**if you don't have good analysis or data at the lowest level of the pyramid it can
  lead to the wrong albeit logically constructed conclusion**" — i.e. answer-first makes a *wrong*
  answer land harder, not softer. Second, **the grouping is the hard part, not the layout:** StrategyU
  observes that "**most writers simply group ideas without completing the thinking**… [tying] together
  ideas that have a general rather than a specific relationship," so most real-world pyramids are
  MECE-shaped but insight-empty. The recurring coaching note: pure pyramid logic reads as cold — pair
  the structure with story for high-stakes pitches. Bezos's 6-pager is the long-form cousin
  ([[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]]).
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** the mechanism is **top-down communication** — lead with the
    answer, then support it. The EPM explainer states it plainly: "**start with the answer by making
    your recommendation or conclusion immediately**… having the answer up front makes it much easier
    for [the listener] to follow your reasoning" (EPM). Analyst Academy gives the contrast: instead of
    "I like players who play different positions, score, and lead… *so* I like LeBron James," you say
    "**My favorite player is LeBron James *because* he can play different positions, scores, and is a
    team leader**" — answer, then reasons. Above the data sit two rules: **encapsulation** (each box
    "**summarize[s] the main point of all the boxes below it**") and **MECE** at every level —
    "**mutually exclusive**… completely separate and not overlapping… **collectively exhaustive**…
    adding more boxes should not be possible" (EPM).
  - **Real application (mined):** the worked case is "**how do we double profit?**" → answer-first:
    "**to double profit we should enter the health food market because our current growth is anemic,
    it's the most attractive food-related market, and we're uniquely positioned to enter it**" (EPM).
    The payoff is *flexibility under time pressure*: if your board slot gets cut from an hour to ten
    minutes, "**you can simply adapt… a minute on your main point and three minutes on each supporting
    argument**" and "**the actual message you're delivering doesn't change at all**" — because the
    pyramid degrades gracefully. StrategyU's bottom-up drill (**Label → Sort → Insight**) shows the
    reverse move: three data points ("home prices rising in Hartford / Providence / Boston") collapse
    to one standalone insight ("**home prices are increasing in the largest capitals in New England**").
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** the explainers themselves enumerate the failure
    modes. EPM: it "**takes discipline and often a lot of trial and error**," and a clean pyramid built
    on bad data "**can lead to the wrong albeit logically constructed conclusion**" — structure
    launders weak analysis. StrategyU: people "**group ideas without completing the thinking**,"
    producing MECE-correct but insight-free decks. (Note: the r/PublicSpeaking corpus pulled for this
    cluster was entirely about stage-fright/delivery anxiety, not message structure, so the practitioner
    critique here is transcript-sourced — *flagged for a r/consulting/r/managers re-pull*.)

### Rep
Take an email or deck you're drafting. Move the **answer to the top**. Below it, force exactly 3–4
**MECE** supporting points (no overlaps, no gaps). Cut everything that isn't evidence for one of them.
