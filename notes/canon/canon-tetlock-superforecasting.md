# Note · Tetlock — *Superforecasting*

> Canon · Cluster: Risk, Uncertainty & Fragility · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making)

- **Source:** Philip Tetlock & Dan Gardner, *Superforecasting* (2015)
- **Core claim:** Most expert predictions are **barely better than chance** — but a measurable minority
  (**superforecasters**) are consistently better, and what they do is *learnable*.
- **Framework — what superforecasters do:**
  - Think in **probabilities**, not certainties (and use fine-grained numbers — 65%, not "likely").
  - **Update incrementally** as evidence arrives (many small Bayesian revisions, not flip-flops).
  - **Decompose** big questions into smaller tractable sub-questions (Fermi-ize them).
  - Actively **seek disconfirming evidence** and the **outside view / base rate** first.
  - **Keep score** on themselves (calibration: do my 70%s happen ~70% of the time?).
  - The enemies: **overconfidence** and **ideological commitment** (the "hedgehog" with one big idea
    loses to the "fox" who knows many things).
- **Applies when:** any recurring judgment under uncertainty where you can later check the result —
  forecasts, risk calls, planning estimates.
- **Breaks when:** it works best on **specific, time-bound, resolvable** questions ("will X happen by
  Q3?") — it has *less* to say about Taleb's true Black Swans, the unscorable fat-tail events
  ([[canon-taleb-black-swan]]); the two camps genuinely disagree on how much of the world is
  forecastable. Calibration also needs **volume + feedback** to develop, which most real decisions
  don't provide. And good *process* still yields bad *outcomes* sometimes — judge the process, not
  the single result ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/decisionmaking, r/forecasting) — the
  habit that transfers: write your probability *down* before the event, then check it — it's
  humbling and it's how calibration is built. Munger's "avoid overconfidence" made operational
  ([[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]]).
