# Note · Tetlock — *Superforecasting*

> Canon · Cluster: Risk, Uncertainty & Fragility · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making)

- **Source:** Philip Tetlock & Dan Gardner, *Superforecasting* (2015)
- **Core claim:** Most expert predictions are **barely better than chance** — but a measurable minority
  (**superforecasters**) are consistently better, and what they do is *learnable*.
- **Framework — what superforecasters do:**
  - Think in **probabilities**, not certainties (and use fine-grained numbers — 65%, not "likely").
  - **Update incrementally** as evidence arrives (many small Bayesian revisions, not flip-flops).
  - **Decompose** big questions into smaller tractable sub-questions (Fermi-ize them).
  - Actively **seek disconfirming evidence** and the **outside view / base rate** first.
  - **Keep score** on themselves (calibration: do my 70%s happen ~70% of the time?).
  - The enemies: **overconfidence** and **ideological commitment** (the "hedgehog" with one big idea
    loses to the "fox" who knows many things).
- **Applies when:** any recurring judgment under uncertainty where you can later check the result —
  forecasts, risk calls, planning estimates.
- **Breaks when:** it works best on **specific, time-bound, resolvable** questions ("will X happen by
  Q3?") — it has *less* to say about Taleb's true Black Swans, the unscorable fat-tail events
  ([[canon-taleb-black-swan]]); the two camps genuinely disagree on how much of the world is
  forecastable. Calibration also needs **volume + feedback** to develop, which most real decisions
  don't provide. And good *process* still yields bad *outcomes* sometimes — judge the process, not
  the single result ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — risk-fragility transcript corpus; r/decisionmaking pull returned empty — corpus thin, flagged for re-pull)*
  The habit that transfers is the **scorecard**, and the corpus is blunt about why most people never
  build the skill: *"In most organizations, forecasts are made and forgotten, so there is no
  measurement, no revision, and no improvement"* (MindScience 360). The practitioner move is to make
  the commitment **explicit as a number** — *"using numbers makes commitments explicit"* — then keep
  *"a small log of your probabilities and dates, so you can later compare confidence with reality."*
  That single discipline is Munger's "avoid overconfidence" made operational
  ([[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]]).
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)* *(corpus thin — reddit pull empty, flagged for re-pull)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** Forecasting is reframed as a **measurable, learnable skill,
    not a gift** — *"This approach treats forecasting as a measurable skill, not a mysterious gift
    reserved for famous pundits"* (MindScience 360). The mechanism is calibration: *"Calibration
    means that your eighty percent calls should occur about eighty percent of the time, not just
    feeling confident,"* paired with **resolution** — *"staying near fifty-fifty avoids being wrong
    but also avoids being useful."* The summary corpus echoes the trait stack: *"intellectual
    humility… they are also highly curious and open-minded… their ability to think probabilistically"*
    (POCKETUNI), i.e. the **fox**, not the hedgehog.
  - **Real application (mined):** the transcript's worked example is exactly the Rep — a tournament
    question with a clear deadline where *"one person posts sixty percent repeatedly and never learns
    much. Another person revises from forty to fifty-five to seventy as facts change, and the score
    reflects that learning"* (MindScience 360). The method: *"begin by counting similar past votes"*
    (the **outside view / base rate**), then *"adjust that baseline for new factors like shifting
    alliances or public opinion, and compute a rough probability"* (the inside view). **Brier scores**
    are the keeping-score tool: *"forecasts into distances from what happened, so lower scores mean
    closer contact with reality."*
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** the cluster's Taleb side draws the hard line —
    *"antifragility is the solution to the Black Swan problem"* (The Swedish Investor, *Antifragile*
    summary), and fragility is *"something that does not like variability… a nonlinear accelerated
    reaction up to the point of breaking"* (Taleb himself). Tetlock's scoreable, time-bound questions
    don't reach those fat-tail events — you don't calibrate a once-a-century earthquake. A second,
    quieter pushback: the BusinessPsychologyHub transcript notes Tetlock's own work shows people run
    **different rationalities** ("intuitive Economist… Theologian… prosecutor"), so a clean
    probability is only one worldview — sacred-value and rule-enforcement framings resist being
    Fermi-ized into a number.

it resolves, check it. Repeat — that loop *is* the skill.
