# Note · Mauboussin — *The Success Equation*

> Canon · Cluster: Financial Literacy for Operators · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance)

- **Source:** Michael Mauboussin, *The Success Equation* (2012)
- **Core claim:** Most outcomes mix **skill and luck**, but we systematically **attribute results to
  skill** when luck did much of the work — which corrupts how we judge people and decisions.
- **Framework:**
  - **The skill–luck continuum** — pure skill (chess) ↔ pure luck (roulette); most business/investing
    sits in the messy middle.
  - **The paradox of skill** — as everyone's skill rises and converges, **luck plays a *larger* role**
    in *differences* in outcome (why top investors/athletes are separated by variance).
  - **Mean reversion** — the more luck in a domain, the stronger extreme results revert; don't
    extrapolate a hot streak.
  - **Judge process, not outcome** — a good decision with a bad outcome is still a good decision.
    Evaluating on results alone **rewards lucky risk-takers and punishes skilled operators** who hit
    variance.
- **Applies when:** evaluating performance — people, investments, strategy bets — anywhere a single
  outcome tempts you to over-credit (or over-blame) the actor.
- **Breaks when:** **separating skill from luck cleanly is itself hard** — without large samples you
  can't actually distinguish them, so "judge the process" can become an excuse to *never* hold anyone
  accountable for outcomes (the mirror of the results-only error). And "good process, bad outcome" is
  easy to *claim* in hindsight to dodge responsibility. The honest version needs Tetlock's
  scorekeeping discipline to tell a genuinely good process from a rationalized one
  ([[canon-tetlock-superforecasting]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/SecurityAnalysis, r/investing) — investors
  internalize "process over outcome" and **mean reversion** (don't chase last year's winner);
  managers use it to avoid firing good people for variance — Bezos's process-quality bent fits here
  ([[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]]).

### Rep
Take one recent outcome you judged (a hire, a bet, a quarter). Ask: was the *process* sound,
separate from how it turned out? Re-grade it on process. Notice if you'd been crediting luck.
