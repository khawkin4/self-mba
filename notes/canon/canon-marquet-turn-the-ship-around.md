# Note · Marquet — *Turn the Ship Around!*

> Canon · Cluster: Leadership & Presence · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E1** (Leading at Scale)

- **Source:** L. David Marquet, *Turn the Ship Around!* (2012)
- **Core claim:** Replace **leader-follower** (one decides, all execute) with **leader-leader**:
  push authority to where the information already lives.
- **Framework:**
  - **"I intend to…"** — instead of asking permission, people state intent; the leader approves or
    redirects. This forces them to think like a decision-maker and surfaces their reasoning.
  - **Two preconditions (non-negotiable):**
    1. **Competence** — people must know enough to make good calls.
    2. **Clarity** — people must understand the *purpose* well enough to align judgment.
  - Core equation: **empowerment without competence is dangerous; competence without empowerment is
    wasted.**
- **Applies when:** you're the bottleneck — decisions queue at you because authority and information
  have been separated. The cure for the over-centralized org.
- **Breaks when:** the two preconditions aren't met — handing authority to a low-competence or
  low-clarity team produces fast, confident mistakes. Building competence + clarity is *slow*, so
  "I intend to" can't be switched on overnight. Also context-bound: a submarine has clear right
  answers and high training; ambiguous, political, or low-skill environments resist the model.
  Same precondition Bungay names ([[canon-bungay-art-of-action]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers, r/leadership) — managers report
  "I intend to" works only after months of building team judgment; rushed, it becomes abdication.
