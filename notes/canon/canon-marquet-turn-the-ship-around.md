# Note · Marquet — *Turn the Ship Around!*

> Canon · Cluster: Leadership & Presence · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E1** (Leading at Scale)

- **Source:** L. David Marquet, *Turn the Ship Around!* (2012)
- **Core claim:** Replace **leader-follower** (one decides, all execute) with **leader-leader**:
  push authority to where the information already lives.
- **Framework:**
  - **"I intend to…"** — instead of asking permission, people state intent; the leader approves or
    redirects. This forces them to think like a decision-maker and surfaces their reasoning.
  - **Two preconditions (non-negotiable):**
    1. **Competence** — people must know enough to make good calls.
    2. **Clarity** — people must understand the *purpose* well enough to align judgment.
  - Core equation: **empowerment without competence is dangerous; competence without empowerment is
    wasted.**
- **Applies when:** you're the bottleneck — decisions queue at you because authority and information
  have been separated. The cure for the over-centralized org.
- **Breaks when:** the two preconditions aren't met — handing authority to a low-competence or
  low-clarity team produces fast, confident mistakes. Building competence + clarity is *slow*, so
  "I intend to" can't be switched on overnight. Also context-bound: a submarine has clear right
  answers and high training; ambiguous, political, or low-skill environments resist the model.
  Same precondition Bungay names ([[canon-bungay-art-of-action]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/leadership + r/managers corpus + Marquet transcripts)*
  The model *works* when both preconditions are present: a r/leadership "**I handed off a project to a
  new hire, and what happened next surprised me.**" thread (1847↑) is leader-leader in the wild — the
  manager "gave them one clear goal, shared context, and said: *'You don't need to run everything by
  me, own this'*" and got a self-starting owner instead of a check-in machine. **But the recurring
  failure mode is the inverse: leader-follower atrophy.** r/managers' "**The 'silent resignation'
  nobody notices**" (9400↑) describes exactly what Marquet's "134 people doing what they're told"
  produces — a good employee who "stops pushing… No more bold ideas. No more challenging bad
  processes," usually "after too many ignored ideas, decisions made without them." The honest caveat:
  control is hard to surrender. r/leadership "**The one sentence my therapist said that completely
  changed how I lead**" (421↑) — *"I used to think leadership was about control… controlling outcomes,
  controlling my image"* — names the leader's own fear that keeps authority hoarded at the top.

- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** Marquet's mechanism is a deliberate change of *language* that
    relocates ownership: *"every other submarine captain request[s] permission… On the Santa Fe it's
    'Captain, I intend to submerge the ship.' …it might seem like a very small nuance change of
    language but it was hugely powerful because the psychological ownership now shifts to them — they
    need to discover the answer, otherwise you're always the answer man"* (Leadership Academy). On the
    same ship he made the two preconditions explicit and *interrogable*: he'd ask "convince me it's
    safe" (**competence** — "is it safe?") and "is it the right thing to do?" (**clarity** — "because
    our mission requires…"). *"These are the two pillars… the technical competence and the
    organizational clarity. You put those things in place and then you can give control."*
  - **Real application (mined):** Marquet's own origin story is the cleanest case — *"I was trained
    for one submarine, my guys were trained to do what they were told. That's a deadly combination."*
    The crew's diagnosis was blunt — *"there's only one logical solution… you shut up"* — so he vowed
    *"never to give another order"* (retaining only weapons release as a moral line). The payoff:
    *"another submarine had one guy in charge, one guy giving orders, one guy thinking and 134 people
    doing what they're told… on my submarine I got 135 thinking, active, passionate, creative,
    proactive people"* (Leadership Academy), and the next inspection drew "the highest grade they had
    ever seen." The corporate mirror is the r/leadership new-hire handoff above.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** the submarine had *clear right answers, heavy
    training, and a fear culture to dismantle* — Marquet concedes *"fear is always the biggest
    problem… I've done this to over 100 audiences in 20 different countries and every single time fear
    has been the biggest word"* (Talks at Google). In ambiguous, political, or low-skill orgs the
    pillars aren't pre-built; r/managers' "silent resignation" and burnout threads show that "give
    control" without first installing competence + clarity (or while people are too depleted to think)
    just yields silence, not initiative. Empowerment can't precede the slow work of building judgment.
