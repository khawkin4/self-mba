# Note · Neustadt & May — *Thinking in Time*

> Canon · Cluster: History & Judgment · Maps to **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro), **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making)

- **Source:** Richard Neustadt & Ernest May, *Thinking in Time: The Uses of History for Decision
  Makers* (1986)
- **Core claim:** Decision-makers reflexively reach for a **historical analogy** ("this is like
  Munich," "this is another Vietnam") — and the error is applying it **wholesale** without testing
  what's actually similar and different.
- **Framework — the discipline:**
  - **Known / Unclear / Presumed** — separate what you *know* from what you *assume*; most errors come
    from **treating presumptions as facts.**
  - **Likenesses and Differences** — explicitly list how the current situation *resembles* and
    *differs from* the analogy; superficial resemblance ≠ deep structural similarity.
  - **"What's the story?"** — surface the narrative you're telling yourself; is it built on evidence
    or on pattern-matching?
  - **Placement** — understand a person's/org's history to predict behavior.
- **Applies when:** any high-stakes decision where you (or your team) are reasoning from a past
  parallel — which is almost always, usually unconsciously.
- **Breaks when:** the method is **deliberate and slow** — great for set-piece decisions, impractical
  for the fast, recognition-primed calls where expertise *should* run on pattern-matching (Klein,
  [[canon-klein-sources-of-power]]); the two are in tension, and knowing *which mode the situation
  calls for* is the meta-skill. It also can't manufacture good analogies if your historical knowledge
  is thin — garbage in. Availability bias makes the *vivid* analogy feel apt
  ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/strategy, policy/history threads) — the
  portable habit: when someone says **"this is just like X,"** force the **likenesses *and*
  differences** list before acting on the analogy. The Thucydides Trap is an analogy this book would
  insist you *test* ([[canon-thucydides-peloponnesian-war]]).
