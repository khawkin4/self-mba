# Note · Parrish — *The Great Mental Models*

> Canon · Cluster: Mental Models & Thinking · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **E11** (Personal OS)

- **Source:** Shane Parrish (Farnam Street), *The Great Mental Models* (Vols 1–4, 2019–)
- **Core claim:** Better thinking is a **skill, not a talent** — a curated, cross-disciplinary
  toolkit that compounds. A practical, accessible companion to [[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]].
- **Framework — the core models:**
  - **The map is not the territory** — your model of reality isn't reality; act accordingly.
  - **Circle of competence** — know where your knowledge is strong vs. weak, and behave differently
    in each zone (most errors are competence-boundary violations).
  - **First-principles thinking** — decompose to fundamental truths instead of reasoning by analogy.
  - **Probabilistic thinking** — outcomes aren't binary; think in likelihoods and base rates.
  - **Second-order thinking** — *and then what?*
  - **Inversion**, **Occam's razor**, **Hanlon's razor**, etc.
- **Applies when:** you want a shared vocabulary for clearer reasoning and a checklist to run before
  big decisions — the "starter latticework."
- **Breaks when:** a catalog of models invites **hindsight model-shopping** — picking the model that
  flatters the conclusion you already reached. Knowing the names ≠ using them under pressure, where
  System 1 runs the show ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]). And "first principles" is
  expensive — reasoning by analogy is often the *correct*, efficient default; reserve first
  principles for the few decisions that merit it.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/decisionmaking, productivity threads) —
  readers warn the models are most useful as a *post-hoc debugging* checklist for bad decisions, not
  a real-time oracle.
