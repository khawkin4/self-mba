# Note · Parrish — *The Great Mental Models*

> Canon · Cluster: Mental Models & Thinking · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **E11** (Personal OS)

- **Source:** Shane Parrish (Farnam Street), *The Great Mental Models* (Vols 1–4, 2019–)
- **Core claim:** Better thinking is a **skill, not a talent** — a curated, cross-disciplinary
  toolkit that compounds. A practical, accessible companion to [[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]].
- **Framework — the core models:**
  - **The map is not the territory** — your model of reality isn't reality; act accordingly.
  - **Circle of competence** — know where your knowledge is strong vs. weak, and behave differently
    in each zone (most errors are competence-boundary violations).
  - **First-principles thinking** — decompose to fundamental truths instead of reasoning by analogy.
  - **Probabilistic thinking** — outcomes aren't binary; think in likelihoods and base rates.
  - **Second-order thinking** — *and then what?*
  - **Inversion**, **Occam's razor**, **Hanlon's razor**, etc.
- **Applies when:** you want a shared vocabulary for clearer reasoning and a checklist to run before
  big decisions — the "starter latticework."
- **Breaks when:** a catalog of models invites **hindsight model-shopping** — picking the model that
  flatters the conclusion you already reached. Knowing the names ≠ using them under pressure, where
  System 1 runs the show ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]). And "first principles" is
  expensive — reasoning by analogy is often the *correct*, efficient default; reserve first
  principles for the few decisions that merit it.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — mental-models transcript corpus; r/decisionmaking pull came back empty, flagged for re-pull)*
  The people who actually run a latticework warn it only pays off when you stop *collecting* models
  and start *committing* to one. A Farnam-Street-style SXSW talk frames the whole bedrock as a
  discipline of conviction: *"you take a simple idea and you take it seriously… you have to go all
  in. And if you don't go all in, then it just kind of doesn't work"* (SXSW, "The Latticework of
  Mental Models"). The Swedish Investor adds the un-glamorous practitioner reality: *"it isn't the
  learning that's so hard. It's the unlearning."* And the live constraint is that under stress the
  fast system overrides the catalog — the bat-and-ball trap (*"the ball is 10 cents right? Well that
  answer is wrong… the ball is five cents"*) shows a named model on the page losing to System 1 in
  real time. The corpus reads the models less as a real-time oracle than as a post-hoc *debugging*
  checklist you have to force yourself to run.

- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** the mechanism is a **latticework** — models cascade in the
    same direction to produce outsized effects: *"it's really when you start cascading the models
    together… in the same direction for the same effort, that's when you get what Munger would call
    Lollapalooza effects. 1 + 1 becomes 11"* (SXSW, "The Latticework of Mental Models"). The Swedish
    Investor frames the *cost of not* building it: *"the man who is in need of a new thinking tool,
    but hasn't yet acquired it, is already paying for it."*
  - **Real application (mined):** **inversion** in the wild — Munger tells of stumping seven of his
    children with a riddle, until the physicist *"just turned it around… led him to checkers, a game
    you could almost master with enough experience… took him about 15 seconds"* (The Free Bull,
    "Charlie Munger on His Favorite Mental Model"). The SXSW speaker's own *map-is-not-the-territory*
    lesson: a bank CIO refused to leave "slide 10" because it was *"a very severe pain point he
    had"* — the deck (the map) wasn't the buyer's reality until the speaker located the one slide
    that was.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** knowing the names ≠ deploying them. Kahneman's own
    framing in the corpus — *"most of us feel that we have reasons for what we're doing, but in fact
    we do what we're doing very largely"* automatically (Inc.) — and the bat-and-ball miss show the
    models lose to System 1 under load. The hard part practitioners flag isn't acquiring a model,
    it's the *"unlearning"* (Swedish Investor) of the analogy you'd otherwise reach for by default.
