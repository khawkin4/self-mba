# Note · Cialdini — *Influence*

> Canon · Cluster: Negotiation & Influence · Maps to **Course 08** (Negotiation), **Course 05** (Marketing & Brand)

- **Source:** Robert Cialdini, *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion* (1984)
- **Core claim:** Persuasion runs on a handful of automatic **"click, whirr" triggers**. The value
  isn't learning to manipulate — it's **recognizing the levers being pulled on you.**
- **Framework — the six (later seven) principles:**
  1. **Reciprocity** — people feel obligated to return favors.
  2. **Commitment & consistency** — a small first step → continued commitment in that direction.
  3. **Social proof** — under uncertainty, people copy what others do.
  4. **Authority** — people defer to perceived expertise (titles, uniforms, credentials).
  5. **Liking** — we say yes to people we like (similarity, compliments, cooperation).
  6. **Scarcity** — we want what's disappearing or restricted.
  7. **Unity** (added later) — shared identity ("one of us").
- **Applies when:** designing or defending against persuasion — marketing, pricing, fundraising,
  and spotting when a counterpart is engineering your "yes."
- **Breaks when:** the triggers are **probabilistic, not deterministic** — they shift base rates,
  they don't compel; over-relying on one (manufactured scarcity, fake social proof) breeds backlash
  once detected. Used cynically they corrode trust and brand (the opposite of persuasion long-term).
  And in low-emotion, high-deliberation decisions (System 2,
  [[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]) the levers weaken. This is applied "psychology of
  misjudgment" — Munger's list overlaps heavily ([[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/marketing, r/sales) — practitioners report
  reciprocity + social proof are the workhorses; the ethical line is whether the favor/proof is real.
