# Note · Alchian & Demsetz — *Production, Information Costs, and Economic Organization*

> Canon · Cluster: Economics & Incentive Design · Maps to **Course 03** (Micro & Strategy Foundations), **Course 09** (Leadership & OB)

- **Source:** Armen Alchian & Harold Demsetz, *Production, Information Costs, and Economic
  Organization* (AER, 1972)
- **Core claim:** **Why do firms exist?** Because **team production creates a measurement problem** —
  when output is joint, you can't easily tell who contributed what, so individuals are tempted to
  shirk. The firm exists as a **monitoring-and-reward mechanism** to solve it.
- **Framework:**
  - **Team production** — output > sum of separable individual outputs, but individual contributions
    are *not separately observable* → **shirking incentive.**
  - The solution is a **monitor** (the "boss") who watches inputs, meters productivity, and **holds
    the residual claim** (profit) so the monitor is themselves incentivized not to shirk.
  - **Every org structure is an attempt to solve an information + incentive problem.** When you design
    teams, comp, or reporting lines, you're really designing **monitoring and reward systems.**
- **Applies when:** designing team structure, compensation, or org charts — the question is always
  *does this align individual incentives with collective output?*
- **Breaks when:** the **monitor-centric** story under-weights **intrinsic motivation, professional
  norms, and culture** — high-trust teams produce without heavy monitoring (and over-monitoring
  *destroys* the goodwill that drove the output — Ariely's social-vs-market norms,
  [[canon-ariely-predictably-irrational]]). It also doesn't fully explain *firm boundaries* (Coase's
  transaction costs / Williamson are the complement). And in modern knowledge work, "metering inputs"
  is often impossible — you can only manage outcomes (Cagan/Larson,
  [[canon-cagan-inspired]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers, org-econ threads) — the durable
  lens: **a confusing org chart is usually a botched answer to "who can we measure, and how do we
  reward them?"** The agency-cost sibling is Jensen & Meckling
  ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]).
