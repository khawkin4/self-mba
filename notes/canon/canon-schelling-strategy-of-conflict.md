# Note · Schelling — *The Strategy of Conflict*

> Canon · Cluster: Game Theory & Strategic Interaction · Maps to **Course 03** (Micro & Strategy Foundations), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro)

- **Source:** Thomas Schelling, *The Strategy of Conflict* (1960)
- **Core claim:** In strategic interaction, the power often lies in **constraining your own
  options** — and much coordination happens **without communication**, via shared expectations.
- **Framework:**
  - **Focal points (Schelling points)** — when people can't communicate, they converge on the
    solution that seems *natural/obvious* (meet in NYC with no details → Grand Central at noon). Norms,
    conventions, and defaults are organizational focal points.
  - **Credible commitment** — **burning bridges, public commitments, or limiting your own choices**
    makes a threat or promise believable. Counterintuitively, *removing* your options gives you power.
  - **The appearance of irrationality can be rational** — a party who *can't* back down (or seems
    unable to) wins the standoff.
- **Applies when:** negotiation, deterrence, deadlines, brinkmanship, and any coordination problem
  solved by convention rather than instruction.
- **Breaks when:** **commitment tactics are dangerous if both sides use them** — mutual bridge-
  burning is how you get the catastrophic outcome neither wanted (the brinkmanship that horrified
  Schelling himself). Focal points depend on **shared culture**; across cultures the "obvious"
  answer differs and coordination fails. And feigned irrationality, called wrong, becomes real
  disaster. Pairs with credible-commitment in [[canon-dixit-nalebuff-thinking-strategically]] and
  the raw power realism of [[canon-thucydides-peloponnesian-war]].
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/negotiation, r/geopolitics) — the business
  translation: a **public, hard-to-reverse commitment** (a price guarantee, a burned boat) is a real
  source of bargaining power — and "let's just default to the obvious focal point" resolves most
  coordination without a meeting.
