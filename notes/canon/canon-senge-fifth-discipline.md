# Note · Senge — *The Fifth Discipline*

> Canon · Cluster: Systems Thinking & Complexity · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making)

- **Source:** Peter Senge, *The Fifth Discipline* (1990)
- **Core claim:** A **learning organization** is built from five disciplines, integrated by systems
  thinking — the "fifth" that ties the rest together.
- **Framework — the five disciplines + archetypes:**
  1. **Personal mastery** — continuous individual learning.
  2. **Mental models** — surface and test your assumptions ([[canon-parrish-great-mental-models]]).
  3. **Shared vision** — alignment around a real purpose.
  4. **Team learning** — collective intelligence (dialogue over debate).
  5. **Systems thinking** — see structures and feedback, not events.
  - **Systems archetypes** (the payoff): **shifting the burden** (a quick fix that atrophies the real
    solution), **limits to growth** (a reinforcing engine hits a balancing constraint), **tragedy of
    the commons** (shared resources overused because individual incentives ignore collective cost).
    Once you know the archetypes you spot them everywhere and intervene earlier.
- **Applies when:** diagnosing recurring organizational dysfunction and trying to build a culture that
  *learns* rather than firefights.
- **Breaks when:** "learning organization" is **inspiring but hard to operationalize** — the book is
  long on vision, short on the gritty *how*, and many "learning org" programs became consultancy
  theater. The archetypes are the durable, portable part; the five disciplines resist measurement and
  can become aspirational wallpaper (Schein's espoused-values trap,
  [[canon-schein-organizational-culture]]). For *quantitative* rigor you need Sterman
  ([[canon-sterman-business-dynamics]]); for ranked interventions, Meadows
  ([[canon-meadows-leverage-points]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers) — practitioners keep the
  **archetypes** (especially "shifting the burden") and quietly drop the grander learning-org
  program. Same structural-not-personal lesson as Meadows ([[canon-meadows-thinking-in-systems]]).
