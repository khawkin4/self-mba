# Note · Bezos — *Shareholder Letters (1997–2020)*

> Canon · Cluster: Org Design & Mechanisms · Maps to **E8** (Stakeholder & IR), **E2** (Capital Allocation), **Course 10** (Entrepreneurship)

- **Source:** Jeff Bezos, Amazon annual shareholder letters (1997–2020)
- **Core claim:** A masterclass in **mechanisms over good intentions** — building systems that scale
  culture past what any individual can personally enforce. ("Good intentions don't work; mechanisms
  do.")
- **Framework — the durable ideas:**
  - **Day 1 vs. Day 2** — Day 2 is stasis, irrelevance, decline, death. The job is to preserve Day 1
    mentality (customer obsession, fast decisions, embrace of external trends).
  - **Two-way vs. one-way doors** — reversible decisions should be made *fast* by small groups;
    irreversible ones deserve deliberation. Most decisions are two-way doors treated as one-way.
  - **High-velocity decision-making** — decide at **~70%** of the information you wish you had;
    waiting for 90% is slowness.
  - **Disagree and commit** — you can disagree with a decision, but once made, commit fully.
  - **Customer obsession > competitor obsession** — customers are "divinely discontent," a renewable
    source of pressure to improve.
- **Applies when:** scaling an org past the point where you can be in every decision — Bezos's answer
  is to install mechanisms (the every-letter-reattaches-1997, the 6-pager, the bar-raiser).
- **Breaks when:** "70% and decide fast" assumes the decision is reversible — applied to one-way
  doors it's recklessness (Bezos explicitly carves these out, but readers flatten the nuance).
  Mechanisms can ossify into bureaucracy (the very Day 2 they're meant to prevent). And the letters
  are a *curated* narrative — survivorship bias; the failures (Fire Phone) get a sentence, the wins
  a doctrine. Pairs with high-velocity decision quality from
  [[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]].
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/startups, *Acquired* Amazon episodes) —
  operators love the two-way-door framing but note Amazon's culture was also famously brutal; the
  letters omit the human cost.
