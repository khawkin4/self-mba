# Note · Bossidy & Charan — *Execution*

> Canon · Cluster: Execution & Operations · Maps to **E5** (Transformation), **Course 09** (Leadership & OB)

- **Source:** Larry Bossidy & Ram Charan, *Execution: The Discipline of Getting Things Done* (2002)
- **Core claim:** **Strategy without execution is hallucination.** Execution isn't a tactical
  afterthought — it's a *discipline and a system* the leader personally owns.
- **Framework — the three core processes (and their linkage):**
  1. **People** — do you have the right people in the right roles? (Bossidy: the #1 job.)
  2. **Strategy** — is it linked to reality *and* to your people's actual capability?
  3. **Operations** — how does work actually get done; what's the operating plan?
  - The three must be **linked**: strategy is set by the people, delivered through operations.
  - Leaders who execute **stay close to the detail without micromanaging** — they know which
    questions to ask and which gaps to probe; they run robust, honest dialogue.
- **Applies when:** an org is strategy-rich and delivery-poor — the leadership team spends all its
  energy on the plan and none on whether the org can deliver it.
- **Breaks when:** "stay close to the detail" is a **razor's edge** — the same behavior, done wrong,
  *is* micromanagement that strips the autonomy execution needs (cf. mission command,
  [[canon-bungay-art-of-action]], and Marquet's authority-pushing,
  [[canon-marquet-turn-the-ship-around]]). The book is strong on *what* execution requires, thinner
  on *how* in a knowledge-work / ambiguous-output context (it's rooted in industrial/GE-style ops).
  And "right people in right roles" presumes you can assess fit accurately — itself error-prone.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers, r/ExperiencedDevs) — operators
  agree the "honest operating review" is where execution lives or dies, but warn it degrades into
  status theater unless the leader actually probes the gaps.

### Rep
Take one strategic goal. Score the three processes: are the *people* right, is the strategy *linked
to reality*, does the *operating plan* exist? The weakest of the three is why it'll stall.
