# Note · Walsh — *The Score Takes Care of Itself*

> Canon · Cluster: Leadership & Presence · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E12** (Culture as Strategy)

- **Source:** Bill Walsh, *The Score Takes Care of Itself* (2009)
- **Core claim:** Set the **standard of performance** high enough across *every detail* — how you
  practice, prepare, conduct yourself — and results follow. Don't chase winning; build the standard,
  and winning is the byproduct.
- **Framework — the Standard of Performance:**
  - Define what "professional" looks like at every level of detail (Walsh specified how players
    dressed, how the locker room looked, how a phone was answered).
  - **Culture = accumulated daily standards**, not inspirational speeches.
  - The leader's job: define the standard, then hold to it *relentlessly*; the scoreboard is a
    lagging indicator of the standard.
- **Applies when:** you're rebuilding a demoralized or mediocre team and the temptation is to focus
  on outcomes. Walsh's move is to ignore outcomes and obsess over execution quality.
- **Breaks when:** "standards over scoreboard" can curdle into **process worship** — polishing
  detail while losing the actual game; you still need a feedback loop that the standard *produces*
  results (Walsh's standard was battle-tested; not every leader's pet standards are). It also
  demands a leader with genuine domain mastery to know which details matter; imposed-but-arbitrary
  standards just become bureaucracy. And the relentlessness risks burnout without the "care
  personally" axis ([[canon-scott-radical-candor]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/managers + r/Leadership + Talks-at-Google/Marquet transcript corpus)*
  Operators live the standard-of-performance thesis as *behaviors trained to the detail level*, not values on the
  wall. The most upvoted proof is r/managers **"Three staff didnt know how to call 911"** (5,367↑): a grocery owner
  whose cashiers froze when a customer collapsed spent the next morning making each one *demonstrate* the call —
  Walsh's "specify how the phone is answered" made literal. r/Leadership **"What makes someone look 'executive' vs
  just senior?"** (891↑) is the same move for *presence*: a director, told only to find "gravitas," reverse-engineered
  it into observable behaviors ("they almost never speak first… they don't get defensive… 'good point, let me think
  about that'"). And Marquet names the failure mode directly: *"most people say… think like a team. Here's some
  posters… that doesn't make team performance happen"* (Talks at Google) — the scoreboard/poster being a lagging
  indicator of the actual daily standard.
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** The cluster corpus restates Walsh's mechanism as *culture is built by daily
    behaviors, not exhortation.* David Marquet (a direct cross-link) draws the exact line Walsh does between standard
    and scoreboard: *"most people say… think like a team. Here's some posters that tell us. Send some emails to
    encourage people, whatever. That doesn't make team performance happen"* — instead you change the standing behavior
    ("we just said the word *we*… six months later we had rewired our brains and it felt like a team") (Talks at
    Google). Grove supplies the leverage logic underneath: *"your job is one thing and one thing only — output… if
    your team does not produce, you are failing. It does not matter how hard you work"* (Insight Atom) — i.e. the
    leader's standard is the highest-leverage act, and the score is downstream of it.
  - **Real application (mined):** r/managers **"Three staff didnt know how to call 911"** (5,367↑) is Walsh's
    locker-room detail in the wild — when a customer collapsed, three cashiers stood frozen, so the owner *"spent all
    morning going over with staff how to call 911 and have them show me"* and scripted the exact words. The standard
    wasn't a value ("be prepared"); it was a rehearsed behavior, verified. r/Leadership **"What makes someone look
    'executive' vs just senior?"** (891↑) decomposes the fuzzy adjective "presence" into trainable detail-level
    behaviors — let others speak first then synthesize, never get defensive under challenge — exactly Walsh's "name
    the behavior, not the adjective."
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** the relentless-standard ethic has a human cost the book underplays.
    r/Leadership **"I almost lost my best employee to burnout"** (354↑) and **"One of my top performers almost burned
    out quietly"** (737↑) are the predictable failure mode of standards-without-the-care-personally axis. And
    **"People don't quit jobs, they quit bosses"** (1,356↑) is the inverse warning: a leader can enforce an immaculate
    standard and still bleed talent if the standard isn't paired with trust — which is why the corpus keeps routing
    back to [[canon-scott-radical-candor]] and Marquet's *give-control* model rather than pure top-down rigor.
