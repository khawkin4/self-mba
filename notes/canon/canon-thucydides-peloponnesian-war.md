# Note · Thucydides — *History of the Peloponnesian War*

> Canon · Cluster: History & Judgment · Maps to **Course 11** (Global Strategy & Macro), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro)

- **Source:** Thucydides, *History of the Peloponnesian War* (c. 400 BCE)
- **Core claim:** The original case study in **strategic competition between an established power and a
  rising one** — and a clear-eyed account of how **initial advantages erode through overextension,
  internal division, and hubris.**
- **Framework — the enduring lessons:**
  - **The Thucydides Trap** (Graham Allison's later coining) — when a rising power threatens a ruling
    one, structural fear makes conflict likely; "what made war inevitable was the growth of Athenian
    power and the fear it caused in Sparta."
  - **The Melian Dialogue** — *"the strong do what they can and the weak suffer what they must"* — the
    starkest statement of **power realism** ever written (and Schelling-relevant,
    [[canon-schelling-strategy-of-conflict]]).
  - **Pericles's Funeral Oration** — a masterclass in **wartime leadership communication** and
    binding people to a cause.
  - **The Sicilian Expedition** — the canonical **strategic overreach**: a rich, dominant power
    destroys itself through ambition past its culminating point ([[canon-clausewitz-on-war]]).
  - Central tension: **strategic patience vs. strategic overreach.**
- **Applies when:** thinking about incumbent-vs-challenger dynamics (geopolitical or competitive),
  long-horizon strategy, and how internal decay — not just external rivals — undoes the strong.
- **Breaks when:** the **Thucydides Trap is contested history** — critics argue it's an over-tidy
  analogy retrofitted onto messy events and (per Neustadt & May,
  [[canon-neustadt-may-thinking-in-time]]) applying it wholesale to, say, US–China **smuggles in
  presumptions as facts.** Ancient power-politics also maps imperfectly onto **positive-sum,
  interdependent** modern economies ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]). Read it for the
  *pattern of hubris and overreach*, not as a deterministic law.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/geopolitics, r/strategy) — the line leaders
  keep is the **Sicilian Expedition as cautionary tale**: dominant players most often lose to *their
  own overreach*, not to a stronger enemy. Grove's "only the paranoid" is the corporate echo
  ([[canon-grove-only-the-paranoid-survive]]).
