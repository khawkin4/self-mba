# Note · Heath & Heath — *Switch*

> Canon · Cluster: Culture & Change · Maps to **E5** (Transformation & Change Management)

- **Source:** Chip & Dan Heath, *Switch: How to Change Things When Change Is Hard* (2010)
- **Core claim:** Change is a conflict between the **rational mind (Rider)** and the **emotional mind
  (Elephant)**, traveling a **Path (the environment)**. Most change efforts fail because they coach
  the Rider and ignore the Elephant.
- **Framework — Direct / Motivate / Shape:**
  - **Direct the Rider** — give crystal-clear direction (what looks like resistance is often
    *confusion*). Find the **bright spots** (what's already working) and clone them; script the
    critical moves.
  - **Motivate the Elephant** — make people *feel* something (analysis doesn't move people, emotion
    does); **shrink the change** so it feels doable; grow a "growth mindset" identity.
  - **Shape the Path** — make the right behavior *easier*: tweak the environment, build habits,
    rally the herd. Change the situation, not the person.
- **Applies when:** you need behavior change in individuals or small groups and willpower-based
  approaches have failed — especially when "resistance" might actually be ambiguity or a hostile
  environment.
- **Breaks when:** it's strongest on **individual/team** behavior, weaker on the *political* and
  *structural* obstacles of large transformations where Kotter's coalition-building is needed
  ([[canon-kotter-leading-change]]). "Make it easy / shape the path" can mask that the underlying
  *system* (Meadows) or *culture assumptions* (Schein) are the real blockers, not the path. And
  "bright spots" assumes a working example exists — sometimes nothing yet works.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/managers corpus + Kotter/Schein transcripts)*
  The corpus confirms Switch's central diagnosis — **"resistance" is usually unclear direction (Rider)
  or a hostile Path, not bad will.** In r/managers' "**How Do You Talk to an Employee Who Isn't
  Getting Promoted…**" (4,470↑, 2,187 comments) a manager's *own* first instinct is to **Shape the
  Path** — "**Change the policy / have core hours… I would love that**" — but, blocked, defaults to
  coaching the Rider alone, which "**just keeps him apace.**" The book's blind spot also shows: the
  most-upvoted change story, "**The 'silent resignation' nobody notices**" (9,404↑, 1,026 comments),
  is a *demotivated Elephant* — "**they still deliver, still smile in meetings but the spark's gone**"
  after "**too many ignored ideas**" — and the thread's verdict is brutally un-Heath: "**once they
  hit that point, it's rare to get them back.**" Shrinking-the-change and bright-spots don't reload a
  burned-out Elephant. The Rider/Elephant is Kahneman's System 2/1 in change clothing
  ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]).

- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** Switch's core move is to stop coaching only the rational
    Rider and **make people *feel* the change** — the same lever Kotter's own team names: *"This is
    about what is it going to **feel like** and what is it going to **look like** when we're
    successful… how do we ensure people can **see themselves in that success**"* (Kotter International).
    The EPM summary frames it identically — *"you want to **win the hearts and minds**… create a
    willing army of people who **want** to make the change happen"* — i.e. **Motivate the Elephant**
    before you Direct the Rider.
  - **The Path, verified:** the corpus echoes Switch's "change the situation, not the person" — Schein's
    model literally makes *"create the **right environment**"* a discrete change step (EPM, Schein
    explainer), and stresses *"culture ultimately comes about through the **behavior** of individuals"*
    — behavior first, beliefs follow, exactly Heath's Path → habit logic.
  - **Real application (mined):** the Craig case (r/managers, 4,470↑) is a clean three-lever miss — the
    Path is locked (flex-time policy the manager "**can't change**"), the Elephant isn't engaged
    (Craig is "**frustrated… passed over so many times**"), and the Rider was never given a *clear,
    scripted next move* ("**should I have a frank discussion why other people tend to grow faster?**").
    Switch's prescription — script the critical move + remove one friction point — is precisely what's
    absent.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** practitioners report a failure mode Switch
    under-weights — **the Elephant that's already quit.** In "the silent resignation" (9,404↑), the
    spark dies from *structural* causes (ignored ideas, endless urgent work) and is "**rare to get
    back**"; bright-spots and shrink-the-change assume a willing animal. And Kotter's emphasis on a
    **guiding coalition** + **sense of urgency** is the political/structural layer Heath admits Switch
    is weak on ([[canon-kotter-leading-change]]).

    Talk to an Employee Who Isn't Getting Promoted…" (4,470↑), "The 'silent resignation' nobody
    notices" (9,404↑).

### Rep
For a change that's stuck, do all three: script *one* clear next move (Rider), find a feeling or
bright spot (Elephant), and remove *one* point of friction (Path). Notice which was actually missing.
