# Note · Stone, Patton & Heen — *Difficult Conversations*

> Canon · Cluster: Information & Communication · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **E7** (Exec Presence & Comms)

- **Source:** Douglas Stone, Bruce Patton & Sheila Heen, *Difficult Conversations* (1999, Harvard Negotiation Project)
- **Core claim:** Every difficult conversation is really **three conversations at once**, and most
  people try to have only the first — then are baffled when it fails.
- **Framework — the three layers:**
  1. **The "What Happened" conversation** — facts, interpretations, blame. (Move from *who's right* to
     *each person's story*; from blame to **contribution**.)
  2. **The Feelings conversation** — the emotions driving behavior but going unspoken. (You must
     *name* them or they run the conversation from underneath.)
  3. **The Identity conversation** — what this means about *me*: am I competent, am I a good person?
     (The real reason hard conversations feel threatening.)
  - The skill: **recognize which layer is actually active** and surface it. Move from **certainty to
    curiosity**; replace "you did X" with "**I experienced X — help me understand your perspective.**"
- **Applies when:** any high-stakes interpersonal conversation — feedback, conflict, a broken
  commitment — where the surface "facts" fight isn't the real issue.
- **Breaks when:** the three-layer move **takes time, safety, and a willing counterpart** — against a
  bad-faith or coercive party, "move to curiosity" can become getting steamrolled (you still need a
  BATNA, [[canon-fisher-ury-getting-to-yes]]). The "contribution not blame" frame can also be
  **misused to launder real accountability** ("we both contributed" when one party is clearly at
  fault). And surfacing feelings/identity requires emotional skill the book assumes more than it
  builds.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers, r/cscareerquestions) — managers
  cite the **identity layer** as the unlock: people get defensive not over facts but over the threat
  to their self-image. The execution-pressure cousin is Crucial Conversations
  ([[canon-patterson-crucial-conversations]]); the candor frame is Scott
  ([[canon-scott-radical-candor]]).

### Rep
Before your next hard conversation, write the **three layers**: the facts dispute, the unspoken
*feeling*, and the *identity* threat (theirs and yours). Open with curiosity about their story, not
your verdict.
