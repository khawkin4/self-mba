# Note · Sinek — *Start with Why*

> Canon · Cluster: Communication & Storytelling · Maps to **Course 05** (Marketing & Brand), **E7** (Exec Presence & Comms)

- **Source:** Simon Sinek, *Start with Why* (2009)
- **Core claim:** **People don't buy *what* you do — they buy *why* you do it.** Inspiring leaders and
  brands communicate from the inside out, leading with purpose.
- **Framework — the Golden Circle:**
  - **Why** (purpose/belief) at the center → **How** (your differentiating process) → **What**
    (products/services) on the outside.
  - **Most organizations communicate outside-in** (here's what we make, here's how, …, and never get
    to why). **Inspiring ones start at the center** — lead with the belief.
  - **Biological hook (as Sinek frames it):** "why" speaks to the **limbic brain** (emotion,
    decision); "what" speaks to the **neocortex** (analysis). People **decide emotionally and justify
    rationally** — so lead with the emotion.
- **Applies when:** brand positioning, mission/vision communication, recruiting, rallying a team — any
  message whose job is to **inspire belonging or belief**, not to transact information.
- **Breaks when:** it's a **powerful frame riding on shaky science** — the "limbic vs. neocortex"
  neurology is pop-simplified, and the marquee Apple/Wright-Brothers examples are **survivorship-
  picked** (plenty of purpose-led companies failed; plenty of "what-led" ones won). "Why" can become
  **vague inspirational wallpaper** that substitutes for a real product or strategy — purpose doesn't
  pay if the *what* is weak. For a *decision* memo, Minto's answer-first beats a "why" monologue
  ([[canon-minto-pyramid-principle]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/PublicSpeaking corpus + Sinek/Duarte transcripts)*
  Communicators keep the **lead-with-purpose / inspire-don't-inform** instinct while rolling their eyes
  at "why-washing." Duarte makes the same bet from the storytelling side — *"if you express an idea in
  a way that resonates, change will happen"* (TEDxEast) — but the corpus's loudest, most-upvoted voices
  expose the gap Sinek leaves open: **knowing your "why" doesn't get the words out.** r/PublicSpeaking's
  top threads aren't about purpose at all — "**I want to be more articulate**" (247↑), "**Does this
  exercise of pen between your teeth actually help…**" (330↑), "**I used to freeze up every time I
  spoke**" (195↑). One poster nails the practitioner reality: *"I can think clearly, I know my stuff, I
  rehearse everything in my head perfectly. But the moment I stand in front of people my brain just
  exits"* ("The human brain is a wonderful thing…", 143↑). The emotion-first lesson rhymes with Heath's
  Elephant ([[canon-heath-switch]]) and Kahneman's decide-then-justify
  ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]) — but a clear belief is necessary, not sufficient: you
  still have to deliver it under pressure.
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** Sinek's whole mechanism is **reversing the order of
    information** — lead with belief, not product: *"All I did was reverse the order of the
    information. What we've learned is that people don't buy what you do; people buy why you do it"*
    (TED, "How Great Leaders Inspire Action"). He grounds it in biology, not psychology: the **limbic
    brain** *"is responsible for all our feelings… and all decision-making, and has no capacity for
    language,"* while the neocortex handles *"rational and analytical thinking and language"* — so
    outside-in messaging *"does not affect behavior"* (TEDxPugetSound). The job is to talk to the part
    of the brain that decides, then let people rationalize with the "what."
  - **Real application (mined):** Sinek's own worked case is the **Apple reversal** — most firms say
    *"We make great computers… want to buy one?"*, whereas Apple says *"Everything we do, we believe in
    challenging the status quo… we just happen to make great computers"* — and the counter-case is
    **TiVo**, *"the highest quality product on the market… extremely well funded,"* a *"commercial
    failure"* because it pitched features (*"pauses live TV, skips commercials…"*) and the market
    answered *"We don't believe you. We don't need this. You're scaring us"* (TED). Duarte's corpus
    supplies the recruiting/movement version: *"some of you tried to pitch your idea but it was
    rejected and some other mediocre idea was approved — the only difference was how they were
    presented"* (TEDxEast).
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** the r/PublicSpeaking corpus reframes the real
    bottleneck as **delivery, not purpose.** Practitioners who *know* their "why" still report *"my
    voice shook, my heart raced"* and *"the moment I stand in front of people my brain just exits."*
    The fixes that get upvoted are mechanical and rehearsal-based — *"practicing in the actual room
    ahead of time,"* *"recording myself,"* *"memorize just the very first sentence"* ("I used to
    freeze up…", 195↑) — i.e. the unglamorous reps Sinek's inspiration narrative skips. A clear belief
    is the *content* of a great message; it doesn't substitute for the *craft* of saying it.
