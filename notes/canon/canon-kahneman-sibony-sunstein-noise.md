# Note · Kahneman, Sibony & Sunstein — *Noise*

> Canon · Cluster: Behavioral Design & Decision Science · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **E6** (Crisis Leadership)

- **Source:** Daniel Kahneman, Olivier Sibony & Cass Sunstein, *Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment* (2021)
- **Core claim:** **Bias's overlooked twin is noise.** Bias is *systematic* error (everyone skews the
  same way); **noise is random scatter** (different people — or the same person on different days —
  reach wildly different conclusions from identical facts). Orgs obsess over bias and **almost never
  measure noise**, though noise may do more damage.
- **Framework:**
  - **Total error² = bias² + noise²** — they add; reducing only bias leaves half the problem.
  - Evidence: judges give very different sentences for identical cases; underwriters quote very
    different premiums for the same risk; doctors diagnose the same symptoms differently.
  - **Decision hygiene** to cut noise: gather **independent judgments before discussion** (avoid
    anchoring/cascades), use **shared scales / reference classes**, **aggregate** multiple
    assessments, structure judgments into independent components, and favor rules/algorithms for
    repeated judgments.
- **Applies when:** any organization makes **repeated professional judgments** — hiring, pricing,
  grading, underwriting, performance review — where consistency itself has value.
- **Breaks when:** noise reduction can over-rotate into **rigid algorithmization** that strips
  legitimate, expert, case-sensitive discretion (Klein's recognition-primed expertise is *not*
  noise, [[canon-klein-sources-of-power]] — telling them apart is hard). "Independent judgments
  first" is procedurally costly and culturally resisted. And some variance is signal, not noise —
  flattening it can erase real differences.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — Nudge/decision-science transcript corpus; Reddit pull empty, flagged for re-pull)*
  The actionable kernel holds up on the practitioner side: in hiring/review, **have people score
  independently *before* the meeting** — the loudest voice otherwise sets the anchor. The corpus
  reframes *why* this is so hard: Sunstein's own co-authored work insists error is **predictable and
  structural, not personal** — *"we make mistakes and importantly, we make them in very predictable
  ways"* (Pete Judo, *Nudge in less than 10 minutes*). The same Thaler/Sunstein logic that says
  *"there's no neutral way to do it… everything matters"* about choice architecture (Thaler, Sydney
  Executive Plus) applies to **judgment architecture**: if you don't design the process, the default
  process is noisy. Pairs with Tetlock's calibration ([[canon-tetlock-superforecasting]]).
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)* *(corpus thin — Reddit pull empty, flagged for re-pull; YouTube transcripts only)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** *Noise* extends the Thaler/Sunstein program from *biased*
    humans to *inconsistent* ones. The premise the corpus keeps returning to: humans are not the
    rational "Econs" of textbook theory — *"Traditional economics assumes we're all these perfectly
    rational creatures called Econs. But in the real world… we're just humans"* who *"make mistakes
    and… make them in very predictable ways"* (Fatter Wallets / Pete Judo summaries of *Nudge*).
    *Noise*'s twist: those humans don't even err *consistently* — identical inputs yield scattered
    outputs, so **Total error² = bias² + noise²**, and you must engineer the judgment environment to
    cut the scatter.
  - **Real application (mined):** Thaler's **choice-architecture** frame is the operational sibling
    of *Noise*'s **decision hygiene**. *"Choice architecture is just the environment in which we
    choose… to a first approximation everything matters"* and *"there's no neutral way to do it"*
    (Thaler, Sydney Executive Plus). Translate to judgment: there is **no neutral way to run a hiring
    panel or an underwriting desk** — order of speaking, whether scores are independent, what scale
    is shared — all of it shapes the spread. Design it, or inherit a noisy default. Thaler's mantra
    *"if you want people to do something, make it easy"* is exactly why **independent-first scoring**
    has to be built into the form, not left to willpower.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** Gary Klein is the sharpest check — his
    **recognition-primed decision** research shows expert firefighters who *"don't look at any
    comparison between options… they're using their experience to size up the situation, relying on
    patterns built up over 10, 15, 20 years"* (Klein, Lootok). That expert variance is **signal, not
    noise** — over-aggressive algorithmization (the very fix *Noise* favors) can flatten exactly the
    case-sensitive expertise Klein says is the source of good fast decisions. Telling Klein's
    pattern-matching apart from true noise is the unsolved hard problem ([[canon-klein-sources-of-power]]).

### Rep
Run a noise audit on one repeated decision: have 3 people independently score the *same* case
without conferring. The spread is your noise. Then add one hygiene step (independent-first) and
re-measure.
