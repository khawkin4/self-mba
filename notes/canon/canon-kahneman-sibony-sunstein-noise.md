# Note · Kahneman, Sibony & Sunstein — *Noise*

> Canon · Cluster: Behavioral Design & Decision Science · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **E6** (Crisis Leadership)

- **Source:** Daniel Kahneman, Olivier Sibony & Cass Sunstein, *Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment* (2021)
- **Core claim:** **Bias's overlooked twin is noise.** Bias is *systematic* error (everyone skews the
  same way); **noise is random scatter** (different people — or the same person on different days —
  reach wildly different conclusions from identical facts). Orgs obsess over bias and **almost never
  measure noise**, though noise may do more damage.
- **Framework:**
  - **Total error² = bias² + noise²** — they add; reducing only bias leaves half the problem.
  - Evidence: judges give very different sentences for identical cases; underwriters quote very
    different premiums for the same risk; doctors diagnose the same symptoms differently.
  - **Decision hygiene** to cut noise: gather **independent judgments before discussion** (avoid
    anchoring/cascades), use **shared scales / reference classes**, **aggregate** multiple
    assessments, structure judgments into independent components, and favor rules/algorithms for
    repeated judgments.
- **Applies when:** any organization makes **repeated professional judgments** — hiring, pricing,
  grading, underwriting, performance review — where consistency itself has value.
- **Breaks when:** noise reduction can over-rotate into **rigid algorithmization** that strips
  legitimate, expert, case-sensitive discretion (Klein's recognition-primed expertise is *not*
  noise, [[canon-klein-sources-of-power]] — telling them apart is hard). "Independent judgments
  first" is procedurally costly and culturally resisted. And some variance is signal, not noise —
  flattening it can erase real differences.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-06 — book-specific *Noise* summary transcript, "Noise by Daniel Kahneman: Animated Book Summary," youtube.com/watch?v=KntsGhV3rvM)*
  The actionable kernel is the book's own archer image: a team all *"missing low and to the left"* is
  **biased** but coachable; a team *"missing the bullseye all over the target"* is **noisy** — and
  *"a team that is noisily missing is much harder to coach than a team that is missing due to bias"*
  (KntsGhV3rvM). That is exactly why noise is the neglected problem: bias is *"easy to identify,"* a
  noisy process *"is much harder to identify and fix."* The practitioner translation is concrete and
  uncomfortable — *"it is likely that an assessment would depend more on the person doing the review
  than the performance of the person being judged"* (KntsGhV3rvM). So in hiring/review, **score
  independently *before* the meeting**: the same transcript warns that *"who speaks first or last,
  who speaks with the most confidence, who is smiling"* contaminates group judgment. Pairs with
  Tetlock's calibration ([[canon-tetlock-superforecasting]]).

- **Researched layer** *(updated 2026-06-06)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** The book splits error into two species. *"Some judgments are
    biased — they are systematically off target. Other judgments are noisy, as people who are expected
    to agree end up at very different points around the target"* (Kahneman, quoted in "Noise by Daniel
    Kahneman: Animated Book Summary," KntsGhV3rvM). The wedge is the word *judgment* itself: unlike an
    opinion or taste, *"the word judgment implies that everyone should agree and come to the same
    conclusion… we expect judgments to be agreed upon"* (KntsGhV3rvM) — and they don't. A second
    book summary nails the operative definition: noise is *"the random variation in judgments that
    should be consistent when evaluating the same information… the background static that can disrupt
    the decision-making process"* ("Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment (Book Summary)," 0qVNXLZygDs).
    Hence **Total error² = bias² + noise²**: kill bias and you still leave the scatter.
  - **Real application (mined):** the transcript lists the book's own **six fixes**, which read as a
    field checklist: *"accept that decisions are about accuracy and not individual expression… think
    statistically and take an outside view… structure judgment into independent tasks… take
    independent judgments from multiple judges and average them out… and favor relative judgments,
    which tend to be less noisy"* (KntsGhV3rvM). The second summary frames the same as **decision
    hygiene** — *"using checklists, algorithms and standardized criteria"* and *"establishing clear
    decision criteria, providing feedback and promoting a culture of accountability"* (0qVNXLZygDs).
    The canonical examples to audit first: *"the variability in legal sentencing where identical cases
    can result in different sentences depending on the judge,"* and *"medical diagnosis can vary widely
    among doctors"* (0qVNXLZygDs).
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** the same transcript that sells the fix also flags
    its limit — *"algorithms are not immune to biases"* (0qVNXLZygDs). The book's preferred remedy
    (rules/algorithms for repeated judgments) trades scatter for whatever the rule bakes in, so
    aggressive algorithmization can flatten legitimate, case-sensitive expertise — Klein's
    recognition-primed variance is **signal, not noise**, and telling the two apart is the unsolved
    hard problem ([[canon-klein-sources-of-power]]).

### Rep
Run a noise audit on one repeated decision: have 3 people independently score the *same* case
without conferring. The spread is your noise. Then add one hygiene step (independent-first) and
re-measure.
