# Note · Axelrod — *The Evolution of Cooperation*

> Canon · Cluster: Game Theory & Strategic Interaction · Maps to **Course 03** (Micro & Strategy Foundations), **Course 09** (Leadership & OB)

- **Source:** Robert Axelrod, *The Evolution of Cooperation* (1984)
- **Core claim:** Cooperation can emerge among self-interested players **without trust or central
  authority** — if the game is *repeated.* In a famous tournament of iterated-prisoner's-dilemma
  strategies, the **simplest won: Tit for Tat.**
- **Framework:**
  - **Tit for Tat** — cooperate first, then do whatever the other player did last move.
  - **Four properties of winning strategies:**
    1. **Nice** — never defect first.
    2. **Retaliatory** — punish defection immediately (don't be a pushover).
    3. **Forgiving** — return to cooperation as soon as they do (don't hold grudges).
    4. **Clear** — be easy to read, so others can predict and cooperate with you.
  - The lesson: in repeated interactions, **predictable fairness beats cleverness**; trust is built
    through *consistency*, not maneuvering.
- **Applies when:** any ongoing relationship — partners, vendors, teammates, repeat negotiations —
  where you'll meet again and reputation compounds.
- **Breaks when:** Tit for Tat is **vulnerable to noise** — one *misread* defection triggers an
  endless retaliation spiral (the fix, "Tit for Tat with forgiveness" / generous TfT, matters in
  noisy real life). It also assumes a **genuinely repeated game with a long/unknown horizon** — in
  one-shot or end-game situations, defection can pay (which is why "is this repeated?" is the first
  question). And against a committed predator, niceness-first can be exploited at least once.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/negotiation, r/sales) — the operating
  rule people derive: **be cooperative and transparent by default, retaliate once and clearly, then
  forgive.** It's the game-theoretic spine under Carnegie's consistency
  ([[canon-carnegie-win-friends]]) and Fisher-Ury's relationship-preservation
  ([[canon-fisher-ury-getting-to-yes]]).
