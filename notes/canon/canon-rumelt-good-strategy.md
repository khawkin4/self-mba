# Note · Rumelt — *Good Strategy / Bad Strategy*

> Canon · Cluster: Strategy · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy), **Course 12** (Capstone)

- **Source:** Richard Rumelt, *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* (2011)
- **Core claim:** Most "strategy" is bad strategy — goals dressed up as strategy, or a list of
  priorities that aren't prioritized. Strategy is **problem-solving, not aspiration**.
- **Framework — the kernel (3 parts):**
  1. **Diagnosis** — name what's actually going on; identify the single most critical challenge.
  2. **Guiding policy** — the overall approach you're choosing to meet that challenge.
  3. **Coherent action** — steps that *reinforce each other* (not a scattered to-do list).
  - "Bad strategy" = **fluff** (restating the obvious) + **failure to make hard choices**.
- **Applies when:** you face a complex, ambiguous challenge and need to focus finite energy
  against the *one* thing that matters most. The diagnosis step is where almost everyone cheats.
- **Breaks when:** the "single critical challenge" framing tempts oversimplification — some
  situations have two genuinely coupled constraints, and forcing one diagnosis loses the other.
  Also: a brilliant kernel still dies in execution if intent isn't transmitted downward (see
  [[canon-bungay-art-of-action]]). And diagnosis is only as good as your systems-model of the
  problem ([[canon-meadows-thinking-in-systems]]) — a wrong diagnosis is confidently wrong.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/strategy corpus + Lenny's Podcast / Rumelt transcripts)*
  The corpus confirms Rumelt's core complaint *and* complicates it. r/strategy practitioners
  repeatedly describe the exact "fluff" failure: a high-traffic **"Most organizations don't need a
  new strategy"** thread (45↑) argues most "strategy refreshes" are misdiagnosed — *"the strategy
  isn't broken — the communication and operating rhythm are"* (priorities shifting, KPIs tracked
  but never reviewed, "meetings full of updates but short on decisions"). A **"Leaders who run
  strategy workshops"** thread (27↑) lists the same rot from the inside: *"Bad strategy that good
  execution won't fix"* and "good ideas don't turn into action." **But the recurring pushback is
  that diagnosis isn't where most teams actually fail — execution and alignment are.** Rumelt
  half-concedes this himself: *"organizational Dynamics are often the biggest hindrance to winning
  strategies"* — which is why the corpus keeps pointing past the kernel toward [[canon-bungay-art-of-action]].
- **The discipline that defines it:** deciding **what you will *not* do**.

- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** Rumelt insists strategy is *"a design for overcoming a
    high-stakes challenge… a mixture of policy and action designed to deal with a challenge"* —
    not a goal, mission, or vision. He'd rather you *"don't call it a strategy, call it an action
    agenda"* (Lenny's Podcast). The work itself is **diagnosis first**: *"part of what a diagnosis
    is is a decision of what you're going to pay attention to"* — naming the *one or two* key
    challenges, then choosing a guiding policy and coherent actions to attack them.
  - **The kernel, verified across transcripts:** the explainer "What IS Strategy? (No Fluff!)"
    walks the same three parts — *"diagnosis defines WHY, the guiding policy defines WHAT, coherent
    actions explain HOW"* — and catalogs Rumelt's four flavors of **bad strategy**: fluff (the
    real bank line *"Our fundamental strategy is one of customer-centric intermediation"*), failure
    to face the challenge, mistaking goals/vision for strategy, and bad strategic objectives.
  - **Real application (mined):** the transcript's worked case is **Blockbuster vs. Netflix** —
    Blockbuster *"didn't really understand the challenge and just launched a few online services
    without a real analysis of the commitments and investments required."* It's a textbook missed
    *diagnosis*: a guiding-policy reflex (add a channel) bolted onto a problem nobody had named.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** r/strategy practitioners argue the binding
    constraint is usually *downstream* of the kernel. The **"Most organizations don't need a new
    strategy"** thread (45↑) and the **"Leaders who run strategy workshops"** thread (27↑) both
    locate failure in alignment, operating rhythm, and execution — *"good ideas don't turn into
    action,"* *"bad execution that good strategy won't fix (more likely)."* A clean kernel is
    necessary but not sufficient; transmitting intent downward is the part Rumelt's three boxes
    under-weight (hence [[canon-bungay-art-of-action]]).
