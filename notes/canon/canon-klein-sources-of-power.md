# Note · Klein — *Sources of Power*

> Canon · Cluster: Behavioral Design & Decision Science · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **E6** (Crisis Leadership)

- **Source:** Gary Klein, *Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions* (1998)
- **Core claim:** In **high-stakes, time-pressured** settings, experts **don't weigh pros and cons** —
  they pattern-match. This is the pro-intuition counterweight to Kahneman's "distrust System 1."
- **Framework — Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD):**
  - The expert **recognizes a pattern** from experience → **mentally simulates a single course of
    action** → if it works in the head, **executes**; only generates alternatives if the first option
    fails simulation.
  - This is why **experience matters** and why **expertise often can't be articulated** — it's tacit
    pattern-matching built from thousands of cases (firefighters, ICU nurses, military commanders).
  - Org implication: some decisions need analysis; **much of the time you should trust experienced
    operators** and give them room to act on recognition.
- **Applies when:** time-critical, recurring, feedback-rich domains where deep expertise exists —
  incident response, operations, clinical/field judgment.
- **Breaks when:** RPD is **only as good as the pattern library** — in **low-validity** environments
  (noisy, slow/no feedback, non-recurring) the same confident intuition is *exactly* the System-1
  trap Kahneman warns of ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]). Kahneman & Klein's joint
  "adversarial collaboration" resolved it: trust expert intuition **only** where the environment is
  regular *and* the expert had repeated feedback to learn from. Misread the domain and recognition
  becomes overconfident bias; it can also be hard to distinguish from noise
  ([[canon-kahneman-sibony-sunstein-noise]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-06 — Naturalistic Decision Making Association talk, Gary Klein)*
  Klein's own firefighter field study *is* the practitioner voice. His team interviewed 26 experts
  averaging ~23 years on the job, and *"what they told us was we never make decisions… you just look
  at a situation and you know what to do."* That **created two mysteries**: how could they be so
  confident the *first* option would work, and how do you evaluate an option *"except by comparing
  it to others"* when they insisted they never compared at all? The answer was pattern recognition
  built on decades of cases — and Klein is blunt about how durable this is: RPD *"accounts for
  probably 90% of the decisions people make in tough situations… and a much higher percentage in
  routine situations."* The operator's discipline is knowing *which* domains earn that trust: it
  only shows up where experience is real. As Klein quips about a lab researcher who gave subjects
  *"10 hours of training"* — *"10 hours as opposed to 23 years, that's not even close."*

- **Researched layer** *(updated 2026-06-06)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** RPD is **pattern-match → single mental simulation →
    execute**, not weigh-and-compare. Klein on the firefighters: *"there would be a pattern matching
    process… here's a prototype of what this situation has been like in the past, here are the cues
    to watch, here's what to expect is going to happen next… and here's a set of actions that are
    likely to be successful"* (NDM Association). They evaluate the *one* option by **mental
    simulation** — in his words, *"in the old days of popping a DVD into a DVD player they mentally
    simulated each action; if the action they thought of initially would work then they carried it
    out; if it almost worked they would improve it; and if it didn't work they would say what else
    do I have in my repertoire"* (NDM Association).
  - **Real application (mined):** the rock-fishing case study makes the mechanism vivid. ~10
    Australian rock-fishers die yearly; the government prescribed life vests and weather forecasts,
    but Klein notes those *"solutions were not particularly valuable"* — experts didn't want the
    regional forecast, they wanted to know *"which way was the wind blowing and which way were the
    waves running."* The fatal novice error was **swimming back to the rock ledge** after being
    swept in (*"your solid base of support was now a hazard"*); experts had pre-decided to swim
    toward a sandy beach. It's RPD as **tacit risk-pattern**: experts ask *"what can go wrong here
    and where are the tough decisions,"* not the surface features a checklist captures.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** RPD as written is an *individual* story, but
    Klein's own program flags its frontier at the **team and organizational** level. He lists
    **common ground** and *"flexecution"* (revising plan *and* goals mid-execution) as macrocognition
    problems beyond single-operator recognition, and says NDM's future *"will likely expand to
    include issues such as wicked problems and team and organizational dynamics"* (NDM Association) —
    i.e., the model is strongest exactly where the book set it (one expert, regular domain) and
    thinnest where modern ops actually live. And in the TEDx talk, Klein warns the tidy "connect the
    dots" framing of insight *"simplifies and trivializes"* the real work, which is filtering noise
    before any pattern is even visible. (Reddit pull for this cluster returned empty; no practitioner
