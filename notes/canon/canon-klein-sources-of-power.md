# Note · Klein — *Sources of Power*

> Canon · Cluster: Behavioral Design & Decision Science · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **E6** (Crisis Leadership)

- **Source:** Gary Klein, *Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions* (1998)
- **Core claim:** In **high-stakes, time-pressured** settings, experts **don't weigh pros and cons** —
  they pattern-match. This is the pro-intuition counterweight to Kahneman's "distrust System 1."
- **Framework — Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD):**
  - The expert **recognizes a pattern** from experience → **mentally simulates a single course of
    action** → if it works in the head, **executes**; only generates alternatives if the first option
    fails simulation.
  - This is why **experience matters** and why **expertise often can't be articulated** — it's tacit
    pattern-matching built from thousands of cases (firefighters, ICU nurses, military commanders).
  - Org implication: some decisions need analysis; **much of the time you should trust experienced
    operators** and give them room to act on recognition.
- **Applies when:** time-critical, recurring, feedback-rich domains where deep expertise exists —
  incident response, operations, clinical/field judgment.
- **Breaks when:** RPD is **only as good as the pattern library** — in **low-validity** environments
  (noisy, slow/no feedback, non-recurring) the same confident intuition is *exactly* the System-1
  trap Kahneman warns of ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]). Kahneman & Klein's joint
  "adversarial collaboration" resolved it: trust expert intuition **only** where the environment is
  regular *and* the expert had repeated feedback to learn from. Misread the domain and recognition
  becomes overconfident bias; it can also be hard to distinguish from noise
  ([[canon-kahneman-sibony-sunstein-noise]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — Lootok/TEDx transcript corpus; reddit pull empty — flagged for re-pull)*
  Klein's own field interviews *are* the practitioner voice here: firefighters told his team
  *"we don't look at any comparison between options… we don't make decisions"* (Lootok) — which
  staggered the researchers, because for the firefighters a "decision" *meant* comparing options
  and they simply weren't doing it. The intuition is real ("oh, this is one of those situations…
  I know how to handle it"), and replication studies put it at **90–95% of real decisions, even
  tough ones.** But the operator's discipline is knowing *which* domains earn that trust and
  watching for **expectancy violations** — the cue that *"maybe they've got the wrong pattern and
  they need to rethink it."* Grove's task-relevant maturity is the management hook
  ([[canon-grove-high-output-management]]).
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)* *(corpus thin — reddit pull empty, flagged for re-pull)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** RPD is **pattern-match → single mental simulation →
    execute**, not weigh-and-compare. Klein: firefighters *"were using their experience to size up
    the situation, basically relying on patterns they'd built up over 10, 15, 20 years… in a very
    short period of time make a pattern match"* (Lootok). They don't evaluate by comparison — they
    evaluate the *one* option by **mental simulation**: *"like popping a DVD into a player, doing it
    forward — how is this going to play out if I initiate this action?… if they like what they see
    they're ready to put it into play; if they almost like it they improve it; if they don't like it
    at all, what else are my options?"* (Lootok).
  - **Real application (mined):** the firefighting field studies — the researchers expected experts
    to compare "maybe two, possibly three" options and were *"staggered"* when commanders said they
    compared **none**. Klein notes this was missed for decades because lab research used *"college
    sophomores performing tasks they'd never seen before… so the expertise part of the equation had
    been ignored"* (Lootok) — a direct mechanism-level explanation for why RPD only shows up in
    feedback-rich, real-world expertise.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** RPD is an *individual* story; the corpus surfaces
    its failure mode at the **team** level. Klein: *"that makes it much nastier… that's where we see
    a lot of failures — at the team level, not the individual level"* — when operators haven't
    *"worked together,"* shared cues get misinterpreted and **common ground breaks down** *"until
    it's too late."* The fix is mundane: practice together, and *"pick up the early signs that common
    ground is eroding"* (Lootok). And from the TEDx talk, Klein warns the tidy "connect the dots"
    framing *"simplifies and trivializes"* — most of the work is **filtering noise** before any
    pattern is even visible. (Reddit practitioner threads — r/sysadmin, r/emergencymedicine — were
    not in this pull; flagged for re-pull.)
