# Note · Bungay — *The Art of Action*

> Canon · Cluster: Strategy · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **Course 04**, **E5** (Transformation)

- **Source:** Stephen Bungay, *The Art of Action* (2011)
- **Core claim:** The gap between plans and outcomes is structural, not a discipline problem. The
  answer is **not more detailed plans** but clearer *intent* communicated at every level.
- **Framework — the three gaps:**
  1. **Knowledge gap** — what we know vs. reality. (Close by: don't over-plan; decide what matters.)
  2. **Alignment gap** — what we intend vs. what people understand. (Close by: communicate intent +
     have people *back-brief* it.)
  3. **Effects gap** — what we expect vs. what actually happens. (Close by: give people freedom to
     adjust *how*, within the boundaries of *what* and *why*.)
  - Rooted in von Moltke / Prussian doctrine: *no plan survives contact with the enemy* →
    **mission command**: tell people what to achieve and why, not how.
- **Applies when:** executing strategy across an org where front-line conditions differ from HQ's
  model — i.e., almost always. The bridge from a good kernel ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]) to
  actual results.
- **Breaks when:** mission command **requires competence + shared context** — empowerment without
  it produces confident error (the same precondition Marquet names,
  [[canon-marquet-turn-the-ship-around]]). In low-trust or high-compliance environments
  (safety-critical, regulated) "freedom in the how" can be exactly wrong. And intent only aligns
  action if the *diagnosis* behind it was right.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers, r/ExperiencedDevs) — engineers
  describe this as the difference between a manager who assigns tickets vs. one who explains the
  goal and lets the team route — the latter scales, the former bottlenecks.

### Rep
Take a directive you recently gave (or received). Rewrite it as intent: the *what* + the *why*,
with the *how* left open. Notice how much "how" you were over-specifying.
