# Note · Kotter — *Leading Change*

> Canon · Cluster: Culture & Change · Maps to **E5** (Transformation & Change Management)

- **Source:** John Kotter, *Leading Change* (1996)
- **Core claim:** Change fails for **predictable reasons** — and it fails on *emotion*, not logic.
  You need both the analytical argument and the emotional case.
- **Framework — the eight steps:**
  1. Create a sense of **urgency**.
  2. Build a **guiding coalition**.
  3. Form a **strategic vision** (and strategy).
  4. **Enlist** a volunteer army (communicate the vision relentlessly).
  5. **Remove barriers** / enable action.
  6. Generate **short-term wins**.
  7. **Sustain acceleration** — don't let up.
  8. **Institute** change (anchor it in culture).
  - The two classic killers: **insufficient urgency** at the start, and **declaring victory too
    early** (step 7).
- **Applies when:** leading a large, deliberate organizational transformation that needs broad
  buy-in over a long horizon.
- **Breaks when:** it's a **linear, top-down, big-bang** model — it fits slow industrial change far
  better than continuous, iterative, or bottom-up change, where Heath's "shrink the change" and
  Switch's environment-design beat an 8-step program ([[canon-heath-switch]]). "Create urgency" is
  routinely faked into *manufactured crisis*, which breeds cynicism. And step 8 (anchor in culture)
  collides with Schein's truth that you can't simply *install* assumptions
  ([[canon-schein-organizational-culture]]) — the model under-prices how slow that last step is.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/managers + Kotter-model transcripts)*
  The corpus confirms Kotter's emotion-over-logic thesis from the *failure* side. r/managers'
  "**The 'silent resignation' nobody notices**" (9,404↑) names the exact mechanism behind a stalled
  coalition: a great employee "stops pushing… No more bold ideas," and "**it usually happens after
  too many ignored ideas, decisions made without them or endless urgent work that kills anything
  meaningful.**" That is a step-6 failure — no visible wins, no progress, so the volunteer army
  starves. "**Got them a raise. They used it to quit.**" (2,923↑) shows the same: an analytical fix
  (comp correction) with no emotional engagement just funds the exit. Kotter International itself
  reframes the model around this: *"there's so much research that shows motivation really comes from
  progress… the more success we have along the way, the more momentum we build"* — short-term wins
  aren't a checkbox, they're the fuel.
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** the model's engine is **urgency built on feeling, not data**.
    The animated summary states it flatly: *"you can't appeal to people with data and facts alone you
    must also account for how people feel… this step is all about igniting the hearts and minds of
    your people"* (BigIdeasGrowingMinds). EPM adds the operational threshold Kotter set —
    *"a minimum of 75 percent of management needs to back an initiative… for it to be successful"* —
    so urgency is **shared** belief, not an announcement.
  - **Real application (mined):** the corpus' clearest live case is the **inverse** of step 6. In
    r/managers' "silent resignation" thread (9,404↑) a high performer goes quiet *"after too many
    ignored ideas… and endless urgent work that kills anything meaningful"* — i.e., all manufactured
    urgency, zero short-term wins. The fix Kotter prescribes (step 4 enlist + step 6 wins) is exactly
    what the manager describes missing: people who *"want to help make this change a reality,"* not
    *"somebody else should do it"* (Kotter International).
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** practitioners flag the model's **top-down blind
    spot**. EPM's own teardown lists the disadvantages: *"it's a top-down model so you can miss
    potential opportunities because not everyone is involved in the co-creation of the vision… and
    while the model is really good for initiating change it doesn't help you as much when it comes to
    sustaining change."* Kotter International quietly concedes this, recasting the "8 steps" as
    *"eight accelerators… not a linear process"* run in parallel — an admission the original big-bang
    sequencing was too rigid.
