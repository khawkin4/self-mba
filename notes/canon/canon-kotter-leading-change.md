# Note · Kotter — *Leading Change*

> Canon · Cluster: Culture & Change · Maps to **E5** (Transformation & Change Management)

- **Source:** John Kotter, *Leading Change* (1996)
- **Core claim:** Change fails for **predictable reasons** — and it fails on *emotion*, not logic.
  You need both the analytical argument and the emotional case.
- **Framework — the eight steps:**
  1. Create a sense of **urgency**.
  2. Build a **guiding coalition**.
  3. Form a **strategic vision** (and strategy).
  4. **Enlist** a volunteer army (communicate the vision relentlessly).
  5. **Remove barriers** / enable action.
  6. Generate **short-term wins**.
  7. **Sustain acceleration** — don't let up.
  8. **Institute** change (anchor it in culture).
  - The two classic killers: **insufficient urgency** at the start, and **declaring victory too
    early** (step 7).
- **Applies when:** leading a large, deliberate organizational transformation that needs broad
  buy-in over a long horizon.
- **Breaks when:** it's a **linear, top-down, big-bang** model — it fits slow industrial change far
  better than continuous, iterative, or bottom-up change, where Heath's "shrink the change" and
  Switch's environment-design beat an 8-step program ([[canon-heath-switch]]). "Create urgency" is
  routinely faked into *manufactured crisis*, which breeds cynicism. And step 8 (anchor in culture)
  collides with Schein's truth that you can't simply *install* assumptions
  ([[canon-schein-organizational-culture]]) — the model under-prices how slow that last step is.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers) — change leaders say short-term
  wins (step 6) are what actually sustain momentum; without them the coalition starves. Logic-only
  change plans (the Rider with no Elephant) reliably stall.
