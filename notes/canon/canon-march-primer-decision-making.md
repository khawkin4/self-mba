# Note · March — *A Primer on Decision Making*

> Canon · Cluster: Behavioral Design & Decision Science · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **Course 09** (Leadership & OB)

- **Source:** James March, *A Primer on Decision Making* (1994)
- **Core claim:** Real organizational decisions look **nothing like the rational model.** Preferences
  are often **discovered through action**, not known in advance; information is gathered to *justify*
  decisions, not inform them; and the bottleneck is usually **attention, not information.**
- **Framework:**
  - **The garbage-can model** — problems, solutions, participants, and choice opportunities float
    around *independently* and collide semi-randomly. A **solution looking for a problem** is at
    least as common as the reverse.
  - **Logic of appropriateness** vs. logic of consequences — people often ask "what does someone in
    my role *do* here?" rather than "what maximizes my payoff?"
  - **Exploration vs. exploitation** — orgs must balance searching for the new against refining the
    known (March's other landmark idea).
- **Applies when:** explaining *why* organizations adopt solutions that don't fit the problem, why
  more data doesn't yield better choices, and why timing/attention drive outcomes.
- **Breaks when:** the garbage-can lens is **descriptively brilliant but prescriptively thin** — it
  explains the mess far better than it tells you what to *do* in it (it can even excuse fatalism:
  "it's all random"). Pushed too far it understates that *some* orgs make genuinely rational,
  high-quality decisions. Best paired with a prescriptive layer — decision hygiene
  ([[canon-kahneman-sibony-sunstein-noise]]) or Allison's three models
  ([[canon-allison-essence-of-decision]]) — to turn the diagnosis into action.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/managers, org-behavior threads) — leaders
  recognize "a solution looking for a problem" instantly (every pet initiative ever). The usable
  move: manage **attention and timing**, since that — not information — is the scarce resource.
  Pfeffer's politics is the power-layer cousin ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]).
