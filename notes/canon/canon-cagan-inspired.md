# Note · Cagan — *Inspired*

> Canon · Cluster: Product Thinking & Innovation · Maps to **Course 10** (Entrepreneurship), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation)

- **Source:** Marty Cagan, *Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love* (2008; rev. 2017)
- **Core claim:** How the best tech companies actually build product — and the central move is
  separating **product discovery** (figuring out *what* to build) from **product delivery** (building
  it). Most features fail; PM's job is to *de-risk before* spending engineering.
- **Framework:**
  - **Discovery vs. delivery** — validate ideas *before* committing build resources.
  - **The four risks to validate:**
    1. **Value** — will customers use/buy it?
    2. **Usability** — can they figure out how?
    3. **Feasibility** — can we build it (with what we have)?
    4. **Business viability** — does it work for *our* business (legal, sales, finance, brand)?
  - **Empowered product teams vs. feature teams** — the difference is whether the team owns the
    **outcome** (a problem to solve, with discretion) or just the **output** (a roadmap of features
    to ship). Empowerment is the whole game.
- **Applies when:** building software products and deciding how to structure teams and de-risk
  ideas — the operating manual for modern product management.
- **Breaks when:** "empowered teams" **requires senior leadership to genuinely cede control** —
  bolted onto a top-down, roadmap-driven org it's aspirational theater (the same competence +
  clarity precondition as Marquet, [[canon-marquet-turn-the-ship-around]]). The four-risks discovery
  rigor can also become its own bureaucracy that *slows* obviously-right bets. And it's written from
  a well-resourced product-company context; services/agency/regulated orgs fit it awkwardly.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/ProductManagement) — PMs cite the
  feature-team-vs-empowered-team distinction as the truest thing in the book — and the hardest to
  actually get, because it's an *org-power* change, not a process change. Larson's empowered-eng
  framing rhymes ([[canon-larson-elegant-puzzle]]).

### Rep
Take a feature on your roadmap. Score it on the four risks (value / usability / feasibility /
viability). The lowest-scoring risk is what you should test *this week*, before building.
