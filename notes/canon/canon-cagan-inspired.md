# Note · Cagan — *Inspired*

> Canon · Cluster: Product Thinking & Innovation · Maps to **Course 10** (Entrepreneurship), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation)

- **Source:** Marty Cagan, *Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love* (2008; rev. 2017)
- **Core claim:** How the best tech companies actually build product — and the central move is
  separating **product discovery** (figuring out *what* to build) from **product delivery** (building
  it). Most features fail; PM's job is to *de-risk before* spending engineering.
- **Framework:**
  - **Discovery vs. delivery** — validate ideas *before* committing build resources.
  - **The four risks to validate:**
    1. **Value** — will customers use/buy it?
    2. **Usability** — can they figure out how?
    3. **Feasibility** — can we build it (with what we have)?
    4. **Business viability** — does it work for *our* business (legal, sales, finance, brand)?
  - **Empowered product teams vs. feature teams** — the difference is whether the team owns the
    **outcome** (a problem to solve, with discretion) or just the **output** (a roadmap of features
    to ship). Empowerment is the whole game.
- **Applies when:** building software products and deciding how to structure teams and de-risk
  ideas — the operating manual for modern product management.
- **Breaks when:** "empowered teams" **requires senior leadership to genuinely cede control** —
  bolted onto a top-down, roadmap-driven org it's aspirational theater (the same competence +
  clarity precondition as Marquet, [[canon-marquet-turn-the-ship-around]]). The four-risks discovery
  rigor can also become its own bureaucracy that *slows* obviously-right bets. And it's written from
  a well-resourced product-company context; services/agency/regulated orgs fit it awkwardly.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/ProductManagement + SVPG/Product Faculty transcripts corpus)*
  The corpus confirms the empowered-vs-feature-team distinction *and* shows where it breaks in
  practice — it's an org-power change, not a process one. The top r/ProductManagement coaching
  thread, "**8 lessons I keep seeing after working with 100s of PMs**" (2043↑), opens with the same
  fight Cagan picks: "**Visibility beats execution… You don't get promoted for being useful. You get
  promoted for being visible and driving outcomes**" — and pushes outcome language over output
  ("'We reduced churn by 10%' hits harder than 'we rebuilt the dashboard'"). But the recurring pain
  is that most PMs are *not actually* empowered: Cagan himself names it bluntly — a feature-team PM
  is "**really a project manager with a pretty name**… herd the cats, get everything from design into
  engineering and out" (Lean Product Meetup). The fix he found wasn't process, it was **coaching**:
  *"the reason they do good work is because they have somebody showing them how it's done"* (Product
  Faculty). Larson's empowered-eng framing rhymes ([[canon-larson-elegant-puzzle]]).

- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** Cagan's core move is splitting the two kinds of work every
    team does: *"every product team is really doing two kinds of things — they're doing discovery
    work, in other words figuring out what to build, and they're doing delivery work"* (Product
    Faculty — Marty Cagan, SVPG). The discovery "core team" is three people mapping to three of the
    four risks: *"the product owner responsible for the functionality… the lead designer responsible
    for the usability… the lead developer responsible for the feasibility — is it possible to do what
    you'd like to do"* (OpenView). They converge on the smallest product "**that people will choose to
    buy, can figure out how to use, and you can deliver when you need it.**"
  - **Real application (mined):** the corpus shows the *org* failure mode Cagan warns about. In
    "**Our lead engineer quit and the whole company went into mini-panic mode**" (545↑), an 8-year
    engineer leaves and feasibility knowledge evaporates — sales can no longer answer "can this feature
    do XX?", support can't decode errors — a live picture of a team that never distributed discovery
    ownership. Counterpoint: a feasibility "core team" of one is a single point of failure.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** practitioners report that "empowerment" rarely
    survives contact with the org. Cagan's own crusade admits most teams are "**literally not allowed
    to work the way a good team works**" (Lean Product Meetup), and the r/ProductManagement reality is
    that PMs get judged on visibility and politics ("**Nobody really knows what PMs do, not even your
    manager**"), and that "**breaking through dysfunction in orgs is a skill**" (595↑) — i.e. the book
    prescribes the destination but the day-job is the org-power fight Cagan under-weights.

### Rep
Take a feature on your roadmap. Score it on the four risks (value / usability / feasibility /
viability). The lowest-scoring risk is what you should test *this week*, before building.
