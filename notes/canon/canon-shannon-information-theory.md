# Note · Shannon — *A Mathematical Theory of Communication*

> Canon · Cluster: Information & Communication · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **E7** (Exec Presence & Comms)

- **Source:** Claude Shannon, *A Mathematical Theory of Communication* (1948)
- **Core claim:** **Information is the reduction of uncertainty.** A message is more informative the
  more *surprising* it is — and reliable communication over a noisy channel requires **redundancy**.
- **Framework:**
  - **Information = surprise** — a perfectly predictable message carries zero information; entropy
    measures uncertainty/surprise.
  - **Channel capacity** — there's a hard limit to how much information a channel can reliably carry.
  - **Noise** corrupts messages; **redundancy is error-correction, not waste** — repeating /
    re-encoding the signal is how it survives the channel.
  - Leadership translation: **most communication fails because the channel is noisy** (distraction,
    competing messages, misunderstanding). The fix is **intentional redundancy** — say the important
    thing multiple ways through multiple channels. *If you said it once, you haven't said it.*
- **Applies when:** any communication that *must* land — a strategy, a change message, a safety-
  critical instruction — across a noisy organization.
- **Breaks when:** **redundancy has a ceiling** — past a point, repetition becomes noise itself
  (everything "urgent" = nothing urgent), and over-communication breeds tune-out. The math is about
  *fidelity of transmission*, **not meaning, persuasion, or whether the idea is any good** — a
  perfectly transmitted bad message is still bad (that's Minto/Sinek/Duarte's domain,
  [[canon-minto-pyramid-principle]]). Don't over-literalize a 1948 engineering theorem into a theory
  of leadership.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-06 — "Claude Shannon Explains Information Theory" transcript, youtube.com/watch?v=1afrzErFy_k)*
  The book-specific re-pull surfaces Shannon **in his own recorded words**, and he draws the line the
  leadership translation glosses over: **the 1948 math is deliberately about transmission, not
  meaning.** *"What I realized was, to measure information you have to look at it without regard to
  meaning — that upset a lot of people, that content is irrelevant."* He defines the payload precisely:
  *"the point of sending a message is to remove uncertainty — something you didn't know, now you
  know."* The famous redundancy maxim leaders relearn — that you have to say a thing more than once
  before it lands — is just Shannon's error-correction in the wild, but the theorem only certifies the
  *bits* arrive, not that the *idea* was good. Bungay's *alignment gap* is the same redundancy idea in
  command language ([[canon-bungay-art-of-action]]).

- **Researched layer** *(updated 2026-06-06)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** Shannon's core move is to strip meaning out so information
    can be *measured* like energy. In his own recorded words: *"I wanted to try to find a way to treat
    information like a physical thing, like energy, that you could measure"* and *"to measure
    information you have to look at it without regard to meaning"* ("Claude Shannon Explains
    Information Theory", youtube.com/watch?v=1afrzErFy_k). The unit is the **bit** — one fair coin
    toss: *"a coin toss is an example of the simplest form of information… a true or false, heads or
    tails, a one or a zero, a bit."* And the boundary conditions: *"if something's completely
    predictable there's zero information, but if something's completely random you need information
    about everything."*
  - **The mechanism, verified across transcripts:** **entropy** is the *average* surprise, and the Art
    of the Problem video reframes it operationally as **the minimum number of yes/no questions you'd
    expect to ask.** Two machines emit A/B/C/D: a uniform source needs **2 questions per symbol**, but
    a skewed source (A 50%, D 25%, B/C 12.5%) needs only **1.75** — *"Machine 2 is producing less
    information because there is less uncertainty or surprise about its output"* ("Shannon's
    Information Entropy (Physical Analogy)", youtube.com/watch?v=R4OlXb9aTvQ). Shannon *"calls this
    measure of average uncertainty 'entropy,' and he uses a letter H to represent it."*
  - **Real application (mined):** the corpus grounds the theory in literal channel accounting, not
    metaphor: *"if I toss it a hundred times… I'd need a hundred bits of information, one for each
    toss, to communicate the results"* — but a rigged two-headed coin is fully predictable, so *"I
    didn't need any information from you to know the results"* (1afrzErFy_k). That is the redundancy /
    compression payoff stated as a hard count of bits, which is exactly why leaders must budget
    repetition for *uncertain* messages, not predictable ones.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** Shannon's quantity is **content-free** — *"that
    upset a lot of people, that content is irrelevant"* (1afrzErFy_k). The math certifies that the
    *bits* survive the noisy channel; it says nothing about whether the message persuades or whether
    the idea is worth transmitting. That's exactly the note's "breaks-when": don't over-literalize a
    1948 transmission theorem into a theory of meaning or leadership
    ([[canon-minto-pyramid-principle]]).
