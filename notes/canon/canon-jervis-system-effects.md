# Note · Jervis — *System Effects*

> Canon · Cluster: Systems Thinking & Complexity · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro)

- **Source:** Robert Jervis, *System Effects: Complexity in Political and Social Life* (1997)
- **Core claim:** In **interconnected systems, actions produce consequences far from where they were
  intended** — and policies designed to solve one problem routinely create others. The right posture
  is **radical humility about second- and third-order effects.**
- **Framework:**
  - **Interconnection means indirect effects dominate** — you can't change one element without
    rippling the rest; the system's behavior isn't the sum of its parts.
  - **Self-defeating / self-fulfilling dynamics** — deterrence in one arena emboldens adversaries in
    another; alliances formed *for* security can **provoke the very threat** they were meant to
    prevent (the security dilemma).
  - **The more interconnected the system, the less confident you should be** about predicting any
    intervention's consequences.
- **Applies when:** geopolitics, regulation, org-wide policy, market interventions — anywhere a
  "targeted fix" lives inside a dense web of reactions.
- **Breaks when:** the lesson — "**everything is connected, be humble**" — can curdle into
  **paralysis** ("any action might backfire, so do nothing"), which is itself a choice with system
  effects. It's strong on *why* interventions surprise us, weak on *what to do anyway*; pair it with
  a bias-to-small-reversible-bets posture ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]], Bezos's two-way doors
  [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]]) so you can act *and* stay humble. Munger's second-order
  thinking is the individual-scale version ([[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** *(researched 2026-06-05 — r/systemsthinking corpus + Meadows/Senge transcripts)*
  The corpus confirms Jervis's central warning *and* names the exact failure mode he's weak on. An
  r/systemsthinking thread — "**Feedback appreciated: systems thinking mindset tensions**" (133↑) —
  asks the practitioner's real question: *"you're so aware the Event-level solutions don't last but
  Pattern-level and Structure-level solutions are much harder to achieve that you are caught between
  the urgent but ephemeral and the slow-burn but everlasting… How do you successfully navigate this
  in the Corporate world?"* That's the paralysis trap rendered as a daily-work dilemma. The usable
  discipline stays the same: before any intervention, **ask "and then what — twice"** and assume the
  reaction you *didn't* model is the one that bites. Cousin to Perrow's complexity
  ([[canon-perrow-normal-accidents]]).
- **Researched layer** *(2026-06-05)*
  - **Canonical explanation (sourced):** the system's behavior isn't the sum of its parts, and
    indirect effects dominate — *"a change in one area will cause ripples of change and reactions
    elsewhere, often in unexpected ways… feedback loops are not really loops. We have to consider
    the many balancing and reinforcing influences that continually affect the system"* (Miles
    MacFarlane, *The Fifth Discipline in Three Minutes*). Senge's version of Jervis's humility:
    *"the structure of the system influences the behavior more often than not… while it's easier to
    find someone or something to blame, the problem usually lies in how the game or system was set
    up in the first place"* (Readitfor.me).
  - **Real application (mined):** the **beer game / bullwhip effect** is system effects you can
    watch happen. Four players (retailer → wholesaler → distributor → factory) each make a locally
    rational ordering decision *"in the absence of information exchange and cooperation"*; a small
    demand blip at the retailer amplifies into wild over- and under-ordering upstream —
    *"supply chain delays or increases in demands can cause all kinds of havoc… which we're
    currently experiencing in 2021, and a little bit in 2020 with the initial pandemic with toilet
    paper"* (Niko J). No villain, just structure: exactly Jervis's point that the consequence lands
    far from where the action was taken.
  - **Where the corpus pushes back on the book:** practitioners report Jervis's humility curdling
    into the paralysis he doesn't resolve — the 133↑ "mindset tensions" thread is a person *stuck*
    between knowing event-level fixes don't last and structure-level fixes being too slow. Meadows
    supplies the missing "what to do anyway": intervene at the highest-leverage point you can
    actually reach (*"she lists these leverage points… least effective to most effective,"* paradigm
    shift at the top, parameter tweaks at the bottom — Ashley Hodgson), and don't waste effort on
    *"small parameter adjustments, weak negative feedback loops"* that are *"way too feeble"* (Global
    Change Management). Humility about effects, not paralysis about action.
