# Note · Jervis — *System Effects*

> Canon · Cluster: Systems Thinking & Complexity · Maps to **Course 07** (Data & Decision-Making), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro)

- **Source:** Robert Jervis, *System Effects: Complexity in Political and Social Life* (1997)
- **Core claim:** In **interconnected systems, actions produce consequences far from where they were
  intended** — and policies designed to solve one problem routinely create others. The right posture
  is **radical humility about second- and third-order effects.**
- **Framework:**
  - **Interconnection means indirect effects dominate** — you can't change one element without
    rippling the rest; the system's behavior isn't the sum of its parts.
  - **Self-defeating / self-fulfilling dynamics** — deterrence in one arena emboldens adversaries in
    another; alliances formed *for* security can **provoke the very threat** they were meant to
    prevent (the security dilemma).
  - **The more interconnected the system, the less confident you should be** about predicting any
    intervention's consequences.
- **Applies when:** geopolitics, regulation, org-wide policy, market interventions — anywhere a
  "targeted fix" lives inside a dense web of reactions.
- **Breaks when:** the lesson — "**everything is connected, be humble**" — can curdle into
  **paralysis** ("any action might backfire, so do nothing"), which is itself a choice with system
  effects. It's strong on *why* interventions surprise us, weak on *what to do anyway*; pair it with
  a bias-to-small-reversible-bets posture ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]], Bezos's two-way doors
  [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]]) so you can act *and* stay humble. Munger's second-order
  thinking is the individual-scale version ([[canon-munger-poor-charlies-almanack]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/geopolitics, policy threads) — the usable
  discipline: before any intervention, **ask "and then what — twice"** and assume the reaction you
  *didn't* model is the one that bites. Cousin to Perrow's complexity
  ([[canon-perrow-normal-accidents]]).
