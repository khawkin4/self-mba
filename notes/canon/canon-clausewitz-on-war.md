# Note · Clausewitz — *On War*

> Canon · Cluster: History & Judgment · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy), **E6** (Crisis Leadership)

- **Source:** Carl von Clausewitz, *On War* (1832, posthumous)
- **Core claim:** War — and by extension any competitive contest — is defined by **friction,
  uncertainty, exhaustion, and the supremacy of purpose over method.** Strategy is judgment under
  irreducible uncertainty, not calculation.
- **Framework — the durable concepts:**
  - **Friction** — "everything in war is simple, but the simplest thing is difficult"; plans degrade
    on contact with reality (the lineage to von Moltke and Bungay,
    [[canon-bungay-art-of-action]]).
  - **Fog of war** — uncertainty is **irreducible**, not a problem to be fully solved; act despite it.
  - **The culminating point** — every advance reaches a point where your strength is spent and pushing
    further becomes dangerous (overreach).
  - **"War is politics by other means"** — the **strategic purpose must govern** every operational
    choice; tactics serve policy, never the reverse.
  - **The trinity** — the interplay of **rational calculation, chance, and passion/emotion**.
- **Applies when:** competitive strategy, crisis leadership, any contest where uncertainty and
  exhaustion — not just the plan — determine the outcome.
- **Breaks when:** the **war metaphor can mislead in business** — most commerce is **positive-sum and
  repeated** (co-opetition, [[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]), not a zero-sum fight to
  destroy an enemy; "crush the competitor" thinking misses complementors, customers, and the larger
  game. *On War* is also famously **unfinished, abstract, and contradiction-laden** — easy to
  cherry-pick to justify whatever you already wanted. Use the *concepts* (friction, culminating
  point, purpose-over-method), not the militarism.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/strategy, *Acquired*) — the two ideas
  operators reuse most: the **culminating point** (know when an advance has over-extended) and
  **purpose must govern action** (don't let tactics drift from the strategic aim). Thucydides is the
  case study in overreach ([[canon-thucydides-peloponnesian-war]]).
