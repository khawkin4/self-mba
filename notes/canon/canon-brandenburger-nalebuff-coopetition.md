# Note · Brandenburger & Nalebuff — *Co-opetition*

> Canon · Cluster: Game Theory & Strategic Interaction · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy)

- **Source:** Adam Brandenburger & Barry Nalebuff, *Co-opetition* (1996)
- **Core claim:** Business is **not zero-sum.** You simultaneously **compete and cooperate** within a
  **Value Net**, and the highest-leverage move is often to **change the game itself**, not to win the
  existing one.
- **Framework:**
  - **The Value Net** — four players around you: **customers, suppliers, competitors, and
    complementors** (someone whose product makes yours more valuable — hot dogs & buns, hardware &
    software). Most strategy ignores the complementor axis.
  - **PARTS** — the five levers to change the game: **P**layers, **A**dded value, **R**ules,
    **T**actics, **S**cope.
  - Key move: **adding/strengthening a complementor** can beat fighting a competitor — grow the pie
    before fighting over slices.
- **Applies when:** mapping an ecosystem and looking for non-obvious allies — platform plays,
  standards, bundling, partnerships — rather than head-to-head rivalry.
- **Breaks when:** "co-opete" relationships are **unstable** — today's complementor is tomorrow's
  competitor once they **envelop** your space (the platform tension in
  [[canon-cusumano-business-of-platforms]]). "Change the game" is easy to say, hard to do — most
  firms lack the market power to rewrite rules. And the friendly "grow the pie" framing can lull you
  into underrating a partner who's quietly accumulating bargaining power
  ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]).
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/strategy, *Acquired*) — the durable
  insight: **ask "who gains from my success?"** and invest in complementors — but watch for the day
  the complementor's incentives flip.
