# Note · Grove — *Only the Paranoid Survive*

> Canon · Cluster: Landscape & Competitive Awareness · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro)

- **Source:** Andrew Grove, *Only the Paranoid Survive* (1996)
- **Core claim:** **Strategic inflection points** — moments when a **10X force** hits and the old
  rules stop working — are the make-or-break events of a business, and they're hardest to see *from
  the inside*.
- **Framework:**
  - A **10X change** in any of the competitive forces (a technology, a regulation, a customer
    behavior, a competitor) breaks the existing strategy. (Grove's lived case: memory chips →
    microprocessors at Intel.)
  - The signals are **noisy**; your existing success makes you dismiss the threat; **senior people
    are usually last to see it** because they're most invested in the current model.
  - The signal to trust: **persistent dissonance** between what you observe and what your strategy
    assumes. Listen to the "Cassandras" (often middle managers / field staff who see it first).
- **Applies when:** something feels off — your numbers are fine but a new force is growing
  non-linearly — and you must decide whether it's noise or an inflection point.
- **Breaks when:** "be paranoid" is **un-falsifiable** — every change looks like a possible
  inflection point, and over-reacting to noise (betting the company on a false 10X) is as fatal as
  missing a real one. The framework is brilliant *in hindsight* but gives no clean test to
  distinguish the real inflection from the scare in real time. Grove's own answer — "experiment,
  let a thousand flowers bloom, then concentrate" — is costly and slow.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** (fill via `research` → r/strategy, *Acquired* Intel episode) — the
  irony cited often: Grove preached this, yet Intel later missed mobile and AI inflection points —
  proof that *knowing* the framework doesn't immunize you.
