# Note · Tradeweb equities, ETFs & retail — the fixed-income RFQ playbook, exported to ETFs and turned into a data business

> Practitioner / market-structure note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — adjacency moves, platform positioning), **Course 07** (Data, Analytics & Decision-Making — self-reported metrics, base rates), **Course 06** (Operations — automation, straight-through processing), **Course 11** (Global Strategy & Macro — Europe/US/Asia sequencing), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation) · Written 2026-10-24 · Companion to [[tradeweb-ai-price]] and [[tradeweb-a2a-information-leakage]]

- **Source:** Tradeweb newsroom, **36 items** tagged Equities / Retail Market / Data (plus one Tradeweb-tagged item), **Jan 14 2019 – Mar 11 2026**: 18 "In the News" reprints, 15 blogs, 3 news releases. Raw file: `_ingest/raw/tradeweb/bucket-equities.jsonl`. Almost all of it is ETF RFQ. Cash equities, options, ADRs and converts get one or two items each. Retail/wealth gets two. **Dealerweb (wholesale) does not appear at all.**
- **Core claim:** Tradeweb took the multi-dealer request-for-quote (RFQ) protocol it built for Treasuries in 1998 and applied it to ETF blocks: Europe 2012, US 2016, Asia 2018. Tradeweb says its US ETF platform has done **$3.8 trillion** since launch, including a record **$930 billion in 2025**, across "close to 200 clients". The sales pitch is price improvement versus the exchange plus automation (AiEX). The strategic story is that ETFs connect Tradeweb's bond liquidity to equity desks, and that this flow now supports a data product (**iNAV**). All the numbers are self-reported, in shifting units, and almost never compared with a competitor.

## 2. The narrative, and how it shifted

1. **2019 — "MiFID II made us."** Every early piece credits regulation: trade reporting showed buyers that OTC ETF liquidity existed, and best-execution rules rewarded an auditable multi-quote record. Bruni: "We are the transparency machine." Tradeweb pitches equities RFQ as *compliance-native*.
2. **2019 — "RFQ isn't toxic."** The fight in cash equities was about information leakage. Tradeweb's answer: the protocol is **fully disclosed** (dealers know who you are, as on the phone), clients can request two-way markets (RFM) to hide direction, and an unnamed third-party study found reversion no worse than SIs. Compare [[tradeweb-a2a-information-leakage]]. In 2026, for credit, Tradeweb argues that *broadcast* all-to-all RFQ leaks, and sells targeted selection (SNAP+). Same position, seven years apart: **disclosed, selective RFQ good; blasting bad.**
3. **2020 — "ETFs passed the test."** COVID is the turning point. Fixed-income ETFs traded while cash bonds froze, and Tradeweb used BlackRock and Flow Traders to make that case. From here the story becomes **"ETFs are the liquidity layer for bonds"**, which plays to Tradeweb's home asset class.
4. **2021–22 — "Cross-asset, one risk book."** Credit ETF RFQs go to credit desks and Treasury ETF RFQs to Treasury desks. "That ETF desk sat on the equity floor" five years earlier. Tradeweb is the venue where FI dealers and FI buy-side already sit, so it gains when ETF pricing moves to bond desks.
5. **2022–24 — "Automate the flow."** AiEX becomes the headline metric, ESG switch trades and then bitcoin ETFs get used as topical hooks, and Asia (CONNEQTOR, AiEX Asia, iNAV APAC) becomes the growth market.
6. **2023–26 — "We are a data source."** iNAV (2023) and its 2026 relaunch say outright that "in fixed income, Tradeweb **is** the source." ETF flow feeds a price product, and that product feeds portfolio-trading tickets. Same pattern as [[tradeweb-ai-price]].
7. **2025–26 — "24/7, tokenized, agentic."** The 10-year blog opens with tokenization, agentic AI and quantum computing before getting to ETFs. Fnality fits that ambition. The ETF business is now pitched as the base for that future rather than as a product line in its own right.

**Retail/wealth thread (thin):** retail shows up as (a) wealth managers, robo-advisers, RIAs and private-bank model portfolios using the *institutional* ETF platform, and (b) **Tradeweb Direct** in munis (Investortools, 2019; institutional credit clients reaching "retail liquidity", Meatto 2019). The bucket has nothing on Tradeweb Direct's retail Treasuries/CDs, advisor platform economics, or Dealerweb.

## 3. What the claims don't prove

1. **Units shift between articles.** European volume is in € (2018–21, Jul 2022) or $ (2019 ADV, 2022 annual). AiEX is reported as a share of "transactions", "tickets", "trades", "orders" and "volume". Growth is reported as YoY, average annual or "annualized". Comparing across years is unreliable without the monthly data reports.
2. **The March 2020 spike is reported five different ways.** Global +225.6% (Jun 2020); US +242% / EU +190.6% (Feb 2022); US ADV +226% / EU +242% (May 2022); US +242% / EU +204% (Oct 2022); US +242% (Mar 2026). Most likely, ADV and total volume are being mixed and US/EU labels swapped. Use none of them without the source table.
3. **Growth claims contradict each other.** "ETF volume doubled annually for five years in a row" (Oct 2022) can't be squared with Europe at **32% annualized** over five years (Feb 2023) or **51%/59%** average annual growth (Aug 2024) unless each refers to a different region, start date or measure. None of them says which.
4. **AiEX share is mostly small tickets.** ~75% of European ETF *trades* but 18.4% of *volume* means the average AiEX ticket is about **1/13** the size of a manual one (calculation in the Rep). "Most trades are automated" is true by count and false by risk. The 2026 US figure (49% of *orders*) has the same problem.
5. **Price improvement is measured against a benchmark Tradeweb chose.** 4.2 bp (vs "the order book", 2012–18) and 2.2 bp (vs on-exchange BBO, 2023) for European ETFs, and 13¢ vs NBBO for ADRs. For blocks hundreds of times the displayed size, the touch is not a price you could actually get, so beating it is the minimum test. The comparison that matters is versus a VWAP/arrival benchmark or a competing venue (e.g. Bloomberg RFQE — context, not from Tradeweb — or exchange RFQs such as CONNEQTOR), and none is published. The leakage study in §2 is "third-party" but unnamed and unpublished.
6. **Survivorship in the anecdotes.** "Customer hit rate did not really go down" in March 2020, "a single $50m AiEX trade", "78 clients over $100m" — the denominators are missing (RFQs sent, rejections, clients who stopped using the platform).
7. **Market share is never stated.** No item gives Tradeweb's share of US or European ETF volume or of off-exchange ETF block flow. $930bn sounds large; for context, not from Tradeweb, US-listed ETFs trade trillions of dollars a *month* on exchange, so the platform is a block-and-institutional niche, not the main ETF market.
8. **Third-party enthusiasm comes from the supply side.** BlackRock (issuer), Flow Traders, Citadel Securities and Jane Street (market makers) and TSE (venue partner) all benefit from more ETF RFQ. The only buy-side voice is Schwab's head of trading, on ADRs.
9. **iNAV adoption is one name deep.** BlackRock Europe at launch, APAC "this year" (2024), and in 2026 unnamed "established issuers". No count of funds or issuers is given.

## 4. Strategic read *(inference)*

- **Adjacency, done in the right order.** Tradeweb didn't take on exchanges in single stocks. It went into the part of equities that behaves like fixed income: **blocks far larger than displayed size, a few principal market makers, best-ex audit needs**. ETFs first, then converts, ADRs, options and cash-equity blocks, each smaller. The 2022 split (ETFs record, converts +3%) suggests the RFQ playbook works best where an instrument can be **priced off a hedgeable underlying**, which is what ETFs and ADRs are.
- **Fixed-income ETFs are the bridge.** FI ETFs account for 44% of US notional, 69% of $100m+ trades, 46% of trading in the COVID month, and appear in the PT ticket. Tradeweb's equities business is to a large extent a **credit business wearing an equity wrapper**. That helps (cross-sell to its credit clients and dealers) and concentrates risk: a bond-market downturn hits both lines.
- **Automation is how it locks clients in.** AiEX settings (rules by size, time release, SNAP dealer lists) live inside the client's OMS through FIX, and now inside Fidessa. Once a desk has tuned 50+ parameters, the cost of switching is reconfiguring all of that, not just finding a better price. Same mechanism as clients tuning auto-execution thresholds against Ai-Price ([[tradeweb-ai-price]] §7).
- **Partner with exchanges where they are strong.** EuroCCP (clearing), TSE CONNEQTOR (Japan market makers), ION (OMS distribution). In Asia Tradeweb aggregates rather than competes. That is coopetition: let the exchange keep clearing and the local market makers, and own the institutional client's ticket.
- **The data line is where the margin is.** iNAV turns order flow (executable bond quotes) into a licensed price that issuers display publicly. Each new issuer customer also legitimizes Tradeweb's composite as *the* fair value for bond ETFs. That makes it a reference-price play, not mainly a fee line.
- **Retail is about access to flow, not a direct-to-consumer business.** Tradeweb reaches retail through advisers, SMAs, RIAs and model portfolios, routed via institutional or wholesale workflows (Tradeweb Direct). That avoids competing with Schwab and Fidelity for end investors.

## 5. Canon lenses

- **Multi-sided platforms** ([[canon-hagiu-wright-multi-sided-platforms]], [[canon-parker-platform-revolution]]): the RFQ platform needs LPs (4 → 9 in cash equities, 6 → 22 → 33 in ETFs) before clients come, and clients before LPs will quote tight. The early numbers in this note are evidence of Tradeweb solving that chicken-and-egg problem.
- **Switching costs & counter-positioning** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): exchanges can't easily offer disclosed principal RFQ without hurting their anonymous order book. That is counter-positioning. AiEX configuration and OMS integrations are switching costs.
- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): TSE, EuroCCP and ION are complementors and also competitors (CONNEQTOR is itself an RFQ venue). Map the value net before calling any of them a partner.
- **Information goods** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): iNAV has a high fixed cost and near-zero marginal cost, and Tradeweb gives the public price away free while selling the bid/mid/ask feed. That is versioning, straight from the textbook.
- **Information asymmetry** ([[canon-akerlof-market-for-lemons]]): the 2019 toxicity debate is an adverse-selection problem. Disclosure (dealers know the client's name) is how Tradeweb keeps market makers quoting tight to buy-side clients who may know more.
- **Measurement** ([[canon-kahneman-sibony-sunstein-noise]] / Course 07): count-based vs volume-based automation shares (§3.4) is a standard trap — the denominator is the claim.

## 6. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For:** ETF block traders do use multi-dealer RFQ as their default for size; the creation/redemption mechanism means a market maker can price far more than screen depth, and RFQ simply makes that competition explicit and auditable. COVID and April 2025 are real stress tests the platform got through with volume *up*.
- **Against:** many US ETF block desks still work orders algorithmically against the exchange or go via a single risk-pricing broker; RFQ reveals size and side (RFQ) or size (RFM) to several firms that also make the lit market. Tradeweb's 2019 leakage study has never been published, and Bloomberg and exchange RFQ venues compete directly (context, not from Tradeweb) — none of which appears in Tradeweb's newsroom.
- **Both:** the honest buy-side test is to split your own ETF fills by protocol, size bucket and FI-vs-equity underlying, then measure against arrival price and against iNAV/NAV at execution, not against the touch.

### Rep
Using only numbers in this note:

(a) **Automation by count vs by size.** In 2022, AiEX was "~75%" of European ETF trades but 18.4% of European ETF volume. What is the average AiEX ticket size relative to a non-AiEX ticket? *(Hint: AiEX avg ∝ 0.184/0.75; manual avg ∝ 0.816/0.25.)*

(b) **Growth deceleration.** US ETF volume was $628bn in 2022 (+236% vs 2020) and $930bn in 2025. Back out 2020 volume, then compute the 2020→22 and 2022→25 compound annual growth rates.

(c) **Fill in the gap.** Cumulative US volume is $3.8tn since 2016. Add 2018 ($106.9bn), 2020 (from b), 2021 (from the +107%), 2022 and 2025. Assuming 2016, 2017 and 2019 are small, roughly how much must 2023 + 2024 together have been? Is that consistent with ">$1tn global ETF volume in Q1–Q3 2024"?

(d) **Decide.** Tradeweb claims 2.2 bp price improvement vs on-exchange BBO on European ETFs. Apply it to €700bn of annual volume. One paragraph: why this is an upper bound on client savings, and what benchmark you'd demand before telling your CIO the platform "saves" that much.

*Answers:* (a) 0.245 vs 3.264 → AiEX tickets are about **1/13** the size. (b) 2020 ≈ 628/3.36 ≈ **$187bn**; 2020→22 ≈ **83%/yr**; 2022→25 = (930/628)^(1/3) − 1 ≈ **14%/yr**. (c) 2021 ≈ 628/2.07 ≈ $303bn; known years ≈ 107 + 187 + 303 + 628 + 930 ≈ $2.16tn, leaving **≈ $1.6tn for 2023–24 (~$0.8tn/yr)**. That is plausible next to >$1tn *global* in nine months of 2024. (d) 700bn × 0.00022 ≈ **€154m/yr** — measured against a touch that block sizes could never have hit.
