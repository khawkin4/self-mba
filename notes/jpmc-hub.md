# Note · JPMorganChase hub — a fortress dealer that argues its case in public: capital rules, nonbanks and tokens are the fight; succession, AI and the venues are the things it talks about least

> Hub / company-study note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy), **Course 11** (Global Strategy, Macro & Responsible Business), **Course 09** (Leadership & OB), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance), **E2** (Capital Allocation), **E4** (Governance), **E8** (Investor Relations), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation) · Written 2026-10-25 · Entry point for the nine JPMorganChase notes

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/jpmc/` holds **165 records, ~527,000 words** (index: `index.tsv`; texts: `articles.jsonl`; read-first digest: `DIGEST.md`). They cover:
  - Dimon's shareholder letters for AR2019–AR2025, read in full.
  - The line-of-business and COO letters for the same years.
  - Ten earnings calls: 4Q19 to 4Q25 Q4s, plus 1Q23, 2Q26 and 3Q26.
  - The 2025 Investor Day transcript.
  - 140 newsroom, IR, Payments/Kinexys, Markets, technology and research items, filtered from ~1,500 candidates; the newsroom holds 468 press releases.

  Skipped: consumer, branch and philanthropy items, awards-only posts, quantum minutiae and research outlooks. Investor Day transcripts for 2019–2024 are no longer hosted. JPM mentions in the Tradeweb corpus are used where marked [TW]. **This study is about JPMC as a company.** J.P. Morgan *research* on software and AI is covered separately in [[jpm-software-sector-spotlight-2026]].
- **Core claim:** JPMorganChase runs one model: **fortress balance sheet + complete client franchise + scale technology**, and it argues for that model in public more than any peer. Dimon's letters are policy essays. From 2019 to 2026 the business shifted three ways:
  - **Markets growth moved to financing and Prime.** Record $35.8B Markets revenue in 2025, while FICC share sits below pre-COVID.
  - **Payments and Kinexys became the defence of the deposit** against stablecoins.
  - **Excess capital built against the Basel endgame is now being redeployed** into financing, Apple Card and a $1.5T security initiative, not buybacks.

  The stories told most loudly are regulation and competition. Those told least are **succession** (a co-president race with no date), **AI returns** (a ~$19.8B tech budget, AI benefits "aren't observable") and **venues** (zero mentions of Tradeweb, MarketAxess or Trumid, despite JPM playing nine roles toward them).

## 3. Five patterns across the notes

1. **Doctrine over targets.** On capital ("output, not an input"), tech ("table stakes"), AI ("trust me") and succession ("intent"), management answers with principles and declines numbers. Analysts' only quantitative win was the 3Q26 AI-spend correction ([[jpmc-outside-voices]]).
2. **The balance sheet is the moat, and regulation is its price.** Markets growth (financing, Prime), the deposit-token defence and the SRI all use balance sheet. That is why six years of letters fight G-SIB, SLR and the endgame ([[jpmc-capital-regulation]], [[jpmc-capital-allocation]]).
3. **Self-disruption in a separate unit, with the core economics kept.** Kinexys keeps the deposit and changes the rail. The CIB keeps the client and automates the trade. Fusion keeps the data. Each innovation protects an existing profit pool ([[jpmc-kinexys-deposit-tokens]], [[jpmc-markets-venues]]).
4. **The competitor list tracks the threat.** It runs Big Tech and Walmart (2020) → Apple, Stripe and shadow banks (2023) → Block, Citadel Securities, Revolut, Stripe, stablecoins and "market infrastructure providers" (2025–26) ([[jpmc-mission-forces]]).
5. **Pairs everywhere, one voice on top.** Co-presidents, co-CEOs and co-heads below; Dimon alone as strategist, lobbyist and allocator. The succession question is really about who inherits the voice ([[jpmc-people]], [[jpmc-succession]]).
