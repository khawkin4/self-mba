# Note · JPMorganChase capital allocation — the fortress balance sheet as an option: buybacks last, ROTCE "an output, not an input," and in 2026 the excess goes to financing, Apple Card and a $1.5T security initiative instead of buybacks

> Corporate-finance / capital-allocation note · Maps to **E2** (Capital Allocation — thinking like a CEO/CFO), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — ROTCE, cost of equity, payout), **E3** (M&A — First Republic, Apple Card), **E8** (Investor Relations — the buyback conversation) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[jpmc-capital-regulation]] and [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/jpmc/articles.jsonl`: ten earnings calls (4Q19 → 3Q26), Dimon's letters AR2019–AR2025, Barnum's capital section at the 2025 Investor Day, the 2020-06-29 capital update, the 2024-06-27 stress-test comment, the First Republic (2023-05-01), Apple Card (2026-01-07), SRI (2025-10-13) and Combs (2025-12-08) releases. **Earnings releases and 10-Ks were not read.** Figures are the ones management states on calls and in letters. "Excess capital" is JPMC's own estimate and depends on assumed rules.
- **Core claim:** JPMC states the same capital hierarchy every year: **buffer for uncertainty → organic growth → (inorganic, "always on the table") → a competitive dividend → buybacks last**. ROTCE ran at 18–23% from 2021 to 2025 against a 17% "through-the-cycle" target, which Barnum calls "an output, not an input". Excess capital was built up as insurance against a harsh Basel endgame (~$54B in Mar 2024, ~$60B in May 2025). In 2025–26 management decided that excess was too costly to hold (Dimon: "effectively earning a 4% after-tax return") and too expensive to buy back at ~3× tangible book. So it is being **redeployed organically**: Markets financing (RWA +$103B in one quarter), the Apple Card portfolio, and the **$1.5T, 10-year Security & Resiliency Initiative** with up to $10B of direct equity. Buybacks fell to ~$1B in 3Q26 and CET1 from 15.7% to 13.9%. Meanwhile expenses grew from ~$68B (2021 guide) to ~$107.5B (2026), framed as investment, not cost.

## 4. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Excess" is a model output.** It depends on assumed G-SIB, SCB and Basel outcomes ("making many assumptions around regulatory reform"). The fall from ~$60B to ~$30B in 18 months mixes deployment, rule changes and OCI losses. The calls don't split them.
2. **ROTCE above target flatters capital deployment.** 20%+ came with rate tailwinds (NII ex-Markets from ~$50B to ~$96.5B) and record Markets years. The test is ROTCE on *new* capital (financing, Apple Card, SRI), which is never disclosed.
3. **The SRI number is mostly facilitation.** $1.5T includes underwriting and lending JPM "had already planned". Only up to $10B is JPM's own equity. Comparing the headline with the buyback cut would be misleading.
4. **Expense growth is framed as investment by assertion.** Dimon: "We're not going to try to meet some expense target". Mayo's arithmetic (+$7B, +$9B a year) never gets a line-item return.
5. **The First Republic economics are partly accounting.** The $2.6B gain was a bargain-purchase gain on an FDIC-assisted deal. Dimon concedes about a third of the later earnings is just deploying excess liquidity.

## 5. Strategic read (inference — not JPMC's words)

- **Excess capital was an option, and 2026 is the exercise.** Holding $50–60B while the endgame was uncertain was insurance. When the re-proposals came in "mixed" but lighter ([[jpmc-capital-regulation]]), the option's value fell, and the cost of carry (a 4% after-tax yield) dominated. Redeploying at a ~17% target return is the obvious move. Doing it through balance-sheet-heavy Markets financing concentrates risk in hedge-fund leverage, which Barnum calls "known unknowns".
- **Buybacks are now explicitly price-sensitive.** Dimon's 2025–26 line, buy less when the stock is dear, is Buffett's rule. It contradicts the steady buyback programmes most banks run. The tell is the 3Q26 cut to ~$1B.
- **Big M&A is ruled out by rules, not appetite.** "Hard to imagine" (AR2024) sits beside a decade of complaints that the G-SIB surcharge "punishes consolidation". The firm buys distressed or partnership deals (First Republic, Apple Card) and builds the rest.
- **Contrast with Tradeweb.** Tradeweb funds growth from operating cash and buys platforms at rising prices ([[tradeweb-strategy-financials]]). JPMC funds growth by deploying regulatory capital, so its "M&A" is mostly balance sheet. Both talk about returns. Neither discloses return on the new capital.

## 6. Canon lenses

- **The CEO as capital allocator** ([[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]]): Dimon's letters, unlike Tradeweb's client letters, do argue capital allocation in public: hierarchy, excess, buyback price. Bezos would add free cash flow per share and return on incremental capital.
- **Margin of safety** ([[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]]): the fortress balance sheet is a margin of safety. Spending it near "as good as it gets" (Dimon, 2Q26) is the classic late-cycle risk.
- **Luck vs skill** ([[canon-mauboussin-success-equation]]): separate rate and volatility tailwinds from franchise share gains before crediting ROTCE to allocation skill.
- **Agency costs of free cash** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): when a firm has more capital than good projects, managers tend to grow. The SRI and the $105B+ expense base are where a skeptic would look.

## 7. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For management:** compounding tangible book value at 2× peers (Barnum's claim, Investor Day 2025), refusing to buy back at 3× book, and keeping powder dry for crises (2008, 2020, 2023) has worked. Each crisis made JPM bigger.
- **Skeptic:** a $107.5B expense base, a CET1 falling toward 13.9% while risk-on markets peak, and a $1.5T politically aligned initiative look like a firm spending at the top. Mayo's line from 2021, "you're spending the rate hikes for investments over the next 10 years.", still applies.
- **Synthesis:** the doctrine is coherent and publicly argued. Its weak point is that returns on incremental capital are never shown.

### Rep
Using only §1–§2: (a) If $40B of excess capital earns 4% after tax, what is the annual earnings drag versus deploying it at a 17% ROTCE? (b) At ~3× tangible book, a buyback earns roughly ROTCE ÷ 3 in yield terms. Compare that with Dimon's "6% or whatever the earnings yield". (c) Write a three-sentence reply to Mayo's 2021 line as Barnum in 2026, using one number from §1.

*Answers:* (a) $40B × (17% − 4%) ≈ **$5.2B a year** of forgone earnings. (b) 20% ÷ 3 ≈ 6.7%, consistent with Dimon's ~6% earnings yield. At 17% ROTCE on new capital, deploying beats buying back.
