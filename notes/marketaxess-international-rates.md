# Note · MarketAxess international, EM, rates & munis — the half of the business that kept growing, built on index inclusion, mandates and all-to-all in EM, while Treasuries stayed a $4-a-million challenger

> Practitioner / market-structure note · Maps to **Course 11** (Global Strategy, Macro & Responsible Business — index inclusion, market-access mandates, China/India/Brazil), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — challenger economics in Treasuries vs incumbency in EM), **Course 07** (Data, Analytics & Decision-Making — a target and a metric that share a word but not a definition; recast denominators), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — paying $150M for a low-fee rates business), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro for Decision-Makers), **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[marketaxess-hub]]; companions [[marketaxess-data-cp-plus]], [[marketaxess-open-trading]], [[marketaxess-ice-deal]], [[tradeweb-rates-em]]

- **Source:** MarketAxess corpus (`_ingest/raw/marketaxess/articles.jsonl`), **~70 items, Feb 2019 – Oct 2026**: IR releases on LiquidityEdge (2019), MuniBrokers (2020–21), China CFETS (2021), EM local markets (2019, 2021, 2023), India (2025), the $1T EM milestone (Dec 2025), the record Treasury day (Apr 2025), Moment (2026); 32 earnings releases; the FY19–FY25 letters; the 2021 Investor Day and Dec 2025 Goldman Sachs decks; ~35 regional research pieces and media reprints (GlobalCapital, Markets Media, Asset Servicing Times, Hubbis). **Caveats:** earnings releases are cut before the full tables, so some annual figures are derived from quarters (labelled). **Several Knowledge Center dates are wrong** in the corpus index: *Brazil's fixed income market turns electronic* cites Q1 and April 2026 data, so it is about **May 2026**; *Fed-day dynamics* (tagged 2024) analyses 2024–26, so it is **2026**; *LATAM Local Markets Outpacing…* (tagged 2020) is **2021**; *Asian local currency bonds… H1* (tagged 2021) is **2022**; *Bringing transparency to the muni marketplace* (tagged 2017) is **~2024–25**. The GS deck's revenue-component labels are scrambled in extraction. No call transcripts.
- **Core claim:** By the Dec 2025 GS deck, **"Approximately 50%"** of revenue came from *non-U.S. credit*, which grew at **~15%** a year over 2020–24 while US credit shrank **(2%)** a year. FY25 revenue outside US credit was **$406M (+10%)**, ~48% of $846.3M (derived). The engine was **EM**: volume $131B in 3Q19 → $562B in 2020 → ~$860B in 2024 → **over $1T** by Nov 2025, 30 local markets plus 100+ hard-currency markets, the only platform covering **100% of the GBI-EM** after India (Jun 2025). It was driven by **index inclusion** (China 2021, India 2025), **mandates** (Brazil's 2026 accreditation, one of four platforms) and **Open Trading**, which carries **40%+** of EM hard-currency volume (42% in 2020; 47% of MENA in 2025). But *non-U.S. credit* is not *international*: it also counts **US Treasuries, US munis and services**. The 2021 Investor Day target was **50% of revenue from international**, from 28% then. Strip out services and rates and the FY25 international-type remainder is ~31% (derived), so the target was reached by redefinition. **Treasuries** (LiquidityEdge, ~$150M, 2019) stayed a challenger: **2.0–3.7%** share and **~$4–5 per million** in fees against $127–185 in credit. **Munis** were the real share story: **2.2%** (3Q21) → record **8.7%** (3Q24), partly on a recast denominator.

**Reading the table.** Treasuries brought volume, not money. In 1Q22 a record $25.1B UST ADV produced $6.3M of commissions, about 3% of that quarter's revenue. ## 4. The machinery — why EM grew and how the metrics are built

- **Index inclusion pulls flow.** China (Bond Connect/CIBM, 2021), India (GBI-EM, the 2023 piece's "poised to reach 10% weighting") and, per the dealers piece, Korea, Saudi Arabia and the Philippines to come. Concannon at $1T: "even more countries set to join leading emerging market indices in 2026". MKTX's answer is coverage: once IGBs went live it was "the only fixed income electronic trading platform that covers 100% of the J.P. Morgan GBI-EM Index".
- **Mandates make conversion faster than choice.** Brazil's early-2026 mandate "specifically encourages the greater use of electronic trading". MKTX's spin is that onshore growth (+24% through April) shows it is "more structurally electronic by choice, not just by mandate". Four accredited platforms means the mandate is not a monopoly.
- **Open Trading is the EM moat.** It is "the only all-to-all offering available for LATAM credit" (2024 awards piece), 42% of hard-currency EM in 2020 and 47% of MENA in 2025. In local markets the block protocol is RFM, not all-to-all: "Request-for-Market (RFM), Switch Trading and now EM Portfolio Trading have all seen rapid and significant adoption" (2021). Open Trading came to local currencies only in Oct 2023.
- **Volatility episodes double as marketing.** Argentina's election, MENA conflict risk, the Apr 2025 Treasury day, Fed days: Open Trading's share of won UST trades rises to "just under 30%" right after a Fed release vs 23% on a normal Wednesday (2026 piece). Every event piece reports a spike, and none reports the cost of a quiet year.
- **EM definitions move.** The $1T milestone counts the *Emerging Markets* credit line "combined with China-related trading volume that is currently reported in the "Agency and other government bonds" line" in rates. The ~$5bn 2025 EM ADV is therefore not comparable with the $3.5B 4Q24 product-line ADV. EM and Eurobond **share** was dropped in Jan 2024 because it was "derived from MarketAxess TraX data" (see [[marketaxess-data-cp-plus]]).
- **Treasuries: the all-to-all thesis that hasn't landed.** The UST blog (undated, ~2023–24) reads the PIMCO all-to-all paper as validation and lists Live Markets, then CP+ for USTs, then RFQ, then Open Trading. It admits market structure "may be slow to evolve with us". Two years later the Apr 2025 release still "plans to introduce enhanced Request for Quote (RFQ) for U.S. Government Bonds".

## 5. Same word, different metric — the 2021 target vs the 2025 headline

| | 2021 Investor Day | FY22 letter | Dec 2025 GS deck |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metric | *International* % of revenue | Revenue from *international clients* | *Non-U.S. credit* % of revenue |
| Value | 15% (2016) → **28%** (2021 TTM) → **50% target** | **20%** (2022), from 16% (2018) | **"Approximately 50%"** (2024), growing ~15% a year |
| What's in it | Not defined on the slide | Client domicile | EM, Eurobonds, **rates (US Treasuries)**, **"Other"** (footnote: "municipal bonds, leveraged loans and other variable transaction fees"), **services** |

*Derived reconciliation, FY25:* $406M outside US credit − $111.6M services − ~$28.5M rates ≈ **$266M**, or ~**31%** of revenue, before taking out munis and other US *Other* revenue. That is close to the 28% international figure of 2021, not to 50%. The 2021 and 2022 numbers don't even agree with each other (28% vs 20% one year apart), which suggests a product-based measure in the deck and a domicile-based one in the letter. **The 2025 metric meets the 2021 target in name only.**

## 6. How the narrative moved

1. **2019–20, global network.** "Nearly half" of active clients (825) were international (FY19 letter). EM is the "world’s best EM trading platform" (2021 deck). Rates is a new product to bundle with credit hedging.
2. **2021, geography as a strategic pillar.** Geographic diversification was the fourth Investor Day pillar, with a 50% target. China, frontier currencies, MuniBrokers, Charles Li on the board.
3. **2022–23, records abroad offset a stalled core.** Record EM, Eurobond and muni shares carry the "composite" headline while US HG stalls. The FY23 letter: "Emerging Market industry volumes were lower in 2023". Then the EM/Eurobond share disclosure stops.
4. **2024–26, *non-US credit* as half the company.** The GS deck makes non-US credit growth (+15%) the counterweight to US credit's (2%). India, Brazil, MENA and Argentina are showcased. Moment and the ICE Bonds link bring retail into the story that ICE later claims as its contribution.

## 7. What the claims don't prove

1. ***~50% non-US credit* is not international revenue.** It includes US Treasuries, US munis and services (§5). The international target was quietly replaced, not met.
2. **EM growth is reported in volume, not dollars.** EM commission revenue was a record $104.1M in 2021 (4Q21 release). Later narrative releases give only growth rates, e.g. a 21% rise in combined EM and Eurobond commissions in 1Q26, not a dollar figure. Volume milestones ($1T) use an expanded definition.
3. **Share disclosure stopped where MKTX was strongest.** EM and Eurobond share went from 29–31% and 15–18% to *not published* (Jan 2024), and the reason given is that MKTX's own TraX data was the denominator.
4. **Open Trading's EM share depends on the slice.** 42% of hard currency (2020) and 47% of MENA (2025) sit beside 27% of all EM volume (2020–24 average). In USD Mexican names it fell from 41% to 26% between the 2020 and 2021 pieces, a drop the 2021 piece doesn't flag.
5. **Muni and UST share series were rebased.** The Jan 2024 change (MSRB flags for munis, FINRA Treasury TRACE replacing Fed primary-dealer data) lowered past shares and restated history. Trend claims across 2023/24 need the recast series.
6. **Event pieces are a selected sample.** Argentina, MENA, Brazil and Fed days are where volume spiked. The corpus has no piece on a market where MKTX lost share or exited (Russia appears only as an index weight "diluted to zero").
7. **Small inconsistencies.** Open Trading "Launched in 2012" (2023 release) vs "launched for credit back in 2013" (UST blog). China's onshore market is $15trn (2021) and then $17 trillion (2024), different measures for a figure used as a headline.

## 8. Strategic read *(inference — not stated by MarketAxess)*

- **EM was the right market for MKTX's model.** Fragmented dealers across time zones, no TRACE equivalent, many small and medium tickets, and index-driven global buyers. Those are the conditions where an anonymous all-to-all pool adds the most and where the trade data (TraX, CP+) is scarcest. The franchise is a genuine *cornered resource* ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]). The 40%+ Open Trading share in hard currency is far above the mid-30s in total credit.
- **But growth is policy-granted and lumpy.** Index inclusions, mandates and access schemes (CFETS, NDS-OM, Brazil's accreditation) are decided by states. They open doors for every accredited platform, which is the risk [[tradeweb-rates-em]] flags for Tradeweb's country-by-country model. MKTX entered China four years after Tradeweb's 2017 Bond Connect launch (context, from [[tradeweb-rates-em]]), so in Asian government bonds it is the follower, while in EM credit it is the incumbent.
- **Treasuries were a hedge-and-bundle bet, not a profit bet.** At ~$4–5 a million and ~50% variable costs, a 3% share of a $600bn+/day market generates ~$28M of revenue. The value was letting credit clients hedge and trade spreads in one workstation. Tradeweb did >$120bn a day of USTs in 2021 (context, from [[tradeweb-rates-em]]), several times MKTX's record $27bn rates ADV. Without a dominant D2C position or an inter-dealer CLOB, all-to-all Treasuries depend on regulators acting.
- **Munis and wealth are where ICE's thesis lands.** ICE's retail/wealth muni ATS link (2024), Moment (2026) and Axess IQ explain why the deal rationale stresses retail plus institutional. Muni share gains are the part of *non-US credit* that is actually US ([[marketaxess-ice-deal]], [[ice-hub]]).
- **Holding the target constant would have told a harder story.** Swapping a 50% international target for a 50% non-US-credit metric is the same move as HG share → composite share → channels in US credit ([[marketaxess-strategy-financials]]). The pattern is goalposts moving to where the growth is.

## 9. Canon lenses

- **Multi-sided platforms** ([[canon-hagiu-wright-multi-sided-platforms]]): each local market needs local dealers and global buyers at once. MKTX's lever is the 2,100-firm global buy side; Open Trading for local currency arrived only after onshore dealers were on board.
- **Coopetition with states** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): CFETS, CCIL and Brazil's Treasury are partners and gatekeepers. Accreditation lowers entry cost for all four Brazilian platforms.
- **Counter-positioning** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): in EM, voice dealers can't match anonymous all-to-all without cannibalising relationships. In Treasuries, incumbents' CLOB and D2C depth leave MKTX no such opening.
- **Moving goalposts** ([[canon-kahneman-sibony-sunstein-noise]], [[canon-tetlock-superforecasting]]): a forecast (50% international) scored against a redefined outcome can't be learned from.
- **Geopolitics as demand** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): elections, sanctions and tariffs raise EM e-volumes as long as the venue stays open. Russia's index weight going to zero is the reminder that the same shocks can close a market.

## 10. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For:** BlackRock executed the first electronic IGB trade on MKTX; CFETS, CCIL and Brazil's Treasury chose it as an access point; dealers and clients in 90 markets trade EM on it. A 100% GBI-EM coverage claim is checkable and matters to index-tracking managers. A Tier 1 dealer on MENA concedes volumes are "relatively even between e-trading and voice", which leaves a long runway.
- **Against:** the growth half of the company is defined to include US Treasuries, US munis and data. The cleanest international metric (EM/Eurobond share) was withdrawn. In Treasuries, five years after LiquidityEdge, MKTX had ~2–3% share, still announcing RFQ upgrades.
- **Both:** the EM franchise is real and probably the most valuable thing ICE is buying in credit after the US network. The *half the revenue* framing overstates how international the business became.
