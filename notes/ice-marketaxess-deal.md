# Note · ICE × MarketAxess — the evaluator buys the flow after seven years of failing to build it

> Deal / competitive-strategy note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — build vs buy, vertical integration of price and venue), **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation — premium, EV/EBITDA, synergy value, accretion), **Course 03** (Microeconomics — protocol design, information leakage), **E3** (M&A — antitrust, close risk), **E2** (Capital Allocation — debt-funded deal vs buyback) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[ice-hub]]; companions [[ice-strategy-financials]], [[ice-fixed-income-data]], [[tradeweb-credit]], [[tradeweb-ai-price]], [[marketaxess-hub]]

- **Source:** ICE IR newsroom and ice.com, **24 items, Oct 25 2018 – Jul 30 2026**, read in full from `_ingest/raw/ice/articles.jsonl`. They are: the deal release (2026-07-30); **15 ICE Bonds / fixed-income execution releases** (launch 2019-02-14 → MultiLynq RMA 2026-07-22); the Ben Jackson podcast (2018-10-25); the Edmonds podcast (2026-01-14) and profile (Apr 2024, in-text); the Compass (2026-06-09) and MCP (2026-07-29) releases; and the "Fixed Income Execution" revenue lines in the 29 earnings releases, 4Q20–2Q26. Tradeweb and Trumid facts come only from [[tradeweb-hub]], [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]], [[tradeweb-credit]] and [[tradeweb-ai-price]]. **The ICE corpus has no MarketAxess financials** beyond what the deal release implies, and no news after signing: no proxy, no HSR filing, no shareholder vote.
- **Core claim:** ICE entered bond execution through the data door. It bought IDC's evaluated prices (2015), then two retail-born order books, BondPoint and TMC (2018). In 2019 it branded them **ICE Bonds**, built around the claim that order books leak less than the RFQ venues run by “MarketAxess Bloomberg and Tradeweb” (Jackson, 2018-10-25). Seven years later, its **Fixed Income Execution line was $125M for FY25, about $31M a quarter**. That is 1.3% of ICE's revenue, and it peaked at $35M in a quarter (4Q22, 4Q23). Volume records came from munis, retail and dealer sweeps, not institutional RFQ. On **2026-07-30** ICE agreed to pay **$167/share in cash (33% premium; ~$6.0B equity, ~$5.7B EV, ~10.6x LTM EBITDA counting full synergies)**, all of it new debt, for the ~2,100-firm institutional network it could not build. The deal re-levers ICE to **3.4x gross**, yet buybacks *rise* to $400M a quarter. **81 days later, Tradeweb bought Trumid** ([[tradeweb-hub]]). US credit e-trading is sorting into two vertical stacks: **price + index + venue (ICE)** and **venue + model price + LSEG distribution (Tradeweb)**.

**Three readings of the build.**
1. **Volume came, revenue mostly didn't.** Corporate notional rose from $212B to $232.5B (2024 → 2025) and munis rose 27%. Yet execution revenue rose only from $117M to $125M, and quarterly revenue never beat 4Q22's $35M. On rough numbers, 2025 revenue / (corporate + muni notional) ≈ $125M / $458B ≈ **$270 per $1M** (derived; it overstates the fee because the line also includes Treasuries, agencies, MBS and CDs). H2 2025 corporates were ≈ $232.5B − $120B = **$112.5B, below H1's $120B**, so growth was slowing into the deal.
2. **The protocol thesis bent.** The 2018 claim was that order books beat leaky RFQ. By 2025 ICE had added RFQ for MBS, a spread-based click-to-trade order and dealer sweeps (RMA/PIVC), all priced off **ICE's own evaluated price**. The franchise that grew was a *sweep-and-match* layer around CEP, not an institutional order book. ICE then bought the largest RFQ venue Jackson had named in 2018 (inference).
3. **Retail venue, institutional gap.** ICE TMC's 475 providers and 250,000 users are retail and wealth (Tigress, TS Imagine, Canada). Concannon names exactly that as what ICE brings: “ICE brings additional retail and wealth trading protocols, strong data, connectivity, and a broader set of product capabilities” (2026-07-30). The Open Trading link (Aug 2024) and the MSCI MarketAxess futures (Dec 2024) were two years of working-level partnership before the bid.

## 4. The mirror deal: Tradeweb–Trumid, 81 days later

From [[tradeweb-hub]] and [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]]: on **Oct 19 2026** Tradeweb agreed to buy **Trumid** for **~$1.97B** ($1.75B for the business plus ~$220M of tax assets), paid as ~$1.89B cash and $82M equity, with close targeted for **H1 2027**. That is the same close window as ICE–MarketAxess. Jul 30 → Oct 19 is **81 days**. Per [[tradeweb-ai-price]], The DESK's Jun 2026 count had Tradeweb at **18.0%** and MarketAxess at **16.0%** of IG+HY TRACE ADV, Tradeweb's widest lead. MarketAxess's US credit PT share was a record 21% in FY25, and its fee per million fell 7.6%.

| | ICE → MarketAxess | Tradeweb → Trumid |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer's core | Evaluated prices, indices, futures, clearing | Multi-asset RFQ venue, Ai-Price, LSEG distribution |
| Target | Largest incumbent credit venue (A2A brand) | Challenger credit venue (hedge funds, new issues) |
| Price | ~$5.7B EV, ~10.6x synergized LTM EBITDA | ~$1.97B, ~1.8x *Tradeweb's own* FY25 adj. EBITDA (target EBITDA not disclosed) |
| Funding | 100% new debt | Mostly cash on hand + $82M stock |
| Logic (inference) | Buy the flow to feed the price | Buy the third venue before anyone else can |

**The read (inference).** The ICE deal turns a two-and-a-half-venue market (Tradeweb, MarketAxess, plus Trumid/Bloomberg) into a contest between two vertically integrated stacks. Tradeweb's best move was to take Trumid off the table. Each venue argues for the protocol it sells. ICE's 2018 line was that RFQ leaks. Tradeweb's 2026 whitepaper argues that broadcasting to A2A leaks ([[tradeweb-a2a-information-leakage]]). ICE now owns the A2A pool that paper was aimed at.

## 5. Antitrust and close risks

1. **ICE's own record.** Black Knight took **16 months** from signing (May 2022) to close (Sep 2023). The FTC sued, the price was cut from $85 to $75, and **two divestitures** went to one buyer (Constellation), with ICE financing $500M of the $700M Optimal Blue price ([[ice-mortgage-technology]]). The H1 2027 target allows at most 11 months.
2. **Horizontal overlap is real, even if small.** Both firms run anonymous A2A pools in corporates *and* munis, and the pools were linked in 2024. ICE's munis are its largest growth line (+27% in 2025). Expect any review to focus on munis and odd-lot/retail corporates, not institutional IG (inference).
3. **Vertical foreclosure is the bigger question.** ICE would own the evaluated price used for NAVs and best-execution checks, a model price (CP+) licensed to a rival evaluator (S&P), indices and a venue. A reviewer could ask whether CEP or CP+ access to rival venues and evaluators gets degraded (inference). No ICE statement addresses this.
4. **Two credit deals under review at once.** ICE–MarketAxess and Tradeweb–Trumid target the same close window. Regulators could review the two together, or require remedies in one because of the other (inference).
5. **Financing and rates.** All-debt funding at 3.4x leaves less room if a volume shock hits energy revenue, which swung from +46% (1Q26) to −13% (2Q26) ([[ice-strategy-financials]]).
6. **People and culture.** MarketAxess is a Nasdaq-listed, institution-facing firm with its own CEO and data business. The corpus says nothing on retention or integration leadership. CIO Mark Wassersug has spoken of “33 acquisitions and integrations” (podcast, 2023-01-03).

## 6. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Accretive" is adjusted.** It excludes amortization of the intangibles this deal creates (release footnote). With ~$5.7B paid against an unknown book value, GAAP EPS could be diluted for years (inference).
2. **The 10.6x is flattering by construction.** It counts *full run-rate* synergies, which arrive by year 3, against *trailing* EBITDA. Before synergies it is ~13.0x (derived). No MarketAxess revenue, EBITDA, growth or margin appears anywhere in the ICE corpus.
3. **"Record" ICE Bonds volume is measured in notional.** Revenue is the test, and revenue stayed in a $25–35M quarterly band from 2Q22 to 2Q26.
4. **The counts don't reconcile.** RMA had 50 registered firms (Feb 2025), but its network was 15 firms via GUI/API plus 6 via MultiLynq (Jul 2026). Registration and connection may be different measures. ICE does not say.
5. **The 2018 leakage argument was never tested.** ICE published no CLOB-vs-RFQ execution-quality study on its own data in this corpus. Its evaluations accuracy claim is relative (see [[ice-fixed-income-data]]).
6. **The e-trading denominator is disputed by ICE itself.** Edmonds pushed back on the claim that about half of IG trades electronically: “I might challenge you on that statistic.” (2026-01-14). If ICE thinks the addressable market is much larger, the deal case is a forecast about bonds that don't yet trade (inference).

## 7. Strategic read (inference — not stated by ICE)

- **Build vs buy, decided by the data.** ICE's comparative advantage is the *price*: CEP feeds RMA, PIVC, Portfolio Auction, Compass and Aladdin. A price needs trades to learn from, and institutional prints sit mostly on MarketAxess, Tradeweb and Bloomberg. Buying the flow improves the price, and the price sells more data. That is Jackson's 2018 "golden record" logic run in reverse.
- **Why now.** MarketAxess was losing share to Tradeweb, fee per million was falling, and it had a pre-existing link to ICE. That made it a *motivated* seller at a 13x stand-alone multiple. ICE paid ~23x for Ellie Mae and ~15x synergized (~20x unsynergized) for Black Knight ([[ice-strategy-financials]]), so this is the cheapest of its three mega-deals on EBITDA.
- **The franchise risk is reflexive.** If ICE's evaluated price becomes the reference for execution on ICE's venue, best-execution checks are graded by the venue's owner. That is the self-grading problem flagged for Tradeweb's Ai-Price ([[tradeweb-ai-price]]), now with a bigger ruler.
- **Buybacks up while leverage goes up** breaks ICE's own pattern. After Ellie Mae and Black Knight it suspended buybacks, for ~4 and ~11 quarters. Raising them now signals confidence in free cash flow, and shareholder pressure (inference; compare the eBay retreat, [[ice-strategy-financials]]).

## 8. Canon lenses

- **Vertical integration and counter-positioning** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): evaluators can't see flow and venues can't certify NAVs. Owning both is a cornered resource only if regulators let the two reinforce each other.
- **Complements and coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): MarketAxess was a complementor (link, futures) before it became a subsidiary. S&P turns from CP+ customer into a rival's customer. Tradeweb stays a data supplier to IBA ([[ice-benchmarks-iba]]) while becoming the main rival.
- **Multi-sided platforms** ([[canon-hagiu-wright-multi-sided-platforms]], [[canon-parker-platform-revolution]]): retail/wealth (ICE TMC) plus institutional (MarketAxess) is a side-adding deal. The value is cross-side liquidity, which the 2024 link was already testing.
- **Information goods** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): CEP plus CP+ means the clients' TCA history and auto-ex thresholds are tuned to ICE rulers. That is switching cost through measurement.
- **Margin of safety** ([[canon-klarman-margin-of-safety]]): a $1.49B premium against ~$0.75–1.06B of capitalised cost synergies (Rep, b) leaves the rest to unstated revenue synergies.
- **Disruption** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): ICE's retail order books were the low-end entrant, and they never moved up-market. The incumbent bought the incumbent.

## 9. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For the deal:** ICE gets a profitable, scaled network at its lowest mega-deal multiple, plus the data that sharpens its core pricing business. The working link since 2024 lowers integration risk. Clients asked for fewer platforms (see [[tradeweb-strategy-financials]] §7), and one venue can now serve the retail, wealth and institutional sides.
- **Against:** buy-side desks value having several venues. Some may shift flow to Tradeweb or Bloomberg to avoid depending on one vendor, the way they spread dealer relationships (inference). MarketAxess's share trend was negative before the bid. And all-debt funding plus higher buybacks bets that energy volatility keeps funding the plan.
- **Synthesis:** the strategy is coherent. The open questions are price discipline and access rules, not logic. A desk should watch whether CP+ stays available to non-ICE venues and evaluators on the same terms after close.

### Rep
Deal math, using only this note's numbers. State assumptions out loud.

**(a) Premium and multiples.** From $167 and a 33% premium, back out the unaffected price and the aggregate premium. From $5.7B and 10.6x, back out synergized and stand-alone EBITDA and the stand-alone multiple.
*Unaffected = 167/1.33 = **$125.56**; aggregate premium ≈ 6.0 − 6.0/1.33 = **$1.49B**. EBITDA ≈ 5.7/10.6 = **$538M** synergized, **$438M** stand-alone, so **13.0x** stand-alone. Compare Ellie Mae at 11/0.47 ≈ 23x and Black Knight at ~15x synergized.*

**(b) Synergy value vs premium.** Take $100M pre-tax and a 25% tax rate (assumption). Capitalise after-tax synergies at 10x, then capitalise pre-tax synergies at the deal's 10.6x EBITDA multiple. What share of the premium does each cover?
*After-tax $75M × 10 = **$0.75B (50% of premium)**; $100M × 10.6 = **$1.06B (71%)**. Both ignore the three-year phase-in, which lowers the values further. The rest of the premium must come from revenue synergies or strategic value, and ICE names none.*

**(c) Leverage path.** Pro forma debt ≈ $25.8B at 3.4x. How much debt must ICE repay to reach 3.0x if EBITDA is flat? How much if EBITDA grows 5% a year for two years?
*Implied EBITDA = 25.8/3.4 ≈ **$7.6B**. Flat case: 3.0 × 7.6 = $22.8B, so repay **~$3.0B**. Growth case: EBITDA ≈ 7.6 × 1.05² ≈ $8.4B; 3.0x allows $25.1B, so repay only **~$0.7B**. EBITDA growth, not repayment, does most of the work. That is why $1.6B a year of buybacks can continue.*

**(d) Deal vs buyback, on adjusted EPS.** Assume 5% interest on $6.0B, a 25% tax rate, $50M of MarketAxess organic D&A, ~576M ICE shares (FY25 adj. NI $4.0B / $6.95), and a $175 share price (assumption, about a $100B market cap). Compare the two uses of $6.0B.
*Deal: (538 − 50 − 300) × 0.75 = **+$141M** → 4,141 / 576 = **$7.19 (+3.4%)**. Buyback: 34.3M shares retired; NI falls by $225M → 3,775 / 541.7 = **$6.97 (+0.3%)**. On adjusted EPS the deal wins easily, because the after-tax pre-interest yield (≈6.1%) beats ICE's own earnings yield (≈4.0%), and because adjusted EPS ignores deal amortization. Redo it on GAAP once the purchase-price allocation is filed.*
