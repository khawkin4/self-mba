- **Core claim:** The competitor map in [[impactcom-mission-forces]] §5 has shifted three ways.
  1. **The networks are consolidating, and the biggest one now runs on impact.com.** Awin swallowed ShareASale (2025). Partnerize swallowed Pepperjam (2020). Rakuten Advertising rents impact.com's contracting, tracking and payments (Apr 2026). Publicis has said nothing about CJ's numbers since it put CJ under a 'strategic review' in 2019.
  2. **Rakuten rented because buying was off the table.** Its overseas unit, which holds Rakuten Rewards and Rakuten Advertising, earned **$51.8M** of operating income on **$2.1B** revenue in FY2025. Rewards revenue fell **~5%** in each of Q1 and Q2 2026. The group is selling securities (**¥200bn**, May 2026) to deleverage from Mobile. Buying impact.com at 5–8x ARR would cost **¥200–325bn** (*derived*). That is one more year of asset sales for a non-core unit.
  3. **The growth threat is the platforms that own the checkout.** YouTube (**1.3M+** affiliate creators), Amazon (Creator Connections, plus Associates rate cuts of up to 50%), TikTok Shop (~**$15B** US GMV, 2025) and Shopify Collabs (free app) each bring their own tracking, payouts and creator pool. They need no partnership platform. On PMA numbers, US affiliate spend grows ~**14%** a year and impact.com's ARR ~**20%**, so it still gains share of a pool that is ~$14B in the US. It is best placed to grow as the brand's neutral ledger across those platforms, not as a better network.

## 2. Where Rakuten Advertising and Rakuten Rewards sit

Rakuten Rewards (ex-Ebates) and the overseas advertising business, i.e. Rakuten Advertising (ex-LinkShare, ex-Rakuten Marketing), sit inside **Internet Services → International Business Unit → 'Open Commerce'**. Per the deck footnote, Open Commerce is Rewards (US, Europe, Canada), Fillr and the 'overseas Ads business'. Rewards is the only one broken out. **Rakuten Advertising has no revenue line of its own anywhere in the decks.** PPC Land (outside) on Rakuten Marketing's history: it "acquired LinkShare in 2005 for $425 million in cash". (Context, not from Rakuten's IR: Ebates was bought in 2014 for about $1B.)

It is shrinking in 2026 and makes its profit from cost cuts. The headline 'advertising business' (¥65.6bn in Q2/26, +15.6%) is **domestic** ads on Ichiba and Travel. Rakuten's own footnote says ad revenue outside Japan sits in the International BU, so the growing ad line says nothing about Rakuten Advertising. *Inference:* Rakuten shut its own creator platform in the same quarter it signed with impact.com, whose bundle includes a creator product.

| FinTech reorganisation | Bank, Card and Securities HD merged into a Rakuten Bank Group from **Oct 1 2026**; ‘not intended for fundraising’, a net capital outflow for Rakuten; synergy target >¥85bn by FY to Mar 2030 | Q2/26 CEO deck |

Rakuten's stated policy (FY2025 release): "The Group will maintain a policy of securing funds for Rakuten Mobile for 2026 onwards primarily through self-funding." And in Q2: "In May 2026, Rakuten raised approximately 200 billion yen through the sale of held securities."

**Build vs buy vs ally (inference, not stated by Rakuten).**
- **Buy:** impact.com at 5–8x ~$270M ARR ([[impactcom-strategy-financials]] §7) is $1.35–2.2B, or **¥200–325bn** at ¥150/$. That is 1–1.6x the May 2026 securities sale and 2–3x the group's FY2025 Non-GAAP OI. It would sit on top of a non-FinTech leverage ratio Rakuten has promised to cut to 5x. The target would earn ~26–42x the International BU's whole FY2025 OI.
- **Build:** rebuilding contracting, tracking and payouts in 60–70 currencies takes years of capex. Rakuten has ring-fenced capex for base stations (>¥200bn in 2026), in a unit whose OI is ~$50M a year.
- **Ally:** costs little or no capital and keeps the override and the service relationship (the alliance says "Rakuten Advertising will continue to develop AI, analytics, automation and monitoring capabilities"). It turns technology capex into a vendor fee. Rewards also gets a second distribution channel at a time its revenue is falling.

The alliance is the only option that fits a deleveraging plan. The same logic means Rakuten is unlikely to bid for impact.com before 2027 unless the target is paid in stock (*inference*).

## 4. What Rakuten says about the alliance in its own IR

- **Nothing in the group's IR materials.** The FY2025, Q1 and Q2 FY2026 releases and the Q4/25, Q1/26 and Q2/26 data and CEO decks do not mention impact.com (text search, 2026-11-01). The Q1 deck (May 14, two weeks after signing) does not mention the alliance either.
- **Announced one level down.** The alliance came from the overseas subsidiary through PR Newswire on rakutenadvertising.com: "Rakuten International and impact.com today announced a strategic alliance". The 2026 English group press list we fetched (53 items) has no release on it.
- **Rakuten's framing** is data, not cost: "Through Rakuten Rewards’ direct consumer signals, advertisers can better understand incrementality". No financial terms are given anywhere ([[impactcom-strategy-financials]] §6).
- *Inference:* at group level the deal is immaterial (Open Commerce is a fraction of an International BU whose ¥7.8bn FY2025 OI is ~7% of group Non-GAAP OI, *derived*). For impact.com it is the largest channel deal it has. Each side's disclosure matches its own materiality.

**By model (inference).** *Network override* players (CJ, Awin, Rakuten) sell publisher access and services; their weak spot is price, which impact.com already exploited. *SaaS peers* (Partnerize, PartnerStack, Everflow) sell the same rails and compete on vertical fit and price. *Creator platforms* (CreatorIQ, LTK) compete for a newer budget line that is often flat-fee, not CPA. *Native platforms* compete on the thing impact.com cannot own: the shopper, the checkout and the attribution data.

## 8. What the claims don't prove

1. **Every rival number is a self-description.** Awin's and CJ's 'revenue for advertisers' are attributed sales, not incremental sales. Neither page gives a year.
2. **Rakuten's segment data hides Rakuten Advertising.** The decline in 'Open Commerce' could come from Rewards, Fillr or Advertising. Only Rewards is visible.
3. **No alliance economics anywhere.** Rakuten's IR is silent, and impact.com's releases give no terms. Whether Rakuten pays impact.com per program or per transaction, or shares revenue, is unknown.
4. **Native-platform scale claims are unaudited**: YouTube's 13x GMV, TikTok GMV from third-party trackers, Amazon's rate cuts from seven sources speaking to Adweek.
5. **Market-size sources have owners.** The PMA is a members' body. eMarketer sits in the same group as Awin.

## 9. Strategic read (inference — not stated by impact.com or Rakuten)

- **The network-vs-SaaS war is over, and SaaS won the rails.** The network side is consolidating (Awin+ShareASale, Partnerize+Pepperjam), going quiet (CJ inside Publicis) or renting (Rakuten). What is left to win there is execution: migrating Rakuten's advertisers without losing them to Partnerize, which offers SaaS plus services under one roof.
- **Rakuten's weakness is impact.com's opportunity and risk.** A deleveraging parent with a shrinking Rewards line and a shut creator platform will keep the overseas unit capital-light. That makes impact.com the de facto technology owner of Rakuten's affiliate business. It also makes the business fragile if Rakuten later sells Rakuten Advertising to someone else, such as a PE roll-up or a holding company (*speculative*).
- **The real share fight is with the checkout owners.** Amazon cutting Associates rates while growing brand-funded Creator Connections shows the pattern: the platform keeps the ledger and charges the brand. YouTube, TikTok and Shopify are each building the same thing. impact.com cannot beat them on the shopper. It can be the place where a brand contracts, pays, taxes and reconciles partners **across** them, as Trackonomics already does across networks ([[impactcom-strategy-financials]] §4).
- **Creator budgets are the biggest new pool, and they are contested.** CreatorIQ (1,300+ customers) and LTK ($6B+ sales) are bigger in creators than impact.com's bundled Creator product. Rakuten exiting Collective Voice removes one rival. Bundling Creator free (Jan 2026) is a defensive price move, not a moat.

## 10. Canon lenses

- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): Rakuten is complementor and competitor at once. Awin is a rival network and also YouTube's UK onboarding partner, as impact.com is in the US.
- **Counter-positioning** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): it worked against networks. Native platforms counter-position against impact.com in turn: free tools, funded by the checkout margin.
- **Disruption** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): Shopify Collabs and the $30 tier are the low end. Amazon and TikTok are new-market entrants with their own value networks.
- **Multi-sided platforms** ([[canon-hagiu-wright-multi-sided-platforms]], [[canon-parker-platform-revolution]]): LTK and YouTube own the consumer side; impact.com owns the brand and partner sides only.
- **Five forces / where to play** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]], [[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): the crux is supplier power of the demand gatekeepers ([[impactcom-mission-forces]] §4).
- **Platform hybrids** ([[canon-cusumano-business-of-platforms]]): integrations (YouTube API, Shopify, MCP) are how a non-owner of demand stays relevant.

## 11. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For impact.com:** every structural move of 2025–26 went its way. The #3 network rents its rails, the #2 swallowed the cheapest rival but kept the old fee model, and Amazon's cuts push publishers toward brand-direct programs. Growth of ~20% in a ~12–14% pool is share gain.
- **Against:** the alliance has no disclosed economics, a counterparty under financial strain, and a group IR that does not mention it. Creator and native platforms grow faster than the PMA pool. impact.com is a guest on all of them.
- **Both:** a neutral ledger across closed platforms is valuable only if brands run many platforms at once. That holds at enterprise scale, not for the Shopify SMB.
