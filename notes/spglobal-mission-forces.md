# Note · S&P Global's mission and the forces on it — the slogan changed three times; the business underneath never did. Volatility and refinancing are fuel, and the 2026 danger is LLM front ends and venue-owned prices turning the data layer into a commodity

> Strategy / environment-scan note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy: five forces, coopetition, platform power), **Course 11** (Global Strategy & Macro: China ratings, Russia exit, tariffs, Middle East energy shock), **E5** (Transformation: conglomerate → "operating company" → four-division data firm), **E6** (Crisis Leadership: COVID, the 2022 issuance collapse, 2025 tariffs), **E9** (Geopolitics & Macro), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[spglobal-hub]]; companions [[spglobal-esg-reversal]], [[spglobal-strategy-financials]], [[spglobal-ai-data-layer]]

- **Source:** the S&P Global corpus at `_ingest/raw/spglobal/` (492 full texts, 2019-01-01 → 2026-10-23). It includes 476 press and IR releases, the CEO and chairman letters for 2019–2025, and the Investor Day decks of Dec 2022 and Nov 2025. "About S&P Global" boilerplate was traced phrase by phrase, with first and last dates (§1b). Every line here is S&P's own text or text it chose to publish, so the note reads it for what it reveals and what it leaves out. Earnings releases after Q1 2023 are stubs in the corpus, so yearly figures come from the letters and decks (see `_ingest/raw/spglobal/DIGEST.md` §1).
- **Core claim:** S&P Global rewrote its slogan three times in seven years:
  - **"Powering the Markets of the Future"** (2018–2021), with the purpose line *Accelerating Progress*.
  - **"Powering Global Markets"** and *Accelerate Progress* (Dec 2022 – 2024).
  - **"Advancing Essential Intelligence"** (Nov 2025 →).

  The business did not change. S&P sells **proprietary benchmarks, ratings and data that regulated workflows must use**: in 2025 it said these make up "over 95 percent of our revenue". **Volatility and refinancing waves are fuel, not threats**: 2020 was a record year, and the 2026–28 "maturity wall" is a selling point. Two things changed: *what kind of company S&P says it is*, and *what threatens it*.
  - **Identity:** the story moved from a conglomerate of four divisions, to a scale merger with IHS Markit, to a narrower firm that sells off non-core lines.
  - **Threat:** in 2019 the risk was cyclical, meaning Ratings issuance. By 2026 it is structural. AI front ends (Claude, Copilot, Gemini) now sit between S&P and the user. A trading venue's prices (MarketAxess CP+, soon to be ICE's) feed S&P's own bond evaluations. Both push S&P's data toward commodity status. **S&P admits the cyclical risks and lists generative AI as a competitive risk. It never discusses fee pressure on its own products, the CP+ dependency after ICE's bid for MarketAxess, or its Ratings franchise's conflicts.**

**The arc:**
- **2019–21:** *power the markets of the future*, plus a purpose line.
- **2022–24:** *power global markets*, *accelerate progress*.
- **2025–26:** *advance essential intelligence*, aimed at AI and DeFi.

The purpose line *Accelerate Progress* runs from the 2019 annual report to the Oct 2025 boilerplate. It is **absent from the 2025 CEO and chairman letters**.

**What the boilerplate shows (inference unless noted):**
1. **Two verbs never reconciled: *confidence* vs *conviction*.**
   - Corporate texts and CEO quotes have said "make decisions with conviction" since 2019 (36 items).
   - S&P DJI and Ratings boilerplates still say "make decisions with confidence" (60 items, last seen 2026-10-22).
   - After seven years and a merger, the divisions still do not speak with one brand voice.
2. **The legal self-description followed strategy only after the fact.**
   - "Automotive" stayed in until after Investor Day 2025.
   - "ESG" stayed until mid-2024, months after the company had begun to reposition.
   - Read the boilerplate as a lagging indicator.
3. **It names tailwinds, not products.** The 2026 text lists *energy expansion, AI and private markets*, the three growth stories of Investor Day 2025. Ratings, the biggest profit pool, is described only as "benchmarks".

### 1c. Values and culture claims

- **Values:**
  - 2019: *excellence, relevance, integrity*.
  - 2021: rewritten as *discovery, partnership, integrity*, which Cheung repeats in 2024.
- **People:**
  - "Putting Our People First" (AR 2019) became the *People First philosophy* (2023 DEI report), then *People Forward* (AR 2024).
  - In 2025 *People Forward* is tied to mandatory "AI for Everyone" training and the StepForward youth programme ($10M over three years, 2025-12-17).
- **Integrity and independence** are repeated every year by S&P DJI ("As an independent index provider, we hold ourselves to the highest standards of integrity and transparency", Draper, 2020-08-05). The same index business paid a $9M SEC penalty in May 2021 ([[spglobal-indices-benchmarks]]).
- **Purpose as an executive role, then not:** a Chief Purpose Officer sat on the 2022 Investor Day executive committee. The role ended in the Oct 2024 leadership reshuffle, and corporate-responsibility disclosures moved under the CFO.

### 1d. What S&P actually optimises for (inference, judged by actions)

| Stated | Revealed by action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Powering the Markets of the Future" (breadth) | **Scale first, then subtract** | The $44B IHS Markit merger (2020). Within four years it sold or spun off Engineering Solutions ($975M), Fincentric, PrimeOne, OSTTRA ($3.1B, split with CME), Mobility and EDM/thinkFolio, plus the forced remedies ([[spglobal-ihs-markit-merger]]) |
| "Essential intelligence" | **Own the must-use standard**: ratings, indices, identifiers, benchmarks | CUSIP was given up only under an EC remedy. LoanX IDs were extended to private credit (2025). Capital IQ IDs were published free on DUNL.org (2025): lock-in through the identifier |
| "Advancing Market Leadership" | **Margin and capital return** | Adjusted operating margin 44.9% (2022) → 50.4% (2025). Since 2022, 145% of adjusted FCF returned vs an "at least 85%" framework (Investor Day 2025). Cheung on the July 2026 reorganisation: "growing our revenue and improving margins" |
| "Amplifying … AI" | **Distribution through others' front ends** | Kensho LLM-ready API (2024); MCP into Claude (2025), AWS, Google, Microsoft, Cohere (2026) ([[spglobal-ai-data-layer]]) |
| "Bridging traditional and decentralized finance" | **License the brand onto new venues; keep the risk small** | S&P 500 perpetuals on Hyperliquid, tokenized iBoxx TRS on Canton, Kaiko stake, OpenZeppelin. All deal values undisclosed and "not material" ([[spglobal-digital-assets]]) |

**The tension (inference):** the mission says *essential*, meaning customers cannot do without it. The 2026 actions say *distribute everywhere*. Those fit together only while S&P's data is the standard that every LLM and venue must license. If an AI front end or a venue (CP+, Tradeweb's Ai-Price) can produce a *good enough* number, *essential* turns into *one input among several*.

**What S&P acknowledges vs leaves out:** it openly discusses cyclicality, geopolitics, tariffs and "AI" as a generic risk. It does **not** discuss its own pricing power, its dependence on CP+ after the ICE deal, the conflict between selling ratings and selling data, or why ESG was folded.

## 4. Forces map, 2026 (five forces + regulation + technology)

Pressure score: 1 = benign, 5 = severe (judgement). Arrows show the change since 2019.

| Force | Score | Trend | One-line evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rivalry** | 3 | ↑ | The peer group S&P names in every annual report: Moody's, CME, MSCI, FactSet, Verisk, ICE. ICE is now also a pricing rival that will own CP+. Bloomberg is both a rival and a partner (TSOX–InvestorAccess, 2023) |
| **New entrants** | 2 | ↑ slowly | Ratings and benchmark licences (EU BMR, NRSRO, CRA rules) are high barriers. AI-native analytics firms (Farsight, Rogo) enter at the workflow layer, not the data layer |
| **Supplier power** | 3 | ↑ | Evaluated pricing depends on dealer contributions (Barclays, 2025) and venue prices (CP+). Visible Alpha depends on bank contributors |
| **Buyer power** | 3 | ↑ | Fee-compressed passive managers. Enterprise deals run through the Chief Client Office. Hyperscalers and LLM vendors are new distribution buyers |
| **Substitutes** | 3 | ↑ | Model prices (Ai-Price), free identifiers (S&P itself published CIQ IDs), open-source carbon data (CDOP). Ratings have no close substitute (NRSRO oligopoly) |
| **Regulation** | 2 (net tailwind) | ↔ | Rules that require independent numbers (BMR, IOSCO, Rule 2a-5) vs conduct penalties and merger remedies |
| **Technology (AI / DLT)** | 4 (double-edged) | ↑ | Tailwind T3 and headwind H1 at the same time. Tokenization opens a new licensing channel ([[spglobal-digital-assets]]) |
| **Complementors** | — | ↑ | CME (S&P DJI JV partner, 27% non-controlling interest), Cboe (VIX, SPX options), MSCI (GICS co-owner), AWS/Google/Microsoft/Anthropic, Kaiko |

**Read:** S&P sits where most of the power is: must-use standards protected by regulation. The two forces getting worse, **technology** and **suppliers**, attack the same thing. They both threaten S&P's control over *who computes the number and where it is consumed*.

**Pattern:** for S&P, *volatility* is a tailwind and *issuance freezes* are the headwind. Those are different shocks. The company is hurt most by rate shocks that close primary markets, not by crises that force borrowers to raise cash.

## 7. What the claims don't prove

1. **"Over 95 percent" proprietary.** S&P defines the category itself. The share of revenue whose value depends on being the *only* source is a different and unpublished number.
2. **"More tailwinds than headwinds"** (AR 2025) is a CEO's year-end framing, published in the same quarter the Investor Day risk factors added generative AI.
3. **Mission statements are not measured.** No release reports progress against "Advancing Essential Intelligence". The 2022 *Vitality Index* (revenue from new or enhanced products: 8% → >10% → 11%) is the closest thing to a scorecard, and S&P defines it.
4. **Crisis resilience is selective.** S&P publicises record crisis quarters (2020). It suspended guidance quietly (one release, 2022) and never published a "what broke" piece.

## 8. Canon lenses

- **Industry structure** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): S&P's best businesses sit behind regulatory entry barriers (NRSRO, EU BMR). Section 4 shows the pressure moving to suppliers and substitutes, not to rivals.
- **Power** ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]): *cornered resource* (the S&P 500 brand, rating history) plus *switching costs* (identifiers, benchmarks written into fund documents). AI threatens neither directly. It threatens the *process power* of the MI desktop.
- **Information goods** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): giving away CIQ IDs to sell linked data is classic version-and-lock-in.
- **Disruption** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): model prices and LLM answers are "good enough" low-end entrants against evaluated pricing and desktop research, not against ratings.
- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): MSCI (GICS co-owner and index rival), Bloomberg (partner on primary bonds and desktop rival), CME (JV partner) and ICE (Brent co-governor, pricing rival and soon CP+ owner).
- **Antifragility** ([[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): volatility helps S&P; issuance freezes hurt it. It is *antifragile to noise and fragile to a closed primary market*.
- **Good strategy** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): three objectives and eight growth adjacencies make a list, not a kernel. See the Rep.

## 9. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For S&P:** the slogans change, but the moat is real and visible outside S&P's own releases. Ratings is a regulated oligopoly. The S&P 500 is in ETF prospectuses. iBoxx and CDX/iTraxx are written into derivatives contracts. None of that depends on a tagline.
- **Against:** the businesses that grew fastest after the merger (Mobility, Engineering Solutions, OSTTRA) are the ones it sold. The core that remains is mature. Growth now has to come from AI distribution and private markets, where S&P is a buyer of assets (With Intelligence at $1.8B for ~$130M of revenue), not the incumbent.

### Rep
**(a) Price the cycle.** In June 2022 S&P said Ratings revenue could fall "by as much as $600 million" against guidance. FY2021 Ratings revenue was $4.10B with a 64.2% adjusted margin. Assume the lost revenue falls through at the incremental margin of ~80% (costs are mostly analysts' salaries). What is the operating-profit hit, and what share of FY2021 Ratings operating profit is it? *(Answer: $600M × 0.8 = **$480M**. FY2021 Ratings adjusted operating profit ≈ $4.10B × 0.642 ≈ $2.63B, so the hit is **~18%**.)* Compare with what actually happened: transaction revenue fell 31% / 44% / 56% in Q1–Q3 2022.

**(b) The refinancing wall.** Investor Day 2025 shows **$8.2T** of S&P-rated corporate debt maturing over three years vs a **$6.5T** historical average. If S&P earns the same fee per dollar refinanced, by how much does the wall lift that slice of transaction revenue over the three years? *(Answer: 8.2 / 6.5 − 1 ≈ **+26%**, the figure S&P prints.)* Name two reasons the real lift will be smaller: refinancing in private credit, which S&P may not rate, and fee caps or frequent-issuer discounts.

**(c) Rumelt's kernel for S&P 2027, in ≤120 words.** Diagnosis: *AI and venues can now produce numbers that used to require S&P; the moat is the regulated, must-cite standard, not the desktop.* Guiding policy: *be the cited source inside every model and venue, paid per use; stop defending the screen.* Write three coherent actions, then score each against H1, H2 and H10. Hint: putting RatingsXpress into LLMs helps H1 but worsens H10.
