# Note · MarketAxess through outside eyes — a chorus that praises all-to-all in a crisis and automation in calm, a handful of independents who name the tradeoff, and competitors who are never in the room

> Stakeholder / outside-view note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy: buyer and supplier power, coopetition, the silent rival), **Course 08** (Negotiation & Influence: reading testimonials for incentive and hedge), **Course 07** (Data & Decisions: selection bias in a curated corpus), **Course 11** (Global Strategy & Macro: China, India, LatAm and MENA entry via local institutions), **E8** (Stakeholder & IR: whose voice gets printed), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation: data partners as distributors and judges) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[marketaxess-hub]]; companions [[marketaxess-people]], [[marketaxess-mission-forces]], [[marketaxess-open-trading]], [[marketaxess-data-cp-plus]]; mirror note [[tradeweb-outside-voices]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/marketaxess/articles.jsonl` (359 items, Jan 2019 – Oct 23 2026): ~119 IR releases (partner releases with co-quotes), 32 earnings narratives, 7 CEO letters, 2 investor decks, ~236 Knowledge Center items (AxessPoint research, panel write-ups, 14 media reprints from Bloomberg, The TRADE, The DESK/Markets Media, GlobalCapital, WatersTechnology, Asian Private Banker, Hubbis). Only items that quote or cite a non-MarketAxess speaker are used. Knowledge Center dates are often estimates (*est.* or *undated*).
- **Selection-bias warning (it applies to every line below):** MarketAxess chose every voice here. Partner quotes are co-drafted releases; client quotes are launch testimonials; panel write-ups are written by MarketAxess staff; reprints were reposted because they were net-positive. There is **no** unhappy client, no fee complaint, no analyst, no activist, no regulator criticising MarketAxess, no competitor speaking for itself, and **no outside voice at all on the ICE sale** other than ICE. The handful of critical sentences that got through therefore count for more. Read each quote as *what this speaker was willing to say on MarketAxess's letterhead*.
- **Core claim:** Outside voices fall into four groups. (1) **Buy-side clients** (BlackRock, DWS, Julius Baer, MacKay Shields, Eagle AM, UBS AM, Nikko, AB, Banque de Luxembourg) praise **workflow relief and benchmark pricing** — automate small tickets, price lists against CP+, fewer errors — not price improvement as such. (2) **Dealers and non-bank liquidity providers** (Goldman, Barclays, JPMorgan, Citadel, Flow Traders, Virtu, Jane Street) praise **their own systematic liquidity** and the protocol, and keep the venue at arm's length. (3) **Partners** (Refinitiv, MSCI, S&P Global, ICE Bonds, Aladdin, Arcesium, DirectBooks, Moment, IMTC, CFETS/Bond Connect, Investortools) want MarketAxess's **data and network inside their own rails**; several (ICE, S&P, MSCI) end up owning or depending on the flow. (4) A thin layer of **independents and sceptics** — Bloomberg News on auctions, an Aberdeen trader on axes, a BNP e-trading head on AI, Hong Kong compliance on PT, Coalition Greenwich on Japan — supply nearly all the friction. Power read: **BlackRock** (seeded Open Trading, sits on the board via an alumnus, is the Aladdin gatekeeper, advises on auctions, anchors India) and **ICE** (partner 2024, index-futures lister, then acquirer 2026) hold the most leverage; **S&P Global** becomes a dependant whose evaluated pricing runs on an ICE-owned feed after close.

## 2. What clients value — and what they fear — in their own words

**What they value (ranked by how often it recurs):**

1. **Automating the small tickets so humans handle the big ones.** Rosskopf (Julius Baer, 2019) above. Surti (UBS AM, est. 2024): "Given the size and number of lines we have, automating trades give us great efficiencies." Chylinski (Eagle AM, 2021 deck): "We’re now providing liquidity to the market, rather than just taking it." Lee (Agilon, 2021 deck): "this new frontier of automation will be a critical tool." Stoll (Banque de Luxembourg, 2020): "Today’s market requires cutting-edge technology, access to the broadest possible network of liquidity".
2. **A benchmark price they can defend.** Ruskulis (MacKay) on CP+ for lists, above. Lenamon (DWS, 2026): "CP+ is a great tool to help us potentially minimize tracking error" — note the *potentially*. Catrambone (DWS): auctions put "the public bond market on its front-foot in competing with other capital markets" (The TRADE, 2025-12-15).
3. **Access to new markets through one screen.** Severino (Nikko): taxable munis offer "an attractive yield pick-up with broader portfolio diversification" (2020-04-01). Vara (BlackRock): "India offers compelling opportunities for foreign investors" (2025-07-30). Leung (BlackRock) and Chang (PIMCO) on China index inclusion (2021-09-27).
4. **Social-purpose dealers in the workflow.** Seelaus: "Expanding market access for women-led organizations is a challenge" (2021-05-03). Switzer (AB): "Technology is the catalyst for driving progress in the fixed income markets" (same release). The program disappears from the corpus after 2023.
5. **Seeing who is reliable.** Harrison (RBC BlueBay): "The Street is much more bought in than it was previously" (TRFQ panel, undated ~2024–25).

**What they fear or complain about (scarce, therefore weighted up):**

- **Axe quality and data reliability.** Aberdeen's Beer: "It’s a data war out there" (TRFQ panel). The same write-up records that "dealers fail to regularly update axes". At the 2026 FIX panel (with Tradeweb's Mike Roffey and Schroders), axes were contested enough that MarketAxess published a rebuttal conceding "there is real work to do on measurement, transparency, and incentive design" and that two-way axes "attract understandable scepticism" (est. 2026-02).
- **Best execution for baskets.** Hong Kong buy-side traders said their firms "are still in discussions internally about how to adhere to best execution requirements" for portfolio trades (undated).
- **Liquidity that disappears in stress.** Chowdhury, reporting the client experience in Asia HY: "a meltdown across the region's high-yield issuers prompted an evaporation of liquidity" (APB, 2022-08-22). MKTX's own Asia blocks piece concedes that dealers "could adopt a more defensive strategy when pricing bonds in anonymous liquidity pools." (2025).
- **Transparency for others, not for me.** Costa, summarising Asian clients: "our clients have an absolute craving for transparency but potentially not their own." (The TRADE, 2025-07-11).
- **AI hype.** BNP Paribas's European Head of E-Trading, Alexandre Guignot: "What I haven't seen materialise yet is how we transform the trading desk cockpit" (AI panel, est. 2026-06).
- **Accountability that cannot be outsourced.** Paul Graham (ex-UBS IB Ops) on regulatory reporting vendors: they can "reconcile your data vs other users of their platform" — but the senior manager stays on the hook (undated).

## 3. Dealers and liquidity providers — participants, customers, rivals

**Banks as launch partners.** Each dealer quote markets the bank's own desk. Goldman connected "our systematic liquidity" to Live Markets (2020). Barclays' Lada expected Live Markets to "lead to more workflow automation on the buy-side and sell-side." (2021). JPMorgan's Glezer spoke about JPMorgan's PT growth, not MarketAxess's (2021). Citadel's Clayton praised automation of "the repo trade confirmation process" (2019). No bank speaks in the corpus about **Open Trading** itself — the protocol that lets clients bypass them — mirroring Tradeweb's dealers' silence on AllTrade ([[tradeweb-outside-voices]] §3).

**The order book nobody named a number for.** Goldman (Oct 2020), Barclays (May 2021) and Flow Traders (Dec 2021) became Live Markets streamers; Schiffman warned it "requires commitment from all participants to succeed." No volume was ever disclosed. *Inference:* dealers lent their names, not their balance sheets.

**Non-banks: from partner to board.** Virtu was a 2019 ETF partner; the RFQ-hub consortium (Virtu, Citadel Securities, Flow Traders, Jane Street, BlackRock) followed in 2022 and MKTX took ~90% control in May 2025; Citadel Securities began pricing US IG PTs (What's New, undated); Cifu joined the board in March 2026. MKTX’s own Dec 2025 Goldman Sachs deck says 22% of Open Trading volume was responded to by non-dealers. **Incentive read:** non-banks need an anonymous pool to reach clients who do not give them lines; banks prefer disclosed RFQ. MKTX's 2024–26 product turn (DealerAxess, Mid-X, Targeted Axes, Disclosed Open Trading) is a courtship of bank dealers — the counterweight to a liquidity mix tilting to algos and non-banks ([[marketaxess-protocols-automation]]).

**Bloomberg's framing of the auction.** The only outside sentence about dealer economics in the corpus: auctions may squeeze "dealer profits tied to the very inefficiencies it would eliminate." Concannon's reply in the same piece is that dealers can "exit inventory" or provide liquidity in the auction. The tension is real and unresolved.

## 5. Official sector, research and independent framing

**Official endorsement (as presented by MarketAxess):**
1. **NY Fed SMCCF (2020-09-09).** MarketAxess named an *Eligible Seller*; the release quotes only McVey. The endorsement is the designation itself.
2. **Fed Governor Brainard, cited on a 2021 Investor Day slide** about wider access to all-to-all platforms. A speech excerpt reused as a sales argument.
3. **PIMCO's Treasury paper (MKTX blog, undated ~2022–23).** MKTX claims: "The views expressed in the paper reflect our own". The blog admits UST adoption "may be slow to evolve with us".
4. **Coalition Greenwich.** McPartland's all-to-all-Treasuries line (2022); the 2021 statistic that all-to-all was "12% of US investment grade volume in 2020, with MarketAxess accounting for the majority" (May 2021 release). On Japan, Greenwich data cut the other way: 41% of Japan credit flow allocated by sales relationship, and dealers "hesitant to fully embrace the electronic shift" (2024-09).

**Sceptics and independent framing (the complete set I could locate):**
1. **Bloomberg News (2025-11-05):** the dealer-profit tradeoff above; plus the reporter's caveat that "success will depend on adoption" — and Bloomberg LP's disclosure that it competes with MarketAxess and Tradeweb.
2. **Aberdeen's Beer:** "It’s a data war out there" (axes).
3. **FIX panel 2026 (Tradeweb, Schroders, Neptune, Susquehanna):** the axe-quality challenge that prompted MKTX's defensive note.
4. **BNP's Guignot:** AI has not yet transformed the cockpit.
5. **DWS's Lenamon:** an endorsement hedged with *potentially*.
6. **Hong Kong buy-side compliance:** PT best-ex unresolved.
7. **Morgan Stanley strategists on Argentina (2025-09-18):** election results "increase the probability of the central downside scenario" — macro scepticism MKTX reprinted to explain a volume spike.
8. **An unnamed Tier 1 dealer on MENA (2025-12-11):** "clearly the voice enquiries can be extremely large" — voice still wins the big tickets.
9. **MarketAxess's own self-critical research** (inside voices conceding outside points): Tradability "does not directly address the challenge of information leakage" (2023); "Larger and less liquid trades may still call for more selective distribution." ## 6. Competitors as seen in the corpus

- **Tradeweb.** Never named as a competitor by a MarketAxess speaker. Appears as: consolidated-tape JV partner (announced 2022-06-22, signed 2023-05-23, ended 2023-12-08); a panellist (Roffey) at the FIX axe debate; and in Bloomberg's disclosure line. **Not said:** share, PT, Ai-Price vs CP+, Trumid.
- **Bloomberg.** JV partner; reprint publisher with a competitor disclosure; in the 2021 deck only inside a Greenwich quote on private banks ("historic strength for BBG" per the deck). **Not said:** Bloomberg's RFQ share, the terminal as distribution.
- **ICE.** Partner (ICE Bonds link 2024, futures listing 2024), then acquirer. **Not said:** how ICE's own evaluated pricing and retail ATS coexist with CP+ and Open Trading.
- **Trumid.** **Absent** in all 359 items, even after Tradeweb agreed to buy it (Oct 2026). The silence is data.
- **IDBs and voice.** Framed as the incumbent to disrupt: DealerAxess and Mid-X pitch against "traditional IDBs"; MENA and Swiss franc blocks stay voice. Brazil: MKTX is "one of four accredited platforms" (~May 2026).
- **Risk-factor boilerplate** is the only place competition is named as a force: "the level and intensity of competition in the fixed-income electronic trading industry and the pricing pressures that may result".
- **Conspicuously absent:** any rival criticising MarketAxess; any client saying it *moved flow away*; any regulator quoted on MarketAxess; antitrust commentary on ICE–MarketAxess; market-data price complaints.

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Coopetition** ([[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]]): Tradeweb and Bloomberg as tape co-bidders; ICE as partner, futures lister and buyer; Virtu as partner, consortium member and director source; LSEG (Refinitiv) as distributor and Tradeweb's owner. Almost every node sits in two boxes.
- **Buyer and supplier power** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): concentrated buyers (BlackRock/Aladdin, DWS, State Street) and concentrated liquidity suppliers (top dealers plus algos and non-banks) squeeze the venue. The 2024–26 dealer suite reads as supplier appeasement.
- **Multi-sided platforms** ([[canon-hagiu-wright-multi-sided-platforms]], [[canon-parker-platform-revolution]]): launch releases pair a client with a dealer; auctions needed an advisory group of four asset managers before dealers would come.
- **Power** ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]): dependence = importance × lack of alternatives. BlackRock and ICE score high on both; S&P's dependence rises after close.
- **Testimonial framing** ([[canon-cialdini-influence]]): authority (NY Fed, Brainard, PIMCO) and social proof (BlackRock first trades). Discount quotes from future employees (Lee, 2021), future directors (Cifu, 2019) and sellers (Mechner).
- **Information goods** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): every partner wants the inquiry data, including trades that did not happen. The fight is over who distributes it — and ICE settled it by buying.
- **Agency** ([[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]]): dealers in releases are selling their desks; buy-side traders want defensible best-ex. *Potentially minimize tracking error* is an agent's sentence.
- **WYSIATI** ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]]): a curated sample; absence of criticism is not evidence of absence.

**Role-play: you run S&P Global Market Intelligence's evaluated bond pricing.** ICE (your competitor in evaluated pricing) will own MarketAxess, whose CP+ feeds your engine (Oct 2024 partnership), by 1H 2027. Using only the voices above, write the four questions you'd put to MarketAxess before close, one line each.
