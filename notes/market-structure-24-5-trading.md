# Note · 24/5 Trading — what "always-on" markets mean for equities and fixed income

> Practitioner / market-structure note · Maps to **Course 02** (Corporate Finance & Valuation), **Course 11** (Global Strategy & Macro — Odd Lots), **E-track** systems thinking · Written 2026-09-21

- **Source:** DTCC — *NSCC now live with 24x5 clearing* (June 29, 2026) and *The Shift to 24x5 Trading* (white paper); SEC accelerated approval of NYSE Arca 22-hour trading (Feb 2025); Nasdaq / Cboe EDGX 24×5 announcements (2025, targeting H2 2026); Blue Ocean ATS volume disclosures (2025–26); Bloomberg — *Citadel Securities Taps Asian Institutions in 24-Hour Push* (Sept 17, 2026); Markets Media — *Blue Ocean ATS resumes after cancelling trades* (Aug 2024).
- **Core claim:** 24/5 is not "more hours." It is the **equity market converging on the structure rates and FX already have** — a continuous global session with a thin overnight core — and the bottleneck is moving from *trading* to *plumbing* (clearing → settlement → funding → humans). Where the plumbing is 24h (Treasuries, futures) the transition is boring; where it isn't (corporate credit, bank funding, fund NAVs) the transition creates dislocation you can price.
- **Framework — the five-layer stack (each layer must be 24/5 or the one above it is fiction):**

  | Layer | Status (Sept 2026) | Who owns it |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1. Venue (matching) | Blue Ocean ATS 8pm–4am since 2023 (~83% of overnight volume); 24X National Exchange approved; NYSE Arca 22h approved, launch "late 2026"; Nasdaq/Cboe H2 2026 | Exchanges, ATSs |
  | 2. Tape (SIP / consolidated quote) | Proposed Sun 8pm → Fri 8pm ET with a short nightly pause; gated on clearing + TRF | CTA / UTP plans |
  | 3. Clearing (novation, margin) | **Live** — NSCC 24x5, Sun 8pm → Fri 8pm ET, since 2026-06-29. Trade date now rolls at 8pm ET. | DTCC |
  | 4. Settlement & funding (cash, collateral) | **Not 24/5.** T+1 since May 2024; Fedwire Funds Service still closes ~7pm and reopens 9pm ET (Fed's 22x7 target is 2027). Overnight margin calls need pre-funded cash. | Fed, banks, tri-party repo |
  | 5. Humans (risk desks, fund ops, compliance) | Mostly 9-to-5 | Buy side, broker-dealers |

  **Rule:** a session is only as "open" as its *least* open layer. Layers 1–3 were solved 2023–2026. The 24/5 story for the next two years is layers 4 and 5.

- **Why it happened — the canon lenses:**
  - **Disruption from below** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): Robinhood + Blue Ocean launched 24/5 in 2023 for a customer incumbents didn't serve — **Asia-Pacific retail, for whom "overnight" is lunchtime** (75–80% of overnight flow). Incumbents dismissed it as tiny (it *was* tiny: ~$375B notional across all of 2025 ≈ $1.5B/night vs. ~$600B+ per regular session, i.e. well under 1%). Then NYSE, Nasdaq and Cboe all filed to follow. Classic: the entrant wins on a dimension (access) the incumbent's best customers didn't ask for.
  - **10X force / inflection** ([[canon-grove-only-the-paranoid-survive]]): three forces stacked — (1) Asian retail demand for US megacaps, (2) T+1 and NSCC modernization making the plumbing cheaper to extend, (3) tokenized stocks/Treasuries trading 24/7 on-chain as a *credible threat* to the exchange franchise. Exchanges are extending hours partly on defense.
  - **Liquidity is a network effect** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]], [[canon-parker-platform-revolution]]): order flow attracts market makers, which tightens spreads, which attracts flow. That's why one ATS has ~83% share overnight (winner-take-most) — and why Citadel Securities recruiting *Asian sovereign funds and institutions* (Sept 2026) matters more than any exchange filing: it's the supply side of the network, not the demand side, that's scarce at 2am.
  - **Adverse selection in thin hours** ([[canon-akerlof-market-for-lemons]] + Kyle's lambda in `quant-backtesting/research/main-compilation.md`): overnight, the ratio of *informed* to *uninformed* flow rises (Asia macro, BoJ/ECB decisions, earnings drift, geopolitical headlines) while the uninformed pool (401k flows, index rebalances) is absent. Market makers price this by **widening spreads and shrinking depth** — the "lemons" premium. 24/5 doesn't fix that; it makes it visible.
  - **The close as a focal point** ([[canon-schelling-strategy-of-conflict]]): the 4pm closing auction is where index funds, mutual-fund NAVs, options settlement and VWAP benchmarks coordinate. Extending hours has *not* diluted it — volume keeps concentrating at the close because everyone expects everyone else there. **Batching beats continuous trading when liquidity is thin** ([[canon-ohno-toyota-production-system]] inverted: continuous flow is efficient only when demand is dense; otherwise batch). Expect the close to matter *more*, not less.
  - **The constraint moves** ([[canon-goldratt-the-goal]]): elevate one bottleneck and the next appears. Exchange hours → clearing (solved June 2026) → SIP → **bank funding hours and intraday liquidity** → staffing. Whoever solves the 2am margin-call funding problem (pre-funding, tokenized collateral, Fedwire 22x7) captures the next rent.
  - **No reset window** ([[canon-perrow-normal-accidents]], [[canon-taleb-antifragile]]): the overnight close was a *forced pause* — a circuit breaker by default. 24/5 removes it. On **Aug 5, 2024** (yen carry unwind) Blue Ocean's system was overwhelmed and it *cancelled an hour of overnight trades* — a normal accident in a tightly coupled system with no slack. The gap risk that used to be "unhedgeable but bounded to the open" becomes "hedgeable but at 2am, at 5x the spread, with your margin clerk asleep."

- **Equities — what changes (and what doesn't):**
  1. **Price discovery shifts, volume doesn't (yet).** Overnight is <1–3% of volume even on record nights (Blue Ocean's record: 490M shares ≈ 3–4% of a normal US day). But *information* arrives 24h, so the overnight print becomes the reference price for the open — like futures already are.
  2. **Earnings and macro get traded directly.** The 4:15pm earnings drop and the 3am ECB decision now hit cash equities, not just futures/ADRs. Expect more of the daily move to occur outside 9:30–4 (it already does: most of the equity risk premium historically accrued *overnight*).
  3. **Best-execution and benchmarks get harder.** Institutions can't easily justify trading at 2am spreads against a VWAP benchmark; FINRA's extended-hours disclosure rules (risk disclosures, no guarantee of liquidity) apply. So the buy side stays mostly at the close → **retail trades against professionals overnight** (the lemons problem, personified).
  4. **Corporate actions and trade-date get weird.** With trade date rolling at 8pm ET, a 9pm Monday trade is a *Tuesday* trade for settlement, ex-dividend and record-date purposes. Ops teams, not traders, absorb this.
  5. **Behavioral cost** ([[canon-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow]], [[canon-ariely-predictably-irrational]]): always-on prices → more checking → more trading → worse returns (Barber & Odean: "Trading is hazardous to your wealth"). 24/5 is a feature for the venue's take rate and a bug for the median retail account.

- **Fixed income — the asymmetric part:**
  1. **Treasuries were already ~23/5.** Cash Treasuries hand off Tokyo → London → New York; CME Treasury futures trade Sun 6pm → Fri 5pm with a 1h break. Equities are *catching up to rates*, not leading. Depth still thins overnight (the Oct 15, 2014 flash rally happened at 9:33am — in *liquid* hours — so overnight fragility is real), but the structure exists.
  2. **Corporate and muni bonds are not continuous at all.** They trade OTC by dealer RFQ (Tradeweb, MarketAxess) during New York hours. There is no overnight cash credit market.
  3. **So bond ETFs become the overnight credit market.** HYG, LQD, JNK trade as equities. When equities go 24/5, these ETFs trade at 2am while *nothing underneath them does*. Authorized participants cannot create/redeem (no bond trading) and cannot hedge with cash bonds (only with CDX or Treasury futures) → **wider ETF spreads, larger premium/discount to NAV, and the ETF price becomes the only credit price on the planet at 2am ET**. This is March 2020 (HYG/LQD at discounts) every night, in miniature — not "broken," but *the* price discovery, with a liquidity charge embedded.
  4. **Dispersion across fixed-income ETFs is structural, not random.** Treasury ETFs (TLT, IEF, SHV) can be hedged with 23h futures → tight overnight. Credit ETFs can't → wide. Expect the overnight bid/ask ratio (HYG : TLT) to be the cleanest empirical measure of "how 24/5 is the underlying, really."
  5. **The plumbing argument is louder in rates.** The SEC's Treasury central-clearing mandate (cash Dec 2026, repo June 2027), T+1, and the Fed's 22x7 Fedwire plan (2027) are all the same story as NSCC 24x5: **the risk-free asset and the risk-free payment rail are being made continuous** because a 24/5 equity market with margin calls needs a 24/5 place to raise cash. Tokenized Treasuries (e.g. BlackRock BUIDL) trading 24/7 on-chain are the outside pressure — a 24/7 risk-free rate makes 9-to-5 collateral look broken.
  6. **NAV-based products are the last holdout.** Mutual funds still strike NAV at 4pm. A 24/5 market with a daily NAV is a *free option for informed traders* if fund flows can be timed against stale NAVs (this was the 2003 mutual-fund late-trading scandal). Watch for fair-value pricing rules and "swing pricing" to expand.

- **Second-order effects** *(added 2026-09-21 — the first-order story is "more hours, thinner liquidity"; these are the consequences of *that*)*

  **The new fact that drives half of them:** on **2026-08-05** the SEC approved **Amendment No. 27 to the LULD Plan** (Rel. 34-106042), creating **Overnight Protections** effective **2026-12-06**:
  - **Overnight Protected Hours = 9:00pm ET Sun–Thu → 4:00am ET.**
  - Bands = **±20%** off the *wider* of two reference prices: the **official closing price** and the **consolidated last round-lot sale at 7:45pm ET**. Leveraged ETPs: 20% × leverage ratio. Sub-$1 stocks: minimum $1 band.
  - **No automatic Trading Pause** when a band is hit — trades and quotes outside the band are simply prevented.
  - The primary listing exchange **may declare a Regulatory Halt** (explicitly including "when orders are consistently being placed outside the bands or the price bands are otherwise limiting price discovery") — and **a halt overnight does not reopen overnight.** Orders are rejected until the next session.
  - Bands **end at 4:00am on purpose**, so pre-market earnings can reprice free of stale prior-day anchors.
  - It's **Phase 1, explicitly interim**; Participants gather data and file a Phase 2.

  1. **Overnight risk transfer degrades in a step function, not a curve — so the hedge fails in the state you bought it for.** Normal → ±20% band → *gone until 9:30am*. A circuit breaker pauses and reopens; this one shuts and stays shut. Second-order: nobody who *needs* the hedge will use the overnight cash session (they use CME futures and options, which have real 23h sessions with reopening pauses). Which means overnight cash flow stays retail, stays adversely selected, spreads stay wide, institutions stay away — **a self-reinforcing equilibrium in which 24/5 is structurally stuck as a retail product.** The most important second-order effect: the rulebook may have locked in the very composition the venues are trying to escape.
  2. **Liquidity provision consolidates; access rises while execution quality falls.** 24h staffing, 24h risk systems and pre-funded overnight capital are fixed costs spread over ~1–3% of daily volume. Only the largest firms clear that hurdle ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]] — scale economies). Fewer wholesalers competing overnight → **less price improvement on the same PFOF order**. The retail investor gains the right to trade at 2am and loses basis points doing it. Citadel Securities recruiting Asian sovereign funds (Sept 2026) is the tell: the scarce side is *supply*, and it's being locked up bilaterally.
  3. **The 4pm close gets *more* powerful, not less — and becomes a bigger manipulation target.** Note what the new rule does: it anchors the overnight bands to *the official closing price*. Regulation is now reinforcing the focal point ([[canon-schelling-strategy-of-conflict]]). Meanwhile thousands of contracts hang off "the official closing price on the principal exchange": index levels, ETF/mutual-fund NAV, options settlement, structured-product barriers and autocallable knock-ins, ISDA valuation time, VWAP collars in merger agreements, 10b5-1 triggers, employee equity. Second-order: the economic value of a print in the closing auction rises as *more of the clock* references it. Expect closing-auction marking to become a live enforcement theme.
  4. **Friday night becomes the new after-hours.** Today "after the close" is a real digestion window; under 24/5 it doesn't exist — and the SEC's stated reason for ending bands at 4am is to let pre-market earnings reprice unconstrained. Second-order: issuer disclosure migrates to the two remaining windows — **4:00–9:30am** (no bands, real liquidity, deliberate regulatory design) for ordinary news, and **Friday 8pm → Sunday 8pm** (the only genuine pause left in the system) for the ugly stuff. Watch 8-K timing distributions shift; the weekend is where bad news and crisis resolutions will concentrate because it is the last place with slack ([[canon-perrow-normal-accidents]]).
  5. **Best execution becomes unverifiable overnight, which is itself a deterrent.** LULD now extends overnight, but the Reg NMS order-protection architecture — NBBO, trade-through — was built for 9:30–4, and one ATS holds ~83% of overnight volume, so the "national" best bid is effectively one book. Second-order: an institution cannot defend a 2am fill against any benchmark, so it won't trade there (loop to #1). Third-order: exchanges will sell proprietary overnight feeds against a thin SIP, so **the pro/retail information gap is widest in the session retail dominates** ([[canon-akerlof-market-for-lemons]] again, one level up).
  6. **Fixed income: price discovery inverts, then goes circular.** Credit ETFs are the only credit price at 2am, with APs unable to create/redeem or hedge in cash bonds. Second-order: overnight ETF prints start feeding *into* the evaluated-pricing services (BVAL, ICE) that mark bond funds, insurance books and bank portfolios. A thin, adversely selected *equity*-session print becomes an input to the valuation of trillions in illiquid credit. Third-order: risk models calibrated on evaluated prices inherit that noise — **VaR and margin move at 2am for reasons that have nothing to do with credit.** Tight coupling plus interactive complexity, which is Perrow's exact recipe.
  7. **Collateral gets pre-positioned → a structural bid for HQLA, and a nightly hole in the backstop.** 24x5 clearing plus a Fedwire that closes in the evening means a margin call can land when the payment rail is shut. Firms respond by holding more T-bills and MMF shares than economics alone would justify — a persistent front-end bid and a drag on market-maker ROE, which is charged back as spread (loop to #2). Third-order: this is the strongest *non-crypto* demand driver for **tokenized Treasuries and MMF shares** (BUIDL and peers), and the real argument for the Fed's 22x7 Fedwire plan. The deepest version: **if the market is open and the central bank's rail is closed, the lender of last resort has a nightly outage window.** Expect the extension to be justified on financial-stability grounds, not efficiency.
  8. **Mutual funds lose to ETFs faster.** A 4pm-struck NAV against a market that prices 23 hours is a stale price — a free option for the informed, which is the legal-form echo of the 2003 late-trading scandal. Second-order: pressure on fair-value and swing pricing, and an accelerant for ETF share classes and outright fund conversions. 24/5 doesn't start that migration; it raises the cost of not doing it.
  9. **"Daily return" stops being well-defined — a regime break in everyone's data.** The NSCC trade date rolls at **8pm**, the protected session runs 9pm–4am, the official close is 4pm, and the band reference is 7:45pm. Close-to-close variance, VIX conventions anchored to 9:30–4:15 SPX settlement, and the well-documented **overnight equity risk premium** (most of the historical equity return accrued outside the session) all sit on definitions that are now ambiguous — and the overnight premium, once directly tradable, is exactly the kind of anomaly that gets arbitraged toward zero. Second-order for *this repo*: any backtest spanning 2026 needs the session definition as an explicit parameter, or it silently splices two different regimes (see `quant-backtesting/`).
  10. **No batch window, no maintenance window, no reset.** Overnight is when reconciliation, corporate-action processing, deploys and batch jobs run. Second-order: firms are forced into real-time architecture, with a cyber attack surface manned by a skeleton crew; those who can't afford it outsource to a new vendor category (overnight risk/ops desk-as-a-service) — which becomes its own concentrated systemic node. Removing slack from a tightly coupled system is precisely what makes accidents "normal."
  11. **The corporate bid is absent overnight.** Rule 10b-18's safe-harbor timing conditions are built around the regular session, so buybacks — the single largest structural bid in US equities — cannot support a 2am drawdown. Second-order: overnight selloffs have one fewer stabilizer, and the gap between overnight and regular-session price behavior is partly a *mechanical* artifact of who is allowed to be in the market.
  12. **It's a prisoner's dilemma for the venues.** Once one exchange goes 24/5, the rest must follow to protect marginal flow. Everyone takes the cost, nobody gains share, and the customer pays wider spreads — rivalry on a dimension that doesn't raise willingness to pay ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]). The durable winners are the ones not competing on it: the incumbent overnight ATS (network effect), the two or three largest market makers (scale), **DTCC** (a clearing monopoly billing the same fee base over more hours), and the plumbing vendors.

  **Seam to watch:** the ATSs open at **8:00pm** and NSCC's trade date rolls at **8:00pm**, but the Processors and the overnight bands don't start until **9:00pm**. That hour trades with clearing live and consolidated protections not yet on. Phase 1 is explicitly interim — that seam, and the ±20% band that only binds in a crash, are the two things Phase 2 will be judged on.

- **Applies when:** evaluating exchange/broker economics (Nasdaq, ICE, Cboe, HOOD, IBKR); designing execution or risk policy for a fund; pricing overnight liquidity in ETFs; reading any headline that says "24-hour stock market" — ask *which layer of the stack* they mean.
- **Breaks when:**
  - Institutions never come: overnight stays <2% of volume, spreads stay wide, and 24/5 remains a retail/Asia product. Then the "price discovery shifts" claim is overstated — the futures market already did this job.
  - A second Aug-2024-style outage during a real crisis (a Sunday-night geopolitical shock) makes regulators impose *more* structure (mandatory pauses, batch auctions overnight) — i.e. the system re-batches itself.
  - Funding layer (4) stays 9-to-5 for years. Then clearing at 24x5 just moves the credit risk to clearing members who must pre-fund, raising the cost of market making overnight → wider spreads, the opposite of the promise.
  - Tokenization leapfrogs it: if tokenized equities/Treasuries with on-chain settlement reach institutional scale, 24/5 on legacy rails is a transitional artifact.
- **Practitioner counterpoint:** the sell side (Citadel Securities, Blue Ocean, exchanges) frames this as *access* and *global inclusion*; the buy side mostly shrugs — no benchmark, no staffing, best-ex risk, and the closing auction is where their liquidity is. Both are right at different layers. The trader's version: **"nothing that matters trades overnight until the day something matters happens overnight."** The Aug 5, 2024 yen unwind was that day; the venue failed. The next test decides whether 24/5 is a product or a market.

### Rep
Pull the overnight (8pm–4am ET) bid/ask for **TLT** and **HYG** on the same night from your broker or Blue Ocean data, and the regular-session spread the next morning. Compute the ratio (overnight ÷ regular) for each. Then answer in one paragraph: *which layer of the five-layer stack explains the difference between the two ratios?* If you can hedge it with a 23-hour future, it's tight; if the underlying is closed, you're paying the lemons premium.
