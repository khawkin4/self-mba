# Note · LSEG Post Trade — LCH, SwapClear and the customer-owned model: LSEG keeps buying back the economics it once shared with its bank members, then re-shares a slice to keep them aligned

> Market-structure / incentive-design note · Maps to **Course 03** (Microeconomics — mutualisation, network effects, revenue-sharing contracts), **Course 08** (Negotiation — buying out a profit share from your own customers), **E3** (M&A — minority buy-ins, Acadia/Quantile bolt-ons, Euronext's exit), **E9** (Geopolitics — EU clearing location policy), **E6** (Crisis — CCP default management) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[lseg-hub]]; companions [[lseg-refinitiv-deal]], [[lseg-capital-allocation]]; compare [[tradeweb-derivatives-regulation]]

- **Source:** LSEG corpus: LCH and Post Trade press releases (~80 kept, 2019–2026), the Post Trade investor webinar (2024-10-01), results calls, the Post Trade Solutions (PTS) release (2025-10-23), Euronext items (2021-11-08, 2023-06-26), and AR principal risks (EU equivalence, CCP risk). **Caveat:** LCH's own disclosures (CPMI-IOSCO quantitative disclosures, LCH Ltd/SA accounts) were not scraped. Numbers are as LSEG states them.
- **Core claim:** LCH's SwapClear is LSEG's deepest moat: ">90% global share of cleared interest rate swap notional", $1,941trn cleared in 2025. It was built in partnership with dealer-members who were paid a share of its "revenue surplus" (~30% to 2035). In October 2025 LSEG paid **£1.15bn** to cut that share to 15% (2025), then 10% (2026), extended to 2045. In the same deal it sold **20% of Post Trade Solutions** to 11 of those banks for £170m. LSEG's own words: "replicating the original LCH model." The same pattern repeats elsewhere: buy out minorities in LCH Group (to ~95%), buy Euronext's 11.1% of LCH SA, absorb Euronext's departure after the Borsa sale. LSEG centralises the economics while keeping customers inside the governance. The bet for the next decade is the uncleared market, which LSEG says is "comparable to that of the cleared segment."

## 2. The SwapClear revenue-share trade, decoded

- **What changed.** Founding SwapClear members had been entitled to ~30% of SwapClear's "revenue surplus" through 2035. The cost to LSEG in 2024 was €0.2bn, booked in cost of sales. The share now falls to 15% (2025, retroactive) and 10% from 2026, and the banks extended the 10% from 2035 to 2045. LSEG paid £1.15bn in two instalments plus up to £200m on targets.
- **What LSEG said it gets.** Accretion to Markets and group EBITDA margin, and "approximately 2-3% accretive to AEPS in 2025". In H1 2026, 140bp of the +260bp margin gain came from this change.
- **What the banks got.** 20% of PTS at a valuation analysts found rich. Arnaud Giblat (BNP Paribas Exane): "Banks are paying over 50 times EBITDA, 9 times sales for their stake." (2025-10-23). They also got three board seats at PTS and a 10% SwapClear share for 20 years.
- **How LSEG framed it.** "Our SwapClear business was at the forefront of innovation when it was founded in collaboration with our clearing members 25 years ago" (Maguire, 2025-10-23). Troy Rohrbaugh (JPMorgan): "Our partnership with SwapClear has been highly successful in growing and scaling the first interest rate swaps clearing service into an established and profitable business."

*Read (inference):* this is a negotiated swap of cash-flow rights between a utility and its members. LSEG paid ~£1.15bn up front for roughly €0.1bn+/yr of saved cost on today's base, which rises with volumes. The banks took cash and kept a governance seat in the *next* utility (PTS). It is a cleaner version of the 1999 SwapClear founding bargain.

## 3. Business model, as LSEG explains it

- **Open access is the pitch.** "We continue to be very committed to our open access model where if you execute somewhere else you can clear with us." (Schwimmer, 2021-10-01). LCH boilerplate: "LCH. The Markets’ Partner."
- **Risk is the product.** "nobody wants to buy the world’s cheapest parachute." (Maguire, 2024-10-01). "As a systemically important institution, LCH’s commitment to ensuring financial stability is paramount." (Maguire, 2021-01-11)
- **Volatility pays.** "In general, market volatility is also an important driver of performance for our Post Trade division." (Maguire, AR 2022). Net Treasury Income on members' cash collateral swings with rates: +31% in Q3 2022, then down as balances fell, then +21.3% in Q3 2026.
- **The next market.** "the uncleared market is, to a large extent, white space." (Maguire, 2024-10-01). "The uncleared opportunity is comparable to that of the cleared segment." (Schwimmer, AR 2025). Acadia, Quantile, SwapAgent and TradeAgent form PTS, which Amy Hong (Goldman Sachs) describes as "LSEG is seeking to import best practices and innovation from the cleared markets to the uncleared markets." (AR 2023)

## 4. Headwinds LSEG admits

- **EU location policy.** LSEG says the absence of equivalence would materially jeopardise LCH Ltd's ability to serve EU clients (AR 2021, wording run together in extraction). Equivalence was extended to June 2022, then June 2025, then "at least through 2028" (Schwimmer, Feb 2025). EMIR 3 active-account rules: "de minimis" impact (FY23 call).
- **Euronext's exit**, a by-product of the Borsa remedy: about £35m drag in 2024 and £30m in 2025. Securities & Reporting −9.8% and NTI −6.3% in Q1 2025.
- **Competition in repo and equity clearing:** "benefit limited by increased competition" (Q3 2022).
- **CCP default risk** is a standing principal risk, managed through margin, default fund and default fire-drills. LSEG cites the VTB default (2022) as a test passed.

## 5. What the claims don't prove

1. **">90% share" is of cleared IRS *notional*.** Notional overstates economic exposure and is inflated by compression-eligible trades. Revenue share would be the better measure, and it is not given.
2. **The PTS valuation is a related-party price.** The buyers are LCH's largest customers and were receiving cash from LSEG in the same transaction. The £850m mark is not an arm's-length market test.
3. **The uncleared-equals-cleared claim** is a TAM claim with no revenue path in the corpus. PTS revenue was £96m in 2024.
4. **Margin uplift is mechanical.** 140bp of 2026 margin expansion is a cost-of-sales reclassification bought for £1.15bn, not operating leverage.

## 6. Strategic read (inference)

- **LSEG's real advantage in Post Trade is governance design, not technology.** It keeps members inside as owners or profit-sharers, so they don't build a rival CCP. Then it buys back the economics when its own cost of capital is cheap relative to the stream.
- **Clearing is the counterweight to the AI story.** Markets (now including Post Trade) grew double digits in 2026 while D&A grew ~5%. Investors who doubt data get a volatility-linked utility.
- **The digital-assets path runs through clearing** (DigitalAssetClear, DiSH tokenised cash via PTS) rather than a crypto exchange. See [[lseg-digital-markets]].

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Cooperation and repeated games** ([[canon-axelrod-evolution-of-cooperation]]): SwapClear is a 25-year repeated game among rivals who agreed to mutualise default risk. Revenue sharing was the payment that kept defection (building a rival CCP) unattractive.
- **The firm as a team** ([[canon-alchian-demsetz-firm]]): who owns the residual claim in a mutualised utility, and why it migrates to the operator over time.
- **Negotiation** ([[canon-fisher-ury-getting-to-yes]]): the PTS deal trades on differing interests. Banks want capital efficiency and a governance seat; LSEG wants margin and an AEPS lift.
- **Normal accidents / tight coupling** ([[canon-perrow-normal-accidents]]): a CCP concentrates risk by design. The "parachute" line is the right frame.
- **Network effects** ([[canon-shapiro-varian-information-rules]]): netting benefits rise with share, which is why >90% is self-reinforcing.

## 8. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For LSEG:** no competitor has dented SwapClear in 25 years, through Eurex's euro push and EU location pressure. Buying back revenue share at LSEG's cost of capital is accretive and permanent.
- **Against:** the banks just showed they can extract £1.15bn from LSEG for rights they negotiated in 1999. The next renegotiation (2045) is long-dated but real. Meanwhile EU policy keeps trying to move euro swaps to Eurex.

**Price the SwapClear revenue-share buyback.**
- (a) The 2024 revenue-share payment was €0.2bn (~£170m). Cutting the share from 30% to 10% saves two-thirds: ~£113m/yr on the 2024 base. Assume SwapClear revenue surplus grows 8%/yr. Value the saving from 2026–2045 at a 9% discount rate, using a growing annuity: PV ≈ C/(r−g) × [1 − ((1+g)/(1+r))^20]. *(Answer: 113/(0.01) × [1 − (1.08/1.09)^20] ≈ 11,300 × 0.168 ≈ **£1.9bn**.)*
- (b) Compare with the £1.15bn + up to £200m paid. Who won?
