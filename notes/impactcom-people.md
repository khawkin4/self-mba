# Note · impact.com's people — a CJ founding team that refused to build "another affiliate network" handed the company to an operator who turned their pricing argument into a worldview, then hired the incumbents' managers country by country until an incumbent itself plugged in

> People & perspectives study · Maps to **E1** (Leading at Scale — founder-to-operator succession, four CFOs since 2018, four holders of the revenue seat since 2015), **E7** (Exec Presence & Comms — one CEO message repeated for nine years, a book as a category weapon, a candid layoff letter), **E12** (Culture as Strategy — the ‘clear box’ origin, people programmes, the 2022 reset), **Course 09** (Leadership & Org Behavior — acquired founders kept in role, talent poached from rivals), **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — counter-positioning against network take rates, then coopetition with Rakuten) · Written 2026-11-01 · Part of [[impactcom-hub]]; companions [[impactcom-strategy-financials]], [[impactcom-outside-voices]], [[impactcom-mission-forces]]

- **Source:** the impact.com corpus at `_ingest/raw/impact/`: `press.jsonl` (**121** releases, Jan 2010 – Aug 26 2026), `posts.jsonl` (75 blog posts, incl. two Todd Crawford podcast transcripts, the Aug 2022 layoff letter and three signed Yovanno notes) and `outside.jsonl` (13 items: Rakuten, trade press, an investor explainer). Reader notes P1 (2010–20) and P2 (2021–26) list every named speaker. Every quote below was re-checked against the full text by script.
- **What the corpus cannot show:** impact.com is private. There is no proxy, no pay data, no board list, and no departure announcements. People leave the record by *going quiet*: a CFO is never mentioned again, a CRO title passes to someone else. Titles below are as stated on the date shown. Pronouns follow the source; otherwise they/them.
- **Core claim:** the leadership story has three acts.
  1. **Founders (2008–Feb 2017).** Ex-Commission Junction people (Crawford was CJ's first employee; Pettersen a CJ founder and CTO, per Crawford) build a ‘clear box’ alternative to the network take rate.
  2. **Operator (Feb 2017 →).** David A. Yovanno, an ex-Conversant executive (CJ traded as ‘CJ Affiliate by Conversant’, per the 2016 Pacific Coast Business Times item and Gramshammer's 2018 bio), re-labels that pricing argument as a market thesis: the *Partnership Economy* (Mar 2019), a book (Feb 2022), and in 2026 an "infrastructure" for AI-era commerce. The CEO message barely changes; the C-suite around it churns (four CFOs, four CRO holders, two CMOs).
  3. **Absorption (2016 →).** Country and customer leaders are hired out of Affiliate Window/Awin, affilinet, CJ, Rakuten, Tradedoubler, Adtraction and Involve Asia. In Apr 2026 Rakuten Advertising itself becomes an alliance partner. The rival-as-talent-pool becomes the rival-as-customer.

**What the chart says.**
- **The CEO is the constant; finance and revenue are the variables.** Yovanno has 9+ years in the seat. The CFO seat averaged about a year per holder from 2018 to 2020; the revenue seat changed hands in 2015, 2020, 2022 and 2025. ## 2. Profiles

### Per Pettersen, Todd Crawford, Lisa Riolo — the CJ founders
**Origin (as Crawford tells it, 2021 podcast).** The founders reconvened in Jan 2008 and Pettersen set the rule: "So we’re all in agreement, we’re not building another affiliate network." Crawford's reason was pricing: "you can’t really demand 30% of spend". He says the plan was to license software "as opposed to taking a commission of a commission".
- **Riolo**, the 2010 manifesto: "Traditionally, our industry has accepted a ‘black box’ approach to conducting business, keeping relationships hidden and key information contained." (Jan 11 2010).
- **Pettersen**, the 2013 Google Affiliate Network exit play: "provide a technologically superior offering at a lower cost to their customer base" (Apr 17 2013). The 2017 handover: "I needed to free my time to continue innovating for our customers", and Yovanno is someone Pettersen has "the opportunity to work together again" with (Feb 2 2017).
- **Crawford** (2021), on rivals: "today an affiliate network is just an agency that happens to have proprietary tracking software". In 2018 he framed Dynamic Payouts the same way: "Dynamic Payouts is a smarter way to compensate partners, enabling advertisers to pay for what they value most" (Mar 6 2018).
- **Read:** the founders' enemy is a *business model* (the override fee), not a firm. That is why the attack could later be pointed at "legacy" networks without naming one.

### David A. Yovanno — CEO since Feb 2017
**Arrival.** "I can help accelerate the company’s vision of global expansion by investing in people, infrastructure, partnerships and acquisitions" (Feb 2 2017). He did all four: offices on four continents, seven acquisitions or investments (Mediarails 2018 to Evertune 2025), $225M of primary rounds (PSG 2019, QIA-led 2021) plus a ~$100M secondary (2022).

**The one message, dated:**
- 2018, fraud-first: "we are the first to be able to identify and cleanse fraud before measurement" (Mar 20 2018).
- 2019, category launch: "partnerships are now representing as much as 25 percent of total enterprise revenue and growing 50 percent or more" (Mar 12 2019). And: "People today are seeking advice and recommendations from other people and businesses they trust, not salespeople or advertising to whom they’ve become numb." (Dec 17 2019).
- 2020, the swipe at incumbents: "While many in our industry focus mostly on servicing traditional coupon and loyalty partnerships" (Aug 25 2020).
- 2022, the book: "we have seen that partnerships have succeeded where traditional sales and marketing have not" (Feb 23 2022).
- 2023: "a shift from an advertiser-controlled environment to one where customers are firmly in the driver’s seat" (Dec 5 2023).
- 2026: "People no longer trust ads – they trust communities." (Jan 20 2026). Then: "AI, creator commerce, and performance marketing are converging into a single layer of the customer journey" (Aug 26 2026).

**Signed notes show a second register.** Funding as validation: "They have made a bet on the market leader" (Jul 13 2021). Crisis plainness: "let’s do everything we can to make sure our business never gets the Coronavirus" (Dec 8 2020). And on SVB: "Transparently, while impact.com did have a banking relationship with SVB, we also work across several banking partners and are not adversely affected." (Mar 13 2023).

### Mike Head — employee ten, partnerships, revenue
The partnerships-as-third-engine voice: "Partnerships are the true economic engine for our company and enterprises all over the world." (Mar 24 2020). In 2021 he sells a macro statistic, a WTO-attributed 75% of commerce "driven through partnerships". He returns in May 2022: "Having already spent over 11 years at impact.com earlier in my career, I am thrilled to rejoin". He is not quoted again, and Morrison holds the revenue seat by Feb 2025.

### Cristy Ebert Garcia — CMO and the events voice
Privacy as tailwind: "With recent changes in tracking and privacy, marketers must leverage alternative methods of advertising that are authentic, cost-effective and helpful for consumers" (Jun 15 2021). Four years later the same idea is a slogan: "The future of marketing is quickly moving from ad-led to partner-led" (Apr 15 2025). She built a marketing team "from a handful of employees to a global team of over 60" (2022 bio). She owns iPX, the podcast and PXA, which become the company's main reported metrics after 2023 ([[impactcom-strategy-financials]]).

### Max Ciccotosto — acquired founder, then product chief
As Mediarails CEO in 2018: "impact.com is spearheading an entirely new strategic software category providing growth through all forms of marketing partnerships". As CPO in 2026, the category moves again: "We believe the category is moving from manual partnership management toward more intelligent, performance-driven partnership orchestration." (Jun 11 2026). On creators: "As creators evolve into true media partners, brands need better data and clear performance insight." (Apr 22 2026). The AI-era vocabulary ("infrastructure layer", "orchestration") is introduced by product before the CEO adopts it.

### The finance seat — Rathje, Kim, Cali, Lynch
Only two of the four CFOs speak. Kim (2019) promises to lead "financial operations and reporting as it matures to such an exciting stage of growth". Lynch (2024): "impact.com is an incredibly vibrant company where I see raw economic potential and market horsepower." Lynch's remit adds legal and corporate development. **Read:** hiring a two-IPO CFO in 2024, when IPO language had vanished from releases since 2022, is the clearest people-signal of exit intent in the corpus *(inference)*.

### People and culture — Denman, Siyani
Denman (Mar 2023) wants to grow "without giving up our entrepreneurial spirit". Yovanno frames her mandate as a ‘People Excellence’ strategy. Siyani, an HR director, carries the strategy line in the 2021 EMEA release: "providing an alternative to over-reliance on the duopoly".

### Regional voices — Gramshammer, Gross, Furness, Randall, Taillier
- Gramshammer (2018) sells the reset: "Impact has recently announced a full re-brand of the business and product suites".
- Taillier (2025) keeps the anti-ad line: "This is a great time for partnerships to take centre stage, as traditional advertising becomes noisier and less effective".
- Randall (Jul 2026) gives the most careful AI sentence any executive offers: "But while discovery continues to evolve, trust remains remarkably consistent."
- Gross and Furness position affiliate in SEA as low-risk spend "beyond traditional online advertising" (Furness, Feb 2025).

**What it implies.**
- **Hire the switching cost, not just the skill.** The 2016 hires ran onboarding and technical services, the exact work of moving a brand's programme off a network. Lenovo had moved "from three networks" months earlier ([[impactcom-outside-voices]]). *(Inference.)*
- **Country entry = one ex-network manager.** Italy, Spain, UK and Indonesia each open with someone who ran a rival network locally. Their local publisher books and advertiser contacts are the asset. In [[canon-helmer-7-powers]] terms that is renting a rival's relationships, not a lasting cornered resource. They can walk back out.
- **No outflow is ever visible.** The corpus records no impact.com leader leaving for a network. Head's Thanx stint surfaces only in his return bio.
- **The pipeline ends in an alliance.** After a decade of hiring Rakuten people, impact.com supplies Rakuten Advertising's "platform infrastructure for contracting, tracking, and payments" (Apr 2026). Yovanno's line: "Partnership marketing has become one of the most important growth channels for modern businesses, but the systems supporting it have remained fragmented" (Apr 28 2026).

**The three sharpest shifts:**
1. Pettersen → Yovanno: from *a cheaper, transparent tool* to *a theory of how consumers buy*.
2. 2021 → Aug 2022: from "high-growth mentality" to a 10% cut, signed jointly by CEO and co-founder.
3. 2023 → 2026: from "partnership management platform" to "infrastructure", with product (Ciccotosto) leading and the CEO following.

## 5. Communication style and narrative craft

- **One sentence, many costumes.** Almost every Yovanno quote from 2019 to 2026 has the same three beats: consumers distrust ads, they trust people, so partnerships win. *Trust* or *authentic* appears in most momentum releases. The book gives the line a canonical form: trust and authenticity as ‘the new currency’ (2022 release text).
- **Category language comes first, data second.** The category name, the PSG money and the commissioned Forrester work arrive in the same quarter (Q1–Q2 2019). Leaders then cite the Forrester 28% as if it were general ([[cross-company-narrative-vs-numbers]]).
- **Prescribe the org chart to clients.** An unsigned 2021 post argues "affiliate should never have been the CMO’s bailiwick in the first place". A 2022 post sketches a Chief Partnerships Officer seat for client firms. Meanwhile impact.com's own CPsO seat stops being evidenced after 2021: it preaches a role it no longer shows on its own chart.
- **Never name a rival, until one is a partner.** Across 121 releases no network is named as a competitor. CJ, Awin, Tradedoubler and Adtraction appear only in hires' bios. Rakuten is named only once it is an ally.
- **Candour is reserved for signed letters.** Press releases never admit a problem. The signed notes do: the layoff letter names its own error, discloses the pay cut and says "This isn’t an attempt to deflect the blame." The SVB note opens with "Transparently". Contrast [[bloomberg-people]], where no leader writes in the first person.
- **What they never talk about:** CFO and CRO departures, churn, pricing, the 2022 layoff in any press release, the Rakuten economics, and any client loss.

## 6. Culture and talent, as described

- **Founder culture:** "entrepreneurial" (Crawford, of himself and Pettersen; Denman's "entrepreneurial spirit" 2023). Santa Barbara HQ; team "more than 140" (Sep 2015) → 275+ (Nov 2016).
- **Scale-up programmes (2020–21):** DEI team created in response to the "resurgence of social injustice outcry". Three new global holidays and a two-week winter shutdown against "always-on" remote work. Meeting-free days (Dec 2020). 26 weeks' paid parental leave (Dec 2021).
- **The 2022 reset:** severance, references, outplacement. The letter calls the company's ‘spark’ its people's creativity and tenacity, then cuts ~10%.
- **2023 rebuild:** CPO hire, ‘People Excellence’, a Chief Productivity Officer, a Global Day of Service, carbon-neutral target (Dec 2023–May 2024).
- **Headcount (self-reported):** 600+ (Jul 2021) → 980+ (May 2022) → 1,300+ (Sep 2025) → 1,400+ (Jan 2026). Rough ARR per employee: ~$167K (2021) → ~$193K (2026) *(computed in P2 from self-reported ARR, not stated by impact.com)*.

## What the claims don't prove
- **That the C-suite churn was healthy or unhealthy.** No exit is explained; the Head return could be a sign of loyalty or of a thin bench.
- **That ex-network hires brought clients with them.** No release ties a hire to a migration.
- **That the ‘trust beats ads’ thesis drives growth.** It is asserted in releases next to self-reported counts that don't reconcile ([[impactcom-strategy-financials]]).
- **That the board governs.** Three directors are named in 16 years; Pettersen speaks for the board in 2022 while also an executive.

## Strategic read (inference — not stated by impact.com)
- **Counter-positioning, staffed.** A network cannot drop its override fee without cannibalising itself ([[canon-helmer-7-powers]]). impact.com's edge was hiring the people who ran those networks to sell the cheaper model. By 2026 a network (Rakuten) chose to rent the platform rather than fight it: [[canon-brandenburger-nalebuff-coopetition]].
- **The operator kept the founders' frame and swapped the audience.** Pettersen sold to affiliate managers; Yovanno sells to CMOs and CEOs, which is why the message is about consumers, not fees.
- **Seat churn tracks the capital calendar.** CFO changes cluster in 2018–20 (pre- and post-PSG); a public-company CFO arrives after the IPO window shut. The revenue seat turns when growth slows (self-reported ARR +35% in 2022 → ~20% in FY Jan 2026).

## Canon lenses
- [[canon-schein-organizational-culture]] — *Artifacts:* iPX, the podcast, the book. *Espoused values:* trust, authenticity, transparency. *Basic assumption:* the network override fee is illegitimate. That assumption explains the founding, the hires and the never-name-a-rival rule.
- [[canon-sinek-start-with-why]] and [[canon-duarte-resonate]] — Yovanno leads with a *why* about consumers ("they trust communities") and keeps the product as the *how*. The risk is a why so stable it can't absorb bad news.
- [[canon-horowitz-what-you-do-is-who-you-are]] — The Aug 2022 letter is the culture test: an admitted mistake, an executive pay cut, outplacement. What leaders did in the layoff says more than ‘People Excellence’ does.
- [[canon-pfeffer-power]] — Power sits with the CEO and the investor bench (Silversmith, PSG, QIA). Founders keep titles but leave the narrative; the CFO and CRO seats are interchangeable.
- [[canon-thiel-zero-to-one]] — Pettersen's "not another affiliate network" is a refusal to fight for share; the *Partnership Economy* is a bid to own a category name.
- [[canon-kotter-leading-change]] — Change runs through category renames, not announced restructurings. The 2018 three-GM structure was dissolved without comment.

## Practitioner counterpoint
- Crawford's podcast partner, Bob Glazer of Acceleration Partners, says on the 2021 podcast that "the market always supplies what demand wants". On that view networks were rational for brands that could not run programmes. A SaaS-plus-alliance model (Rakuten) partly concedes the point: service still matters.
- Operators would read four CFOs and four CRO holders in a decade as a scaling cost, not a scandal. Bloomberg ([[bloomberg-people]]) and Tradeweb ([[tradeweb-people]]) show slow-ladder alternatives; impact.com buys experience from outside and loses it.

4. Is AI framed by the CEO as *trust infrastructure* or by product as *automation*? Watch the iPX 2027 keynote.
