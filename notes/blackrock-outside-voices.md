# Note · Outside voices on BlackRock — the company's own documents never name a critic; the critics appear only where BlackRock can't edit them (AGM floors, lawsuits it rebuts, partners' press releases)

> Stakeholder-map note · Maps to **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations), **E4** (Corporate Governance), **Course 09** (power and influence), **Course 11** (Responsible Business) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[blackrock-hub]], [[blackrock-stewardship-backlash]], [[tradeweb-outside-voices]]

- **Source:** all in `_ingest/raw/blackrock/articles.jsonl`:
  - the 2021–2023 AGM transcripts (about 60 named questioners and proponents, plus vote results);
  - newsroom statements answering officials;
  - partner and counterparty quotes in releases: Tradeweb, Securitize, Microsoft, Temasek, Santander and others;
  - Fink's CNBC interview summaries (the interviewers' questions).

  The Fink letters and Investor Day decks name **no critic**; they refer to critics only in the abstract. AGM transcripts mark phonetic spellings with *[ph]*, so names are given as transcribed.
- **Core claim:** outside voices in the corpus fall into five groups.
  1. **Shareholder proponents on the left.** In 2021, climate investors praised BlackRock. By 2023 one called it "timid", and that proposal reached ~10% support.
  2. **Anti-ESG activists on the right.** NCPPR, Scott Shepard and others get under 1% when they file, but shape the questions BlackRock answers.
  3. **State and federal officials.** These are known only through BlackRock's rebuttals.
  4. **Partners and counterparties.** Tradeweb, Microsoft, Securitize and Santander are mutually flattering, but their wording shows who depends on whom.
  5. **Media interviewers.** CNBC pushes on private-credit risk and the crypto U-turn.

  **The useful signal** is what partners admit (Microsoft: AI capex is beyond any one balance sheet; Tradeweb: Aladdin's client base is the prize) and how votes move.

## 4. What the voices don't prove

1. **The AGM questions were read out by the Corporate Secretary,** who grouped and paraphrased many of them. Some opposing questions were merged into one (*inference*: in 2021 Scott Shepard (anti-ESG) and Tim Smith (pro-ESG) appear in the same grouped question).
2. **The officials' arguments are filtered** through BlackRock's statements. No complaint text is in the corpus.
3. **Partner quotes are co-written press releases.** Read them for what they *must* concede, not for praise.
4. **Rissman's claims** (client attrition, the resignation call) are the proponent's, unverified in the corpus.

## 5. Strategic read (inference — not BlackRock's words)

- **Silence in its own documents is a choice.** Naming critics would legitimize them, so BlackRock answers abstractly in the letters and concretely only in statements.
- **The floor is the only uncurated channel.** The AGM is where opposing views show up in BlackRock's own record. There are no transcripts after 2023 in the corpus, so that window has closed for this study.
- **Dependence shows in the verbs.** Partners "leverage" Aladdin and "access" its clients. BlackRock "partners" and "invests". The grammar matches the power map in [[tradeweb-outside-voices]] §5.

## 6. Canon lenses

- **Power and dependence** ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]): officials hold mandates; venues need flow; hyperscalers need capital. Each group's leverage over BlackRock is different.
- **Influence** ([[canon-cialdini-influence]]): BlackRock uses social proof (record flows) and authority (fiduciary duty), and partners use BlackRock's brand the same way (Santander).
- **Stakeholder ethics** ([[canon-sandel-justice]]): The Shareholder Commons argues that a diversified owner's duty runs to the whole market, not single firms.
- **Narrative fallacy** ([[canon-taleb-black-swan]]): curated quote sets tell a consistent story. Look for the uncurated dissent.

## 7. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For BlackRock's approach:** responding case by case and never naming critics keeps the firm out of a news cycle it can't win.
- **Against:** unanswered critiques accumulate. By 2023, investors on both flanks were filing.
- **Both:** if you run IR, track the *proposal support trend* and the *mandate-loss list*, not the volume of noise.
