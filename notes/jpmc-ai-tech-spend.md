# Note · JPMorganChase's AI and tech-spend ledger — from "I would spend anything" to benefits that "aren't observable": six years of telling investors about a ~$12B → ~$19.8B technology budget

> Digital-transformation / investor-communication note · Maps to **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation for Leaders), **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations — selling an unprovable investment), **Course 07** (Data, Analytics & Decision-Making — measuring ROI you can't isolate), **Course 06** (Operations — automation and headcount) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[jpm-software-sector-spotlight-2026]] (J.P. Morgan *research* on software and AI; this note is about JPMC *as an AI spender*) and [[wss-ai-economics]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/jpmc/articles.jsonl`: Dimon's letters AR2019–AR2025, the COO letters (Piepszak, AR2024 and AR2025), the CIB and CCB letters, ten earnings calls (Mayo asks on all ten), the 2025 Investor Day (Barnum's tech and AI section), and the technology blog items (Evident AI index 2023 and 2025, LLM Suite award 2025-06-03, Innovation Week 2025 and 2026, "Leading with AI", the CISO letter to suppliers). Spend figures are JPMC's own. "Tech spend", "investment spend" and "AI expense" are different bases, flagged where used.
- **Core claim:** The technology budget rose from **~$12B (2019) to ~$17B (2024), ~$18B (2025) and ~$19.8B budgeted for 2026**. The public story about what it buys went through four phases. **(1) Conviction, 2021–22:** "The AI is real," "I would spend anything," "just don't do an NPV." **(2) Counting, 2023–24:** 300 → 400 → 500+ production use cases, an Evident AI #1 ranking and "We're not going to be chasing shiny objects here in AI." **(3) Scale, 2025:** LLM Suite to 200,000 users in eight months, 175+ use cases in the CIB alone, KYC unit cost down ~40%. **(4) Restraint, late 2026:** AI expense put at "something like $700 million annually," benefits "aren't observable in our current quarterly earnings," and gains said to accrue to customers. Jobs language moved from "augment" to "30% or 40%" cuts in discrete areas, and the COO says "the goal is not fewer headcount". The one constant is Mike Mayo asking for the return and not getting a number.

## 3. What the claims don't prove

1. **Use-case counts aren't value.** 300, 400, 500 and 1,000 are inventories. Dimon's own correction ("the really important ones are 50") shows how little the headline count means.
2. **No ROI is ever disclosed.** Barnum said in 2022 it is "impossible to prove". In 2026 he says the benefits aren't observable. Between those, JPMC cited CCB "AI value" growth percentages and KYC unit-cost falls, but never firmwide dollars against firmwide spend.
3. **Rankings measure disclosure as much as impact.** The Evident AI Index scores talent, innovation, leadership and transparency. JPM leads partly by publishing more about AI than peers do.
4. **Jobs claims point both ways.** "30% or 40%" cuts in discrete areas sit beside "not fewer headcount" and 320,000+ employees. Both can be true (redeployment), but the corpus gives no net figure.
5. **"Passed on to the customer"** is a competitive claim: if every bank automates, margins compress. That explains the lack of earnings evidence, but it also makes the investment defensive by JPMC's own logic.

## 4. Strategic read (inference — not JPMC's words)

- **The narrative was walked back on purpose.** In 2025 JPM led its peers on AI claims. By late 2026, with AI capex questioned in markets ([[jpm-software-sector-spotlight-2026]]: token costs fell ~1,000×, while cost per task fell only modestly) and Mayo pressing, management cut the number it would stand behind (~$700M, not ~$2B) and stopped promising visible returns. That lowers the bar it must clear later.
- **Tech spend is a moat by scale, not by AI.** A ~$19.8B budget is roughly what a large regional bank earns in revenue (context, not from JPMC). Dimon's "table stakes... for the rest of eternity" means the edge is being able to fund modernisation, cloud, cyber and AI at once. Smaller banks can't.
- **Control of data access is the real AI defence.** Blocking screen-scraping and outside agents, plus the open-banking fight ("a government-protected subsidy for data middlemen", [[jpmc-outside-voices]]), protects the data that AI runs on. JPM's AI strategy is as much about who may *not* use its data as about its own models.
- **The org chart followed the money.** AI moved from research (Manuela Veloso) to an Operating Committee seat (Heitsenrether, 2023) to the COO's remit (Piepszak, 2025). Technology became a corporate function run by a non-candidate COO ([[jpmc-succession]]).

## 5. Canon lenses

- **Measuring what matters** ([[canon-doerr-measure-what-matters]]): use-case counts are output metrics. Doerr would push for key results like cost per KYC file, minutes saved per banker, or loss rates, which JPM cites only piecemeal.
- **Investment without NPV** ([[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]]): Bezos framed long bets as free-cash-flow-per-share investments with clear kill criteria. Dimon's "just don't do an NPV" skips the second half.
- **Productivity paradox** ([[canon-sterman-business-dynamics]]): delays between investment and observable output are long. Sterman's stock-and-flow view explains why benefits "aren't observable" while the cost is.
- **Who captures the surplus** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): if all rivals adopt the same tools, the surplus goes to buyers. That matches Dimon's own "passed on to the customer".

## 6. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For JPM:** it built a single model-agnostic platform early, kept data in-house, and moved 65% of apps to the cloud, so it can switch models as costs fall. Specific operating wins are real: transaction screening at double the volume with half the manual checks, KYC unit cost down 40%, 55ip trades doubling with "no stress on the system".
- **Skeptic (Mayo and the market):** tech spend rose ~$8B in seven years with no disclosed return, and the 2026 retreat on AI numbers looks like an admission that the payoff is slower than advertised.
- **Synthesis:** the AI programme is probably worth having and impossible to value from outside. Track unit costs (KYC, payments exceptions, cost per trade, [[jpmc-markets-venues]]) rather than use-case counts.

3. Net headcount: does 320,000 hold while revenue grows?
4. Does JPM let any outside agents in through APIs and fees, mirroring its aggregator deals?
5. Does the ~$19.8B budget grow again in 2027 (consensus expense ~$114B)?
