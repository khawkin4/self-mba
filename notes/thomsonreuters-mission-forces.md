# Note · Thomson Reuters' mission and the forces on it — "trust" survived every rewrite of the identity; the danger moved from print decline and a messy portfolio to AI that is good enough, sold by the labs TR rents from

> Strategy / environment-scan note · Maps to **Course 04** (Competitive Strategy — five forces, supplier power, substitutes), **Course 11** (Global Strategy & Responsible Business — Trust Principles, US concentration, e-invoicing mandates), **E5** (Transformation — identity change), **E6** (Crisis Leadership — COVID, 2022, the GenAI shock), **E10** (Digital & AI Transformation), **E12** (Culture as Strategy — the 1941 Trust Principles as an operating constraint) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[thomsonreuters-hub]]; companions [[thomsonreuters-ai-strategy]] and [[thomsonreuters-people]]

- **Source:** the "About Thomson Reuters" boilerplate across 293 items, 2019–2026, and every mission, strategy, risk and outlook passage in the results releases (32), the 2021 Investor Day transcript and slides, the Founders Share Company releases and the 76 research releases (TR's own surveys of its markets). All are in `_ingest/raw/thomsonreuters/articles.jsonl`. This is TR's own account of itself. Tailwinds and headwinds marked **(admitted)** are in TR's words. Those marked **(inferred)** are mine.
- **Core claim:** TR rewrote its identity three times in seven years:
  1. "the world’s leading provider of news and information-based tools to professionals" (Jan 2019).
  2. "a leading provider of business information services" (mid-2019 → 2022).
  3. "a global content and technology company" that "informs the way forward" (2023 →), sold since 2026 as **"fiduciary-grade AI"**.

  The one word that never left is **trust**: the 1941 Trust Principles, then the 2023 "trust gap", then "almost right is not good enough". TR optimizes for **recurring, high-retention revenue from professionals who face liability for mistakes**. The tailwinds are strong and mostly admitted: compliance complexity, e-invoicing mandates, law firms' pricing power, and GenAI demand. The decisive headwind is the one TR never names: **frontier models getting "good enough" for most professional research**, sold directly by the same labs whose models power CoCounsel.

## 1. Mission and identity

### 1a. What TR says it is (verbatim, dated)

| Date | Statement |
|---|---|
| Jan 2019 | "Thomson Reuters is the world’s leading provider of news and information-based tools to professionals." |
| Jun 2019 → 2022 | "a leading provider of business information services", with "highly specialized information-enabled software and tools for legal, tax, accounting and compliance professionals" |
| Feb 2021 | Change Program: "from a content provider to a content-driven technology company." |
| May 2023 → | "informs the way forward by bringing together the trusted content and technology that people and organizations need to make the right decisions"; mission line "to help institutions in their pursuit of justice, truth and transparency"; self-label "a global content and technology company" |
| Mar 2024 | Brand refresh (the first in 16 years): promise "To Clarify the Complex" |
| Jul 2024 → | AI boilerplate: "Thomson Reuters is at the forefront of delivering professional-grade AI" via its "build, partner, buy strategy" |
| Nov 2025 | Wong: "Thomson Reuters is the only company with the complete foundation required for professional grade agentic AI" |
| Feb 2026 | "Thomson Reuters is not participating in the AI race. It is defining how AI operates in the world's highest-stakes work." |
| May 2026 | Hasker: "We call this 'fiduciary-grade AI.'" Standard published May 27. |
| Jul 2026 | KKR print deal "sharpens Thomson Reuters focus on providing innovative fiduciary-grade AI solutions for the legal, tax, audit and compliance industries." |

### 1b. The Trust Principles thread
- The **Founders Share Company** guards the 1941 Trust Principles. Kim Williams (2019) summarized them as "Trust, Integrity, Independence and Freedom from Bias in the output of Reuters News and Media editorial delivery". Stephen Toope (2026): "The Trust Principles remain as vital today as when they were created in 1941."
- David Thomson (2019 AGM): "Trust remains our greatest asset."
- Hasker (2023): "there is a trust gap with professionals".
- Hron (2026): "trust in AI is a property of the system itself, built into the architecture and verifiable at every step".
- **Read:** an editorial ethic built for a news agency became the product spec for AI in legal and tax. That is the most interesting identity move in the corpus.

### 1c. What TR actually optimizes for (inference, judged by actions)
1. **Recurring revenue with ~90% retention** (~80% of revenue is recurring). Big 3 share rose from ~80% (2020) to 83–85% (2026).
2. **Organic growth rate** as the headline metric (1% → 8% guide). Divestitures that lift it mechanically (Print: +60–70bp) are welcome.
3. **Dividend continuity**: 33 consecutive increases, 10% a year for five years.
4. **Content control**: editorial independence (Reuters), editorial authority (Westlaw), and now model control (Thomson).

## 3. Headwinds and challenges

| # | Headwind | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **"Good enough" frontier AI as a substitute** for research and drafting, especially for midsize and small firms | Never named. Implied by "Frontier labs have typically spent billions…", Hron's "table stakes", and the shift of the moat claim from content to *verification* to *own model* | **Inferred** |
| H2 | **Supplier power of model labs** (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) that are also potential direct sellers | CoCounsel "rebuilt on Anthropic's Claude Agent SDK"; CoCounsel inside Claude via MCP; the $40M Thomson model as a hedge | Inferred (dependency admitted) |
| H3 | **Per-seat pricing vs. fewer junior lawyers** | TR's own research: talent models shifting toward experienced laterals, less junior hiring, the billable hour to be "challenged" (Jan 2025); AI to save "12 hours per week" (2024). Fewer hours and fewer juniors mean pressure on seat-based subscriptions | **Inferred** from admitted trends |
| H4 | AI regulation | "uncertainty in the legal regulatory regime relating to AI" (results risk language, 2024–26) | Admitted |
| H5 | AI accuracy and liability | "When we tried to solve claims research issues with AI alone, it didn’t work very well" (2024); RAG and verification products respond to it | Admitted |
| H6 | Print decline | Global Print organic −7% (2024), −5% (2025); sold into a KKR JV with minimum-return support | Admitted |
| H7 | Reuters' narrow economics | Lowest segment margin (20.4%, 2025); AI licensing revenue "largely transactional in nature"; financial channel locked to LSEG until 2048 | Admitted (+ inferred dependency) |
| H8 | Controlled-company constraints | Woodbridge ~66–70%; float cap limits buybacks; a stated risk factor since 2019 | Admitted |
| H9 | US concentration | ~77–80% of revenue from the US (2019–21); international called "unfinished business" (2021) | Admitted |
| H10 | Leverage creeping up to fund returns | Net debt/EBITDA 0.4× → 0.9× (Dec 2024 → Jun 2026); new notes Sep 2026 | Inferred from stated figures |

**What TR admits vs. leaves out:**
- TR is open about print, AI regulation, macro and trust gaps *in its customers*.
- It never discusses AI as a **substitute for its own products**. It never names an AI-native competitor. It never discusses what AI does to **seat counts**.
- Those omissions line up exactly with the inferred headwinds H1–H3.

## 4. Forces map, 2026

| Force | Direction | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry (LexisNexis/RELX, Wolters Kluwer, Bloomberg Law, Intuit in tax) | ↔ | Stable oligopoly by TR's 2021 account ("stable competitive environment"); none named after 2021 |
| Threat of new entrants (AI-native legal and tax startups) | ↑ | Funded by venture money; TR invests in some via Ventures (Spellbook, ZERO, Materia, Noetica), buys others |
| Substitutes (general-purpose AI; in-house builds at large firms) | ↑↑ | The decisive force (H1) |
| Supplier power (model labs; also content licensors) | ↑ | Partly offset by multi-model sourcing and the Thomson model |
| Buyer power (law firms, corporates, governments) | ↔/↑ | Large firms build their own AI programs (Greenberg Traurig "actively shaping the tools"); small firms buy self-serve |
| Complementors (Microsoft 365, Claude, AWS, SAP, Deloitte) | ↑ | TR now lives inside others' platforms |
| Regulation (AI rules, e-invoicing mandates, court digitization) | ↑ both ways | Mandates help (T2); AI rules add uncertainty (H4) |

## 5. Crisis tests — what each one revealed

| Crisis | What happened | What it revealed |
|---|---|---|
| **COVID (2020)** | Guidance cut; $1B revolver drawn as a precaution; Reuters Events lost ~50% of its revenue; Print −17% in Q2; 98% remote. Big 3 still +4% for the year | Subscription content held. Transactional and print didn't. That validated the Big 3 focus |
| **2022 inflation and rates** | LSEG mark-to-market drove diluted losses in some quarters; Change Program spending cut margins by ~260bp; organic still +6–7% | The core was insensitive to rates. The LSEG stake was the volatility source, which was one reason to sell it |
| **The GenAI shock (2023)** | Within ~7 months of ChatGPT: Casetext ($650M), a $100M+ a year AI budget, capex raised to ~8% | TR treated GenAI as an investment cycle, not a threat to deny. It moved fast because the Change Program had already unified its platforms |
| **Reuters AI-licensing reversal (2025)** | Reuters organic −7% in Q1 2025 as one-off 2024 deals rolled off | Selling content to the labs is lumpy, and it arms competitors |

## 6. Scenarios to 2030 (inference, not TR's)

| Scenario | What happens | Signals to watch |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Trust premium holds** | Fiduciary-grade verification becomes the standard for regulated work. TR prices up; Legal margin ~48%+; organic 8–10% | CoCounsel revenue disclosure; price increases holding; court and bar rules requiring cited AI |
| **B. Squeeze from below** | Frontier models plus public law serve the midsize and small market. TR keeps the top tier but loses seats and pricing below it; organic falls back to 4–6% | Seat counts; small-firm retention; labs' direct legal products |
| **C. Become the layer** | TR becomes the content and verification API inside Claude, Copilot and ChatGPT: high volume, lower price per use, less owned interface | Share of CoCounsel usage via MCP and partners; usage-based pricing |
| **D. Owner of a specialist model** | Thomson grows into the default model inside CoCounsel, cutting supplier dependence and inference cost | Thomson deployment beyond Tabular Analysis; Imperial lab output |

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Strategy as a guiding policy** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]], [[canon-rumelt-the-crux]]): the crux in 2026 is H1, whether content plus verification is a durable advantage against general models. Every major action since 2023 (Casetext, multi-model, MCP, Thomson) is aimed at it, though TR never states it.
- **Only the paranoid** ([[canon-grove-only-the-paranoid-survive]]): GenAI is a strategic inflection point in Grove's sense, a 10× change in a substitute. TR's reaction speed (2023) passes Grove's test. Its public silence about the substitute threat does not.
- **Disruption** ([[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]]): scenario B is the textbook path, cheaper and good-enough tools winning the low end first. TR's down-market self-serve push is the right counter, if priced to cannibalize its own products.
- **Culture as constraint** ([[canon-schein-organizational-culture]]): the Trust Principles are an espoused value that has turned into an artifact (the fiduciary-grade standard). The real test is whether TR would *decline* a profitable AI feature it couldn't verify.
- **Five forces** ([[canon-porter-competitive-strategy]]): substitutes and supplier power are rising together, and both come from the same firms (the labs). That combination is rare and dangerous.

## 8. Open questions

1. Will TR ever name AI substitution as a risk factor in its results releases?
2. How do seat-based subscriptions change as junior hiring falls? Watch for usage- or outcome-based pricing.
3. Does "fiduciary-grade" get adopted by any regulator, bar or court, or does it stay TR's own label?
4. Can international revenue (~20% of the total) grow faster with AI-localized products (CoCounsel in 107 countries)?

  - Assume Legal is ~45% of revenue at a ~47% margin, and group margin is ~39%.
  - State your assumptions.

*Answer for (b) (derived, stylized):*
- Assume group revenue of $7.5B, so group EBITDA ≈ $2.9B. A 10% cut is ≈ $290M.
- Assume the lost Legal revenue goes away at roughly its *incremental* margin, say ~70% (subscription content has high operating leverage; this is an assumption).
- Then ≈ $290M / 0.7 ≈ **$415M** of Legal revenue, ≈ **12%** of a ~$3.4B Legal segment.
- So a 12% fall in Legal revenue would cut group profit by a tenth. That is why H1 and H3 are the crux.
