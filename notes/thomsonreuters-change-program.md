# Note · Thomson Reuters' Change Program (2021–2023) — a transformation that published hard targets, admitted its weaknesses on day one, and mostly hit the numbers; capex was the target it chose to miss

> Transformation / execution note · Maps to **E5** (Transformation & Change Management at Scale — holding company → operating company), **E1** (Leading at Scale — the outside change team), **Course 09** (Leadership & Org Behavior — silos, culture shifts), **Course 06** (Operations — procurement, location strategy, cloud exit), **E8** (Investor Relations — pre-registering targets) · Written 2026-10-25 · Part of [[thomsonreuters-hub]]; companions [[thomsonreuters-people]] and [[thomsonreuters-capital-allocation]]

- **Source:**
  - The **2021 Investor Day transcript** (Mar 16 2021, ~29k words; presentations by Hasker, Roth, Wong, Malhotra, segment heads and Eastwood, plus analyst Q&A).
  - Hasker's Investor Day slides.
  - The FY2020 results release (Feb 23 2021) that announced the program.
  - Every quarterly results release through FY2023, for progress and the scorecard.
  - All in `_ingest/raw/thomsonreuters/articles.jsonl`.
  - Net Promoter Score (NPS) results were never disclosed, so the customer-experience side can't be scored.
- **Core claim:** In Feb 2021, with organic growth at ~1% (2020) and a margin of 33%, Hasker launched a **24-month, $500–600M** program with two levers:
  - **Holding company → operating company:** shared functions, "build once and deploy many times".
  - **Content provider → "content-driven technology company":** cloud, AI and self-serve digital.

  It came with **2023 targets** and tied them to management incentives. On TR's own reported numbers, the results were:
  - Organic growth **6%** against a target of 5–6%.
  - Adjusted EBITDA margin **39.3%** against 38–40%.
  - FCF **$1.9B** against $1.8–2.0B.
  - **$540M** of run-rate savings against $600M gross.
  - The miss was **capex at 7.8% of revenue against 6–6.5%**. By then the money was going to generative AI, so the miss reads as a reallocation more than a failure.

  What makes it a good teaching case is the *opening*. The CFO said NPS was "significantly below the B2B industry average", and the COO/CTO admitted TR was "late to the game". The program was sold on candor about weaknesses, not on vision.

## 1. The design (Feb–Mar 2021)

| Element | As presented |
|---|---|
| **Diagnosis** | A holding company of acquired businesses ("portfolio orientation", in Hasker's words) with siloed products, poor UX, many data centres and ~80% US revenue. Strong content, weak experience: customers love the content and people, but product design "does need work" (Roth/Wong in Q&A). Small customers felt undervalued (Hasker played a customer clip). |
| **Lever 1: holding co → operating co** | Pan-TR functions for customer service and support, technology and product. Segments keep go-to-market. Roth: "we will build once and deploy many times." |
| **Lever 2: content → content-driven technology** | Wong: "AI models are only as good as the data that trains them." API-first reuse; Toronto tech centre (350+ engineers); exit TR data centres for cloud. |
| **Four work streams** | Customer experience; product and portfolio simplification; operations and technology simplification; culture and talent. |
| **Spend** | $500–600M over 2021–22, 50–60% opex / 40–50% capex, ~$300–350M in 2021. Split: digital self-serve SMB ~20%, omnichannel ~10%, tech and ops modernization ~35%, org design and location ~35% (Eastwood). |
| **2023 targets** | Organic revenue growth 5–6% (Big 3: 6–7%); adjusted EBITDA margin 38–40%; FCF $1.8–2.0B; FCF/share $3.60–4.00; $600M gross annual opex savings ($200M reinvested, $400M net, ~10% of opex); capex 6.0–6.5% of revenue; ~$100–110M extra annual revenue. Targets embedded in annual incentive plans. |
| **Explicitly organic** | Bolt-on M&A "additive". Hasker on M&A: "rigor"; "pretty full valuations" for SaaS assets. |
| **The team** | Kirsty Roth (Chief Operations & Technology Officer, ex-HSBC, two prior change programs), David Wong (CPO, ex-Facebook), Shawn Malhotra (engineering), Charlie Claxton (Head of Design, ex-Amazon), Pat Wilburn (corporate strategy, ex-Microsoft). All are outside hires from 2020, brought in by a new outside CEO. |

## 5. What the claims don't prove

1. **Savings are run-rate and self-defined.** $540M is "run-rate operating expense savings". The baseline, the counterfactual and the reinvested share are never audited or broken out after 2021.
2. **Organic growth had tailwinds the program didn't create.** TR itself sized a ~100bp COVID comparison effect in Q4 2021. 2022–23 inflation let law firms raise rates fast (TR's own Peer Monitor data shows rate growth of 6%+). A growing customer base buys more content. Program credit should be discounted for both.
3. **The margin target was helped by portfolio moves.** Selling Elite and other non-core units, and lower bonus accruals in 2022, lifted margins independently of the program.
4. **The customer-experience goal disappeared.** NPS was the CFO's own definition of success ("successful be when we significantly improve our Net Promoter Score"). TR has published no NPS figure since.
5. **2023 was the last year the targets were measurable.** From Feb 2024 the story shifted to a new framework (organic 6.5–8% for 2025–26) and to AI. Program benefits after 2023 can't be separated from AI investment.

## 6. Strategic read (inference — not TR's words)

- **Why it worked.**
  - (a) The targets were *conservative*: Big 3 growth was already 4% in 2020 and the markets were growing.
  - (b) The biggest savings were classic ones: procurement on ~$4B of addressable spend, real estate, location, and consolidating overlapping products after many acquisitions. These are low-risk, high-certainty levers.
  - (c) Outside leaders with change-program records ran it under a CEO with a McKinsey/Nielsen operating background.
  - (d) The target-to-incentive link kept it on schedule.
- **What it really changed:** the *organization*. It took TR from a set of businesses to a company that could run one AI platform (CoCounsel) across legal, tax and risk by 2024. Without "build once and deploy many times", the 2024 umbrella move ("GenAI assistant for every professional") would have been five separate projects. This is the program's most important result, and the scorecard never measured it.
- **The capex "miss" as a signal.** Raising capex to ~8% in Aug 2023, against a 6–6.5% target, was the first financial sign that TR treated GenAI as a real investment cycle and not a feature. Investors accepted the miss because it was framed as AI.
- **Change-program pattern:** diagnose with candor, then pre-register numbers, then fund with a fixed envelope, then end on a date. That is the opposite of a rolling "transformation journey". Ending it in Dec 2022, before GenAI, gave TR a clean baseline and a reorganized company just as ChatGPT arrived. That is partly luck.

## 7. Canon lenses

- **Kotter's eight stages** ([[canon-kotter-leading-change]]): urgency from candid diagnosis (NPS below average, "late to the game"); a guiding coalition of outside hires plus the CFO; a two-lever vision; short-term wins reported quarterly (run-rate savings); and institutionalization (the Big 3 operating model). This is a near-textbook sequence.
- **Execution discipline** ([[canon-bossidy-charan-execution]]): people (the hires), strategy (two levers) and operations (quarterly savings and spend tracking) are linked through incentive plans.
- **Lead measures** ([[canon-4dx-four-disciplines-execution]]): TR tracked lagging measures (margin, FCF) and one lead measure (run-rate savings). The lead measures for growth (NPS, self-serve adoption, cross-sell share at ~15% of sales) were promised and never reported. That is a classic 4DX gap.
- **Switch** ([[canon-heath-switch]]): the 30,000+ self-serve customers (about half of small-law customers) by 2021 are the *shape the path* lever. Behaviour changed because buying got easier, not because people were persuaded.
- **Good strategy's kernel** ([[canon-rumelt-good-strategy]]): diagnosis (holding company, silos, UX), guiding policy (operating company plus content-driven technology) and coherent actions (shared platforms, cloud exit, product rationalization). This counts as a strategy under Rumelt's definition, not a list of goals.

## 8. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For the program:** it hit every financial target it could control, ended on time, and left TR able to deploy GenAI at platform scale. It's rare to see a change program pre-register, report quarterly and close out cleanly.
- **Skeptic (from the Investor Day Q&A itself):**
  - Tim Casey (BMO) asked why investors should believe 5–6% organic growth. Hasker answered that 2019's 3.7% had been "driven by price and strong retention rates". The new growth would come from the same pricing power.
  - Sam Hoffman (Lincoln Square) pressed on TR's valuation discount to peers. The answer was a "polar opposite view", with no data.
  - Read sceptically, the program delivered the margin (cost) and borrowed the growth (price, market and inflation).
- **Synthesis:** on cost and organization, the program is a success. Its growth contribution is unproven, and the customer-experience goal was never measured publicly.

## 9. Open questions

3. Did the $200M "reinvested" savings show up as AI headcount, which started rising in 2023?
4. Was there a second program? The 2024 framework reads like one without the name.

Using §1–§3:
(a) TR said $600M of gross savings equals ~10% of opex. Back out the implied opex base, then the net saving ($400M) as a share of 2023 revenue ($6,794M).
(b) Program spend was ~$600M. Treat the $540M of run-rate savings as a perpetuity, *ignoring* the reinvested $200M. What is the simple payback period in years? Then redo it with only the $340M net of reinvestment ($540M − $200M).
(c) Draft three KPIs (one lead measure each for customer experience, digital self-serve and cross-sell) that TR should have reported quarterly. Name the 2021 baseline for each from §1 where one exists.

*Answers for (a)/(b):* (a) $600M / 10% ≈ **$6.0B** opex base; $400M / $6,794M ≈ **5.9%** of revenue. (b) $600M / $540M ≈ **1.1 years**; $600M / $340M ≈ **1.8 years**. Either way, a fast payback. That explains why the margin target was safe even if the growth claims weren't.
