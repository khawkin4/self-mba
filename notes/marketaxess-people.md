# Note · MarketAxess's people — a founder whose all-to-all conviction defined the firm handed power to an exchange operator who rebuilt it to trade like equities; the C-suite churned, the chair split, the board turned over, and the story ended in a sale to ICE

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/marketaxess/articles.jsonl` (359 items, Jan 2019 – Oct 23 2026): every leadership-change and board release (~20), all 32 earnings-release narratives, the seven CEO *Letters to Stockholders and Clients* (FY2019–FY2025), the 2021 Investor Day and Dec 2025 Goldman Sachs decks, the ICE deal release (2026-07-30), and the bylined or interview pieces in which staff speak at length (The TRADE, The DESK, Bloomberg, WatersTechnology, Markets Media reprints, 2025–26). **Caveats:** (1) everything is company-published; reprints were chosen by MarketAxess; (2) **no earnings-call transcripts**, so no leader is heard under analyst questioning; (3) many Knowledge Center dates are estimates; (4) titles are taken from the dated release, not the current byline; (5) pronouns are used only where a source states them (McVey, Concannon, Sweeney, Fiszel Bieler, Berry, Lee, Quan, Hernandez, Gerosa); everyone else is *they*.
- **Core claim:** For 23 years MarketAxess was Rick McVey's firm and its identity was his idea: Open Trading, the anonymous all-to-all pool he called "essential to the functioning of credit markets" in the COVID quarter. In Jan 2019 he hired **Chris Concannon** (ex-Bats CEO, Cboe President, Virtu, Nasdaq, Instinet, SEC attorney) as President & COO; Concannon became CEO on Apr 3 2023 and spent the next three years importing equity-market machinery — order books, algos (Pragma), mid-point sessions (Mid-X), closing auctions, ETF RFQ (RFQ-hub). Around him almost every seat turned over: **CFO** DeLise → Gerosa (2021) → Fiszel Bieler (2024); a new **Group COO** (Berry, ex-LSEG, 2025) with the **CTO** (Baker → Chalamani → Quan, 2026) reporting to him, not the CEO; **CIO** Themelis → Panchal (2022). The **chair split** for the first time (Hernandez, ex-JPMorgan, Jan 2025), and the board's additions shifted from strategy-builders (2019–21: Altobello, Prager, Gmelich, Gibson, Li) to deal-literate operators (2023–26: Hernandez, Hoornweg, Cifu, Schiciano). Severance for "changes in management structure" appeared in three quarters (2Q25, 4Q25, 1Q26). Seven and a half months after publishing standalone targets, the company agreed to sell to ICE for $167 a share. Founder conviction built the moat; the operator found the moat alone no longer grew US credit revenue.

**What the chart says.**
- **One succession, done slowly.** Concannon was visibly the heir for four years (President & COO, director, owner of data and corporate development from Jan 2019). McVey's transition line in the FY22 letter frames it as strength, not crisis: "now is the right time for Chris Concannon, a proven leader deeply experienced in" electronic markets. McVey then stayed as Executive Chairman for 21 months.
- **Everything else turned over under the new CEO.** CFO (2024), a new COO layer (2025), CTO (2026), Americas product (2025), and the earlier EMEA leaders (Roupie, Paranandi) are not seen after 2024. *Inference:* Concannon rebuilt the top team around operators from exchange, data-vendor and big-bank technology backgrounds.
- **The board as a strategy map.** 2019–21 additions map to the growth plan: a buy-side trader who helped seed Open Trading (Prager), a dealer credit head (Gmelich), a diversity-dealer CEO (Gibson), China market structure (Li). 2023–26 additions map to the endgame: a JPMorgan investment-banking chair (Hernandez), a bank markets head (Hoornweg), a market-maker founder (Cifu) and a private-equity adviser (Schiciano). JPMorgan was later MKTX's financial adviser on the ICE sale and ran the Dec 2025 ASR. *(Inference; no release links these.)*
- **Severance as a tell.** The releases record repositioning charges "principally severance costs related to changes in management structure" in 2Q25 ($4.0M), 4Q25 ($1.1M) and 1Q26 ($1.5M).

## 2. Profiles

### Rick McVey — founder (2000), Chairman & CEO to Apr 2023, Executive Chairman to Dec 2024
**Background:** 40 years in fixed income by his own count; launched Open Trading in 2013. **Talks about:** all-to-all liquidity, client savings larger than revenue, automation, volatility as the swing factor. The voice of every earnings release 2019–22 and of four letters.
- **All-to-all is the moat.** "Without the open market — with its large, diverse pool of different types of participants" there would be more risk, not less (FY19 letter, Apr 2020). "We believe Open Trading liquidity was essential to the functioning of credit markets during the quarter" (1Q20, Apr 29 2020). FY20: "a pivotal year in which Open Trading established itself as an essential source of liquidity" (Apr 2021).
- **Savings as proof.** Clients "saved an estimated total of $1.1 billion in transaction costs, which significantly exceeds the Company’s total revenue in 2020" (FY20 letter). The number halved to $570M in FY21, and he wrote it as resilience: "Despite historically low volatility and tight bid/ask spreads" (FY21 letter).
- **Automation as the second engine.** "The trend to automated execution is gaining momentum in fixed income globally" (Apr 2019). "Trading automation and lower transaction costs on MarketAxess are driving a sustainable change in trading behavior among institutional market participants" (Oct 2020).
- **Candour about volatility.** 2Q21 results were "heavily impacted by low levels of credit market volatility in a very tough year over year comparison for MarketAxess" (Jul 2021). FY22 he admits the Fed cycle "negatively impacted our U.S. high-grade average variable fee per million and dampened our top-line growth."
- **Public-purpose register.** On the NY Fed SMCCF: "MarketAxess is honored to participate in this important program to support the recovery of the American economy" (Sep 2020). Purpose: "to open global markets, creating more opportunities for the companies, institutions, and individuals who depend on them" (FY21 letter).
- **How his view changed.** 2019 automation optimist → 2020 crisis-liquidity evangelist → 2021 defensive (volatility, "broader foundation") → 2022 macro optimist ("creates a long runway for growth in fixed income e-trading", Oct 2022). Exit: "it was an honor to lead this organization from a startup to a position of global leadership" (Nov 2024).

### Chris Concannon — President & COO Jan 2019 – Apr 2023, CEO Apr 2023 –
**Background:** CEO of Bats (led its 2016 IPO and sale to Cboe), then Cboe President & COO; earlier Nasdaq, Virtu, Instinet, and attorney at Morgan, Lewis & Bockius and the SEC (2019 release). **Talks about:** digitizing credit, ETFs and indexation, data as product, automation and AI, and from 2024 market share, channels and stockholder value.
- **The import mandate.** On joining: "Having been an active participant in the transformational changes of other asset classes," he would help as MarketAxess "continues to digitize the credit markets and build on its record of innovation and growth" (Jan 7 2019).
- **ETFs and indices first.** "We are still in the early days of the indexation of the fixed income markets" (Sweeney hire, Oct 2021). "MarketAxess has a unique view into the traditionally fragmented and less liquid fixed income market" (MKTX 400 index, Mar 2022).
- **As CEO-designate: automation and data.** Clients need technology "to compete more effectively in markets increasingly shaped by automation, data analytics and the growth in ETFs" (Jan 9 2023). Pragma brings "quantitative, AI-powered technology solutions powered by proprietary data designed to simplify and enhance their workflows" (Aug 2023).
- **2024: the phone is the target.** X-Pro is for "expanding our share of large-sized trades which are principally done over the phone" (1Q24). FY24 letter: "2024 was primarily a “build” year in our technology" and "We have a clear strategy to return to market share growth in U.S. credit across these three channels".
- **2025: open admission.** "To address our challenges in U.S. credit, we are focused on growing our targeted block trading solution in the client-initiated channel" (3Q25, Nov 2025). FY25 letter: "We still have work to do".
- **Auctions as signature bet.** "There’s no tradeable true auctions. This is the first." He also concedes, in the reporter's paraphrase, that success depends on adoption, and predicts: "Auctions will ultimately grow into other areas of the fixed-income market, particularly the US Treasury market" (Bloomberg, Nov 5 2025).
- **Dec 2025 → Jul 2026: from targets to scale.** "Our announcements today reflect the Board’s confidence in our long-term strategy and our commitment to driving stockholder value creation" (Dec 9 2025). Then, at the sale: "Together, we will have the scale to invest more deeply in the areas that matter most to our customers" (Jul 30 2026), conceding that ICE brings "additional retail and wealth trading protocols, strong data, connectivity".
- **Read.** He arrived to make bonds trade like equities and built every piece (order book, algos, sessions, auctions, ETF RFQ). None of it reversed the US-credit revenue decline the Dec 2025 deck admitted (−2% CAGR 2020–24); the operator-CEO sold to an exchange operator. *(Inference.)*

### Kat Sweeney — Head of Index & ETF Solutions (2021) → Global Head of Data & ETF Solutions
**Background:** SSGA Head of Institutional Client Group NA; 18 years at Goldman, building ETF market-making (Oct 2021 release). The ETF/auction/data voice, and the most quotable executive of 2025.
- **Index conviction.** "My career is firmly rooted in the belief that index solutions have benefited all market participants" (Oct 2021). "We’ve seen in our data how index products like ETFs are transforming the bond market" (Mar 2022).
- **Data to valuation.** The S&P deal is meant to "close the gaps between best execution, intraday trading decisions and end-of-day valuation" (Oct 2024).
- **Auctions, borrowed from equities.** "Auctions in derivatives and equity markets have proven to reduce volatility, increase transparency and concentrate liquidity" (Nov 2025). "Within the next couple of years, this could be the dominant protocol in the market" and "we’re now in the beginning of the snowball phase" (The TRADE, Dec 15 2025). The candid caveat: "We have to make sure the auction is a liquid event".

## 4. Communication style and narrative craft

**McVey — the founder-salesman.** Short, declarative, numbers-forward letters: "11th straight", "12th consecutive", "13th consecutive" year of records; savings set against revenue; a TAM framed as big-bank FICC revenue ($84B) against e-trading revenue (~$2B). He reaches for civic language in crises ("recovery of the American economy") and always closes by thanking employees. He rarely forecasts beyond "long runway".

**Concannon — the exchange operator.** He speaks in microstructure: *order-centric*, *multi-protocol*, *channels*, *sessions*, *auctions*, *orchestration*. He organises the business into frameworks that change (three channels from 2024; "MarketAxess advantage" from 2025) and admits problems in operator's terms — *build year*, "challenges in U.S. credit", "We still have work to do" — never with a number for share lost. His best lines are in interviews, not letters ("This is the first.").

**The bench — metaphor-light, data-heavy.** Sweeney's "snowball phase" and "bands of arbitrage"; Costa's "craving for transparency"; Chowdhury's "there’s no Asia"; Berry's "what it means". The research arm (Alexandre, quant analysts) argues by chart; it is the most self-critical voice in the firm (see [[marketaxess-open-trading]]).

## 6. Leadership lenses

- [[canon-kotter-leading-change]] — the founder-to-operator handover was a model guided coalition: four years as President, an announced date, the founder as Executive Chairman. The *change* that followed (protocol rebuild) lacked the second half of Kotter: short-term wins visible in the KPI the market watched (US credit share).
- [[canon-christensen-innovators-dilemma]] — McVey's all-to-all was a disruptive entry against dealer RFQ in 2013. By 2024 MKTX was the incumbent defending RFQ revenue while Tradeweb won by wiring the workflow around the trade ([[tradeweb-credit]]).
- [[canon-pfeffer-power]] — who holds power shows in reporting lines: CTO reports to a COO from LSEG; the Chair is a JPMorgan banker; a market-maker founder joins the board six months before a sale. Power moved from founder to board and buyer.
- [[canon-schein-organizational-culture]] — **artifact:** the *preferred all-to-all* boilerplate, unchanged since 2019. **Espoused value:** open markets. **Basic assumption (founder era):** anonymity and breadth beat relationships. Concannon's protocols quietly reversed that assumption (targeted, disclosed, curated) while the artifact stayed.
- [[canon-grove-high-output-management]] — indicators: the firm swapped its indicators (share → composite → channels) while performance worsened. Grove would call that losing the paired indicator.
- [[canon-bezos-shareholder-letters]] — McVey's letters repeat a metric streak; Concannon's rebuild the framework each year and never restate the last year's forecast (FY24's "benefits in the second half of 2025").
- [[canon-jensen-meckling-agency-theory]] — a debt-funded $300M ASR (Dec 2025) followed by a cash sale at a 33% premium. Ask whose time horizon each decision served.
