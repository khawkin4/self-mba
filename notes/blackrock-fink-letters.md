# Note · Larry Fink's letters, 2019–2026: how a CEO's public voice retreated in stages, with fiduciary duty as the constant that made each retreat sayable

> Communication / stakeholder note · Maps to **E7** (Executive Presence & High-Stakes Communication), **E8** (Stakeholder & Investor Relations), **Course 11** (Global Strategy, Macro & Responsible Business), **E4** (Corporate Governance), **Course 05** (Marketing & Brand: positioning) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[blackrock-hub]], [[blackrock-stewardship-backlash]], [[blackrock-mission-forces]]

- **Source:** all 13 Fink letters in `_ingest/raw/blackrock/articles.jsonl`, read in full:
  - CEO letters 2019–2022;
  - Chairman's letters to shareholders 2019–2022 (the 2019 and 2020 texts come from SEC DEFA14A filings);
  - the January 2020 client letter, signed by the Global Executive Committee (GEC);
  - the combined annual Chairman's letters to investors 2023–2026.

  Also used: the Dec 2025 Fink–Goldstein *Economist* op-ed, and the 2021–2023 AGM transcripts for how the letters were received. Word counts are from the reader notes in `_ingest/raw/blackrock/reading-notes/` (A and B).
- **Core claim:**
  - **Two letters a year until 2022, one from 2023.** Fink wrote a CEO letter (what BlackRock expects of the companies it owns) and a shareholder letter (BlackRock's own strategy). From 2023 there is a single "letter to investors", and the CEO letter, the vehicle for the climate and stakeholder message, disappears.
  - **The language moves in five steps:**
    1. purpose as profit's engine (2019);
    2. "fundamental reshaping of finance" (2020);
    3. "sustainability premium" (2021);
    4. *not woke* (2022);
    5. *not… the environmental police* (2023), then "energy pragmatism" (2024), then power for AI (2025), then "energy abundance" (2026).
  - **Climate drops out entirely.** "Climate" and "energy transition" appear zero times in the 2025 and 2026 letters, and "ESG" zero times in 2023–2026.
  - **One thing never moves.** *It is our clients' money, not ours* works as the hinge: it justified activism in 2020 and neutrality in 2023.
  - **The topics that replace climate** are retirement, capital markets displacing banks, private markets, tokenization, and from 2026 national self-reliance.

## 1. Chronology: the letters as a sequence

| Date | Letter | Headline idea | Signature line (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019-01-17 | CEO: *Purpose & Profit* | Purpose drives long-term profit; companies must fill the gap governments leave | "Purpose is not the sole pursuit of profits but the animating force for achieving them." |
| 2019-04-08 | Shareholder (FY2018) | Three-part strategy: portfolio construction, technology, high-growth markets incl. China | "And while these metrics are an important component of portfolio return, alpha doesn’t pay pensions." |
| 2020-01-14 | CEO: *A Fundamental Reshaping of Finance* | Climate risk = investment risk; threat to vote against directors | "awareness is rapidly changing, and I believe we are on the edge of a fundamental reshaping of finance." |
| 2020-01-14 | Client letter (GEC) | Sustainability as the "new standard": exit thermal coal, 100% ESG integration, join CA100+ | "We believe that sustainability should be our new standard for investing." |
| 2020-03-29/30 | Shareholder (FY2019), written during COVID | Bond ETFs modernize the bond market; "as good at it as any tech firm" | "While I have no intentions of leaving BlackRock anytime soon, I also will not be here forever." |
| 2021-01-26 | CEO | Net zero by 2050; companies to disclose a net zero-compatible plan | "companies with better ESG profiles are performing better than their peers, enjoying a “sustainability premium.”" |
| 2021-04 | Shareholder (FY2020) | Aladdin beyond BlackRock's walls; NZAM joined; culture review admitted | "I know our culture is not perfect." |
| 2022-01-18 | CEO: *The Power of Capitalism* | Stakeholder capitalism defended as capitalism; no divestment policy | "Stakeholder capitalism is not about politics. It is not a social or ideological agenda. It is not “woke.”" |
| 2022-03-24 | Shareholder (FY2021) | Russia ends globalization; energy security; first mention of stablecoins | "But the Russian invasion of Ukraine has put an end to the globalization we have experienced over the last three decades." |
| 2023-03-15 | **First combined letter** to investors, days after SVB | Capital markets replace banks; climate as client choice | "it is for governments to make policy and enact legislation, and not for companies, including asset managers, to be the environmental police." |
| 2024-03-26 | Combined: *Time to rethink retirement* | Retirement age; infrastructure; GIP; "energy pragmatism" | "I started writing about the transition in 2020. Since then, the issue has become more contentious in the U.S." |
| 2025-03-31 | Combined: *The democratization of investing* | No longer a traditional asset manager; 50/30/20; tokenization | "But we’ve been—first and foremost—a traditional asset manager. That’s who we were at the start of 2024. But it’s not who we are anymore." |
| 2025-12-01 | *Economist* op-ed with Rob Goldstein | Tokenization as a *bridge built from both sides* | "Ledgers haven't been this exciting since the invention of double-entry book-keeping" |
| 2026-03 | Combined: *Growing with your country* | Self-reliance; inequality; AI concentrating gains; Trump Accounts | "This is where much of today’s economic anxiety comes from: a deeper feeling that capitalism is working—just not for enough people." |

## 2. The retreat, phrase by phrase

| Theme | 2019–2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025–2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What drives profit** | Purpose is "the animating force" | "a more inclusive capitalism" | "the fair pursuit of profit is still what animates markets" | Fiduciary: "The money we manage belongs to them" (AGM) | Retirement as "core business" | Scale across public and private markets |
| **Climate** | "climate risk is investment risk"; "significant reallocation of capital" | "A Tectonic Shift Accelerates"; net zero | "not because we’re environmentalists"; "shades of brown to shades of green" | Risk only; "environmental police" | "energy pragmatism" | Power for AI and nuclear; "Energy abundance and affordability" |
| **BlackRock's voice** | "does not see itself as a passive observer"; voted against 4,800 directors in 2019 | "increasingly using our voice" | Voting Choice hands votes back | "As minority shareholders, it’s not our place to be telling companies what to do." | — | Voting Choice gone from the letters |
| **Who leads the transition** | Government must lead, companies have a role | Mandatory global disclosure, including private companies | Companies "cannot be the climate police" | Governments make policy | Activists "on the far left and right" | Not discussed |
| **Strategy pillar** | — | "global leader in sustainable investing" | Same | "leading in sustainable investing" | Same | **Gone** |

*How to read it:* in 2022 the verb *animates* moves from purpose to profit. That one-word change sums up the 2019→2022 retreat. In 2024 the backlash is admitted openly ("more contentious in the U.S."). From 2025 the subject simply changes.

## 3. What replaces climate: the topics that grow

- **Retirement.** In 2024 Fink argues the US retirement message is effectively "You’re on your own." The same letter makes the retirement-age remark "originates from the time of the Ottoman Empire". The 2026 letter admits: "It produced some criticism for even suggesting Social Security needed a fix. These comments probably will again now." The letters then back Trump Accounts and a Cassidy–Kaine investment fund.
- **Capital markets over banks.** "Over the longer term, today’s banking crisis will place greater importance on the role of capital markets." (2023)
- **Private markets.** The 50/30/20 portfolio and "index private markets". See [[blackrock-private-markets]].
- **Digital assets.** "Every stock, every bond, every fund—every asset—can be tokenized. If they are, it will revolutionize investing." (2025). See [[blackrock-digital-assets]].
- **Self-reliance and inequality.** 2026: "Now AI threatens to repeat that pattern at an even larger scale—concentrating wealth among the companies and investors positioned to capture it."

## 4. What the claims don't prove

1. **The "sustainability premium" (2021) is asserted, not shown.** The letter cites BlackRock's own fund data, with no independent factor-adjusted test. It disappears without a retraction.
2. **The "significant reallocation of capital" forecast (2020).** It is scored a success the next year by the same author ("accelerated even faster than I anticipated"), using flow numbers BlackRock chose.
3. **The fiduciary defence cuts both ways.** *Not our money* is used in 2020 to justify climate engagement and in 2023 to justify refusing it. The principle doesn't decide the policy; the politics do (*inference*).
4. **The 2023 format change is unexplained.** The letters never say why the CEO letter stopped. The NCPPR proponent at the 2023 AGM read it as tactical ("He's just stop talking about it."). That is a critic's reading, not evidence.
5. **"only 3% share of a fragmented industry’s revenue" (2023)** answers the size critique with one metric. Elsewhere BlackRock reports that it votes the shares of its index funds and runs the "largest stewardship team in the industry" (AGM 2021). Revenue share and voting influence are not the same measure.

## 5. Strategic read (inference — not BlackRock's words)

- **The letters work as positioning documents, and the audience changed.** In 2020–21 the swing audience was European asset owners and US public pensions that wanted climate commitments. By 2023 it was US state treasurers, attorneys general and Republican-led states that saw an energy boycott, plus a federal administration hostile to ESG from 2025. The letters track that change almost month by month ([[blackrock-stewardship-backlash]]).
- **Retreat by subject change, not reversal.** Fink never says the 2020 letter was wrong. Climate is reframed (risk → choice → pragmatism → abundance), then crowded out by retirement, AI power and nationalism. That limits reputational damage on both sides. Critics on the left can't quote a recantation; critics on the right can't quote a recent commitment.
- **The voice became more personal as it became less normative.** The 2024–26 letters open with parents, grandchildren and the record shop. Memoir replaces instruction to CEOs, which keeps the brand warm without new commitments.
- **The nationalism of 2026** ("Growing with your country") lines the firm up with industrial policy in the US, India, Japan and Germany. Those are the countries where BlackRock sells retirement products and infrastructure funds.

## 6. Canon lenses

- **Defining moments** ([[canon-badaracco-defining-moments]]): 2020 is a declared values stand. 2022–24 tests whether it was a value or a market bet. The letters resolve the tension by restating the value (fiduciary duty) and changing the policy.
- **Start with why** ([[canon-sinek-start-with-why]]): the *why* (financial well-being, fiduciary duty) stays stable while the *how* and *what* swing. Sinek would call that healthy. Critics call it opportunism.
- **Pyramid principle** ([[canon-minto-pyramid-principle]]): each letter opens with a governing thought ("fundamental reshaping", "not who we are anymore"). Read the first and last paragraphs of each year side by side as an exercise.
- **Resonate** ([[canon-duarte-resonate]]): the 2024–26 letters move to storytelling (the *what is / what could be* contrast of the retirement narrative).
- **Justice / stakeholder ethics** ([[canon-sandel-justice]]): whose interests a universal owner should weigh is the question the 2021 AGM proponents (The Shareholder Commons) pressed. See [[blackrock-outside-voices]].

## 7. Practitioner counterpoint

- **For Fink:** a fiduciary with clients on both sides of a political divide *should* move from advocacy to choice. Voting Choice, opt-in climate stewardship and the Egan-Jones policy are coherent answers. Record inflows of $641B (2024) and $698B (2025) suggest clients didn't punish the shift.
- **Against:** the 2020 letter moved markets and set expectations. A Paul Rissman proposal asking for climate stewardship got nearly 10% of the vote in 2023, the highest of any shareholder proposal in three years. Rissman claimed lost mandates in Europe. A CEO who leads with values and then retreats spends credibility that a CEO who never led doesn't have to spend.
- **Both:** the letters are best read as a *dated record of what the most important asset manager thought was safe to say*. That is valuable data even if no single letter is a reliable statement of policy.
