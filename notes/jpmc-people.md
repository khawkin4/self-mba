# Note · JPMorganChase's people — one voice (Dimon) argues policy, capital and culture in public; everyone else speaks in a narrower register, and the bench was built by rotating executives through each other's jobs

> Leadership / people note · Maps to **Course 09** (Leadership & Organizational Behavior), **E1** (Leading at Scale), **E7** (Executive Presence & High-Stakes Communication — the shareholder letter as a genre), **E4** (Governance — succession), **E12** (Culture as Strategy) · Written 2026-10-25 · Companion to [[jpmc-succession]] and [[tradeweb-people]]

- **Source:** `_ingest/raw/jpmc/articles.jsonl`: the signature blocks and texts of Dimon's and the line-of-business letters AR2019–AR2025, speakers on ten earnings calls and the 2025 Investor Day, and named executives in 140 web items. Profiles use only what the corpus shows. Mentions of a pronoun follow the source; where the source states none, this note uses names or they/them.
- **Core claim:** JPMorganChase has **one public strategist**. Dimon's letters run 10,000–36,000 words a year on policy, capital, regulation, geopolitics and management "learnings". Others speak in narrower lanes: **Barnum** (numbers, capital, regulation detail), **Piepszak** (technology and operations as COO), **Petno and Rohrbaugh** (the CIB), **Lake** (consumer), **Erdoes** (asset management), and **Farooq and Mallela** (Kinexys). Markets specialists appear only on product pages. The executives who matter for succession were **rotated through each other's jobs** (see [[jpmc-succession]]). The views that shifted most belong to Dimon, on competition, regulation posture and AI. Everyone else's views are stable because they speak only to their own businesses.

## 2. Profiles

### Jamie Dimon — Chairman & CEO
**Record in the corpus:** emergency heart surgery (Mar 2020); seven letters; speaks on every call; solo closing Q&A at Investor Day 2025; chairs the SRI advisory council. **Talks about:** regulation, capital, competition, geopolitics, America, management habits. **Signature lines by year:**
- 2020-04-06: "Entering into a crisis is not the time to figure out what you want to be."
- 2021-01-15: "I expect it to be very, very tough, brutal competition in the next 10 years. I expect to win, so help me God."
- 2022-04-04: "sometimes it truly is change or die"
- 2023-04-04: "Simply taking interest rate risk (which contributed to the downfall of SVB) is not a business."
- 2024-04-08: "Some regulators seem to think that market making is a speculative, hedge fund-like activity"
- 2025-04-07: "I underestimated the importance of cloud technology". Also: "I think our purpose is to lift up society, to help people, which is an enormous responsibility."
- 2025-05-19: "We have to stay humble here, okay."
- 2026-04-06: "Size can often be a tremendous business disadvantage because it frequently comes with the baggage of complexity, bureaucracy and complacency."

**Shifts:** competition (Big Tech → Citadel Securities and blockchain rivals), Basel posture ("adjust" → "un-American"), AI (near-absent in AR2024 → a full section in AR2025), blockchain ("real", 2022 → "spent too much", 2025 → "roll out our own", 2026). Detail in [[jpmc-mission-forces]], [[jpmc-capital-regulation]], [[jpmc-ai-tech-spend]] and [[jpmc-kinexys-deposit-tokens]].

### Jeremy Barnum — CFO (2021–)
**Talks about:** guidance, capital hierarchy, regulation mechanics, AI spend, "token expense". **Register:** careful and precise. Barnum corrects analysts' numbers (AI spend ~$700M, not ~$2B) and corrects Dimon (Rohrbaugh "was actually an options trader"). **Lines:** "it's impossible to prove, in some narrow financial sense, that there's a tangible return payback from that" (2022); "We're not going to be chasing shiny objects here in AI." (2024); "Consistency is part of the strategy." (2025); "the benefits aren't observable in our current quarterly earnings." (2026). Also from Investor Day 2025: "I've recently been indulging in what I've come to learn is known as “vibe coding,” a little bit, and it's actually like pretty amazing." Dimon on Barnum: "He's maturing really nicely."

### Jennifer Piepszak — CFO 2019–21 → CCB co-CEO → CIB co-CEO → COO (2025–)
**Path:** five seats in six years, the widest rotation in the corpus. **Talks about** (as COO): technology as identity, AI at enterprise scale, workforce. **Lines:** "We are a technology-driven company as much as we are a bank, and technology is fundamental to everything that we do." (AR2024). The AR2025 COO letter: "the goal is not fewer headcount; the goal is compounding performance with a growthfirst mindset." As CFO on the 4Q20 call: "satisfying these heightened requirements is certainly not costless". Dimon (4Q24): "I think it's wonderful that Jenn Piepszak, who does not want to be the CEO, will be here as Chief Operating Officer".

### Doug Petno — Commercial Banking CEO → CIB co-CEO → Co-President and CIB CEO (2026–)
**Talks about:** the client franchise, the CB–CIB merger, efficiency, self-disruption. **Lines (Investor Day 2025):** "we have real moats around our franchise." "Our opportunity to self-disrupt spreads across every single client journey in CIB". "CIB has over 175 AI use cases in production." With Rohrbaugh (AR2025): "Commercial success and social impact are mutually reinforcing." The AR2023 CIB letter says almost half of firmwide Payments revenue (>$8B) came from Commercial Banking clients, which was the case for the merger.

### Troy Rohrbaugh — Markets & Securities Services co-head → CIB co-CEO → Co-President and CCB CEO (2026–)
**Background as stated:** "an options trader" (Barnum, 2Q26). **Talks about:** the Markets fee pool, share, private credit, Securities Services. **Lines (Investor Day 2025):** "I think the broad fee pool in Markets won't go back to those pre-COVID levels." "So in Fixed Income it won't be easy, but I think we can gain share." "you need to have skin in the game to be in the ecosystem." With Piepszak (AR2023): "The competitive landscape for our businesses is intensifying, driven by both traditional banks as well as further growth of nonbank financial institutions." The 2026 move to Consumer is the corpus's biggest test of a trader in a retail seat ([[jpmc-succession]]).

### Daniel Pinto — CIB CEO 2012–2023; Co-President/President & COO; Vice Chairman 2025–26
**Talks about:** "global, complete and at scale", the ten-year CIB story, electronification, trading capital. **Lines:** "our steadfast pursuit of three strategic goals: being global, complete and at scale." (AR2019). "From long-established rivals to Big Tech and fintechs, the competitive threat is fierce and varied." (AR2021). "the capital required to fuel our global trading desks has risen significantly over the last five years." (AR2022). Dimon (2024-01-25): "Daniel and his team have built the finest Corporate and Investment Bank in the world". Retires at end-2026.

### Marianne Lake — CFO → Consumer Lending → CCB co-CEO → CCB CEO; retired 2026
**Talks about:** consumer scale, deposits, cards, regulation "barrage", stablecoins (Investor Day 2025: "we're all in" with conditions). AR2023 line: "More financial activity handled by nonbanks outside of the regulatory perimeter, increasing risk for consumers." Dimon on the exit (2Q26): Lake "decided, when she knew about the plan, that she'd rather retire than stay here." The corpus has no statement from Lake about leaving.

### Mary Callahan Erdoes — AWM CEO (2009–)
**Talks about:** fiduciary culture, alpha, flows, AI tools for advisers. **Lines:** "We strive to be the best, not the biggest." (AR2019). On AI (2024-05-28): "we are only scratching the surface of what we can do in the future for our clients and our employees". AWM flows: $194B (2019) → record $553B (2025) per the LOB letters.

### Umar Farooq and Max Neukirchen — Global Co-Heads of Payments; Naveen Mallela, Kara Kennedy, Oli Harris — Kinexys
Farooq is described as the founder of Kinexys. "If blockchain is going to disrupt traditional finance, we should own the disruption, rather than be disrupted by others." (2026-08-05). Neukirchen (Investor Day 2025): "Payments is becoming more and more strategic, evolving from a backoffice function to a key revenue driver for many of our clients." Mallela built JPM Coin and JPMD. Harris came from Goldman Digital Assets in Apr 2026. See [[jpmc-kinexys-deposit-tokens]].

### Technology and AI: Lori Beer (Global CIO), Teresa Heitsenrether (Chief Data & Analytics Officer), Derek Waldron (Chief Analytics Officer), Pat Opet (CISO)
- **Beer** (2021): "JPMorgan Chase is committed to innovation in our role as the unexpected disruptor in banking and in the technology."
- **Heitsenrether**, formerly head of Securities Services (launched Fusion in 2022), joined the Operating Committee in 2023 to run AI strategy.
- **Waldron**: "Our vision is to make JPMorganChase a truly AI-connected enterprise"
- **Opet**'s 2025 open letter to suppliers: "Software providers must prioritize security over rushing features."

### Todd Combs — Head of the SRI Strategic Investment Group (Jan 2026–)
Ex-Berkshire Hathaway investment manager and GEICO CEO; a JPMC director for nine years. Dimon: "Todd Combs is one of the greatest investors and leaders I’ve known". An outside heavyweight placed beside, not in, the CEO line.

## 3. Where perspectives differ or shift

| Topic | Who | Earlier | Later |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fintech and nonbanks | Dimon | Big Tech and Walmart (AR2020) | Block, Citadel Securities, Revolut, Stripe (AR2025) |
| Basel endgame | Dimon / Barnum | "adjust" (2023) / no extra dollars (Barnum, 4Q21) | "+25%" (2024) → "mixed", "un-American" G-SIB (2026) |
| AI payoff | Dimon vs Barnum | Dimon: "I would spend anything" (2021) | Barnum: "aren't observable" (2026); Dimon: "not a speculative bubble" (2026) |
| Blockchain | Dimon vs Farooq | Dimon: "real" (2022), "spent too much" (2025) | Farooq: "own the disruption" (2026); Dimon: "roll out our own" (2026) |
| Headcount and AI | Dimon vs Piepszak | Dimon: "30% or 40%" in discrete areas (2Q26) | Piepszak: "not fewer headcount" (AR2025) |
| Markets outlook | Rohrbaugh vs Dimon | Rohrbaugh: FICC "won't be easy" (2025) | Dimon: "the best FICC business in the world" (2026) |

**The sharpest tension:** Dimon's skepticism about blockchain spending sits beside a payments unit whose brand depends on blockchain. Dimon's job-cut candour sits beside a COO letter that says the goal is not fewer heads. These look less like disagreement and more like a division of labour. The CEO speaks to investors and policymakers; the business heads speak to clients and staff. *(Inference.)*

## 4. Communication style

- **Dimon — the essayist-advocate.** Long letters in numbered sections (I. company, II. management learnings, III. risks, IV. America). Recurring devices: Kipling's "If—"; Buffett's "silent partner"; "fortress balance sheet"; "not a conglomerate"; "walls... not particularly high"; Ted Lasso and Navy SEALs as management metaphors; admitted mistakes (cloud, the London Whale, "left the wrong person in a job for too long"). On calls: blunt, colloquial, impatient with metrics ("just don't do an NPV"), and uses humour to deflect succession questions.
- **Barnum — the translator.** Turns Dimon's positions into mechanics (SCB, G-SIB buckets, NII ex-Markets) and is the one who says "we don't know" ("known unknowns", "aren't observable").
- **Business heads — the scorecard.** LOB letters lead with records, ranks and share (#1 in X for the Nth year), then a strategy list (Pinto's three words; Rohrbaugh and Petno's seven priorities). Few opinions on policy.
- **What they rarely talk about:** venues by name ([[jpmc-markets-venues]]); Kinexys revenue; AI returns in dollars; the succession date; DEI after 2024 (the AR2025 letters use "DOI").

## 5. Leadership lenses

- **Leverage through others** ([[canon-grove-high-output-management]]): Dimon's 400-executive "master class" (AR2024) and town halls (60+ a year) are Grove's managerial leverage at scale.
- **Culture as the product** ([[canon-schein-organizational-culture]], [[canon-horowitz-what-you-do-is-who-you-are]]): "the disciplines and the culture of the place" is Dimon's succession answer. Horowitz would test it by what is built into processes (risk committees, rotations) rather than said.
- **Candour** ([[canon-scott-radical-candor]]): admitted mistakes in every letter are an unusual CEO practice. They make the rest more credible.
- **Power** ([[canon-pfeffer-power]]): one person holds the policy, capital and political voice. That is the transition risk.
