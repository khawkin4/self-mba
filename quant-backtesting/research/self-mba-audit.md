Here is my report on the quantitative finance, markets, trading, and investment-related content in `~/self-mba`.

---

**Existing coverage is fundamentally MBA-style (qualitative/strategic), not quantitative-finance or trading-oriented.** There is zero content on backtesting, systematic/algorithmic trading, factor models, time-series analysis, portfolio construction, Monte Carlo simulation, or risk-parity. The curriculum is a 12-course general MBA program, not a quant-finance track.

**What IS covered, in the investment/markets neighborhood:**

- **Corporate Finance and Valuation** (Course 02, `learn/unit-06-whats-a-business-worth.md`): DCF intuition, present value, discount rates. Beginner-level, conceptual -- no implemented models or code.
- **Stock-picking framework** (`notes/tiger-sisters-stock-picking.md`): a discretionary (non-quantitative) 6-rule process from a hedge fund manager -- understand the business, read filings, assess consensus, valuation last. Purely qualitative.
- **Applied stock analysis** (`notes/apply-stock-picking-nke.md`): the above framework applied to Nike using SEC/EDGAR data. Financial-statement analysis, not quantitative modeling.
- **Value investing theory** (`notes/canon/canon-klarman-margin-of-safety.md`): margin of safety, intrinsic value, downside-first thinking. Philosophical, not computational.
- **Wealth/deal mechanics** (`notes/tiger-sisters-wealth-mechanics.md`): real-estate deal structures, seller financing, sweat equity. Entrepreneurial dealmaking, not markets.
- **SEC filing analysis** (`notes/sec-filing-intelligence.md`): reading 10-Ks, using EDGAR.
- **Capital allocation** (exec Course E2): buybacks vs. dividends, the Outsiders CEO framework.
- **Global macro** (Course 11): geopolitics and country risk, not quantitative macro strategies.
- **Statistics/quant gap** (lesson file `gaps-statistics-quant.html`): explicitly flagged as a gap in the curriculum.

**Bottom line:** The user has a solid foundation in reading financial statements, fundamental (discretionary) stock analysis, and valuation concepts. There is no content whatsoever on systematic trading, backtesting, quantitative modeling, portfolio optimization, or programmatic market strategies. The curriculum itself acknowledges statistics/quant as a gap.
